E1D: Amateur Space and Earth stations; telemetry and telecommand rules; identification of balloon transmissions; one-way communications

Group E1D of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E1D01

What is the definition of telemetry?

  1. A One-way transmission of measurements at a distance from the measuring instrument
  2. B Two-way transmissions in excess of 1000 feet
  3. C Two-way transmissions of data
  4. D One-way transmission that initiates, modifies, or terminates the functions of a device at a distance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. One-way transmission of measurements at a distance from the measuring instrument

Why A is right. A. Telemetry is defined by measurements flowing one way, from the instrument to a distant receiver.

A satellite sending its battery voltage and temperature to the ground is a typical example. Signals that turn functions on or off at a distance are called telecommand instead.

“Telemetry. A one-way transmission of measurements at a distance from the measuring instrument.”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(46)

Why not D? Starting, changing or stopping a device's functions is telecommand, not telemetry.

E1D02

Which of the following may transmit encrypted messages?

  1. A Telecommand signals to terrestrial repeaters
  2. B Telecommand signals from a space telecommand station
  3. C Auxiliary relay links carrying repeater audio
  4. D Mesh network backbone nodes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Telecommand signals from a space telecommand station

Why B is right. B. Codes keep others from taking over a satellite, so the rule makes telecommand to a space station an exception to the ban on hiding a message's meaning.

Other amateur traffic, such as repeater links or mesh networks, may not be encoded to hide its meaning.

“A telecommand station may transmit special codes intended to obscure the meaning of telecommand messages to the station in space operation.”

47 CFR 97.211(b)

Why not A? The exception covers commands to a space station, not to a repeater on the ground.

E1D03

What is a space telecommand station?

  1. A An amateur station located on the surface of the Earth for communication with other Earth stations by means of Earth satellites
  2. B An amateur station that transmits communications to initiate, modify, or terminate functions of a space station
  3. C An amateur station located in a satellite or a balloon more than 50 kilometers above the surface of the Earth
  4. D An amateur station that receives telemetry from a satellite or balloon more than 50 kilometers above the surface of the Earth
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. An amateur station that transmits communications to initiate, modify, or terminate functions of a space station

Why B is right. B. A telecommand station is defined by what it does: it sends commands that control a space station's functions.

Ground controllers use telecommand to switch a satellite's transmitters, change its modes or turn it off. A station in space is a space station. A ground station that talks through a satellite is an Earth station.

“Telecommand station. An amateur station that transmits communications to initiate, modify or terminate functions of a space station.”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(45)

Why not C? A station more than 50 km up is a space station, not a telecommand station.

E1D04

Which of the following is required in the identification transmissions from a balloon-borne telemetry station?

  1. A Call sign
  2. B The output power of the balloon transmitter
  3. C The station's six-character Maidenhead grid locator
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Call sign

Why A is right. A. A balloon telemetry station must send its call sign like any other amateur station.

Balloons fly below 50 km, so they are not space stations, and the space station exemption does not apply. The call sign must go out at the end of each communication and at least every 10 minutes. Power and grid locator are optional extras.

“must transmit its assigned call sign on its transmitting channel at the end of each communication, and at least every 10 minutes during a communication”

47 CFR 97.119(a)

Why not C? A grid locator may be useful, but the rule requires only the call sign.

E1D05

What must be posted at the location of a station being operated by telecommand on or within 50 kilometers of the Earth’s surface?

  1. A A photocopy of the station license
  2. B A label with the name, address, and telephone number of the station licensee
  3. C A label with the name, address, and telephone number of the control operator
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. The station must have a copy of the license and a label naming the licensee and at least one control operator, with addresses and phone numbers.

A station under telecommand often has no one there. If it causes a problem, anyone who finds it needs to know whose it is and how to reach a person who can shut it down.

“A photocopy of the station license and a label with the name, address, and telephone number of the station licensee and at least one designated control operator is posted in a conspicuous place”

47 CFR 97.213(d)

E1D06

What is the maximum permitted transmitter output power when operating a model craft by telecommand?

  1. A 1 watt
  2. B 2 watts
  3. C 5 watts
  4. D 100 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 1 watt

Why A is right. A. Model craft need only short range, and the rule caps their control transmitters at 1 W to keep interference low.

These control signals also do not need to send station ID if the transmitter carries a label with the call sign and the licensee's name and address.

“The transmitter power must not exceed 1 W.”

47 CFR 97.215(c)

Why not C? 5 watts is above the limit.

E1D07

Which of the following HF amateur bands include allocations for space stations?

  1. A 40 meters, 20 meters, 15 meters, and 10 meters
  2. B 30 meters, 17 meters, and 10 meters
  3. C Only 10 meters
  4. D Satellite operation is permitted on all HF bands
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 40 meters, 20 meters, 15 meters, and 10 meters

Why A is right. A. Space stations may use 7.0-7.1 MHz and 14.00-14.25 MHz, plus all of 15 and 10 meters (and 17 and 12), so 40, 20, 15 and 10 meters all qualify.

The space station rule lists whole bands and smaller segments. On 40 and 20 meters only part of the band is open to space stations. The 30 meter band is not on the list, which rules out choice B.

“The 17 m, 15 m, 12 m, and 10 m bands … The 7.0-7.1 MHz, 14.00-14.25 MHz”

47 CFR 97.207(c)

Why not B? 30 meters is not authorized for space stations.

E1D08

Which VHF amateur bands have frequencies authorized for space stations?

  1. A 6 meters and 2 meters
  2. B 6 meters, 2 meters, and 1.25 meters
  3. C 2 meters and 1.25 meters
  4. D 2 meters
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 2 meters

Why D is right. D. The only VHF segment open to space stations is 144-146 MHz, part of 2 meters.

Neither 6 meters nor 1.25 meters appears on the space station list. Satellites using VHF operate between 144 and 146 MHz.

“144-146 MHz”

47 CFR 97.207(c)

Why not B? 6 meters and 1.25 meters are not authorized for space stations.

E1D09

Which UHF amateur bands have frequencies authorized for space stations?

  1. A 70 centimeters only
  2. B 70 centimeters and 13 centimeters
  3. C 70 centimeters and 33 centimeters
  4. D 33 centimeters and 13 centimeters
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 70 centimeters and 13 centimeters

Why B is right. B. Space stations may use 435-438 MHz (70 cm) and 2400-2450 MHz (13 cm).

UHF runs from 300 MHz to 3 GHz. Of the amateur bands in that range, only these two segments are on the space station list. The 33 cm band is not.

“435-438 MHz, 2400-2450 MHz”

47 CFR 97.207(c)

Why not C? 33 cm is not authorized for space stations.

E1D10

Which amateur stations are eligible to be telecommand stations of space stations, subject to the privileges of the class of operator license held by the control operator of the station?

  1. A Any amateur station approved by AMSAT
  2. B Any amateur station so designated by the space station licensee
  3. C Any amateur station so designated by the ITU
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Any amateur station so designated by the space station licensee

Why B is right. B. The space station's licensee is responsible for the satellite, so the rule lets that licensee choose which stations may command it.

No outside group, such as AMSAT or the ITU, makes that choice under the rule. The control operator still stays within their own license privileges.

“Any amateur station designated by the licensee of a space station is eligible to transmit as a telecommand station for that space station”

47 CFR 97.211(a)

Why not A? AMSAT approval is not what the rule requires.

E1D11

Which amateur stations are eligible to operate as Earth stations?

  1. A Any amateur licensee who has successfully completed the AMSAT space communications course
  2. B Only those of General, Advanced or Amateur Extra class operators
  3. C Only those of Amateur Extra class operators
  4. D Any amateur station, subject to the privileges of the class of operator license held by the control operator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Any amateur station, subject to the privileges of the class of operator license held by the control operator

Why D is right. D. The rule sets no special class or training for Earth stations; each operator works within the privileges they already have.

An Earth station communicates through satellites or other objects in space. You still must stay inside your license class's frequencies.

“Any amateur station may be an Earth station.”

47 CFR 97.209(a)

Why not C? Any class may operate an Earth station, not only Extra.

E1D12

Which of the following amateur stations may transmit one-way communications?

  1. A A space station, beacon station, or telecommand station
  2. B A local repeater or linked repeater station
  3. C A message forwarding station or automatically controlled digital station
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A space station, beacon station, or telecommand station

Why A is right. A. Each of these three is named in the rules as allowed to send one-way transmissions, while repeaters and message systems are not.

Most amateur stations must hold two-way contacts, not broadcast. Space stations may send one-way under 97.207(e), beacons under 97.203(g), and telecommand stations under 97.211(d) and 97.111(b)(3).

“A beacon may transmit one-way communications.”

47 CFR 97.203(g)

Why not B? Repeaters relay two-way conversations; they are not on the one-way list.
