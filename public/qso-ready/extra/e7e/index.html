E7E: Modulation and demodulation: reactance, phase, and balanced modulators; detectors; mixers

Group E7E of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E7E01

Which of the following can be used to generate FM phone signals?

  1. A Balanced modulation of the audio amplifier
  2. B Reactance modulation of a local oscillator
  3. C Reactance modulation of the final amplifier
  4. D Balanced modulation of a local oscillator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Reactance modulation of a local oscillator

Why B is right. B. A reactance modulator changes the oscillator's tuning in step with the audio, shifting its frequency to make FM.

A reactance modulator is a circuit, often a varactor diode, whose capacitance changes with applied voltage. Placed in the oscillator's tuned circuit, it moves the frequency up and down with the voice. Later stages amplify or multiply this signal.

Why not C? The final amplifier has no frequency-setting circuit, so modulating its reactance would not produce proper FM.

E7E02

What is the function of a reactance modulator?

  1. A Produce PM or FM signals by varying a resistance
  2. B Produce AM signals by varying an inductance
  3. C Produce AM signals by varying a resistance
  4. D Produce PM or FM signals by varying a capacitance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Produce PM or FM signals by varying a capacitance

Why D is right. D. Changing a capacitance in a tuned circuit shifts its phase or resonant frequency, so varying it with audio gives PM or FM.

A reactance modulator acts like a capacitor whose value follows the audio voltage. In an oscillator it moves the frequency, giving FM. In a later stage it shifts the phase, giving PM. Amplitude is not what it changes.

Why not A? Varying a resistance mostly changes signal strength, not phase or frequency.

E7E03

What is a frequency discriminator?

  1. A An FM generator circuit
  2. B A circuit for filtering closely adjacent signals
  3. C An automatic band-switching circuit
  4. D A circuit for detecting FM signals
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. A circuit for detecting FM signals

Why D is right. D. A frequency discriminator turns changes in frequency into changes in voltage, recovering the audio from an FM signal.

Its tuned circuits are set so the output is zero at the center frequency, positive above it and negative below. As the FM signal swings with the voice, the output voltage swings the same way, reproducing the audio.

Why not A? A discriminator decodes FM; it does not generate it.

E7E04

What is one way to produce a single-sideband phone signal?

  1. A Use a balanced modulator followed by a filter
  2. B Use a reactance modulator followed by a mixer
  3. C Use a loop modulator followed by a mixer
  4. D Use a product detector with a DSB signal
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Use a balanced modulator followed by a filter

Why A is right. A. A balanced modulator makes both sidebands with the carrier canceled, and a sharp filter then removes the unwanted sideband.

A balanced modulator mixes audio with a carrier so that the carrier cancels at the output. What remains is double sideband, upper and lower. A crystal or mechanical filter then passes only one of them, leaving SSB.

Why not D? A product detector recovers audio from SSB in a receiver; it does not create SSB.

E7E05

What is added to an FM speech channel to boost the higher audio frequencies?

  1. A A de-emphasis network
  2. B A harmonic enhancer
  3. C A heterodyne enhancer
  4. D A pre-emphasis network
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. A pre-emphasis network

Why D is right. D. Pre-emphasis boosts the higher audio frequencies before modulation so they stay above the noise that FM adds in that range.

In FM, the noise heard at the receiver output rises with audio frequency, while the high sounds in speech are usually weak. The transmitter lifts the highs, and the receiver lowers them by the same amount with de-emphasis. The overall audio comes out flat, but with less hiss.

Why not A? De-emphasis lowers the highs in the receiver; it does not boost them.

E7E06

Why is de-emphasis used in FM communications receivers?

  1. A For compatibility with transmitters using phase modulation
  2. B To reduce impulse noise reception
  3. C For higher efficiency
  4. D To remove third-order distortion products
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. For compatibility with transmitters using phase modulation

Why A is right. A. Phase modulation naturally boosts higher audio frequencies, so de-emphasis in the receiver restores a flat response for those signals.

In PM, the frequency swing grows with audio frequency, so PM sounds like FM with built-in pre-emphasis. Receiver de-emphasis cuts the highs back to match. As a bonus, high-frequency noise in the receiver drops at the same time.

Why not C? De-emphasis shapes the audio response; it has no effect on efficiency.

E7E07

What is meant by the term “baseband” in radio communications?

  1. A The lowest frequency band that the transmitter or receiver covers
  2. B The frequency range occupied by a message signal prior to modulation
  3. C The unmodulated bandwidth of the transmitted signal
  4. D The basic oscillator frequency in an FM transmitter that is multiplied to increase the deviation and carrier frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The frequency range occupied by a message signal prior to modulation

Why B is right. B. Base refers to the message itself, such as voice audio or data, in its own low frequency range before modulation moves it onto a carrier.

Voice runs from roughly 300 to 3,000 Hz, and that range is its baseband. Modulation shifts it up to the operating frequency, and the receiver's detector brings it back down. Digital systems also speak of baseband for the raw data stream.

Why not A? The lowest band a radio covers is a tuning range, not the unmodulated message.

E7E08

What are the principal frequencies that appear at the output of a mixer?

  1. A Two and four times the input frequency
  2. B The square root of the product of input frequencies
  3. C The two input frequencies along with their sum and difference frequencies
  4. D 1.414 and 0.707 times the input frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The two input frequencies along with their sum and difference frequencies

Why C is right. C. A mixer multiplies its two inputs, which produces their sum and difference frequencies, and the original two leak through as well.

Multiplying two sine waves gives new waves at the sum and the difference of their frequencies. Real mixers also pass some of each input. Mixing 14.2 MHz with 9 MHz gives 23.2 MHz and 5.2 MHz. A filter then picks the one you want.

Why not A? Doubling or quadrupling a frequency is what a multiplier stage does, not a mixer's main output.

E7E09

What occurs when the input signal levels to a mixer are too high?

  1. A Spurious mixer products are generated
  2. B Mixer blanking occurs
  3. C Automatic limiting occurs
  4. D Excessive AGC voltage levels are generated
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Spurious mixer products are generated

Why A is right. A. Overdriving a mixer pushes it far into nonlinear operation, so it creates extra unwanted products, called spurs.

A mixer is meant to produce only sum and difference frequencies. Strong inputs drive it past that range, creating harmonics of each input, and those mix with each other too. The result is many spurious signals, which can show up as false signals in a receiver.

Why not C? Mixers have no built-in limiting; too much drive adds products instead of capping the level.

E7E10

How does a diode envelope detector function?

  1. A By rectification and filtering of RF signals
  2. B By breakdown of the Zener voltage
  3. C By mixing signals with noise in the transition region of the diode
  4. D By sensing the change of reactance in the diode with respect to frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. By rectification and filtering of RF signals

Why A is right. A. The diode rectifies the RF, passing one polarity, and a capacitor smooths the peaks so the output follows the audio envelope.

An AM signal's peaks trace out the audio; that outline is called the envelope. The diode removes half of each RF cycle. A capacitor and resistor charge to each peak and discharge slowly, filtering out the RF while following the slower audio changes.

Why not B? An envelope detector uses an ordinary diode in forward conduction, not Zener breakdown.

E7E11

Which type of detector is used for demodulating SSB signals?

  1. A Discriminator
  2. B Phase detector
  3. C Product detector
  4. D Phase comparator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Product detector

Why C is right. C. A product detector mixes the SSB signal with a locally made carrier, and the difference frequencies that result are the audio.

SSB is sent with no carrier. A product detector multiplies the incoming sideband by a beat frequency oscillator, or BFO, set where the carrier would be. The difference frequencies land back in the audio range.

Why not A? A discriminator responds to frequency swings, which suits FM, not SSB.
