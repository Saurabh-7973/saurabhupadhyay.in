E9A: Basic antenna parameters: radiation resistance, gain, beamwidth, efficiency; effective radiated power (ERP) and effective isotropic radiated power (EIRP)

Group E9A of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E9A01

What is an isotropic radiator?

  1. A A calibrated, unidirectional antenna used to make precise antenna gain measurements
  2. B An omnidirectional, horizontally polarized, precisely calibrated antenna used to make field measurements of antenna gain
  3. C A hypothetical, lossless antenna having equal radiation intensity in all directions used as a reference for antenna gain
  4. D A spacecraft antenna used to direct signals toward Earth
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A hypothetical, lossless antenna having equal radiation intensity in all directions used as a reference for antenna gain

Why C is right. C. An isotropic radiator is an imaginary, perfect antenna that sends equal power in every direction, giving a fixed baseline for gain.

No real antenna radiates equally in all directions, but the idea is useful as a yardstick. Gain stated against it is written dBi, meaning decibels over isotropic. A half-wave dipole, for example, has about 2.15 dBi in its best direction. Because the isotropic radiator is lossless and has no favored direction, every real antenna can be compared to it.

Why not B? An isotropic radiator is hypothetical and has no polarization preference; it cannot be built or calibrated.

E9A02

What is the effective radiated power (ERP) of a repeater station with 150 watts transmitter power output, 2 dB feed line loss, 2.2 dB duplexer loss, and 7 dBd antenna gain?

  1. A 469 watts
  2. B 78.7 watts
  3. C 420 watts
  4. D 286 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 286 watts

Why D is right. D. The net gain is 7 - 2 - 2.2 = 2.8 dB, and 2.8 dB is a power ratio of about 1.91, so 150 W becomes about 286 W.

ERP (effective radiated power) is transmitter power times all gains and losses on the way out, compared with a dipole. Because the antenna gain is in dBd (decibels over a dipole), no conversion is needed. Add gains and subtract losses in dB, then turn the total into a ratio.

ERP = P × 10^(net dB / 10)
net = 7 dBd - 2 dB - 2.2 dB = 2.8 dB
10^(2.8/10) = 10^0.28 = 1.905
ERP = 150 W × 1.905 = 285.8 W, rounded to 286 W
= 286 watts

Why not A? 469 W would come from adding the antenna gain and ignoring the 4.2 dB of losses.

E9A03

What term describing total radiated power takes into account all gains and losses?

  1. A Power factor
  2. B Half-power bandwidth
  3. C Effective radiated power
  4. D Apparent power
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Effective radiated power

Why C is right. C. Effective radiated power starts with transmitter output and applies every loss and the antenna gain to show what is actually sent.

Feed line, duplexers and other parts each eat some power. The antenna then concentrates what is left in a favored direction. ERP rolls all of that into one number: the power a reference dipole would need to give the same signal in the best direction. It is the figure repeater coordinators and rules usually care about.

Why not D? Apparent power is volts times amps in an AC circuit; it says nothing about antenna gain or feed line loss.

E9A04

Which of the following factors affect the feed point impedance of an antenna?

  1. A Transmission line length
  2. B Antenna height
  3. C The settings of an antenna tuner at the transmitter
  4. D The input power level
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Antenna height

Why B is right. B. Ground acts like a reflecting surface, and its distance from the antenna changes the currents in the antenna and so its feed point impedance.

Waves reflected from the ground return to the antenna and add to or subtract from its own current. How they combine depends on height measured in wavelengths. A dipole's feed point impedance swings above and below its free-space value as it is raised. The feed line, tuner and power level change what the transmitter sees, but not the antenna's own impedance.

Why not A? Feed line length changes the impedance seen at the transmitter end, not the impedance at the antenna's feed point.

E9A05

What does the term “ground gain” mean?

  1. A The change in signal strength caused by grounding the antenna
  2. B The gain of the antenna with respect to a dipole at ground level
  3. C To force net gain to 0 dB by grounding part of the antenna
  4. D An increase in signal strength from ground reflections in the environment of the antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. An increase in signal strength from ground reflections in the environment of the antenna

Why D is right. D. Ground gain is the boost a signal gets when waves reflected off the ground add in step with the direct wave.

A horizontal antenna above ground sends some energy straight out and some down to the ground, where it bounces. At certain elevation angles the two waves arrive in phase and add up. In those directions the signal can be up to about 6 dB stronger than the same antenna in free space. Grounding the antenna is not involved.

Why not A? Ground gain comes from reflections, not from connecting part of the antenna to ground.

E9A06

What is the effective radiated power (ERP) of a repeater station with 200 watts transmitter power output, 4 dB feed line loss, 3.2 dB duplexer loss, 0.8 dB circulator loss, and 10 dBd antenna gain?

  1. A 317 watts
  2. B 2,000 watts
  3. C 126 watts
  4. D 300 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 317 watts

Why A is right. A. The losses total 8 dB against 10 dBd of gain, a net 2 dB, which is a factor of about 1.585, so 200 W becomes about 317 W.

Add all the losses in dB: feed line, duplexer and circulator. Subtract them from the antenna gain. The antenna gain is in dBd, so the result is ERP with no dipole conversion. Then turn the net dB into a power ratio and multiply.

ERP = P × 10^(net dB / 10)
net = 10 dBd - 4 dB - 3.2 dB - 0.8 dB = 2 dB
10^(2/10) = 10^0.2 = 1.585
ERP = 200 W × 1.585 = 317 W
= 317 watts

Why not B? 2,000 W applies the full 10 dB gain and ignores all 8 dB of losses.

E9A07

What is the effective isotropic radiated power (EIRP) of a repeater station with 200 watts transmitter power output, 2 dB feed line loss, 2.8 dB duplexer loss, 1.2 dB circulator loss, and 7 dBi antenna gain?

  1. A 159 watts
  2. B 252 watts
  3. C 632 watts
  4. D 63.2 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 252 watts

Why B is right. B. Losses total 6 dB against 7 dBi of gain, a net 1 dB, which is a factor of about 1.26, so 200 W becomes about 252 W.

EIRP (effective isotropic radiated power) compares the result with an isotropic radiator, so it uses gain in dBi. The antenna gain is already in dBi, so apply it directly. Subtract feed line, duplexer and circulator losses, then convert the net dB to a ratio.

EIRP = P × 10^(net dB / 10)
net = 7 dBi - 2 dB - 2.8 dB - 1.2 dB = 1 dB
10^(1/10) = 10^0.1 = 1.259
EIRP = 200 W × 1.259 = 251.8 W, rounded to 252 W
= 252 watts

Why not A? 159 W would come from a net loss of 1 dB; here the net is a 1 dB gain.

E9A08

Which frequency band has the smallest first Fresnel zone?

  1. A 5.8 GHz
  2. B 3.4 GHz
  3. C 2.4 GHz
  4. D 900 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 5.8 GHz

Why A is right. A. Fresnel zone size grows with wavelength, and 5.8 GHz has the shortest wavelength of the choices, so its zone is smallest.

The first Fresnel zone is a football-shaped region around the straight line between two antennas. Objects inside it can block or reflect part of the signal. Its radius at any point is about the square root of the wavelength times the path distances, so it gets smaller as frequency goes up. Higher bands need less clearance around the path.

Why not D? 900 MHz has the longest wavelength listed, so it has the largest Fresnel zone, not the smallest.

E9A09

What is antenna efficiency?

  1. A Radiation resistance divided by transmission resistance
  2. B Radiation resistance divided by total resistance
  3. C Total resistance divided by radiation resistance
  4. D Effective radiated power divided by transmitter output
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Radiation resistance divided by total resistance

Why B is right. B. Efficiency is the share of input power that is radiated, which equals radiation resistance over total resistance.

Radiation resistance is the part of the feed point resistance that stands for power leaving as radio waves. Loss resistance, from wire, coils and ground, stands for power turned into heat. Total resistance is the two added together. For example, 36 ohms of radiation resistance and 36 ohms of loss gives 50 percent efficiency.

Why not C? Total over radiation resistance is the efficiency ratio flipped, and it would give values above 100 percent.

E9A10

Which of the following improves the efficiency of a ground-mounted quarter-wave vertical antenna?

  1. A Installing a ground radial system
  2. B Isolating the coax shield from ground
  3. C Shortening the radiating element
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Installing a ground radial system

Why A is right. A. Radials give the antenna's return current a low-resistance path, cutting the ground loss that wastes power.

A ground-mounted quarter-wave vertical uses the ground as the other half of the antenna. Current flowing back through poor soil turns power into heat. Wires laid out from the base like spokes carry that current instead. More radials lower the loss resistance, so a larger share of the power radiates.

Why not C? A shorter radiator lowers radiation resistance, so losses take a larger share and efficiency drops.

E9A11

Which of the following determines ground losses for a ground-mounted vertical antenna operating on HF?

  1. A The standing wave ratio
  2. B Distance from the transmitter
  3. C Soil conductivity
  4. D Take-off angle
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Soil conductivity

Why C is right. C. Return currents flow through the earth near a vertical, so the soil's conductivity sets how much power is lost as heat.

Good conductors carry current with little loss. Moist, mineral-rich soil conducts fairly well; dry sand or rock conducts poorly. Over poor soil, more of the transmitter power heats the ground near the antenna. That is why radial systems matter most where the soil is poor.

Why not D? Take-off angle is a result of the antenna and ground; it does not cause losses in the ground.

E9A12

How much gain does an antenna have compared to a half-wavelength dipole if it has 6 dB gain over an isotropic radiator?

  1. A 3.85 dB
  2. B 6.0 dB
  3. C 8.15 dB
  4. D 2.79 dB
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 3.85 dB

Why A is right. A. A half-wave dipole has 2.15 dB gain over isotropic, so 6 dBi is 6 - 2.15 = 3.85 dB over a dipole.

dBi measures gain against an isotropic radiator; dBd measures it against a half-wave dipole. The dipole is itself 2.15 dB above isotropic. To change dBi to dBd, subtract 2.15. To go the other way, add 2.15.

dBd = dBi - 2.15
dBd = 6 - 2.15
= 3.85 dB

Why not C? 8.15 dB adds the 2.15 dB instead of subtracting it.
