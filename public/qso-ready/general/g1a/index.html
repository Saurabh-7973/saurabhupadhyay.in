G1A: General class control operator frequency privileges; primary and secondary allocations

Group G1A of the General pool (2023–2027) has 10 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G1A01

On which HF and/or MF amateur bands are there portions where General class licensees cannot transmit?

  1. A 60 meters, 30 meters, 17 meters, and 12 meters
  2. B 160 meters, 60 meters, 15 meters, and 12 meters
  3. C 80 meters, 40 meters, 20 meters, and 15 meters
  4. D 80 meters, 20 meters, 15 meters, and 10 meters
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 80 meters, 40 meters, 20 meters, and 15 meters

Why C is right. C. On each of these four bands, the General table starts above the Extra table's lower edge, so part of the band is closed to General operators.

Compare the General rows in 97.301(d) with the Amateur Extra rows in 97.301(b). General starts at 3.525 MHz on 80 m, 7.025 MHz on 40 m, 14.025 MHz on 20 m and 21.025 MHz on 15 m, and also misses slices of the phone parts of those bands. On 160, 60, 30, 17, 12 and 10 meters, General has the same frequencies as Extra.

“80 m | 3.525-3.600 | 3.525-3.600 | 3.525-3.600 | (a) … 20 m | 14.025-14.150 | 14.025-14.150 | 14.025-14.150”

47 CFR 97.301(d)

Why not D? General may use all of 10 meters, 28.000 to 29.700 MHz.

G1A02

On which of the following bands is phone operation prohibited?

  1. A 160 meters
  2. B 30 meters
  3. C 17 meters
  4. D 12 meters
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 30 meters

Why B is right. B. The emission table lists only RTTY and data for the whole 30-meter band, so voice is not allowed there.

Part 97 says which emission types each band segment allows. For 30 meters (10.100 to 10.150 MHz), the table lists RTTY and data across the entire band, plus CW, which is allowed on any authorized frequency. Phone (voice) and image modes are not listed. The band is narrow and shared, so it is kept for narrow signals.

“(viii) 30 m | Entire band | RTTY, data | (f)(3).”

47 CFR 97.305(c)

Why not A? 160 meters allows phone and image across the entire band.

G1A03

On which of the following bands is image transmission prohibited?

  1. A 160 meters
  2. B 30 meters
  3. C 20 meters
  4. D 12 meters
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 30 meters

Why B is right. B. Only RTTY and data are listed for the 30-meter band, so image modes such as SSTV and fax are not permitted.

Image emissions, like slow-scan TV and facsimile, are allowed only where the 97.305(c) table lists them. On 30 meters (10.100 to 10.150 MHz) the table lists RTTY and data for the entire band, and CW is allowed everywhere. Phone and image are missing from the 30-meter row, so both are banned there.

“(viii) 30 m | Entire band | RTTY, data | (f)(3).”

47 CFR 97.305(c)

Why not C? 20 meters allows image from 14.15 to 14.35 MHz.

G1A05

On which of the following frequencies are General class licensees prohibited from operating as control operator?

  1. A 7.125 MHz to 7.175 MHz
  2. B 28.000 MHz to 28.025 MHz
  3. C 21.275 MHz to 21.300 MHz
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 7.125 MHz to 7.175 MHz

Why A is right. A. On 40 meters the General table jumps from 7.125 MHz to 7.175 MHz, leaving that 50 kHz gap to Extra and Advanced operators.

In 97.301(d), General privileges on 40 meters are 7.025 to 7.125 MHz and, in Region 2, 7.175 to 7.300 MHz. The space between belongs to Amateur Extra (and Advanced) operators. The other two choices are inside General privileges: 10 meters is fully open to General, and 21.275 to 21.450 MHz is a General segment on 15 meters.

“40 m | 7.025-7.125 | 7.025-7.125 | 7.025-7.125 | (i) ; Do | 7.175-7.200 | 7.175-7.300 | 7.175-7.200 | (i)”

47 CFR 97.301(d)

Why not B? General operators may use all of 10 meters, including 28.000 to 28.025 MHz.

G1A06

Which of the following applies when the FCC rules designate the amateur service as a secondary user on a band?

  1. A Amateur stations must record the call sign of the primary service station before operating on a frequency assigned to that station
  2. B Amateur stations may use the band only during emergencies
  3. C Amateur stations must not cause harmful interference to primary users and must accept interference from primary users
  4. D Amateur stations may only operate during specific hours of the day, while primary users are permitted 24-hour use of the band
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Amateur stations must not cause harmful interference to primary users and must accept interference from primary users

Why C is right. C. Secondary means amateurs come second: they must stay out of the primary users' way and put up with interference those users cause.

Each band is given to services as primary or secondary. On a band where amateurs are secondary, stations of the primary service, such as government radars, have first claim. Amateurs may operate at any time, but if they interfere with a primary station they must fix it or stop, and they have no right to complain about interference from it.

“A station in a secondary service must not cause harmful interference to, and must accept interference from, stations in a primary service.”

47 CFR 97.303

Why not D? Secondary status does not limit amateurs to certain hours.

G1A07

On which amateur frequencies in the 10-meter band may stations with a General class control operator transmit CW emissions?

  1. A 28.000 MHz to 28.025 MHz only
  2. B 28.000 MHz to 28.300 MHz only
  3. C 28.025 MHz to 28.300 MHz only
  4. D The entire band
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The entire band

Why D is right. D. CW is allowed on any frequency the control operator may use, and General operators may use all of 10 meters.

Part 97 lets CW go anywhere in a control operator's privileges unless another rule says otherwise. The General table gives the full 10-meter band, 28.000 to 29.700 MHz. So a General operator can send CW from one band edge to the other, including the parts where phone is used.

“an amateur station may transmit a CW emission on any frequency authorized to the control operator.”

47 CFR 97.305(a)

Why not A? 28.000 to 28.025 MHz is a slice of the band, not the limit of General CW privileges.

G1A08

Which HF bands have segments exclusively allocated to Amateur Extra licensees?

  1. A All HF bands
  2. B 80 meters, 40 meters, 20 meters, and 15 meters
  3. C All HF bands except 160 meters and 10 meters
  4. D 60 meters, 30 meters, 17 meters, and 12 meters
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 80 meters, 40 meters, 20 meters, and 15 meters

Why B is right. B. These four bands are the only HF bands where the Extra table starts lower than the General table, or covers phone frequencies General lacks.

The Extra table in 97.301(b) gives 3.500 MHz on 80 meters, 7.000 MHz on 40 meters, 14.000 MHz on 20 meters and 21.000 MHz on 15 meters. General starts 25 kHz higher on each, and also misses parts of their phone segments. On 160, 60, 30, 17, 12 and 10 meters the two classes have identical privileges.

“80 m | 3.500-3.600 | 3.500-3.600 | 3.500-3.600 | (a)”

47 CFR 97.301(b)

Why not D? On 60, 30, 17 and 12 meters, General and Extra share the same frequencies.

G1A09

Which of the following frequencies is within the General class portion of the 15-meter band?

  1. A 14250 kHz
  2. B 18155 kHz
  3. C 21300 kHz
  4. D 24900 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 21300 kHz

Why C is right. C. 21300 kHz is 21.300 MHz, which falls inside the General phone segment of 15 meters, 21.275 to 21.450 MHz.

Convert kHz to MHz by dividing by 1000. 21300 kHz becomes 21.300 MHz, inside the 15-meter row for General. The other choices are on different bands: 14250 kHz is 20 meters, 18155 kHz is 17 meters, and 24900 kHz is 12 meters.

“Do | 21.275-21.450 | 21.275-21.450 | 21.275-21.450”

47 CFR 97.301(d)

Why not A? 14250 kHz, or 14.250 MHz, is on 20 meters, not 15 meters.

G1A10

What portion of the 10-meter band is available for repeater use?

  1. A The entire band
  2. B The portion between 28.1 MHz and 28.2 MHz
  3. C The portion between 28.3 MHz and 28.5 MHz
  4. D The portion above 29.5 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The portion above 29.5 MHz

Why D is right. D. The repeater rule excludes 28.0 to 29.5 MHz, which leaves only 29.5 to 29.7 MHz on 10 meters for repeaters.

Repeaters may work on 10 meters and higher bands, but Part 97 lists segments where they may not receive or retransmit. On 10 meters that blocked segment runs from 28.0 to 29.5 MHz. The band ends at 29.7 MHz, so repeaters fit in the top 200 kHz.

“A repeater may receive and retransmit only on the 10 m and shorter wavelength frequency bands except the 28.0-29.5 MHz”

47 CFR 97.205(b)

Why not A? Most of 10 meters, 28.0 to 29.5 MHz, is closed to repeaters.

G1A11

When General class licensees are not permitted to use the entire voice portion of a band, which portion of the voice segment is available to them?

  1. A The lower frequency portion
  2. B The upper frequency portion
  3. C The lower frequency portion on frequencies below 7.3 MHz, and the upper portion on frequencies above 14.150 MHz
  4. D The upper frequency portion on frequencies below 7.3 MHz, and the lower portion on frequencies above 14.150 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The upper frequency portion

Why B is right. B. On each band where General shares only part of the voice segment, the General range runs up to the top of the band.

Look at the General rows in 97.301(d): 75 meters starts at 3.800 MHz, 40 meters at 7.175 MHz, 20 meters at 14.225 MHz and 15 meters at 21.275 MHz. Each runs to the top of its band. The lower part of each voice segment is for Amateur Extra (and Advanced) operators.

“Do | 14.225-14.350 | 14.225-14.350 | 14.225-14.350”

47 CFR 97.301(d)

Why not A? The lower part of each voice segment is the part General lacks.
