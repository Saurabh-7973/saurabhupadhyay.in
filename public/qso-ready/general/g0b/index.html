G0B: Station safety: electrical shock, grounding, fusing, interlocks, and wiring; antenna and tower safety

Group G0B of the General pool (2023–2027) has 13 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G0B01

Which wire or wires in a four-conductor 240 VAC circuit should be attached to fuses or circuit breakers?

  1. A Only the hot wires
  2. B Only the neutral wire
  3. C Only the ground wire
  4. D All wires
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Only the hot wires

Why A is right. A. Only the hot wires get fuses or breakers.

A four-conductor 240 VAC circuit has two hot wires, a neutral and a safety ground. A fuse or breaker must cut off the wires that carry voltage. Putting one in the neutral or ground could leave equipment live while seeming to be off.

Why not C? Interrupting the ground would remove the safety path and could leave a metal case live.

G0B02

According to the National Electrical Code, what is the minimum wire size that may be used safely for wiring with a 20-ampere circuit breaker?

  1. A AWG number 20
  2. B AWG number 16
  3. C AWG number 12
  4. D AWG number 8
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. AWG number 12

Why C is right. C. The code matches each breaker size to a minimum wire size so the wire cannot overheat before the breaker trips.

Smaller AWG numbers mean thicker wire, and thicker wire carries more current without overheating. For a 20-ampere breaker, the minimum is number 12 copper wire.

Why not B? AWG 16 is thinner than AWG 12 and could overheat at 20 amperes.

G0B03

Which size of fuse or circuit breaker would be appropriate to use with a circuit that uses AWG number 14 wiring?

  1. A 30 amperes
  2. B 25 amperes
  3. C 20 amperes
  4. D 15 amperes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 15 amperes

Why D is right. D. The breaker must protect the wire, and the code caps number 14 copper wire at a 15-ampere breaker.

Thinner wire heats up at lower current. In ordinary home circuits, number 14 copper wire is limited to a 15-ampere breaker.

Why not C? 20 amperes is the breaker size for thicker AWG 12 wire.

G0B04

Where should the station’s lightning protection ground system be located?

  1. A As close to the station equipment as possible
  2. B Outside the building
  3. C Next to the closest power pole
  4. D Parallel to the water supply line
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Outside the building

Why B is right. B. The lightning ground system belongs outside the building.

The goal is to send lightning energy into the earth before it enters your home. Ground rods outside, where the feed lines come in, give the surge a short path down. Bringing it inside first would put people and gear in the path.

Why not A? Grounding near the gear invites the surge inside the building.

G0B05

Which of the following conditions will cause a ground fault circuit interrupter (GFCI) to disconnect AC power?

  1. A Current flowing from one or more of the hot wires to the neutral wire
  2. B Current flowing from one or more of the hot wires directly to ground
  3. C Overvoltage on the hot wires
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Current flowing from one or more of the hot wires directly to ground

Why B is right. B. A GFCI trips when the current out on the hot wire and back on the neutral differ, which means some is leaking to ground.

A GFCI is a ground fault circuit interrupter. Leaking current may be flowing through a person, so it cuts power in a fraction of a second.

Why not A? Hot to neutral is normal current through a load, so it does not trip a GFCI.

G0B06

Which of the following is covered by the National Electrical Code?

  1. A Acceptable bandwidth limits
  2. B Acceptable modulation limits
  3. C Electrical safety of the station
  4. D RF exposure limits of the human body
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Electrical safety of the station

Why C is right. C. The NEC is a wiring safety standard covering wire sizes, breakers and grounding, and a station's AC wiring falls under it.

NEC stands for National Electrical Code. Many local building codes adopt it. It does not set radio signal rules such as bandwidth or modulation.

Why not D? RF exposure limits come from FCC rules, not the National Electrical Code.

G0B07

Which of these choices should be observed when climbing a tower using a safety harness?

  1. A Always hold on to the tower with one hand
  2. B Confirm that the harness is rated for the weight of the climber and that it is within its allowable service life
  3. C Ensure that all heavy tools are securely fastened to the harness
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Confirm that the harness is rated for the weight of the climber and that it is within its allowable service life

Why B is right. B. A harness only protects you if it can hold you, and its strength is limited by both a weight rating and an age or use limit.

Harness straps and stitching weaken with age, sun and use. Inspect the harness and check its labels before every climb.

Why not A? A climber often needs both hands to work aloft; the harness and lanyard, not a one-hand grip, are what hold you.

G0B08

What should be done before climbing a tower that supports electrically powered devices?

  1. A Notify the electric company that a person will be working on the tower
  2. B Make sure all circuits that supply power to the tower are locked out and tagged
  3. C Unground the base of the tower
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Make sure all circuits that supply power to the tower are locked out and tagged

Why B is right. B. Lockout keeps anyone from switching on power to lights, rotators or preamps while you are touching the tower.

Locking out means switching off the circuit and locking the switch so no one can turn it back on. A tag tells others why it is off and who is working.

Why not C? Removing the tower's ground would make it more dangerous, not safer.

G0B09

Which of the following is true of an emergency generator installation?

  1. A The generator should be operated in a well-ventilated area
  2. B The generator must be insulated from ground
  3. C Fuel should be stored near the generator for rapid refueling in case of an emergency
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The generator should be operated in a well-ventilated area

Why A is right. A. Engine exhaust contains carbon monoxide, an odorless gas that can kill, so fresh air must carry it away.

Running a generator in a closed garage or near windows lets the gas build up indoors. Place it outdoors, away from openings into the building.

Why not C? Storing fuel near a hot running generator is a fire risk.

G0B10

Which of the following is a danger from lead-tin solder?

  1. A Lead can contaminate food if hands are not washed carefully after handling the solder
  2. B High voltages can cause lead-tin solder to disintegrate suddenly
  3. C Tin in the solder can “cold flow,” causing shorts in the circuit
  4. D RF energy can convert the lead into a poisonous gas
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Lead can contaminate food if hands are not washed carefully after handling the solder

Why A is right. A. Lead is toxic when swallowed, and solder leaves traces on your fingers that can pass to food.

Wash your hands after soldering and before eating, and keep food away from the workbench.

Why not D? RF does not turn lead into a gas.

G0B11

Which of the following is required for lightning protection ground rods?

  1. A They must be bonded to all buried water and gas lines
  2. B Bends in ground wires must be made as close as possible to a right angle
  3. C Lightning grounds must be connected to all ungrounded wiring
  4. D They must be bonded together with all other grounds
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. They must be bonded together with all other grounds

Why D is right. D. Grounds that are not joined can differ by thousands of volts during a strike, and bonding keeps them all at the same voltage.

Bonding means joining with a solid, low-resistance connection. Bonded grounds rise and fall together during a surge, so current does not jump through your equipment.

Why not B? Sharp bends make it harder for the surge to follow the wire; gentle bends are preferred.

G0B12

What is the purpose of a power supply interlock?

  1. A To prevent unauthorized changes to the circuit that would void the manufacturer’s warranty
  2. B To shut down the unit if it becomes too hot
  3. C To ensure that dangerous voltages are removed if the cabinet is opened
  4. D To shut off the power supply if too much voltage is produced
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. To ensure that dangerous voltages are removed if the cabinet is opened

Why C is right. C. A switch on the cover cuts power, and often shorts stored charge, the moment the cabinet opens, so high voltage is never exposed.

An interlock is a switch that opens when a cover or door is removed. Never defeat one while working on live gear.

Why not B? Shutting down from heat is the job of a thermal cutout, not an interlock.

G0B13

Where should lightning arrestors be located?

  1. A Where the feed lines enter the building
  2. B On the antenna, opposite the feed point
  3. C In series with each ground lead
  4. D At the closest power pole ground electrode
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Where the feed lines enter the building

Why A is right. A. At the entry point the arrestor can send surge energy to the outside ground before it gets inside to people and gear.

An arrestor is a device that sends surge energy from the feed line to ground. Bond it to the outside ground system.

Why not C? An arrestor connects between the line and ground; it does not go in series with a ground lead.
