T0A: Power circuits and hazards: hazardous voltages, fuses and circuit breakers, grounding, electrical code compliance; Lightning protection; Battery safety

Group T0A of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T0A01

Which of the following is a safety hazard of a 12-volt storage battery that lacks internal protection circuitry?

  1. A Touching both terminals with your hands can cause electrical shock
  2. B Shorting the terminals can cause burns, fire, or an explosion
  3. C RF emissions from a nearby transmitter can cause the electrolyte to emit poison gas
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Shorting the terminals can cause burns, fire, or an explosion

Why B is right. B. Shorting the terminals of a 12-volt battery can cause burns, fire or an explosion.

A storage battery can push a huge current through a short circuit. A wrench or ring touching both terminals can get red hot in seconds. The heat can burn you, start a fire or make the battery release gas that can explode.

Why not A? Twelve volts is too low to push much current through dry skin, so shock is not the main danger.

T0A02

What health hazard is posed by electrical current flowing through the body?

  1. A It may cause injury by heating body tissue
  2. B It may disrupt the electrical functions of cells
  3. C It may cause involuntary muscle contractions
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

Current flowing through the body can heat and burn tissue. Your nerves and heart run on small electrical signals, and outside current can upset them. It can also make muscles clench so you cannot let go of the wire.

T0A03

In the United States, what circuit does black wire insulation indicate in a three-wire 120 V AC cable?

  1. A Neutral
  2. B Hot
  3. C Equipment ground
  4. D Negative
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Hot

Why B is right. B. In US 120 V AC wiring, black insulation marks the hot wire.

In a three-wire cable, the black wire is hot and carries voltage. The white wire is neutral. The green or bare wire is the equipment ground. Always treat the black wire as live until power is off and checked.

Why not A? Neutral is white, not black.

T0A04

What is the purpose of a fuse in an electrical circuit?

  1. A To prevent power supply ripple from damaging a component
  2. B To remove power in case of an overload
  3. C To limit current and prevent shocks
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. To remove power in case of an overload

Why B is right. B. A fuse removes power when there is an overload.

A fuse holds a thin metal link that melts when too much current flows. Once it melts, the circuit opens and current stops. This protects the wiring and equipment from overheating and fire.

Why not C? A fuse does not protect you from shock. It acts only after current is already too high.

T0A05

Why should a 5-ampere fuse never be replaced with a 20-ampere fuse?

  1. A The larger fuse would be likely to blow because it is rated for higher current
  2. B The power supply ripple would greatly increase
  3. C Excessive current could cause a fire
  4. D Voltage drop in the higher current fuse could result in excessively low voltage to the device
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Excessive current could cause a fire

Why C is right. C. Using a 20-ampere fuse in place of a 5-ampere fuse could let excessive current cause a fire.

The fuse rating is chosen to protect the wiring and parts in that device. A 20-ampere fuse lets up to four times as much current flow before it opens. Wires sized for 5 amperes can overheat and start a fire before the larger fuse blows.

Why not A? A higher-rated fuse is less likely to blow, not more.

T0A06

What is a good way to guard against electrical shock at your station?

  1. A Use three-wire cords and plugs for all AC powered equipment
  2. B Connect all AC powered station equipment to a common safety ground
  3. C Ensure all capacitors used for high-voltage DC are fully discharged before working inside equipment
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

Three-wire cords give fault current a safe path to ground. A common safety ground keeps all your equipment at the same voltage. High-voltage capacitors can hold a charge after power is off, so you discharge them before reaching inside. Each step lowers your chance of shock.

T0A07

Where should a lightning arrester be installed in a coaxial feed line?

  1. A At the output connector of a transceiver
  2. B At the antenna feed point
  3. C At the AC power service panel
  4. D On a grounded panel near where feed lines enter the building
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. On a grounded panel near where feed lines enter the building

Why D is right. D. A lightning arrester goes on a grounded panel near where feed lines enter the building.

A lightning arrester sends surges from the feed line to ground. Placing it where the cables come in, on a grounded panel, stops the surge before it travels into the house and your equipment.

Why not A? At the radio's connector the surge is already indoors, close to your gear.

T0A08

Where should a fuse or circuit breaker be installed in a 120V AC power circuit?

  1. A In series with the hot conductor only
  2. B In series with the hot and neutral conductors
  3. C In parallel with the hot conductor only
  4. D In parallel with the hot and neutral conductors
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. In series with the hot conductor only

Why A is right. A. A fuse or breaker goes in series with the hot conductor only.

In series means current must flow through the fuse. Putting it in the hot wire means that when it opens, the device is cut off from the live voltage. A fuse in the neutral could open while the device stays connected to the hot wire, which could still shock you.

Why not C? A fuse in parallel would not carry the circuit current, so it could not protect anything.

T0A09

What should be done to all external ground rods or earth connections?

  1. A Waterproof them with silicone caulk or electrical tape
  2. B Keep them as far apart as possible
  3. C Bond them together with heavy wire or conductive strap
  4. D Tune them for resonance on the lowest frequency of operation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Bond them together with heavy wire or conductive strap

Why C is right. C. Bond all external ground rods together with heavy wire or strap.

Bonding means connecting ground points so they stay at the same voltage. If rods are not bonded, a lightning strike or fault can raise one rod's voltage above another's. Current can then flow through your equipment between them. Heavy wire or strap keeps them tied together.

Why not B? Spreading rods apart without bonding them leaves voltage differences between them.

T0A10

What hazard exists when rapidly charging or discharging an unprotected battery?

  1. A Overheating or out-gassing
  2. B Excess output ripple
  3. C Electric shock
  4. D Overvoltage
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Overheating or out-gassing

Why A is right. A. Rapid charging or discharging of an unprotected battery can cause overheating or out-gassing.

Pushing current into or out of a battery too fast heats it up. The heat can make the battery release gas, which may be flammable, and it can damage or swell the cells. Protection circuits limit the current to prevent this.

Why not C? A battery's low voltage makes shock unlikely. The danger here is heat and gas.

T0A11

What hazard exists in a power supply immediately after turning it off?

  1. A Circulating currents in the dc filter
  2. B Leakage flux in the power transformer
  3. C Voltage transients from kickback diodes
  4. D Charge stored in filter capacitors
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Charge stored in filter capacitors

Why D is right. D. Charge stored in filter capacitors is a hazard right after a power supply is turned off.

Filter capacitors smooth the supply's output by storing charge. They can hold a dangerous voltage for some time after the switch is off. Discharge them safely before touching anything inside.

Why not B? Leakage flux in a transformer goes away when power is removed.

T0A12

Which of the following precautions should be taken when measuring high voltages with a voltmeter?

  1. A Ensure that the voltmeter has very low impedance
  2. B Ensure that the voltmeter and its leads are rated for use at the voltages being measured
  3. C Ensure that the circuit is grounded through the voltmeter
  4. D Ensure that the voltmeter is set to the correct frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Ensure that the voltmeter and its leads are rated for use at the voltages being measured

Why B is right. B. Make sure the voltmeter and its leads are rated for the voltages being measured.

Meters and test leads have voltage ratings. Using them above that rating can let the insulation break down, putting high voltage where your hands are. Check the rating before measuring any high-voltage circuit.

Why not A? A voltmeter should have high impedance so it draws little current from the circuit.
