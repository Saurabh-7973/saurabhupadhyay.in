E5D: RF effects in components and circuits: skin effect; real and reactive power; electrical length of conductors

Group E5D of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E5D01

What is the result of conductor skin effect?

  1. A Resistance increases as frequency increases because RF current flows closer to the surface
  2. B Resistance decreases as frequency increases because electron mobility increases
  3. C Resistance increases as temperature increases because of the change in thermal coefficient
  4. D Resistance decreases as temperature increases because of the change in thermal coefficient
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Resistance increases as frequency increases because RF current flows closer to the surface

Why A is right. A. At higher frequencies RF current crowds into a thin layer near the conductor's surface, so less metal carries it and resistance rises.

Changing magnetic fields inside a wire push current outward toward the surface. The higher the frequency, the thinner this layer, called the skin depth. At HF it is a small fraction of a millimeter in copper. With less of the wire in use, its RF resistance goes up.

Why not C? Temperature changes resistance too, but that is not skin effect.

E5D02

Why is it important to keep lead lengths short for components used in circuits for VHF and above?

  1. A To increase the thermal time constant
  2. B To minimize inductive reactance
  3. C To maintain component lifetime
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. To minimize inductive reactance

Why B is right. B. Every bit of wire has inductance, and at VHF that small inductance has enough reactance to upset the circuit.

Inductive reactance rises with frequency (XL = 2πfL). A 2-centimeter lead has roughly 20 nanohenries, which is about 19 ohms of reactance at 150 MHz. That unwanted reactance can detune circuits or cause oscillation. Short leads keep it small.

Why not A? Lead length has little to do with how fast a part heats or cools.

E5D03

What is the phase relationship between current and voltage for reactive power?

  1. A They are out of phase
  2. B They are in phase
  3. C They are 90 degrees out of phase
  4. D They are 45 degrees out of phase
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. They are 90 degrees out of phase

Why C is right. C. With reactive power, current and voltage are 90 degrees apart, so energy moves back and forth instead of being used.

In a pure inductor or capacitor, current and voltage are a quarter cycle apart. For half of each cycle the part takes energy from the source, and for the other half it returns it. Averaged over a cycle, the net power is zero.

Why not D? At 45 degrees the power would be partly real and partly reactive, not purely reactive.

E5D04

Why are short connections used at microwave frequencies?

  1. A To increase neutralizing resistance
  2. B To reduce phase shift along the connection
  3. C To increase compensating capacitance
  4. D To reduce noise figure
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. To reduce phase shift along the connection

Why B is right. B. At microwave frequencies even a short connection is a noticeable fraction of a wavelength, so keeping it short limits the phase shift along it.

A signal takes time to travel along a conductor. At 10 GHz one wavelength in free space is 3 cm, so a 3 mm lead already delays the signal by about 36 degrees. Unwanted phase shift can upset matching, filters and amplifier stability.

Why not D? Noise figure depends on active devices and losses, not on phase shift along a lead.

E5D05

What parasitic characteristic causes electrolytic capacitors to be unsuitable for use at RF?

  1. A Skin effect
  2. B Shunt capacitance
  3. C Inductance
  4. D Dielectric leakage
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Inductance

Why C is right. C. Electrolytic capacitors have a lot of built-in inductance from their rolled foil and leads, so at RF they stop acting like capacitors.

An electrolytic is made of long strips of foil wound into a roll, which behaves partly like a coil. Above some frequency its inductive reactance outweighs its capacitive reactance, and the part acts like an inductor. That is why small ceramic capacitors are used for RF bypassing.

Why not D? Leakage matters at DC; it is not what limits electrolytics at RF.

E5D06

What parasitic characteristic creates an inductor’s self-resonance?

  1. A Skin effect
  2. B Dielectric loss
  3. C Coupling
  4. D Inter-turn capacitance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Inter-turn capacitance

Why D is right. D. The small capacitance between neighboring turns of wire resonates with the coil's own inductance.

Each turn of a coil sits next to the turns beside it, with insulation between, forming tiny capacitors. Together they act like one capacitor across the whole inductor. Where that capacitance and the inductance resonate, the coil stops acting like an inductor; above it, the coil acts like a capacitor.

Why not A? Skin effect raises resistance but does not create a resonance.

E5D07

What combines to create the self-resonance of a component?

  1. A The component’s resistance and reactance
  2. B The component’s nominal and parasitic reactance
  3. C The component’s inductance and capacitance
  4. D The component’s electrical length and impedance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The component’s nominal and parasitic reactance

Why B is right. B. Self-resonance happens where a part's intended reactance and its unwanted (parasitic) reactance are equal and cancel.

Every real component has its main property, called nominal, plus small unwanted ones. A capacitor has lead inductance; an inductor has capacitance between turns. At some frequency those two reactances are equal and the part resonates by itself. Above that frequency it acts like the opposite kind of component.

Why not C? C names inductance and capacitance in general; self-resonance is specifically the part's intended reactance meeting its unwanted parasitic one.

E5D08

What is the primary cause of loss in film capacitors at RF?

  1. A Inductance
  2. B Dielectric loss
  3. C Self-discharge
  4. D Skin effect
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Skin effect

Why D is right. D. Low-loss film dielectrics such as polypropylene waste very little energy, so at RF the main loss is the resistance of the plates and leads, which skin effect raises.

Skin effect pushes RF current into the surface of a conductor, so less metal carries it and its resistance rises with frequency. In a film capacitor that resistance turns some of the RF current into heat.

Why not B? Dielectric loss is small in the low-loss films used at RF, so it is not the main loss here.

E5D09

What happens to reactive power in ideal inductors and capacitors?

  1. A It is dissipated as heat in the circuit
  2. B Energy is stored in magnetic or electric fields, but power is not dissipated
  3. C It is canceled by Coulomb forces in the capacitor and inductor
  4. D It is dissipated in the formation of inductive and capacitive fields
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Energy is stored in magnetic or electric fields, but power is not dissipated

Why B is right. B. An ideal inductor stores energy in its magnetic field and an ideal capacitor in its electric field, then gives it all back, so none is lost as heat.

Reactive power flows into the component during part of each cycle and back to the source during the next. With no resistance, nothing turns into heat. Real parts have a little resistance and warm slightly, but ideal parts have none.

Why not A? Heat comes only from resistance, which ideal parts lack.

E5D10

As a conductor’s diameter increases, what is the effect on its electrical length?

  1. A Thickness has no effect on electrical length
  2. B It varies randomly
  3. C It decreases
  4. D It increases
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It increases

Why D is right. D. A thicker conductor has more capacitance to its surroundings, which slows the wave on it, so it acts electrically longer.

Electrical length is a conductor's length measured in wavelengths at a given frequency. A fat wire has more capacitance, especially at its ends, and that lowers the speed of the wave along it. So a thick dipole resonates lower than a thin one of the same physical length.

Why not A? Thickness does matter; thick antenna elements must be cut shorter to resonate at the same frequency.

E5D11

How much real power is consumed in a circuit consisting of a 100-ohm resistor in series with a 100-ohm inductive reactance drawing 1 ampere?

  1. A 70.7 watts
  2. B 100 watts
  3. C 141.4 watts
  4. D 200 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 100 watts

Why B is right. B. Only the resistor turns energy into heat, so real power is current squared times resistance, 100 watts.

The inductive reactance stores energy and returns it each cycle, so it uses no real power. The same 1-ampere current flows through the resistor, and that is where the power is spent.

P = I^2 × R
P = (1 A)^2 × 100 ohms
P = 100 W
= 100 watts

Why not C? 141.4 watts is the apparent power, which wrongly counts the reactance as if it used power.

E5D12

What is reactive power?

  1. A Power consumed in circuit Q
  2. B Power consumed by an inductor’s wire resistance
  3. C The power consumed in inductors and capacitors
  4. D Wattless, nonproductive power
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Wattless, nonproductive power

Why D is right. D. Reactive power is called wattless because it moves back and forth between source and reactance without doing any work.

In inductors and capacitors, energy flows in during part of each cycle and back out in the next. The average is zero, so no heat or useful output results. Reactive power is measured in volt-amperes reactive (VAR), not watts, but it still loads wires with extra current.

Why not C? Ideal inductors and capacitors do not consume power; they store it and return it.
