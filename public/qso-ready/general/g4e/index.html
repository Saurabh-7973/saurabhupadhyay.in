G4E: Mobile and portable HF stations; alternative energy source operation

Group G4E of the General pool (2023–2027) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G4E01

What is the purpose of a capacitance hat on a mobile antenna?

  1. A To increase the power handling capacity of a whip antenna
  2. B To reduce radiation resistance
  3. C To electrically lengthen a physically short antenna
  4. D To lower the radiation angle
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. To electrically lengthen a physically short antenna

Why C is right. C. The hat adds capacitance at the top of the whip, which lowers its resonant frequency as if the whip were longer.

A capacitance hat is a set of spokes or a disk near the top of a mobile whip. It keeps the current strong farther up the whip, spreading it more evenly. Resonant frequency is the frequency where the antenna naturally works best.

Why not A? The hat changes the antenna's electrical length, not how much power it can handle.

G4E02

What is the purpose of a corona ball on an HF mobile antenna?

  1. A To narrow the operating bandwidth of the antenna
  2. B To increase the “Q” of the antenna
  3. C To reduce the chance of damage if the antenna should strike an object
  4. D To reduce RF voltage discharge from the tip of the antenna while transmitting
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. To reduce RF voltage discharge from the tip of the antenna while transmitting

Why D is right. D. A smooth ball spreads out the strong electric field at the tip, so the air there is less likely to break down and leak RF.

The tip of a short, loaded mobile whip can carry a very high RF voltage. A sharp point concentrates the field, and the air can glow. This is corona discharge, and it wastes power and wears the tip.

Why not C? The ball is about electric fields at the tip, not about cushioning a strike.

G4E03

Which of the following direct, fused power connections would be the best for a 100-watt HF mobile installation?

  1. A To the battery using heavy-gauge wire
  2. B To the alternator or generator using heavy-gauge wire
  3. C To the battery using insulated heavy duty balanced transmission line
  4. D To the alternator or generator using insulated heavy duty balanced transmission line
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. To the battery using heavy-gauge wire

Why A is right. A. The battery steadies the voltage and soaks up alternator noise and spikes, and heavy wire keeps the voltage from dropping at high current.

A 100-watt HF radio can draw 20 amperes or more on transmit. The alternator's output carries electrical noise and voltage spikes. Fuse both leads near the battery.

Why not C? Balanced transmission line is for carrying RF to antennas, not DC power.

G4E04

Why should DC power for a 100-watt HF transceiver not be supplied by a vehicle’s auxiliary power socket?

  1. A The socket is not wired with an RF-shielded power cable
  2. B The socket’s wiring may be inadequate for the current drawn by the transceiver
  3. C The DC polarity of the socket is reversed from the polarity of modern HF transceivers
  4. D Drawing more than 50 watts from this socket could cause the engine to overheat
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The socket’s wiring may be inadequate for the current drawn by the transceiver

Why B is right. B. Accessory sockets are wired and fused for small loads, and a 100-watt radio draws far more current on transmit.

Thin wiring causes the voltage to sag, which can distort your signal, and the fuse may blow. Run heavy wire straight to the battery instead.

Why not D? The engine's temperature has nothing to do with the accessory socket.

G4E05

Which of the following most limits an HF mobile installation?

  1. A “Picket fencing”
  2. B The wire gauge of the DC power line to the transceiver
  3. C Efficiency of the electrically short antenna
  4. D FCC rules limiting mobile output power on the 75-meter band
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Efficiency of the electrically short antenna

Why C is right. C. A whip a few meters tall is tiny next to an HF wavelength, so it sends out little power compared with what its coil and ground connection waste.

Such a short antenna has low radiation resistance, the part of its resistance that stands for power actually sent out as radio waves. Losses in the loading coil and ground then take a large share of your power.

Why not B? The wire gauge is easy to fix with heavier wire; the short antenna cannot be fixed the same way.

G4E06

What is one disadvantage of using a shortened mobile antenna as opposed to a full-size antenna?

  1. A Short antennas are more likely to cause distortion of transmitted signals
  2. B Q of the antenna will be very low
  3. C Operating bandwidth may be very limited
  4. D Harmonic radiation may increase
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Operating bandwidth may be very limited

Why C is right. C. A short antenna needs a loading coil, which gives it a high Q, and a high Q antenna has low SWR over only a small range.

Q describes how sharply a circuit resonates. With a high Q, you may need to retune when you move even a little in frequency.

Why not B? A loaded short antenna has a high Q, not a low one.

G4E07

Which of the following may cause receive interference to an HF transceiver installed in a vehicle?

  1. A The battery charging system
  2. B The fuel delivery system
  3. C The control computers
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these vehicle systems can cause receive interference.

The alternator and its charging circuits create whine. Electric fuel pumps have motors that spark. Engine and body control computers use fast digital signals that radiate noise. Any of these can show up in an HF receiver.

G4E08

In what configuration are the individual cells in a solar panel connected together?

  1. A Series-parallel
  2. B Shunt
  3. C Bypass
  4. D Full-wave bridge
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Series-parallel

Why A is right. A. Solar cells are wired in series-parallel.

One silicon cell makes only about half a volt. Cells wired in series, end to end, add their voltages to reach a useful level. Strings of cells wired in parallel, side by side, add their currents. Combining both gives the voltage and current a panel needs.

Why not D? A full-wave bridge turns AC into DC, and a solar panel already makes DC.

G4E09

What is the approximate open-circuit voltage from a fully illuminated silicon photovoltaic cell?

  1. A 0.02 VDC
  2. B 0.5 VDC
  3. C 0.2 VDC
  4. D 1.38 VDC
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 0.5 VDC

Why B is right. B. A fully lit silicon solar cell gives about 0.5 VDC with no load.

Open-circuit voltage is what a cell produces with nothing connected. A silicon cell's voltage is set by the physics of its junction, not by its size. In bright light it gives about half a volt. That is why panels wire many cells in series.

Why not D? 1.38 volts is far more than a single silicon junction produces.

G4E10

Why should a series diode be connected between a solar panel and a storage battery that is being charged by the panel?

  1. A To prevent overload by regulating the charging voltage
  2. B To prevent discharge of the battery through the panel during times of low or no illumination
  3. C To limit the current flowing from the panel to a safe value
  4. D To prevent damage to the battery due to excessive voltage at high illumination levels
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. To prevent discharge of the battery through the panel during times of low or no illumination

Why B is right. B. A diode passes current in one direction only, so when the panel's voltage drops below the battery's, it blocks current from flowing backward.

In bright light, the panel's voltage is higher than the battery's, so current flows into the battery. In dim light or darkness, the panel's voltage falls. Without the diode, the battery would drain through the panel.

Why not A? A diode does not regulate voltage; a charge controller does that.

G4E11

What precaution should be taken when connecting a solar panel to a lithium iron phosphate battery?

  1. A Ground the solar panel outer metal framework
  2. B Ensure the battery is placed terminals-up
  3. C A series resistor must be in place
  4. D The solar panel must have a charge controller
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The solar panel must have a charge controller

Why D is right. D. A panel's output changes with the sunlight, but a lithium iron phosphate battery needs its charging held within set limits and stopped when full.

Overcharging can damage the cells and can be a fire risk. A charge controller between the panel and the battery manages the voltage and current.

Why not C? A resistor wastes power and cannot hold the voltage steady as sunlight changes.
