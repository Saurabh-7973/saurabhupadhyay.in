T3B: Electromagnetic wave properties: wavelength vs frequency, nature and velocity of electromagnetic waves, relationship of wavelength and frequency; Electromagnetic spectrum definitions: UHF, VHF, HF

Group T3B of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T3B01

What is the relationship between the electric and magnetic fields of an electromagnetic wave?

  1. A They travel at different speeds
  2. B They are in parallel
  3. C They revolve in opposite directions
  4. D They are at right angles
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. They are at right angles

Why D is right. D. The electric and magnetic fields are at right angles to each other.

In a radio wave, the electric field and the magnetic field cross each other at 90 degrees. Both are also at right angles to the direction the wave travels.

Why not A? The two fields are parts of one wave, so they travel together at the same speed.

T3B02

What property of a radio wave defines its polarization?

  1. A The orientation of the electric field
  2. B The orientation of the magnetic field
  3. C The ratio of the energy in the magnetic field to the energy in the electric field
  4. D The ratio of the velocity to the wavelength
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The orientation of the electric field

Why A is right. A. Polarization is set by which way the electric field points.

If the electric field goes up and down, the wave is vertically polarized. If it goes side to side, it is horizontally polarized. A vertical antenna makes a vertical electric field.

Why not B? Polarization is named after the electric field, not the magnetic one.

T3B03

What are the two components of a radio wave?

  1. A Impedance and reactance
  2. B Voltage and current
  3. C Electric and magnetic fields
  4. D Ionizing and non-ionizing radiation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Electric and magnetic fields

Why C is right. C. A radio wave is made of an electric field and a magnetic field.

The two fields rise and fall together and keep each other going as the wave moves through space. Neither one can travel on its own.

Why not B? Voltage and current exist in wires and circuits, not in the wave in space.

T3B04

What is the velocity of a radio wave traveling through free space?

  1. A Speed of light
  2. B Speed of sound
  3. C 0.86 times the speed of light
  4. D 1.86 times the speed of sound
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Speed of light

Why A is right. A. A radio wave travels through free space at the speed of light.

Radio waves and light are both electromagnetic waves. In free space, all of them move at the same speed, about 300,000,000 meters per second.

Why not B? Sound is a pressure wave in air and is about a million times slower.

T3B05

What is the relationship between wavelength and frequency?

  1. A Wavelength gets longer as frequency increases
  2. B Wavelength gets shorter as frequency increases
  3. C Wavelength is constant at all frequencies
  4. D Wavelength and frequency increase as path length increases
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Wavelength gets shorter as frequency increases

Why B is right. B. As frequency goes up, wavelength gets shorter.

Radio waves all travel at the same speed. If more waves pass each second, each one must be shorter. So frequency and wavelength move in opposite directions.

Why not A? That reverses the relationship.

T3B06

What is the formula for converting frequency to approximate wavelength in meters?

  1. A Wavelength in meters equals frequency in hertz multiplied by 300
  2. B Wavelength in meters equals frequency in hertz divided by 300
  3. C Wavelength in meters equals frequency in megahertz divided by 300
  4. D Wavelength in meters equals 300 divided by frequency in megahertz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Wavelength in meters equals 300 divided by frequency in megahertz

Why D is right. D. Wavelength in meters equals 300 divided by the frequency in megahertz.

The number 300 comes from the speed of light, about 300 million meters per second, with the millions canceled out by using megahertz. For example, 150 MHz gives 300 ÷ 150 = 2 meters.

Why not C? That puts the frequency on top, which would make wavelength grow with frequency.

T3B07

In addition to frequency, which of the following is used to identify amateur radio bands?

  1. A The approximate wavelength in meters
  2. B Traditional letter/number designators
  3. C Channel numbers
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The approximate wavelength in meters

Why A is right. A. Bands are also named by their approximate wavelength in meters.

Hams often say "2 meters" or "70 centimeters" instead of quoting frequencies. The name gives the rough wavelength of signals in that band. For example, 146 MHz has a wavelength of about 2 meters.

Why not C? Amateur bands are not named by channel numbers.

T3B08

What frequency range is referred to as VHF?

  1. A 30 kHz to 300 kHz
  2. B 30 MHz to 300 MHz
  3. C 300 kHz to 3000 kHz
  4. D 300 MHz to 3000 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 30 MHz to 300 MHz

Why B is right. B. VHF covers 30 MHz to 300 MHz.

VHF stands for very high frequency. Each named range spans a factor of ten. HF ends at 30 MHz, VHF runs from there to 300 MHz, and UHF picks up after that.

Why not D? 300 MHz to 3000 MHz is UHF.

T3B09

What frequency range is referred to as UHF?

  1. A 30 to 300 kHz
  2. B 30 to 300 MHz
  3. C 300 to 3000 kHz
  4. D 300 to 3000 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 300 to 3000 MHz

Why D is right. D. UHF covers 300 to 3000 MHz.

UHF stands for ultra high frequency. It starts where VHF ends, at 300 MHz, and runs ten times higher, to 3000 MHz. The 70-centimeter band sits in this range.

Why not B? 30 to 300 MHz is VHF.

T3B10

What frequency range is referred to as HF?

  1. A 300 to 3000 MHz
  2. B 30 to 300 MHz
  3. C 3 to 30 MHz
  4. D 300 to 3000 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 3 to 30 MHz

Why C is right. C. HF covers 3 to 30 MHz.

HF stands for high frequency. It runs from 3 MHz up to 30 MHz, where VHF begins. Most long-distance ionospheric contacts happen in this range.

Why not D? 300 to 3000 kHz is the medium-frequency range, below HF.

T3B11

What is the approximate velocity of a radio wave in free space?

  1. A 150,000,000 meters per second
  2. B 300,000,000 meters per second
  3. C 300,000,000 miles per hour
  4. D 150,000,000 miles per hour
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 300,000,000 meters per second

Why B is right. B. About 300,000,000 meters per second.

That is the speed of light, which all radio waves share in free space. It is the same number behind the 300-divided-by-megahertz wavelength formula.

Why not C? The number is right but the unit is wrong; 300,000,000 miles per hour is far too slow.

T3B12

Which of these frequencies travels at the highest velocity in free space?

  1. A Microwaves
  2. B UHF
  3. C VHF
  4. D All radio frequencies travel at the same velocity
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All radio frequencies travel at the same velocity

Why D is right. D. All radio frequencies travel at the same velocity in free space.

Frequency changes a wave's length, not its speed. Microwaves, UHF and VHF all move at the speed of light in free space.

Why not A? Microwaves have shorter waves, but they are no faster.
