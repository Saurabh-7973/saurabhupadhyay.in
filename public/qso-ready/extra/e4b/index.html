E4B: Measurement technique and limitations: instrument accuracy and performance limitations; probes; techniques to minimize errors; measurement of Q; instrument calibration; S parameters; vector network analyzers; RF signals

Group E4B of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E4B01

Which of the following factors most affects the accuracy of a frequency counter?

  1. A Input attenuator accuracy
  2. B Time base accuracy
  3. C Decade divider accuracy
  4. D Temperature coefficient of the logic
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Time base accuracy

Why B is right. B. A counter counts cycles during a gate time set by its time base, so any time base error goes straight into the reading.

The time base is a crystal oscillator that opens the counting gate for an exact interval, such as 1 second. If the crystal is off by 10 parts per million, every reading is off by 10 parts per million. That is why good counters use temperature-controlled or GPS-locked references.

Why not A? The attenuator changes signal level, not the number of cycles counted.

E4B02

What is the significance of voltmeter sensitivity expressed in ohms per volt?

  1. A The full scale reading of the voltmeter multiplied by its ohms per volt rating is the input impedance of the voltmeter
  2. B The reading in volts multiplied by the ohms per volt rating will determine the power drawn by the device under test
  3. C The reading in ohms divided by the ohms per volt rating will determine the voltage applied to the circuit
  4. D The full scale reading in amps divided by ohms per volt rating will determine the size of shunt needed
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The full scale reading of the voltmeter multiplied by its ohms per volt rating is the input impedance of the voltmeter

Why A is right. A. Multiplying full-scale volts by the ohms-per-volt rating gives the meter's input resistance on that range.

A meter rated 20,000 ohms per volt on its 10 V range puts 20,000 × 10 = 200,000 ohms across the circuit. A higher rating means the meter draws less current, so it disturbs the circuit less and reads more accurately.

Why not B? The rating describes the meter's own loading, not the power the circuit delivers.

E4B03

Which S parameter is equivalent to forward gain?

  1. A S11
  2. B S12
  3. C S21
  4. D S22
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. S21

Why C is right. C. S21 compares the signal leaving port 2 with the signal sent into port 1, which is forward gain.

S parameters (scattering parameters) describe a device by the waves going in and out of its ports. The first digit is where the wave leaves; the second is where it entered. So S21 means in at port 1, out at port 2. For an amplifier with port 1 as its input, that is its gain.

Why not B? S12 runs backward, from port 2 to port 1, and measures reverse isolation.

E4B04

Which S parameter represents input port return loss or reflection coefficient (equivalent to VSWR)?

  1. A S11
  2. B S12
  3. C S21
  4. D S22
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. S11

Why A is right. A. S11 compares the wave reflected back out of port 1 with the wave sent into port 1, so it measures input reflection.

In S parameter names, the first digit is the port the wave leaves and the second is the port it entered. S11 means in at port 1 and back out at port 1, which is a reflection. From it you can find return loss and SWR (standing wave ratio).

Why not D? S22 is reflection at the output port, port 2.

E4B05

What three test loads are used to calibrate an RF vector network analyzer?

  1. A 50 ohms, 75 ohms, and 90 ohms
  2. B Short circuit, open circuit, and 50 ohms
  3. C Short circuit, open circuit, and resonant circuit
  4. D 50 ohms through 1/8 wavelength, 1/4 wavelength, and 1/2 wavelength of coaxial cable
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Short circuit, open circuit, and 50 ohms

Why B is right. B. A short, an open and a precise 50-ohm load give three known reflections the analyzer uses to measure and remove its own errors.

A short and an open each reflect all the signal, with opposite phase, while a perfect 50-ohm load reflects none. The vector network analyzer measures each standard, works out the errors added by its cables and connectors, and subtracts them from later measurements.

Why not A? Three resistive loads would not give the full-reflection references the correction needs.

E4B06

How much power is being absorbed by the load when a directional power meter connected between a transmitter and a terminating load reads 100 watts forward power and 25 watts reflected power?

  1. A 100 watts
  2. B 125 watts
  3. C 112.5 watts
  4. D 75 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 75 watts

Why D is right. D. The reflected 25 watts is subtracted from the 100 watts sent forward, leaving 75 watts in the load.

A directional meter reads power heading to the load (forward) and power coming back (reflected) separately. The power the load actually absorbs is the difference between the two.

P(load) = P(forward) - P(reflected)
P(load) = 100 W - 25 W
P(load) = 75 W
= 75 W

Why not B? 125 watts adds the readings; reflected power is not extra power delivered.

E4B07

What do the subscripts of S parameters represent?

  1. A The port or ports at which measurements are made
  2. B The relative time between measurements
  3. C Relative quality of the data
  4. D Frequency order of the measurements
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The port or ports at which measurements are made

Why A is right. A. Each subscript is a port number: the first is where the wave comes out, the second is where it went in.

A two-port device such as a filter or amplifier has an input (port 1) and an output (port 2). S21 means a wave entered port 1 and was measured leaving port 2. S11 means it entered and left at port 1, which is a reflection.

Why not D? The subscripts have nothing to do with frequency; the whole set is measured at each frequency.

E4B08

Which of the following can be used to determine the Q of a series-tuned circuit?

  1. A The ratio of inductive reactance to capacitive reactance
  2. B The frequency shift
  3. C The bandwidth of the circuit's frequency response
  4. D The resonant frequency of the circuit
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The bandwidth of the circuit's frequency response

Why C is right. C. Q equals the resonant frequency divided by the bandwidth, so measuring the bandwidth lets you find Q.

Q (quality factor) tells how sharply a tuned circuit picks out its resonant frequency. Sweep the frequency and find the two points where the response is 3 dB below the peak; the gap between them is the bandwidth. A narrow response means high Q.

Why not D? The resonant frequency alone tells where the peak is, not how sharp it is.

E4B09

Which of the following can be measured by a two-port vector network analyzer?

  1. A Phase noise
  2. B Filter frequency response
  3. C Pulse rise time
  4. D Forward power
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Filter frequency response

Why B is right. B. A two-port network analyzer sends a swept signal into the filter and measures what comes out, which is the filter's frequency response.

A vector network analyzer (VNA) measures both the size and the phase of signals at each frequency. With the filter between its two ports, it shows how much signal passes at each frequency and how well the filter is matched at its input.

Why not D? A VNA uses low test power; transmitter forward power is read with a wattmeter.

E4B10

Which of the following methods measures intermodulation distortion in an SSB transmitter?

  1. A Modulate the transmitter using two RF signals having non-harmonically related frequencies and observe the RF output with a spectrum analyzer
  2. B Modulate the transmitter using two AF signals having non-harmonically related frequencies and observe the RF output with a spectrum analyzer
  3. C Modulate the transmitter using two AF signals having harmonically related frequencies and observe the RF output with a peak reading wattmeter
  4. D Modulate the transmitter using two RF signals having harmonically related frequencies and observe the RF output with a logic analyzer
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Modulate the transmitter using two AF signals having non-harmonically related frequencies and observe the RF output with a spectrum analyzer

Why B is right. B. Two audio tones that are not harmonically related make the SSB transmitter put out two RF tones, and any mixing products show up on a spectrum analyzer.

In SSB, each audio tone becomes one RF signal. If the amplifier is not linear, the two RF signals mix and create extra signals at nearby frequencies. A spectrum analyzer shows how far down those products are. Choosing tones that are not harmonics of each other keeps the products from overlapping their harmonics.

Why not A? An SSB transmitter is driven through its audio input, so the test tones are audio, not RF.

E4B11

Which of the following can be measured with a vector network analyzer?

  1. A Input impedance
  2. B Output impedance
  3. C Reflection coefficient
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. A VNA measures how signals reflect from each port of a device, which gives all three quantities.

The reflection coefficient is the fraction of a wave bounced back from a port, along with its phase. From the reflection at a device's input you can calculate its input impedance; from the reflection at its output you get its output impedance.
