G5C: Resistors, capacitors, and inductors in series and parallel; transformers

Group G5C of the General pool (2023–2027) has 14 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G5C01

What causes a voltage to appear across the secondary winding of a transformer when an AC voltage source is connected across its primary winding?

  1. A Capacitive coupling
  2. B Displacement current coupling
  3. C Mutual inductance
  4. D Mutual capacitance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Mutual inductance

Why C is right. C. Mutual inductance couples the primary to the secondary.

AC in the primary winding makes a magnetic field that keeps changing. That field passes through the secondary winding too. A changing magnetic field through a coil creates a voltage in it. Two coils linked this way share mutual inductance.

Why not D? A transformer couples its windings through a magnetic field, not through capacitance.

G5C02

What is the output voltage if an input signal is applied to the secondary winding of a 4:1 voltage step-down transformer instead of the primary winding?

  1. A The input voltage is multiplied by 4
  2. B The input voltage is divided by 4
  3. C Additional resistance must be added in series with the primary to prevent overload
  4. D Additional resistance must be added in parallel with the secondary to prevent overload
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The input voltage is multiplied by 4

Why A is right. A. Fed from its small winding, the transformer works as a 4:1 step-up instead of a step-down.

A 4:1 step-down transformer has four times as many turns on the primary as on the secondary. Voltage scales with the number of turns, so the large winding gives four times the voltage.

Why not B? Dividing by 4 is what happens in the normal direction, primary to secondary.

G5C03

What is the total resistance of a 10-, a 20-, and a 50-ohm resistor connected in parallel?

  1. A 5.9 ohms
  2. B 0.17 ohms
  3. C 17 ohms
  4. D 80 ohms
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 5.9 ohms

Why A is right. A. 10, 20 and 50 ohms in parallel come to about 5.9 ohms.

For resistors in parallel, add the reciprocal of each one, then take the reciprocal of the sum. The total is always smaller than the smallest resistor.

1 / R = 1 / R1 + 1 / R2 + 1 / R3
1 / R = 1/10 + 1/20 + 1/50 = 0.10 + 0.05 + 0.02 = 0.17
R = 1 / 0.17 = 5.88 ohms, about 5.9 ohms
= 5.9 ohms

Why not B? 0.17 is the sum of the reciprocals before you invert it.

G5C04

What is the approximate total resistance of a 100- and a 200-ohm resistor in parallel?

  1. A 300 ohms
  2. B 150 ohms
  3. C 75 ohms
  4. D 67 ohms
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 67 ohms

Why D is right. D. 100 and 200 ohms in parallel come to about 67 ohms.

For two resistors in parallel, multiply them and divide by their sum. The result is smaller than the smaller resistor.

R = (R1 × R2) / (R1 + R2)
R = (100 × 200) / (100 + 200)
R = 20,000 / 300 = 66.7 ohms, about 67 ohms
= 67 ohms

Why not A? 300 ohms is the series total, not the parallel one.

G5C05

Why is the primary winding wire of a voltage step-up transformer usually a larger size than that of the secondary winding?

  1. A To improve the coupling between the primary and secondary
  2. B To accommodate the higher current of the primary
  3. C To prevent parasitic oscillations due to resistive losses in the primary
  4. D To ensure that the volume of the primary winding is equal to the volume of the secondary winding
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. To accommodate the higher current of the primary

Why B is right. B. Power out roughly equals power in, so the low-voltage side of the transformer must carry the higher current, and that side is the primary.

Power is voltage times current. In a step-up transformer the secondary has the higher voltage and so the lower current. Thicker wire has less resistance and stays cooler.

Why not D? Wire size is chosen for the current it carries, not to match the volume of the windings.

G5C06

What is the voltage output of a transformer with a 500-turn primary and a 1500-turn secondary when 120 VAC is applied to the primary?

  1. A 360 volts
  2. B 120 volts
  3. C 40 volts
  4. D 25.5 volts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 360 volts

Why A is right. A. Three times as many secondary turns gives 3 × 120 = 360 volts.

In a transformer, the voltage ratio equals the turns ratio. Multiply the primary voltage by the secondary turns divided by the primary turns.

Es = Ep × Ns / Np
Es = 120 V × 1500 / 500
Es = 120 V × 3 = 360 V
= 360 volts

Why not C? 40 volts comes from dividing by 3, which would be a step-down transformer.

G5C07

What transformer turns ratio matches an antenna’s 600-ohm feed point impedance to a 50-ohm coaxial cable?

  1. A 3.5 to 1
  2. B 12 to 1
  3. C 24 to 1
  4. D 144 to 1
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 3.5 to 1

Why A is right. A. A turns ratio of about 3.5 to 1 matches 600 ohms to 50 ohms.

A transformer changes impedance by the square of its turns ratio. So to find the turns ratio, take the square root of the impedance ratio.

turns ratio = √(Zp / Zs)
turns ratio = √(600 / 50) = √12
turns ratio = 3.46, about 3.5 to 1
= 3.5 to 1

Why not B? 12 to 1 is the impedance ratio, not the turns ratio.

G5C08

What is the equivalent capacitance of two 5.0-nanofarad capacitors and one 750-picofarad capacitor connected in parallel?

  1. A 576.9 nanofarads
  2. B 1,733 picofarads
  3. C 3,583 picofarads
  4. D 10.750 nanofarads
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 10.750 nanofarads

Why D is right. D. Parallel capacitances add, giving 10.750 nanofarads.

Capacitors in parallel act like one capacitor with bigger plates, so their values add. First put all values in the same unit. 750 picofarads is 0.750 nanofarads, since 1 nanofarad is 1000 picofarads.

C = C1 + C2 + C3
750 pF = 0.750 nF
C = 5.0 nF + 5.0 nF + 0.750 nF = 10.750 nF
= 10.750 nanofarads

Why not A? 576.9 is close to what the three would give in series, and that result is in picofarads, not nanofarads.

G5C09

What is the capacitance of three 100-microfarad capacitors connected in series?

  1. A 0.33 microfarads
  2. B 3.0 microfarads
  3. C 33.3 microfarads
  4. D 300 microfarads
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 33.3 microfarads

Why C is right. C. Three equal 100-microfarad capacitors in series give about 33.3 microfarads.

Capacitors in series combine like resistors in parallel: add the reciprocals and invert. For equal values, divide one value by how many there are. The total is smaller than any single capacitor.

1 / C = 1 / C1 + 1 / C2 + 1 / C3
equal values: C = 100 μF / 3
C = 33.3 μF
= 33.3 microfarads

Why not D? 300 microfarads is the parallel total, not the series one.

G5C10

What is the inductance of three 10-millihenry inductors connected in parallel?

  1. A 0.30 henries
  2. B 3.3 henries
  3. C 3.3 millihenries
  4. D 30 millihenries
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 3.3 millihenries

Why C is right. C. Three equal 10-millihenry inductors in parallel give about 3.3 millihenries.

Inductors in parallel combine like resistors in parallel: add the reciprocals and invert. For equal values, divide one value by how many there are. This assumes the coils are not magnetically coupled.

1 / L = 1 / L1 + 1 / L2 + 1 / L3
equal values: L = 10 mH / 3
L = 3.33 mH, about 3.3 mH
= 3.3 millihenries

Why not D? 30 millihenries is the series total, not the parallel one.

G5C11

What is the inductance of a circuit with a 20-millihenry inductor connected in series with a 50-millihenry inductor?

  1. A 7 millihenries
  2. B 14.3 millihenries
  3. C 70 millihenries
  4. D 1,000 millihenries
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 70 millihenries

Why C is right. C. Series inductances add: 20 + 50 = 70 millihenries.

Inductors in series combine like resistors in series, so you add them. This assumes the coils are not magnetically coupled.

L = L1 + L2
L = 20 mH + 50 mH
L = 70 mH
= 70 millihenries

Why not B? 14.3 millihenries is what the two would give in parallel.

G5C12

What is the capacitance of a 20-microfarad capacitor connected in series with a 50-microfarad capacitor?

  1. A 0.07 microfarads
  2. B 14.3 microfarads
  3. C 70 microfarads
  4. D 1,000 microfarads
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 14.3 microfarads

Why B is right. B. 20 and 50 microfarads in series give about 14.3 microfarads.

Capacitors in series combine like resistors in parallel. For two of them, multiply and divide by the sum. The total is smaller than the smaller capacitor.

C = (C1 × C2) / (C1 + C2)
C = (20 × 50) / (20 + 50) μF
C = 1000 / 70 = 14.29 μF, about 14.3 μF
= 14.3 microfarads

Why not C? 70 microfarads is what the two would give in parallel.

G5C13

Which of the following components should be added to a capacitor to increase the capacitance?

  1. A An inductor in series
  2. B An inductor in parallel
  3. C A capacitor in parallel
  4. D A capacitor in series
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A capacitor in parallel

Why C is right. C. Capacitances in parallel add, much like making the plate area bigger.

Capacitors in series give a total smaller than either one.

Why not D? A capacitor in series lowers the total capacitance.

G5C14

Which of the following components should be added to an inductor to increase the inductance?

  1. A A capacitor in series
  2. B A capacitor in parallel
  3. C An inductor in parallel
  4. D An inductor in series
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. An inductor in series

Why D is right. D. Inductances in series add, as resistances in series do.

Inductors in parallel give a total smaller than either one. This assumes the coils are not magnetically coupled.

Why not C? An inductor in parallel lowers the total inductance.
