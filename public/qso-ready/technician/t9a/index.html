T9A: Antennas: vertical and horizontal polarization, concept of antenna gain, definition and types of beam antennas, antenna loading, common portable and mobile antennas, relationships between resonant length and frequency, dipole pattern

Group T9A of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T9A01

What is a beam antenna?

  1. A An antenna built from square aluminum beams
  2. B An omnidirectional antenna invented by Clarence Beam
  3. C An antenna that concentrates signals in one direction
  4. D An antenna that focuses the signal into two intense rays
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. An antenna that concentrates signals in one direction

Why C is right. C. A beam antenna concentrates signals in one direction.

A beam antenna sends more of your signal one way and less in other directions. It also hears best from that same direction. This makes signals stronger where you aim and cuts interference from other directions.

Why not B? A beam is directional, the opposite of omnidirectional.

T9A02

Which of the following describes a type of antenna loading?

  1. A Electrically lengthening by inserting inductors in radiating elements
  2. B Inserting a resistor in the radiating portion of the antenna to make it resonant
  3. C Installing a spring in the base of a mobile vertical antenna to make it more flexible
  4. D Strengthening the radiating elements of a beam antenna to better resist wind damage
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Electrically lengthening by inserting inductors in radiating elements

Why A is right. A. Inserting inductors in the radiating elements is a type of antenna loading.

An inductor is a coil of wire. Adding one to an antenna element makes the element act electrically longer than it really is. This lets a short antenna, such as a mobile whip, resonate on a lower frequency than its physical size would allow.

Why not B? A resistor wastes power as heat and does not make an antenna resonant.

T9A03

How is the polarization of an antenna described?

  1. A By the shape of the driven element
  2. B By the orientation of the electric field
  3. C By the orientation of the magnetic field
  4. D By the direction of radiation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. By the orientation of the electric field

Why B is right. B. Polarization is described by the direction of the electric field.

A radio wave has an electric field and a magnetic field at right angles to each other. Polarization names the direction of the electric field. A vertical antenna makes a vertical electric field, so its signal is vertically polarized.

Why not C? The magnetic field is at right angles to the electric field, and polarization is not named by it.

T9A04

What is a disadvantage of a handheld radio transceiver’s short flexible antenna compared to a full-sized quarter-wave antenna?

  1. A It has low efficiency
  2. B It transmits only circularly polarized signals
  3. C It is more susceptible to receiver desensitization
  4. D It only works on analog signals, not digital ones
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It has low efficiency

Why A is right. A. A short flexible handheld antenna has low efficiency.

A full quarter-wave antenna on 2 meters is about 19 inches long. Short flexible antennas are much shorter, so they need loading to resonate. The loading and small size mean more power is lost as heat and less is radiated.

Why not B? These short antennas are linearly polarized, not circularly polarized.

T9A05

Which of the following increases the resonant frequency of a dipole antenna?

  1. A Lengthening it
  2. B Inserting coils in series with radiating wires
  3. C Shortening it
  4. D Adding capacitive loading to the ends of the radiating wires
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Shortening it

Why C is right. C. Shortening a dipole increases its resonant frequency.

A dipole resonates when its length is about half a wavelength. Higher frequencies have shorter wavelengths. So a shorter antenna matches a shorter wavelength, which means a higher frequency.

Why not B? Coils make the antenna act longer, which lowers the resonant frequency.

Remember: Shorter wire, higher frequency.

T9A06

Which of the following types of antennas offers the greatest gain?

  1. A 5/8 wave vertical
  2. B Isotropic
  3. C J pole
  4. D Yagi
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Yagi

Why D is right. D. A Yagi offers the greatest gain of these antennas.

A Yagi has one driven element plus extra elements in front of and behind it. These extra elements shape the signal into a beam aimed in one direction. That focus gives it more gain than the other choices, which spread their signal around rather than concentrating it.

Why not B? An isotropic antenna is an imaginary reference that radiates equally everywhere, so it has no gain at all.

T9A07

What is a potential drawback of using a handheld VHF transceiver inside a vehicle that lacks an externally mounted antenna?

  1. A Signal strength is reduced due to the shielding effect of the vehicle
  2. B The bandwidth of the antenna will decrease, increasing SWR
  3. C The SWR might decrease, decreasing the signal strength
  4. D The handheld will overheat due to reflected power in the vehicle
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Signal strength is reduced due to the shielding effect of the vehicle

Why A is right. A. A vehicle's metal body shields the signal and reduces its strength.

A car body is mostly metal, which blocks and reflects radio waves. With the handheld antenna inside, much of the signal stays trapped in the car. An antenna mounted outside on the roof avoids this problem.

Why not D? Reflected power inside the car does not make the handheld overheat. The problem is weak signal.

T9A08

Why is a 19-inch-long vertical antenna often used on 2 meters?

  1. A It has high gain
  2. B It is a resonant half-wave
  3. C It is a resonant quarter-wave
  4. D It has low RF radiation exposure
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. It is a resonant quarter-wave

Why C is right. C. A 19-inch vertical is a resonant quarter-wave on 2 meters.

Wavelength in meters equals 300 divided by the frequency in MHz. At 146 MHz, a wavelength is about 2.05 meters, so a quarter wave is about 20 inches. Real antennas are cut a little shorter than the formula gives, which brings it to about 19 inches.

wavelength = 300 / f(MHz); quarter wave = wavelength / 4
wavelength = 300 / 146 MHz = 2.05 m
wavelength / 4 = 2.05 m / 4 = 0.514 m ≈ 20.2 inches
about 5% shorter for real antennas: 20.2 × 0.95 ≈ 19.2 inches
= about 19 inches

Why not B? A half wave on 2 meters would be about 39 inches.

T9A09

What is an advantage of a 5/8-wavelength whip antenna for VHF or UHF mobile service compared to a 1/4-wave antenna?

  1. A It has more gain
  2. B It radiates at a higher angle
  3. C It has lower SWR
  4. D It has lower impedance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It has more gain

Why A is right. A. A 5/8-wave whip has more gain than a 1/4-wave whip.

A 5/8-wave whip is longer and squeezes more of its signal toward the horizon. For mobile work, the stations you want to reach are near the horizon. Sending more power in that direction gives you more gain where it counts.

Why not B? It radiates at a lower angle, not a higher one.

T9A10

In which direction does a half-wave dipole antenna radiate the strongest signal?

  1. A Equally in all directions
  2. B Off the ends of the antenna
  3. C In the direction of the feed line
  4. D Broadside to the antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Broadside to the antenna

Why D is right. D. A half-wave dipole radiates most strongly broadside to the antenna.

Broadside means at right angles to the length of the wire. A dipole sends its strongest signal straight out from its sides and very little off its ends. Seen from above, the pattern looks like a figure eight.

Why not B? The ends of a dipole are where it radiates the least.

T9A11

What is antenna gain?

  1. A The additional power that is added to the transmitter power
  2. B The additional power that is required in the antenna when transmitting on a higher frequency
  3. C The increase in signal strength in a specified direction compared to a reference antenna
  4. D The increase in impedance on receive or transmit compared to a reference antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The increase in signal strength in a specified direction compared to a reference antenna

Why C is right. C. Antenna gain is the increase in signal strength in one direction compared to a reference antenna.

An antenna cannot create power. Gain comes from focusing power. A gain antenna takes energy that would go in other directions and sends more of it one way. Gain is measured by comparing it with a reference antenna, such as a dipole.

Why not A? Gain does not add power to the transmitter. It only redirects the power you already have.
