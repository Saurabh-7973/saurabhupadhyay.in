E9H: Receiving antennas: radio direction finding (RDF) techniques; Beverage antennas; single- and multiple-turn loops

Group E9H of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E9H01

When constructing a Beverage antenna, which of the following factors should be included in the design to achieve good performance at the desired frequency?

  1. A Its overall length must not exceed 1/4 wavelength
  2. B It must be mounted more than 1 wavelength above ground
  3. C It should be configured as a four-sided loop
  4. D It should be at least one wavelength long
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It should be at least one wavelength long

Why D is right. D. A Beverage gains directivity from its length, and it needs to be at least about one wavelength long to work well.

A Beverage is a long, low wire running toward the stations you want to hear, a few meters above ground. The incoming wave builds up a signal along the wire as it travels. A longer wire gives a narrower, stronger forward pattern. Below about one wavelength, directivity drops off. That is why Beverages for 160 meters can be hundreds of meters long.

Why not B? A Beverage works best low to the ground; mounting it high would ruin how it picks up the wave.

E9H02

Which is generally true for 160- and 80-meter receiving antennas?

  1. A Atmospheric noise is so high that directivity is much more important than losses
  2. B They must be erected at least 1/2 wavelength above the ground to attain good directivity
  3. C Low loss coax transmission line is essential for good performance
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Atmospheric noise is so high that directivity is much more important than losses

Why A is right. A. On 160 and 80 meters, outside noise is so strong that cutting it with a directional pattern matters more than antenna loss.

At these low frequencies, noise from distant thunderstorms and other sources is very high. An inefficient antenna lowers both signal and noise equally, so the ratio does not change. What helps is directivity: hearing the wanted direction while rejecting noise from others. That is why small, lossy but directional receive antennas work so well.

Why not C? Low feed line loss matters little when outside noise far exceeds the receiver's own noise.

E9H03

What is receiving directivity factor (RDF)?

  1. A Forward gain compared to the gain in the reverse direction
  2. B Relative directivity compared to isotropic
  3. C Relative directivity compared to a dipole
  4. D Peak antenna gain compared to average gain over the hemisphere around and above the antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Peak antenna gain compared to average gain over the hemisphere around and above the antenna

Why D is right. D. Low-band noise arrives from all directions, so an antenna's peak gain over its average gain on the hemisphere predicts its signal-to-noise edge.

Noise on the low bands arrives from many directions at once. An antenna whose peak stands far above its average picks up the wanted direction while hearing less of that noise. RDF (receiving directivity factor) puts a number on that. A higher RDF means a better signal-to-noise ratio in the favored direction.

Why not A? Forward gain over reverse gain is the front-to-back ratio, a different measure.

E9H04

What is the purpose of placing an electrostatic shield around a small-loop direction-finding antenna?

  1. A It adds capacitive loading, increasing the bandwidth of the antenna
  2. B It eliminates unbalanced capacitive coupling to the antenna’s surroundings, improving the depth of its nulls
  3. C It eliminates tracking errors caused by strong out-of-band signals
  4. D It increases signal strength by providing a better match to the feed line
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. It eliminates unbalanced capacitive coupling to the antenna’s surroundings, improving the depth of its nulls

Why B is right. B. The shield blocks uneven electric-field coupling to nearby objects, which keeps the loop balanced and its nulls deep.

A small loop should respond only to the magnetic part of the wave, giving two sharp nulls. Stray capacitance to the ground, the operator or nearby objects lets the electric field leak in. That leakage is not equal on both sides, so the nulls fill in and shift. A shield with a small gap stops the electric coupling but lets the magnetic field through.

Why not D? The shield is about balance and null depth, not about matching the loop to its feed line.

E9H05

What challenge is presented by a small wire-loop antenna for direction finding?

  1. A It has a bidirectional null pattern
  2. B It does not have a clearly defined null
  3. C It is practical for use only on VHF and higher bands
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It has a bidirectional null pattern

Why A is right. A. A small loop's two nulls point in opposite directions, so one bearing cannot tell you which side the signal is on.

Turn a small loop until the signal disappears. The null lies along a line through the loop, but the same null appears 180 degrees around. The transmitter could be either way. Taking bearings from a second location, or adding a sense antenna, resolves the doubt.

Why not B? Small loops are known for sharp, well-defined nulls; that is why they are used for direction finding.

E9H06

What indicates the correct value of terminating resistance for a Beverage antenna?

  1. A Maximum feed point DC resistance at the center of the desired frequency range
  2. B Minimum low-angle front-to-back ratio at the design frequency
  3. C Maximum DC current in the terminating resistor
  4. D Minimum variation in SWR over the desired frequency range
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Minimum variation in SWR over the desired frequency range

Why D is right. D. With the right termination, no wave reflects from the far end, so the feed impedance stays steady and SWR barely changes with frequency.

A Beverage acts like a long transmission line over the ground. If its end resistor matches that line, all energy reaching the end is absorbed. With nothing reflecting, the feed point sees the same impedance across the band. A wrong resistor causes reflections that make the SWR rise and fall as you tune.

Why not B? A good termination gives the highest front-to-back ratio, not the lowest.

E9H07

What is the function of a Beverage antenna’s termination resistor?

  1. A Increase the front-to-side ratio
  2. B Absorb signals from the reverse direction
  3. C Decrease SWR bandwidth
  4. D Eliminate harmonic reception
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Absorb signals from the reverse direction

Why B is right. B. The resistor absorbs signals arriving from the back, so they do not reflect to the receiver, making the antenna one-way.

Signals from the front travel along the wire toward the receiver end. Signals from the back travel the other way, toward the far end. Without a resistor, they would bounce back and reach the receiver too. The matched resistor turns that unwanted energy into heat, so the Beverage hears mainly one direction.

Why not A? The resistor mainly removes the rear response, not the sides.

E9H08

What is the function of a sense antenna?

  1. A It modifies the pattern of a DF antenna to provide a null in only one direction
  2. B It increases the sensitivity of a DF antenna array
  3. C It allows DF antennas to receive signals at different vertical angles
  4. D It provides diversity reception that cancels multipath signals
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It modifies the pattern of a DF antenna to provide a null in only one direction

Why A is right. A. A sense antenna adds a signal to the loop's output that cancels one null and reinforces the other, leaving a single null.

A small loop alone has a figure-eight pattern with two opposite nulls. A short vertical, the sense antenna, picks up signals equally from all directions. Combined with the loop in the right phase and level, it adds to one lobe and subtracts from the other. The result is a heart-shaped cardioid with one null, so the bearing is no longer ambiguous.

Why not B? The sense antenna fixes direction ambiguity; it is not there to boost sensitivity.

E9H09

What type of radiation pattern is created by a single-turn, terminated loop such as a pennant antenna?

  1. A Cardioid
  2. B Bidirectional
  3. C Omnidirectional
  4. D Hyperbolic
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Cardioid

Why A is right. A. A pennant adds the loop's figure-eight response to the all-around response of its vertical wires, and the resistor sets their phase, giving a cardioid.

A small loop alone hears two opposite directions equally, a figure-eight. Its vertical wires also act like a short vertical antenna, which hears all directions equally. The terminating resistor sets the level and phase of that second response. In one direction the two add; in the opposite direction they cancel. The result is a heart-shaped cardioid with a deep rear null.

Why not B? An unterminated small loop is bidirectional; the terminating resistor is what removes one direction.

E9H10

How can the output voltage of a multiple-turn receiving loop antenna be increased?

  1. A By reducing the permeability of the loop shield
  2. B By utilizing high impedance wire for the coupling loop
  3. C By increasing the number of turns and/or the area enclosed by the loop
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. By increasing the number of turns and/or the area enclosed by the loop

Why C is right. C. A loop's voltage grows with each turn and with the area it encloses, since both increase the magnetic field it collects.

A changing magnetic field passing through a loop induces a voltage. A larger area catches more of that field. Each extra turn adds another induced voltage in series. So output voltage rises roughly in step with the number of turns times the area, for a given signal and frequency.

Why not A? A loop shield is not part of the voltage equation; turns and area are.

E9H11

What feature of a cardioid pattern antenna makes it useful for direction-finding antennas?

  1. A A very sharp peak
  2. B A single null
  3. C Broadband response
  4. D High radiation angle
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A single null

Why B is right. B. A cardioid has one null, so turning the antenna until the signal fades points to a single direction.

Nulls are sharper than peaks, so direction finders search for the null. A figure-eight pattern has two nulls, leaving a 180-degree doubt. A cardioid has only one, at the back of the heart shape. Pointing that null at the signal gives a bearing that tells you which side the transmitter is on.

Why not A? A cardioid's front peak is broad; its usefulness comes from the single sharp null.
