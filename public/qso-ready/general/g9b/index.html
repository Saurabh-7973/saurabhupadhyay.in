G9B: Basic dipole and monopole antennas

Group G9B of the General pool (2023–2027) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G9B01

What is a characteristic of a random-wire HF antenna connected directly to the transmitter?

  1. A It must be longer than 1 wavelength
  2. B Station equipment may carry significant RF current
  3. C It produces only vertically polarized radiation
  4. D It is more effective on the lower HF bands than on the higher bands
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Station equipment may carry significant RF current

Why B is right. B. With no feed line, the radio's case and wiring become the other half of the antenna, so RF current flows on them.

A random wire connects straight to the radio with no separate feed line. RF current can then flow on equipment, cables and even the microphone, causing RF burns, a buzzing mic or erratic gear.

Why not A? A random wire can be any length; it need not be longer than one wavelength.

G9B02

Which of the following is a common way to adjust the feed point impedance of an elevated quarter-wave ground-plane vertical antenna to be approximately 50 ohms?

  1. A Slope the radials upward
  2. B Slope the radials downward
  3. C Lengthen the radials beyond one wavelength
  4. D Coil the radials
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Slope the radials downward

Why B is right. B. Sloping the radials downward raises the feed point impedance to near 50 ohms.

A quarter-wave vertical with flat radials has a feed point impedance of about 36 ohms. Tilting the radials down, like the ribs of a drooping umbrella, raises it. At a slope of roughly 30 to 45 degrees it gets close to 50 ohms, a good match for common coax.

Why not A? Sloping radials upward lowers the impedance, moving it away from 50 ohms.

G9B03

Which of the following best describes the radiation pattern of a quarter-wave ground-plane vertical antenna?

  1. A Bi-directional in azimuth
  2. B Isotropic
  3. C Hemispherical
  4. D Omnidirectional in azimuth
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Omnidirectional in azimuth

Why D is right. D. A quarter-wave ground-plane vertical radiates equally in all compass directions.

Azimuth means compass direction around the horizon. A vertical element has no favored side, so seen from above its pattern is a circle. It sends little energy straight up, so it is not radiating equally in every direction in space.

Why not B? Isotropic means equal in all directions, including up and down, which no real antenna does.

G9B04

What is the radiation pattern of a dipole antenna in free space in a plane containing the conductor?

  1. A It is a figure-eight at right angles to the antenna
  2. B It is a figure-eight off both ends of the antenna
  3. C It is a circle (equal radiation in all directions)
  4. D It has a pair of lobes on one side of the antenna and a single lobe on the other side
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It is a figure-eight at right angles to the antenna

Why A is right. A. Current along the wire radiates mostly sideways and almost nothing along the wire's own line, so both lobes stand broadside.

Picture the dipole lying across a page. In that plane, the pattern looks like an upright number 8 with the wire running through its narrow waist. Almost nothing comes off the ends.

Why not B? The ends of a dipole are the weakest directions, not the strongest.

G9B05

How does antenna height affect the azimuthal radiation pattern of a horizontal dipole HF antenna at elevation angles higher than about 45 degrees?

  1. A If the antenna is too high, the pattern becomes unpredictable
  2. B Antenna height has no effect on the pattern
  3. C If the antenna is less than 1/2 wavelength high, the azimuthal pattern is almost omnidirectional
  4. D If the antenna is less than 1/2 wavelength high, radiation off the ends of the wire is eliminated
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. If the antenna is less than 1/2 wavelength high, the azimuthal pattern is almost omnidirectional

Why C is right. C. A low dipole's ground reflection sends much of its energy upward, and at those high angles the figure-eight fills in until it is nearly round.

Low here means below a half wavelength above the ground. High-angle radiation suits close-in contacts using skywave.

Why not D? Low height fills in the end directions; it does not eliminate radiation off the ends.

G9B06

Where should the radial wires of a ground-mounted vertical antenna system be placed?

  1. A As high as possible above the ground
  2. B Parallel to the antenna element
  3. C On the surface or buried a few inches below the ground
  4. D At the center of the antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. On the surface or buried a few inches below the ground

Why C is right. C. Ground-mounted radials only need to give return current a low-loss path near the base, and on or slightly under the soil does that while staying out of the way.

The return current would otherwise flow through poor soil and waste power. The radials spread outward from the base like spokes.

Why not A? Raised radials belong to an elevated ground-plane antenna, not a ground-mounted one.

G9B07

How does the feed point impedance of a horizontal 1/2 wave dipole antenna change as the antenna height is reduced to 1/10 wavelength above ground?

  1. A It steadily increases
  2. B It steadily decreases
  3. C It peaks at about 1/8 wavelength above ground
  4. D It is unaffected by the height above ground
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. It steadily decreases

Why B is right. B. As the dipole is lowered to a tenth of a wavelength, its feed point impedance steadily drops.

Ground acts like a mirror that interacts with the antenna. Close to the ground, the reflected signal works against the antenna's own field. The radiation resistance falls, so the feed point impedance keeps going down as the antenna gets lower.

Why not A? The impedance falls, not rises, as the antenna gets close to the ground.

G9B08

How does the feed point impedance of a 1/2 wave dipole change as the feed point is moved from the center toward the ends?

  1. A It steadily increases
  2. B It steadily decreases
  3. C It peaks at about 1/8 wavelength from the end
  4. D It is unaffected by the location of the feed point
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It steadily increases

Why A is right. A. The impedance rises steadily as the feed point moves from the center toward the ends.

On a half-wave dipole, current is largest at the center and smallest at the ends, while voltage does the opposite. Impedance is voltage divided by current. Moving outward means more voltage and less current, so impedance climbs.

Why not B? Moving outward increases voltage and lowers current, so impedance goes up, not down.

G9B09

Which of the following is an advantage of using a horizontally polarized as compared to a vertically polarized HF antenna?

  1. A Lower ground losses
  2. B Lower feed point impedance
  3. C Shorter radials
  4. D Lower radiation resistance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Lower ground losses

Why A is right. A. Horizontal HF antennas have lower ground losses.

A vertical antenna's return current flows through the ground near its base. Soil is a poor conductor, so it turns some power into heat unless many radials are installed. A horizontal antenna does not depend on that ground current.

Why not C? Horizontal dipoles do not use radials at all, so radial length is not their advantage.

G9B10

What is the approximate length for a 1/2 wave dipole antenna cut for 14.250 MHz?

  1. A 8 feet
  2. B 16 feet
  3. C 24 feet
  4. D 33 feet
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 33 feet

Why D is right. D. The rule of thumb 468 ÷ 14.250 gives 32.8 feet, which rounds to 33 feet.

A free-space half wavelength is about 492 ÷ MHz feet. The 468 is that figure made about 5% shorter, because a real wire acts slightly longer than it is.

length in feet = 468 ÷ frequency in MHz
length = 468 ÷ 14.250
length = 32.8 feet, about 33 feet
= 33 feet

Why not B? 16 feet is close to a quarter wavelength, half the dipole's length.

G9B11

What is the approximate length for a 1/2 wave dipole antenna cut for 3.550 MHz?

  1. A 42 feet
  2. B 84 feet
  3. C 132 feet
  4. D 263 feet
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 132 feet

Why C is right. C. A half-wave dipole for 3.550 MHz is about 132 feet.

Use the rule of thumb that a half-wave dipole in feet is 468 divided by the frequency in MHz. Lower frequencies have longer wavelengths, so 80 meter dipoles are long.

length in feet = 468 ÷ frequency in MHz
length = 468 ÷ 3.550
length = 131.8 feet, about 132 feet
= 132 feet

Why not D? 263 feet is close to a full wavelength, twice the dipole's length.

G9B12

What is the approximate length for a 1/4 wave monopole antenna cut for 28.5 MHz?

  1. A 8 feet
  2. B 11 feet
  3. C 16 feet
  4. D 21 feet
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 8 feet

Why A is right. A. A quarter-wave monopole for 28.5 MHz is about 8 feet.

A quarter wavelength is half of a half wavelength. So the rule of thumb becomes 234 divided by the frequency in MHz, giving the length in feet.

length in feet = 234 ÷ frequency in MHz
length = 234 ÷ 28.5
length = 8.2 feet, about 8 feet
= 8 feet

Why not C? 16 feet is close to a half wavelength, twice the monopole's length.
