G6B: Analog and digital integrated circuits (ICs); microwave ICs (MMICs); display devices; RF connectors; ferrite cores

Group G6B of the General pool (2023–2027) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G6B01

What determines the performance of a ferrite core at different frequencies?

  1. A Its conductivity
  2. B Its thickness
  3. C The composition, or “mix,” of materials used
  4. D The ratio of outer diameter to inner diameter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The composition, or “mix,” of materials used

Why C is right. C. The blend of materials sets the core's permeability and the frequency where its losses start to rise.

Ferrite is a ceramic made of iron oxide blended with other metal oxides. Permeability means how strongly it concentrates a magnetic field. Makers sell many mixes, each suited to a different frequency range.

Why not B? Thickness changes how much inductance you get, not which frequencies the material suits.

G6B02

What is meant by the term MMIC?

  1. A Multi-Mode Integrated Circuit
  2. B Monolithic Microwave Integrated Circuit
  3. C Metal Monolayer Integrated Circuit
  4. D Mode Modulated Integrated Circuit
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Monolithic Microwave Integrated Circuit

Why B is right. B. MMIC stands for Monolithic Microwave Integrated Circuit.

Monolithic means made from a single piece. An MMIC builds a complete amplifier or other RF circuit on one chip. Many are small gain blocks that work from VHF into the microwave range with few outside parts.

Why not A? The first M in MMIC stands for monolithic, not multi-mode.

G6B03

Which of the following is an advantage of CMOS integrated circuits compared to TTL integrated circuits?

  1. A Low power consumption
  2. B High power handling capability
  3. C Better suited for RF amplification
  4. D Better suited for power supply regulation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Low power consumption

Why A is right. A. CMOS chips use very little power.

CMOS logic draws almost no current while it holds a steady state. It uses power mainly at the moment it switches. TTL logic draws current all the time, so CMOS is a better choice for battery-powered gear.

Why not B? Logic chips are built for small signals, not for handling high power.

G6B04

What is a typical upper frequency limit for low SWR operation of 50-ohm BNC connectors?

  1. A 50 MHz
  2. B 500 MHz
  3. C 4 GHz
  4. D 40 GHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 4 GHz

Why C is right. C. 50-ohm BNC connectors work with low SWR up to about 4 GHz.

A BNC connector locks with a quick push and twist. It is common on test gear and VHF and UHF radios. Its size and design keep the impedance steady up to about 4 GHz. Above that, the SWR rises.

Why not D? 40 GHz needs much smaller precision connectors.

G6B05

What is an advantage of using a ferrite core toroidal inductor?

  1. A Large values of inductance may be obtained
  2. B The magnetic properties of the core may be optimized for a specific range of frequencies
  3. C Most of the magnetic field is contained in the core
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All three are advantages of a ferrite toroid.

A toroid is a doughnut-shaped core. Ferrite's high permeability gives a large inductance with few turns. You can choose a ferrite mix suited to your frequency range. Because the core is a closed ring, it keeps most of the magnetic field inside, so the coil couples little with nearby parts.

G6B06

What kind of device is an integrated circuit operational amplifier?

  1. A Digital
  2. B MMIC
  3. C Programmable Logic
  4. D Analog
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Analog

Why D is right. D. An operational amplifier is an analog device.

An op amp amplifies the difference between its two input voltages. Its output can be any value within its supply range, smoothly following the input. That makes it analog, unlike digital chips that work with only on and off states.

Why not A? A digital device works only with two states, but an op amp's output varies smoothly.

G6B07

Which of the following describes a type N connector?

  1. A A moisture-resistant RF connector useful to 10 GHz
  2. B A small bayonet connector used for data circuits
  3. C A low noise figure VHF connector
  4. D A nickel plated version of the PL-259
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A moisture-resistant RF connector useful to 10 GHz

Why A is right. A. The type N is threaded with gaskets that keep water out, and its inner shape holds a steady impedance to very high frequencies.

That makes it a good choice for outdoor and microwave connections, useful to about 10 GHz.

Why not D? Type N is a separate design from the PL-259, not a plated version of it.

G6B08

How is an LED biased when emitting light?

  1. A In the tunnel-effect region
  2. B At the Zener voltage
  3. C Reverse biased
  4. D Forward biased
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Forward biased

Why D is right. D. An LED gives off light when it is forward biased.

Forward bias means the anode is more positive than the cathode, so current flows across the junction. As electrons cross and recombine, they release energy as light.

Why not C? A reverse-biased LED blocks current, so it does not light.

G6B10

How does a ferrite bead or core reduce common-mode RF current on the shield of a coaxial cable?

  1. A By creating an impedance in the current’s path
  2. B It converts common-mode current to differential mode current
  3. C By creating an out-of-phase current to cancel the common-mode current
  4. D Ferrites expel magnetic fields
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. By creating an impedance in the current’s path

Why A is right. A. Only the current on the outside of the shield has a magnetic field that reaches the ferrite, so the ferrite adds opposition to that current alone.

The signal inside coax flows out on the center conductor and back on the inside of the shield. Their magnetic fields cancel outside the cable, so the signal is not affected.

Why not C? Ferrite does not create a canceling current; it opposes the unwanted one.

G6B11

What is an SMA connector?

  1. A A type-S to type-M adaptor
  2. B A small threaded connector suitable for signals up to several GHz
  3. C A connector designed for serial multiple access signals
  4. D A type of push-on connector intended for high-voltage applications
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A small threaded connector suitable for signals up to several GHz

Why B is right. B. SMA connectors screw together and hold a steady impedance in a tiny size, so they work well into the GHz range.

They are common on handheld radios, small antennas and microwave gear.

Why not D? An SMA connector screws on rather than pushing on, and it is made for RF, not high voltage.

G6B12

Which of these connector types is commonly used for low frequency or dc signal connections to a transceiver?

  1. A PL-259
  2. B BNC
  3. C RCA Phono
  4. D Type N
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. RCA Phono

Why C is right. C. RCA phono connectors are common for low-frequency and DC connections.

RCA phono jacks are simple and cheap. Radios use them for audio, keying and control lines on the back panel. They are not built to hold a steady impedance at RF, so they are not a good choice for antenna connections.

Why not D? Type N connectors are made for RF, often at high frequencies.
