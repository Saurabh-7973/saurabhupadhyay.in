G4C: Interference to consumer electronics; grounding and bonding

Group G4C of the General pool (2023–2027) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G4C01

Which of the following might be useful in reducing RF interference to audio frequency circuits?

  1. A Bypass inductor
  2. B Bypass capacitor
  3. C Forward-biased diode
  4. D Reverse-biased diode
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Bypass capacitor

Why B is right. B. A bypass capacitor sends RF to ground while letting audio through.

Reactance is the opposition a capacitor or inductor offers to AC, and it depends on frequency. A capacitor has low reactance at RF and high reactance at audio. Connected from an audio line to ground, it gives RF an easy path to ground while audio stays on the line.

Why not A? An inductor has low reactance at audio, so connected to ground it would short out the audio instead of the RF.

G4C02

Which of the following could be a cause of interference covering a wide range of frequencies?

  1. A Not using a balun or line isolator to feed balanced antennas
  2. B Lack of rectification of the transmitter’s signal in power conductors
  3. C Arcing at a poor electrical connection
  4. D Using a balun to feed an unbalanced antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Arcing at a poor electrical connection

Why C is right. C. Arcing at a poor connection makes noise across a wide range of frequencies.

A spark starts and stops current extremely fast. Such sudden changes produce energy spread across a broad range of frequencies. A loose or corroded connection on power wiring can arc again and again, and you hear it as a buzz over much of the band.

Why not A? Feeding a balanced antenna without a balun mainly affects your own operating frequency, not a wide range.

G4C03

What sound is heard from an audio device experiencing RF interference from a single sideband phone transmitter?

  1. A A steady hum whenever the transmitter is on the air
  2. B On-and-off humming or clicking
  3. C Distorted speech
  4. D Clearly audible speech
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Distorted speech

Why C is right. C. SSB interference sounds like distorted speech.

An audio device can rectify RF, meaning its parts act like a crude detector. The detected result follows the strength of the SSB signal, which rises and falls with the voice. Without proper SSB detection, the voice comes out garbled.

Why not D? The device has no SSB detector, so the speech cannot come through clearly.

G4C04

What sound is heard from an audio device experiencing RF interference from a CW transmitter?

  1. A On-and-off humming or clicking
  2. B A CW signal at a nearly pure audio frequency
  3. C A chirpy CW signal
  4. D Severely distorted audio
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. On-and-off humming or clicking

Why A is right. A. The rectified carrier appears and disappears with the keying, so each dot and dash comes through as a hum or click.

A CW signal is a carrier that switches on and off with the code. An audio device can act as a crude detector of RF. Each burst of carrier makes a thump, click or hum in the rhythm of the code.

Why not B? Hearing a clean tone needs an oscillator to mix with the carrier, and audio devices do not have one.

G4C05

What is a possible cause of high voltages that produce RF burns?

  1. A Flat braid rather than round wire has been used for the ground wire
  2. B Insulated wire has been used for the ground wire
  3. C The ground rod is resonant
  4. D The ground wire has high impedance on that frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The ground wire has high impedance on that frequency

Why D is right. D. When the ground wire's impedance is high at the operating frequency, RF voltage builds up at the station end where you can touch it.

At radio frequencies, a ground wire is not always a good ground. If its length is close to a quarter wavelength, or 3/4, 5/4 and so on, it has high impedance at the station end. A half-wave lead is low impedance again. A touch on the equipment can then give an RF burn.

Why not A? The shape of the strap is not the issue; length compared with the wavelength sets the impedance.

G4C06

What is a possible effect of a resonant ground connection?

  1. A Overheating of ground straps
  2. B Corrosion of the ground rod
  3. C High RF voltages on the enclosures of station equipment
  4. D A ground loop
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. High RF voltages on the enclosures of station equipment

Why C is right. C. A ground wire about a quarter wavelength long has almost no RF voltage at the rod but high RF voltage at the far end, which connects to your equipment.

At that length the wire acts like a resonant circuit instead of a ground. So the equipment cases it connects to can carry RF voltage, and touching them can cause burns.

Why not D? A ground loop comes from more than one ground path between pieces of equipment, not from resonance.

G4C07

Why should soldered joints not be used in lightning protection ground connections?

  1. A A soldered joint will likely be destroyed by the heat of a lightning strike
  2. B Solder flux will prevent a low conductivity connection
  3. C Solder has too high a dielectric constant to provide adequate lightning protection
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A soldered joint will likely be destroyed by the heat of a lightning strike

Why A is right. A. Lightning heats the joint enough to melt the solder and open the connection.

A lightning strike pushes an enormous current through the ground path for a short time. That heats every joint. Solder melts at a low temperature, so a soldered joint can come apart at the moment it is needed most. Use clamps or welded joints instead.

Why not C? Dielectric constant describes insulators; solder is a metal conductor.

G4C08

Which of the following would reduce RF interference caused by common-mode current on an audio cable?

  1. A Place a ferrite choke on the cable
  2. B Connect the center conductor to the shield of all cables to short circuit the RFI signal
  3. C Ground the center conductor of the audio cable causing the interference
  4. D Add an additional insulating jacket to the cable
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Place a ferrite choke on the cable

Why A is right. A. Ferrite adds impedance to current flowing the same way on all of the cable's conductors, while the audio, which goes out and back, passes through.

That same-direction current is called common-mode current, and it often rides on the outside of the shield. Impedance means opposition to AC, so the ferrite holds back the RF that causes the interference.

Why not B? Tying the center conductor to the shield would short out the audio signal itself.

G4C09

How can the effects of ground loops be minimized?

  1. A Connect all ground conductors in series
  2. B Connect the AC neutral conductor to the ground wire
  3. C Avoid using lock washers and star washers when making ground connections
  4. D Bond equipment enclosures together
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Bond equipment enclosures together

Why D is right. D. When the cases are tied together, they stay at the same voltage, so no voltage difference is left to drive current around the loop.

A ground loop forms when equipment is grounded through more than one path. Small voltage differences between the paths push current around the loop, often through cable shields. Use short, heavy bonding conductors.

Why not B? Connecting the AC neutral to the ground wire is an electrical safety hazard.

G4C10

What could be a symptom caused by a ground loop in your station’s audio connections?

  1. A You receive reports of “hum” on your station’s transmitted signal
  2. B The SWR reading for one or more antennas is suddenly very high
  3. C An item of station equipment starts to draw excessive amounts of current
  4. D You receive reports of harmonic interference from your station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. You receive reports of “hum” on your station’s transmitted signal

Why A is right. A. Power-line current flowing through an audio cable's shield adds a low-frequency hum to the audio that goes to your transmitter.

This can happen when a ground loop gives power current a path through your audio wiring. Stations hearing you then report hum on your signal.

Why not B? SWR depends on the antenna system, not the audio wiring.

G4C11

What technique helps to minimize RF “hot spots” in an amateur station?

  1. A Building all equipment in a metal enclosure
  2. B Using surge suppressor power outlets
  3. C Bonding all equipment enclosures together
  4. D Placing low-pass filters on all feed lines
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Bonding all equipment enclosures together

Why C is right. C. Tying all cases together with short, wide conductors holds them at the same RF voltage, so no case stands out as a hot spot.

An RF hot spot is a place in your station where a case or cable carries RF voltage. Without bonding, different pieces of equipment can end up at different RF voltages.

Why not D? Low-pass filters reduce harmonics; they do not stop RF voltage on the chassis.

G4C12

Why must all metal enclosures of station equipment be grounded?

  1. A It prevents a blown fuse in the event of an internal short circuit
  2. B It prevents signal overload
  3. C It ensures that the neutral wire is grounded
  4. D It ensures that hazardous voltages cannot appear on the chassis
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It ensures that hazardous voltages cannot appear on the chassis

Why D is right. D. If a loose wire inside touches a grounded case, the fault current flows to ground and opens the fuse or breaker.

Without the ground, the case could sit at a deadly voltage until someone touches it. The ground connection gives the fault somewhere safe to go.

Why not A? Grounding actually makes the fuse blow on a short; that is how it protects you.
