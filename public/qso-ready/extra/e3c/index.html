E3C: Propagation prediction and reporting: radio horizon; effects of space-weather phenomena

Group E3C of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E3C01

What is the cause of short-term radio blackouts?

  1. A Coronal mass ejections
  2. B Sunspots on the solar equator
  3. C North-oriented interplanetary magnetic field
  4. D Solar flares
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Solar flares

Why D is right. D. A flare's X-rays reach Earth in about 8 minutes and add D-layer ionization on the sunlit side, which suddenly absorbs HF signals.

The bands can go quiet for minutes to hours, then recover as the extra ionization fades.

Why not A? CMEs take a day or more to arrive and cause longer storms, not short blackouts.

E3C02

What is indicated by a rising A-index or K-index?

  1. A Increasing disturbance of the geomagnetic field
  2. B Decreasing disturbance of the geomagnetic field
  3. C Higher levels of solar UV radiation
  4. D An increase in the critical frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Increasing disturbance of the geomagnetic field

Why A is right. A. Both indices are built from measured swings in Earth's magnetic field, so higher numbers mean more disturbance.

The K-index covers three hours on a scale of 0 to 9. The A-index gives one average level for a whole day. A disturbed field usually hurts HF, especially at high latitudes.

Why not B? Higher index numbers mean more disturbance, not less.

E3C03

Which of the following signal paths is most likely to experience high levels of absorption when the A-index or K-index is elevated?

  1. A Transequatorial
  2. B Through the auroral oval
  3. C Sporadic-E
  4. D NVIS
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Through the auroral oval

Why B is right. B. When the field is disturbed, particles pour into the auroral oval and add ionization there, so paths through it suffer the most absorption.

The auroral oval is a ring around each magnetic pole. It grows during disturbances, so polar paths are hit hardest.

Why not A? Transequatorial paths are far from the auroral zone.

E3C04

What does the value of Bz (B sub z) represent?

  1. A Geomagnetic field stability
  2. B Critical frequency for vertical transmissions
  3. C North-south strength of the interplanetary magnetic field
  4. D Duration of long-delayed echoes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. North-south strength of the interplanetary magnetic field

Why C is right. C. The z in Bz names the direction along Earth's north-south magnetic axis, so Bz is the north-south part of the interplanetary magnetic field.

The solar wind carries the Sun's magnetic field out into space. Bz shows how strongly that field points north or south.

Why not A? Bz is a direction and strength, not a stability rating.

E3C05

What orientation of Bz (B sub z) increases the likelihood that charged particles from the Sun will cause disturbed conditions?

  1. A Southward
  2. B Northward
  3. C Eastward
  4. D Westward
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Southward

Why A is right. A. A southward Bz links more easily with Earth's magnetic field, letting solar particles in and causing disturbances.

Earth's field points north on the side facing the Sun. A southward field from the solar wind is opposite, so the two fields can connect. That opens a path for energy and particles to enter, leading to storms and aurora.

Why not B? A northward Bz lines up with Earth's field and tends to keep particles out.

E3C06

How does the VHF/UHF radio horizon compare to the geographic horizon?

  1. A It is approximately 15 percent farther
  2. B It is approximately 20 percent nearer
  3. C It is approximately 50 percent farther
  4. D They are approximately the same
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It is approximately 15 percent farther

Why A is right. A. Air bends VHF and UHF waves slightly downward, so the radio horizon is about 15 percent farther than the visible horizon.

Air density falls with height, so waves bend gently toward the ground. Engineers model this by treating the Earth as 4/3 its real size. Horizon distance grows with the square root of the radius.

radio horizon / geographic horizon = square root of (4/3)
square root of 1.333 = 1.155
1.155 - 1 = 0.155, about 15%
= 15%

Why not C? 50 percent overstates the bending in normal air.

E3C07

Which of the following indicates the greatest solar flare intensity?

  1. A Class A
  2. B Class Z
  3. C Class M
  4. D Class X
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Class X

Why D is right. D. The flare scale runs A, B, C, M, X, each step ten times stronger, so X is the top.

Flares are rated by their X-ray brightness. An X flare can black out HF on the sunlit side of Earth.

Why not C? Class M is one step below X.

E3C08

Which of the following is the space-weather term for an extreme geomagnetic storm?

  1. A B9
  2. B X5
  3. C M9
  4. D G5
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. G5

Why D is right. D. Geomagnetic storms are rated on a G scale from G1 (minor) to G5 (extreme), so G5 is the extreme level.

Other space-weather scales use R for radio blackouts and S for solar radiation storms. X5 is a flare rating.

Why not B? X5 describes a solar flare, not a geomagnetic storm.

E3C09

What type of data is reported by amateur radio propagation reporting networks?

  1. A Solar flux
  2. B Electric field intensity
  3. C Magnetic declination
  4. D Digital-mode and CW signals
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Digital-mode and CW signals

Why D is right. D. These networks use automated receivers that decode digital and CW signals, so what they report is which signals were heard.

They post time, frequency and signal strength to the internet, so you can see where your signal reaches in near real time.

Why not A? Solar flux comes from solar observatories, not ham networks.

E3C10

What does the 304A solar parameter measure?

  1. A The ratio of X-ray flux to radio flux, correlated to sunspot number
  2. B UV emissions at 304 angstroms, correlated to the solar flux index
  3. C The solar wind velocity at an angle of 304 degrees from the solar equator, correlated to geomagnetic storms
  4. D The solar emission at 304 GHz, correlated to X-ray flare levels
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. UV emissions at 304 angstroms, correlated to the solar flux index

Why B is right. B. The name gives the wavelength: 304 angstroms is in the ultraviolet, and that light rises and falls with the solar flux index.

An angstrom is one ten-billionth of a meter. Ultraviolet at this wavelength helps ionize the upper atmosphere.

Why not D? 304 here is a wavelength in angstroms, not a frequency in GHz.

E3C11

What does VOACAP software model?

  1. A AC voltage and impedance
  2. B VHF radio propagation
  3. C HF propagation
  4. D AC current and impedance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. HF propagation

Why C is right. C. VOACAP was built to predict how the ionosphere carries HF signals between two places.

You enter the locations, time, power and antennas. It predicts which HF frequencies are likely to work and how strong signals will be.

Why not B? VOACAP focuses on HF, not VHF.

E3C12

Which of the following is indicated by a sudden rise in radio background noise across a large portion of the HF spectrum?

  1. A A temperature inversion has occurred
  2. B A coronal mass ejection impact or a solar flare has occurred
  3. C Transequatorial propagation on 6 meters is likely
  4. D Long-path propagation on the higher HF bands is likely
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A coronal mass ejection impact or a solar flare has occurred

Why B is right. B. Flares and CME impacts bring solar radio bursts and storm-time noise, which can raise the noise floor across much of HF at once.

At the same time, extra ionization in the D layer absorbs signals, so signals fade as the noise climbs.

Why not A? A temperature inversion affects VHF and UHF ducting, not HF noise.
