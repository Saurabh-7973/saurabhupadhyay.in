G2D: Volunteer Monitor Program; HF operations

Group G2D of the General pool (2023–2027) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G2D01

What is the Volunteer Monitor Program?

  1. A Amateur volunteers who are formally enlisted to monitor the airwaves for rules violations
  2. B Amateur volunteers who conduct amateur licensing examinations
  3. C Amateur volunteers who conduct frequency coordination for amateur VHF repeaters
  4. D Amateur volunteers who use their station equipment to help civil defense organizations in times of emergency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Amateur volunteers who are formally enlisted to monitor the airwaves for rules violations

Why A is right. A. The program enlists trained amateurs to listen on the bands and report possible rule violations.

Volunteer Monitors are licensed amateurs who are trained and formally signed up. They listen for rule problems, send friendly notices to operators, and refer serious cases for FCC action. The FCC currently runs the program together with the ARRL.

Why not B? Exams are given by Volunteer Examiners, a separate program.

G2D02

Which of the following are objectives of the Volunteer Monitor Program?

  1. A To conduct efficient and orderly amateur licensing examinations
  2. B To provide emergency and public safety communications
  3. C To coordinate repeaters for efficient and orderly spectrum usage
  4. D To encourage amateur radio operators to self-regulate and comply with the rules
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. To encourage amateur radio operators to self-regulate and comply with the rules

Why D is right. D. The program's goal is for amateurs to police themselves, helping operators follow the rules before enforcement is needed.

Most rule problems on the air are mistakes, not bad intent. Volunteer Monitors point them out early, often with a friendly note, so the operator can fix them. Good conduct then spreads by example. Only serious or repeated cases are passed on to the FCC.

Why not B? Emergency communications are the job of groups such as RACES, not monitors.

G2D03

What procedure may be used by Volunteer Monitors to localize a station whose continuous carrier is holding a repeater on in their area?

  1. A Compare vertical and horizontal signal strengths on the input frequency
  2. B Compare beam headings on the repeater input from their home locations with that of other Volunteer Monitors
  3. C Compare signal strengths between the input and output of the repeater
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Compare beam headings on the repeater input from their home locations with that of other Volunteer Monitors

Why B is right. B. Bearings taken from several known spots form lines on a map, and the lines cross near the transmitter.

A stuck transmitter shows up on the repeater's input frequency. Each monitor turns a beam antenna until that signal is strongest and notes the heading. Drawn on a map from each monitor's home, the lines meet near the source. This is called triangulation.

Why not C? Comparing input and output strength does not show which direction the signal comes from.

G2D04

Which of the following describes an azimuthal projection map?

  1. A A map that shows accurate land masses
  2. B A map that shows true bearings and distances from a specific location
  3. C A map that shows the angle at which an amateur satellite crosses the equator
  4. D A map that shows the number of degrees longitude that an amateur satellite appears to move westward at the equator with each orbit
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A map that shows true bearings and distances from a specific location

Why B is right. B. An azimuthal map is centered on one place and shows the true heading and distance from that place to anywhere.

Ordinary flat maps bend directions over long distances. An azimuthal equidistant map puts your location at the center. A straight line from the center to any point gives the right beam heading and the true distance. The trade-off is that land far from the center looks badly stretched.

Why not A? Land shapes far from the center are distorted on this kind of map.

G2D05

Which of the following indicates that you are looking for an HF contact with any station?

  1. A Sign your call sign once, followed by the words “listening for a call” -- if no answer, change frequency and repeat
  2. B Say “QTC” followed by “this is” and your call sign -- if no answer, change frequency and repeat
  3. C Repeat “CQ” a few times, followed by “this is,” then your call sign a few times, then pause to listen, repeat as necessary
  4. D Transmit an unmodulated carried for approximately 10 seconds, followed by “this is” and your call sign, and pause to listen -- repeat as necessary
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Repeat “CQ” a few times, followed by “this is,” then your call sign a few times, then pause to listen, repeat as necessary

Why C is right. C. CQ is the general call to any station, sent a few times with your call sign, followed by a pause to listen.

CQ asks for a reply from any station. Say CQ a few times, then this is and your call sign a few times, then listen. Keep each call short so you spend more time listening than talking, and repeat if no one answers.

Why not D? An unmodulated carrier is a tune-up signal and only causes interference.

G2D06

How is a directional antenna pointed when making a “long-path” contact with another station?

  1. A Toward the rising sun
  2. B Along the gray line
  3. C 180 degrees from the station’s short-path heading
  4. D Toward the north
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 180 degrees from the station’s short-path heading

Why C is right. C. The long path goes the other way around Earth, so its heading is opposite the short path's.

Two stations lie on a great circle, a full circle around the globe whose center is Earth's center. The short path is the shorter arc of that circle; the long path is the other way around. That makes the long-path heading exactly 180 degrees from the short-path heading. If the short path is 45 degrees, aim at 225 degrees.

Why not B? The gray line is the band of twilight around Earth, not a beam heading.

G2D07

Which of the following are examples of the NATO Phonetic Alphabet?

  1. A Able, Baker, Charlie, Dog
  2. B Adam, Boy, Charles, David
  3. C America, Boston, Canada, Denmark
  4. D Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta

Why D is right. D. Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta are the opening words of the NATO alphabet, which is the international standard.

Phonetic alphabets give each letter a word so call signs are clear over noise. The set that starts Alpha, Bravo was adopted internationally in the 1950s and is used by the military, aviation and amateurs. The other choices are older or made-up sets that are not the standard.

Why not A? Able, Baker was an older military alphabet replaced by the NATO set.

G2D08

Why do many amateurs keep a station log?

  1. A The FCC requires a log of all international contacts
  2. B The FCC requires a log of all international third-party traffic
  3. C The log provides evidence of operation needed to renew a license without retest
  4. D To help with a reply if the FCC requests information about your station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. To help with a reply if the FCC requests information about your station

Why D is right. D. A log gives you a record to answer questions about your station, for example if the FCC asks.

Part 97 does not require most US amateur stations to keep a log of ordinary contacts. Many operators keep one anyway. It records what you sent, when and where, which helps if the FCC looks into an interference complaint. It also helps with awards and contests.

Why not C? Renewing a license does not call for proof that you operated.

G2D09

Which of the following is required when participating in a contest on HF frequencies?

  1. A Submit a log to the contest sponsor
  2. B Send a QSL card to the stations worked, or QSL via Logbook of The World
  3. C Identify your station according to normal FCC regulations
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Identify your station according to normal FCC regulations

Why C is right. C. Contest sponsors set their own optional rules, but the FCC identification rules always apply.

During a contest you still must give your call sign at the end of each contact and at least every 10 minutes. Sending logs to the sponsor and confirming contacts are up to you, though needed for scoring or awards.

“must transmit its assigned call sign on its transmitting channel at the end of each communication, and at least every 10 minutes during a communication”

47 CFR 97.119(a)

Why not A? Submitting a log is a sponsor's request for scoring, not a legal duty.

G2D10

What is QRP operation?

  1. A Remote piloted model control
  2. B Low-power transmit operation
  3. C Transmission using Quick Response Protocol
  4. D Traffic relay procedure net operation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Low-power transmit operation

Why B is right. B. QRP is the amateur name for operating with low transmitter power.

As a Q signal, QRP means reduce power. Amateurs use it as a label for low-power operating, commonly 5 W output or less on CW. QRP operators enjoy making contacts with small, simple stations. QRO is the opposite, meaning higher power.

Why not C? QRP is a Q signal, not a protocol name.

G2D11

Why are signal reports typically exchanged at the beginning of an HF contact?

  1. A To allow each station to operate according to conditions
  2. B To be sure the contact will count for award programs
  3. C To follow standard radiogram structure
  4. D To allow each station to calibrate their frequency display
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. To allow each station to operate according to conditions

Why A is right. A. Signal reports tell each side how well it is heard, so both can adjust speed, power or mode to suit conditions.

If the other station says you are weak and noisy, you might speak more slowly, repeat key details, or move to CW or a digital mode. If you are strong, you can keep it short. Trading reports early sets up the rest of the contact.

Why not B? Award programs do not depend on a signal report being exchanged early.
