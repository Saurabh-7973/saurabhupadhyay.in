G8B: Frequency changing; bandwidths of various modes; deviation; intermodulation

Group G8B of the General pool (2023–2027) has 13 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G8B01

Which mixer input is varied or tuned to convert signals of different frequencies to an intermediate frequency (IF)?

  1. A Image frequency
  2. B Local oscillator
  3. C RF input
  4. D Beat frequency oscillator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Local oscillator

Why B is right. B. The local oscillator is tuned so each wanted signal converts to the same IF.

A mixer combines the incoming signal with the local oscillator, or LO. The difference between them is the intermediate frequency, or IF. To receive a new frequency, the radio changes the LO so the difference stays at the IF the filters are built for.

Why not D? The beat frequency oscillator is used later to make CW and SSB audible, not to convert to the IF.

G8B02

What is the term for interference from a signal at twice the IF frequency from the desired signal?

  1. A Quadrature response
  2. B Image response
  3. C Mixer interference
  4. D Intermediate interference
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Image response

Why B is right. B. A signal twice the IF away from the wanted signal causes image response.

A mixer outputs the IF for signals on either side of the local oscillator. The wanted signal is on one side. A signal the same distance away on the other side, which is twice the IF from the wanted one, also lands on the IF. That unwanted twin is the image.

Why not A? Quadrature refers to a 90-degree phase relationship, not to this kind of interference.

G8B03

What is another term for the mixing of two RF signals?

  1. A Heterodyning
  2. B Synthesizing
  3. C Frequency inversion
  4. D Phase inversion
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Heterodyning

Why A is right. A. Mixing two RF signals is also called heterodyning.

Heterodyne means combining two different frequencies. The mix produces new signals at their sum and their difference. Most receivers use this to move signals to a fixed IF.

Why not B? Synthesizing means generating frequencies, not mixing two existing ones.

G8B04

What is the stage in a VHF FM transmitter that generates a harmonic of a lower frequency signal to reach the desired operating frequency?

  1. A Mixer
  2. B Reactance modulator
  3. C Balanced converter
  4. D Multiplier
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Multiplier

Why D is right. D. A multiplier stage makes a harmonic to reach the final frequency.

Many FM transmitters start with an oscillator at a lower frequency. A multiplier stage is deliberately non-linear, so it produces harmonics, which are whole-number multiples. A filter selects the harmonic at the operating frequency, for example three times the input.

Why not A? A mixer makes sum and difference frequencies of two inputs, not harmonics of one.

G8B05

Which intermodulation products are closest to the original signal frequencies?

  1. A Second harmonics
  2. B Even-order
  3. C Odd-order
  4. D Intercept point
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Odd-order

Why C is right. C. Odd-order products land closest to the original frequencies.

Two strong signals at F1 and F2 can mix in a non-linear stage. Products like 2F1 minus F2 add up to an odd order, three here. They fall right beside F1 and F2, often inside the same band. Even-order products land far away, where filters can remove them.

Why not B? Even-order products such as F1 plus F2 fall far from the originals.

G8B06

What is the total bandwidth of an FM phone transmission having 5 kHz deviation and 3 kHz modulating frequency?

  1. A 3 kHz
  2. B 5 kHz
  3. C 8 kHz
  4. D 16 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 16 kHz

Why D is right. D. Doubling the deviation plus the audio frequency, 2 × (5 + 3) kHz, gives 16 kHz.

This estimate adds the deviation and the highest audio frequency. The doubling covers sidebands both above and below the carrier.

bandwidth = 2 × (deviation + modulating frequency)
bandwidth = 2 × (5 kHz + 3 kHz)
bandwidth = 2 × 8 kHz
= 16 kHz

Why not C? 8 kHz counts only one side of the carrier; FM spreads both above and below.

G8B07

What is the frequency deviation for a 12.21 MHz reactance modulated oscillator in a 5 kHz deviation, 146.52 MHz FM phone transmitter?

  1. A 101.75 Hz
  2. B 416.7 Hz
  3. C 5 kHz
  4. D 60 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 416.7 Hz

Why B is right. B. The oscillator must deviate 416.7 Hz, because the multipliers raise it 12 times.

Frequency multipliers raise the deviation by the same factor as the frequency. First find that factor from the two frequencies. Then divide the final deviation by it.

oscillator deviation = final deviation ÷ (output frequency ÷ oscillator frequency)
multiplication = 146.52 MHz ÷ 12.21 MHz = 12
oscillator deviation = 5000 Hz ÷ 12
oscillator deviation = 416.67 Hz, rounded to 416.7 Hz
= 416.7 Hz

Why not C? 5 kHz is the final deviation after multiplying, not the oscillator's deviation.

G8B08

Why is it important to know the duty cycle of the mode you are using when transmitting?

  1. A To aid in tuning your transmitter
  2. B Some modes have high duty cycles that could exceed the transmitter’s average power rating
  3. C To allow time for the other station to break in during a transmission
  4. D To prevent overmodulation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Some modes have high duty cycles that could exceed the transmitter’s average power rating

Why B is right. B. Duty cycle sets how much heat builds up, and a steady full-power mode heats the final stage far more than voice does.

Duty cycle is the share of time the transmitter puts out full power. SSB voice has pauses, so its average is low. Modes like FT8 or RTTY send a steady signal, so a radio rated for SSB may need its power turned down.

Why not D? Overmodulation comes from too much audio drive, not from duty cycle.

G8B09

Why is it good to match receiver bandwidth to the bandwidth of the operating mode?

  1. A It is required by FCC rules
  2. B It minimizes power consumption in the receiver
  3. C It improves impedance matching of the antenna
  4. D It results in the best signal-to-noise ratio
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It results in the best signal-to-noise ratio

Why D is right. D. A filter as wide as the signal keeps all of it while shutting out the noise on either side.

Noise is spread across all frequencies, so a wider filter lets in more noise. If the filter is narrower than the signal, part of the signal is lost.

Why not A? No FCC rule sets the receiver's filter bandwidth.

G8B10

What is the relationship between transmitted symbol rate and bandwidth?

  1. A Symbol rate and bandwidth are not related
  2. B Higher symbol rates require wider bandwidth
  3. C Lower symbol rates require wider bandwidth
  4. D Bandwidth is half the symbol rate
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Higher symbol rates require wider bandwidth

Why B is right. B. Faster changes in tone or phase create sidebands farther from the center, so the signal spreads wider.

A symbol is one change in the signal, such as a new tone or phase. Sending more symbols per second means faster changes, which makes the signal wider.

Why not C? Lower symbol rates allow a narrower signal, not a wider one.

G8B11

What combination of a mixer’s Local Oscillator (LO) and RF input frequencies is found in the output?

  1. A The ratio
  2. B The average
  3. C The sum and difference
  4. D The arithmetic product
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The sum and difference

Why C is right. C. A mixer's output contains the sum and the difference of the LO and RF frequencies.

For example, a 14.2 MHz signal mixed with a 5.2 MHz LO gives 19.4 MHz and 9.0 MHz. The radio then filters out the one it wants, usually as its IF.

Why not D? A mixer multiplies the signals' waveforms, but the output frequencies are sums and differences, not a product of the two frequencies.

G8B12

What process combines two signals in a non-linear circuit to produce unwanted spurious outputs?

  1. A Intermodulation
  2. B Heterodyning
  3. C Detection
  4. D Rolloff
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Intermodulation

Why A is right. A. Intermodulation is the unwanted mixing of signals in a non-linear circuit.

Any stage that does not respond in a straight line, such as an overloaded amplifier, acts like a mixer. Two strong signals passing through create new signals that were never sent. These false signals can cover weak stations.

Why not B? Heterodyning is mixing done on purpose to make a wanted frequency.

G8B13

Which of the following is an odd-order intermodulation product of frequencies F1 and F2?

  1. A 5F1-3F2
  2. B 3F1-F2
  3. C 2F1-F2
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 2F1-F2

Why C is right. C. 2F1-F2 is odd-order, because its order is 2 + 1 = 3.

The order of a product is the sum of the multipliers, ignoring signs. 5F1-3F2 has order 5 + 3 = 8, which is even. 3F1-F2 has order 3 + 1 = 4, also even. Only 2F1-F2 gives an odd number.

Why not D? Two of the three choices are even-order, so they cannot all be correct.
