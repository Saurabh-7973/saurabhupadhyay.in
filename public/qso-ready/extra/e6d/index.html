E6D: Inductors and piezoelectricity: permeability, core material and configuration; transformers; piezoelectric devices

Group E6D of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E6D01

What is piezoelectricity?

  1. A The ability of materials to generate electromagnetic waves of a certain frequency when voltage is applied
  2. B A characteristic of materials that have an index of refraction which depends on the polarization of the electromagnetic wave passing through it
  3. C A characteristic of materials that generate a voltage when stressed and that flex when a voltage is applied
  4. D The ability of materials to generate voltage when an electromagnetic wave of a certain frequency is applied
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A characteristic of materials that generate a voltage when stressed and that flex when a voltage is applied

Why C is right. C. Piezoelectric materials make a voltage when squeezed or bent, and bend when a voltage is applied.

In crystals such as quartz, the electric charges are arranged so that squeezing the crystal shifts them and creates a voltage. The reverse also works: a voltage makes the crystal change shape slightly. A quartz crystal in an oscillator uses both effects to vibrate at a very steady frequency.

Why not D? Piezoelectricity responds to mechanical stress, not to incoming radio waves.

E6D02

What is the equivalent circuit of a quartz crystal?

  1. A Series RLC in parallel with a shunt C representing electrode and stray capacitance
  2. B Parallel RLC, where C is the parallel combination of resonance capacitance of the crystal and electrode and stray capacitance
  3. C Series RLC, where C is the parallel combination of resonance capacitance of the crystal and electrode and stray capacitance
  4. D Parallel RLC, where C is the series combination of resonance capacitance of the crystal and electrode and stray capacitance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Series RLC in parallel with a shunt C representing electrode and stray capacitance

Why A is right. A. A quartz crystal acts like a series RLC circuit, from its vibration, with a capacitor in parallel from its electrodes and holder.

The vibrating quartz behaves like an inductor (its mass), a tiny capacitor (its springiness) and a resistor (its losses) in series. The metal plates on each side of the crystal, plus wiring, add a separate capacitance across the whole thing. That gives the crystal a series resonance and a slightly higher parallel resonance.

Why not B? The vibration part is a series RLC; the electrode capacitance is separate and sits across it.

E6D03

Which of the following is an aspect of the piezoelectric effect?

  1. A Mechanical deformation of material due to the application of a voltage
  2. B Mechanical deformation of material due to the application of a magnetic field
  3. C Generation of electrical energy in the presence of light
  4. D Increased conductivity in the presence of light
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Mechanical deformation of material due to the application of a voltage

Why A is right. A. Applying a voltage to a piezoelectric material makes it change shape mechanically.

This is one half of the piezoelectric effect; the other half is making a voltage when the material is stressed. Piezo buzzers, ultrasonic transducers and crystal oscillators all use a voltage to make a material flex or vibrate.

Why not C? Making electricity from light is the photovoltaic effect.

E6D04

Why are cores of inductors and transformers sometimes constructed of thin layers?

  1. A To simplify assembly during manufacturing
  2. B To reduce power loss from eddy currents in the core
  3. C To increase the cutoff frequency by reducing capacitance
  4. D To save cost by reducing the amount of magnetic material
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. To reduce power loss from eddy currents in the core

Why B is right. B. Thin, insulated layers break up the loops of current that the changing magnetic field drives through the core, cutting power loss.

A changing magnetic field induces swirling currents, called eddy currents, inside any conductive core. They heat the core and waste power. Stacking thin sheets, called laminations, insulated from each other, limits each loop to a thin slice with high resistance. Less current flows, so less power is lost.

Why not D? Laminated cores use about the same amount of metal; the goal is lower loss.

E6D05

How do ferrite and powdered iron compare for use in an inductor core?

  1. A Ferrite cores generally have lower initial permeability
  2. B Ferrite cores generally have better temperature stability
  3. C Ferrite cores generally require fewer turns to produce a given inductance value
  4. D Ferrite cores are easier to use with surface-mount technology
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Ferrite cores generally require fewer turns to produce a given inductance value

Why C is right. C. Ferrite usually has higher permeability than powdered iron, so it needs fewer turns for the same inductance.

Permeability measures how easily a material carries magnetic field. Higher permeability gives more field for each turn of wire, so inductance per turn goes up. Ferrite mixes are often hundreds or thousands of times more permeable than air; powdered iron is usually under 100. The trade-off is that powdered iron is steadier with temperature.

Why not B? Powdered iron, not ferrite, generally has better temperature stability.

E6D06

What core material property determines the inductance of an inductor?

  1. A Permittivity
  2. B Resistance
  3. C Reactivity
  4. D Permeability
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Permeability

Why D is right. D. The core's permeability, its ability to carry magnetic field, sets how much inductance each turn produces.

Inductance depends on the number of turns, the coil's shape and the core material. A core with higher permeability concentrates the magnetic field and raises the inductance. An air core has a permeability of 1, while ferrite can be in the thousands.

Why not A? Permittivity is the electric counterpart and affects capacitance, not inductance.

E6D08

Which of the following materials has the highest temperature stability of its magnetic characteristics?

  1. A Brass
  2. B Powdered iron
  3. C Ferrite
  4. D Aluminum
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Powdered iron

Why B is right. B. Powdered iron cores keep their magnetic properties steadier than ferrite as temperature changes.

Powdered iron is fine iron particles held in a binder. Its permeability is low but changes little with heat, which keeps tuned circuits on frequency. Ferrite has higher permeability but drifts more with temperature. Brass and aluminum are not magnetic at all.

Why not C? Ferrite has higher permeability but drifts more with temperature.

E6D09

What devices are commonly used as VHF and UHF parasitic suppressors at the input and output terminals of a transistor HF amplifier?

  1. A Electrolytic capacitors
  2. B Butterworth filters
  3. C Ferrite beads
  4. D Steel-core toroids
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Ferrite beads

Why C is right. C. Ferrite beads add loss at VHF and UHF, damping unwanted oscillations while barely affecting HF signals.

A parasitic oscillation is an unintended oscillation, often at VHF or UHF, caused by stray inductance and capacitance around a transistor. A ferrite bead slipped over a lead acts like a resistor at those high frequencies but has little effect at HF. The bead turns the parasitic energy into heat.

Why not A? Electrolytic capacitors act like inductors at RF and would not suppress VHF oscillation.

E6D10

What is a primary advantage of using a toroidal core instead of a solenoidal core in an inductor?

  1. A Toroidal cores confine most of the magnetic field within the core material
  2. B Toroidal cores make it easier to couple the magnetic energy into other components
  3. C Toroidal cores exhibit greater hysteresis
  4. D Toroidal cores have lower Q characteristics
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Toroidal cores confine most of the magnetic field within the core material

Why A is right. A. A toroid's closed ring keeps most of the magnetic field inside the core, so it couples little to nearby parts.

A toroid is a doughnut-shaped core with wire wound around the ring. The magnetic field loops around inside the ring instead of spreading out of the ends, as it does from a straight coil, called a solenoid. Less stray field means less unwanted coupling, so a toroid can sit close to other parts.

Why not B? Keeping the field inside makes coupling to other parts harder, not easier.

E6D12

What causes inductor saturation?

  1. A Operation at too high a frequency
  2. B Selecting a core with low permeability
  3. C Operation at excessive magnetic flux
  4. D Selecting a core with excessive permittivity
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Operation at excessive magnetic flux

Why C is right. C. Saturation happens when the magnetic flux in the core reaches the most the material can carry, so more current adds little more field.

Magnetic flux is the amount of magnetic field passing through the core. A core can only carry so much. Push past that, often with too much current, and its permeability drops sharply. Inductance falls, current rises fast, and the core may heat and distort signals.

Why not A? High frequency raises core loss, but saturation is about too much flux.
