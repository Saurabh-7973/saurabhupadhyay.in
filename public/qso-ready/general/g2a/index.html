G2A: Phone operating procedures: USB/LSB conventions, breaking into a contact, transmitter setup for voice operation; answering DX stations

Group G2A of the General pool (2023–2027) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G2A01

Which mode is most commonly used for voice communications on frequencies of 14 MHz or higher?

  1. A Upper sideband
  2. B Lower sideband
  3. C Suppressed sideband
  4. D Double sideband
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Upper sideband

Why A is right. A. By long custom, amateurs put SSB voice on the upper sideband on 20 meters (14 MHz) and on every higher band.

Single sideband (SSB) sends only one copy of the voice signal, either right above the suppressed carrier (upper sideband) or right below it (lower sideband). Either works. Amateurs agreed on upper sideband from 20 meters up, so every station's radio is set the same way and voices sound normal when tuned in.

Why not B? Lower sideband is the custom on 160, 75 and 40 meters, not above 14 MHz.

Remember: Low bands, low side; 20 meters and up, up side.

G2A02

Which mode is most commonly used for voice communications on the 160-, 75-, and 40-meter bands?

  1. A Upper sideband
  2. B Lower sideband
  3. C Suppressed sideband
  4. D Double sideband
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Lower sideband

Why B is right. B. The custom on these lower bands, all below 10 MHz, is to send SSB voice on the lower sideband.

Single sideband (SSB) voice can sit right below the suppressed carrier (lower sideband) or right above it (upper sideband). Amateurs settled on lower sideband for 160, 75 and 40 meters. If you tune in a voice there with the radio set to upper sideband, it sounds garbled, so following the custom matters.

Why not A? Upper sideband is the custom on 20 meters and above.

Remember: Low bands, low side; 20 meters and up, up side.

G2A03

Which mode is most commonly used for SSB voice communications in the VHF and UHF bands?

  1. A Upper sideband
  2. B Lower sideband
  3. C Suppressed sideband
  4. D Double sideband
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Upper sideband

Why A is right. A. VHF and UHF are above 14 MHz, and SSB above that point is sent on the upper sideband by custom.

On 6 meters, 2 meters and higher, most voice is FM, but long-distance weak-signal work uses SSB. Those SSB signals follow the same custom as the upper HF bands: upper sideband. That way two stations can find and understand each other without guessing which side to use.

Why not B? Lower sideband is the custom only on 160, 75 and 40 meters.

G2A04

Which mode is most commonly used for voice communications on the 17- and 12-meter bands?

  1. A Upper sideband
  2. B Lower sideband
  3. C Suppressed sideband
  4. D Double sideband
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Upper sideband

Why A is right. A. The 17- and 12-meter bands sit above 14 MHz, where the SSB custom is upper sideband.

17 meters is near 18 MHz and 12 meters is near 24.9 MHz. Both are above the 14 MHz dividing line, so voice on them uses upper sideband, the same as 20, 15 and 10 meters. Set your radio to USB on these bands.

Why not B? Lower sideband is the custom only below 10 MHz.

G2A05

Which mode of voice communication is most commonly used on the HF amateur bands?

  1. A Frequency modulation
  2. B Double sideband
  3. C Single sideband
  4. D Single phase modulation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Single sideband

Why C is right. C. SSB is narrow and power-efficient, which is why most HF voice contacts use it.

HF bands are crowded and signals often arrive weak. Single sideband (SSB) uses about 3 kHz of space and puts the transmitter's power into the voice itself. AM and FM voice do appear on HF, but far less often, and FM is mainly found near the top of 10 meters.

Why not A? FM takes much more space than SSB and is rare on HF outside 10 meters.

G2A06

Which of the following is an advantage of using single sideband, as compared to other analog voice modes on the HF amateur bands?

  1. A Very high-fidelity voice modulation
  2. B Less subject to interference from atmospheric static crashes
  3. C Ease of tuning on receive and immunity to impulse noise
  4. D Less bandwidth used and greater power efficiency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Less bandwidth used and greater power efficiency

Why D is right. D. SSB drops the carrier and one of the two matching sidebands, so it is narrower and all its power goes into the voice.

An AM signal has a steady carrier plus two sidebands that carry the same voice information. The carrier carries none. SSB removes the carrier and one sideband. The signal then fits in about 3 kHz instead of about 6 kHz, and none of the transmitter's power is spent on parts you do not need.

Why not A? SSB audio is narrow and clipped, not high fidelity.

G2A07

Which of the following statements is true of single sideband (SSB)?

  1. A Only one sideband and the carrier are transmitted; the other sideband is suppressed
  2. B Only one sideband is transmitted; the other sideband and carrier are suppressed
  3. C SSB is the only voice mode authorized on the 20-, 15-, and 10-meter amateur bands
  4. D SSB is the only voice mode authorized on the 160-, 75-, and 40-meter amateur bands
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Only one sideband is transmitted; the other sideband and carrier are suppressed

Why B is right. B. The carrier carries no voice information and the second sideband repeats the first, so SSB filters out both before sending.

Modulating a carrier with voice creates two sidebands, one above and one below the carrier, each holding the full voice signal. Single sideband (SSB) keeps one of them and removes the other and the carrier. The receiver puts back a stand-in carrier to recover the audio.

Why not C? Other voice modes, such as AM, are also allowed on those bands.

G2A08

What is the recommended way to break into a phone contact?

  1. A Say “QRZ” several times, followed by your call sign
  2. B Say your call sign once
  3. C Say “Breaker Breaker”
  4. D Say “CQ” followed by the call sign of either station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Say your call sign once

Why B is right. B. Giving your call sign one time, in a pause, tells both stations you are there without taking over their contact.

Wait for a gap between the two stations' transmissions and give your call sign once. Either station can then invite you in. Long calls waste their time and may cover what they are saying.

Why not C? That phrase is CB slang, not amateur practice.

G2A09

Why do most amateur stations use lower sideband on the 160-, 75-, and 40-meter bands?

  1. A Lower sideband is more efficient than upper sideband at these frequencies
  2. B Lower sideband is the only sideband legal on these frequency bands
  3. C Because it is fully compatible with an AM detector
  4. D It is commonly accepted amateur practice
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It is commonly accepted amateur practice

Why D is right. D. Neither sideband works better on these bands; lower sideband is used there because amateurs agree to use it.

Upper and lower sideband carry the same voice equally well. The choice of lower sideband on 160, 75 and 40 meters is a habit amateurs have kept for decades. Part 97 does not require it. Following it means your radio is set the same way as the station you want to hear.

Why not B? Part 97 allows either sideband on these bands.

G2A10

Which of the following statements is true of VOX operation versus PTT operation?

  1. A The received signal is more natural sounding
  2. B It allows “hands free” operation
  3. C It occupies less bandwidth
  4. D It provides more power output
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. It allows “hands free” operation

Why B is right. B. VOX switches the radio to transmit when it hears your voice, so you do not have to press a button.

With push-to-talk (PTT), you hold a switch to transmit. Voice-operated transmit (VOX) senses sound at the microphone, keys the transmitter, and returns to receive a moment after you stop talking. It changes only how the radio switches, not the signal's power, width or sound.

Why not C? Bandwidth depends on the mode, not on how you switch to transmit.

G2A11

Generally, who should respond to a station in the contiguous 48 states calling “CQ DX”?

  1. A Any caller is welcome to respond
  2. B Only stations in Germany
  3. C Any stations outside the lower 48 states
  4. D Only contest stations
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Any stations outside the lower 48 states

Why C is right. C. A CQ DX call asks for distant stations, which for a caller in the lower 48 means anyone outside those states.

DX means distant stations. A station calling CQ DX from the contiguous 48 states wants contacts beyond them: other countries, and also Alaska, Hawaii and US territories. Other stations in the lower 48 should hold off and wait for a general CQ.

Why not A? A CQ DX is a request for distant stations only, not an open call.

G2A12

What control is typically adjusted for proper ALC setting on a single sideband transceiver?

  1. A RF clipping level
  2. B Transmit audio or microphone gain
  3. C Antenna inductance or capacitance
  4. D Attenuator level
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Transmit audio or microphone gain

Why B is right. B. ALC reacts to how hard the transmitter is driven, and on SSB the drive comes from the microphone audio.

Automatic level control (ALC) is a feedback circuit that turns down the drive when a transmitter is pushed too hard, to keep the signal clean. On SSB you set the transmit audio (mic gain) so the ALC meter stays in its normal zone while you talk. Too much gain causes distortion and splatter onto nearby frequencies.

Why not D? An attenuator reduces incoming signals on receive; it does not set transmit drive.
