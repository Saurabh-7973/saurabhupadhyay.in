G1C: Transmitter power regulations; data emission standards; 60-meter operation requirements

Group G1C of the General pool (2023–2027) has 8 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G1C01

What is the maximum transmitter power an amateur station may use on 10.140 MHz?

  1. A 200 watts PEP output
  2. B 1000 watts PEP output
  3. C 1500 watts PEP output
  4. D 2000 watts PEP output
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 200 watts PEP output

Why A is right. A. 10.140 MHz is in the 30-meter band, where Part 97 sets a lower power cap of 200 W PEP output.

Most amateur bands allow up to 1500 W PEP (peak envelope power, the transmitter's output at the peak of its signal). The 30-meter band, 10.10 to 10.15 MHz, is an exception. Amateurs share it with fixed stations of other nations, so power there is held to 200 W PEP output.

“No station may transmit with a transmitter power output exceeding 200 W PEP: (1) On the 10.10-10.15 MHz segment”

47 CFR 97.313(c)(1)

Why not C? 1500 W is the general limit, but 30 meters has its own lower cap.

G1C02

What is the maximum transmitter power an amateur station may use on the 12-meter band?

  1. A 50 watts PEP output
  2. B 200 watts PEP output
  3. C 1500 watts PEP output
  4. D An effective radiated power equivalent to 100 watts from a half-wave dipole
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 1500 watts PEP output

Why C is right. C. 12 meters has no special power cap, so the general Part 97 limit of 1.5 kW PEP applies.

Part 97 sets one overall ceiling of 1.5 kW (1500 W) PEP. Lower caps apply only in listed cases, such as 30 meters, the 60-meter band, and some segments for Novice and Technician operators. The 12-meter band is not on that list.

“No station may transmit with a transmitter power exceeding 1.5 kW PEP.”

47 CFR 97.313(b)

Why not D? An ERP limit tied to a half-wave dipole is the 60-meter rule, not a 12-meter rule.

G1C03

What is the maximum bandwidth permitted by FCC rules for amateur radio stations transmitting on USB frequencies in the 60-meter band?

  1. A 2.8 kHz
  2. B 5.6 kHz
  3. C 1.8 kHz
  4. D 3 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 2.8 kHz

Why A is right. A. Part 97 says 60-meter emissions must not take up more than 2.8 kHz of spectrum.

A voice signal on upper sideband (USB) is about 2.4 to 2.8 kHz wide. The 60-meter rules cap every emission there at 2.8 kHz, whether phone, data or RTTY. Today's rule says the cap covers all 60-meter spectrum, both the four channels and the 5351.5 to 5366.5 kHz segment added in February 2026.

“For all 60 m spectrum, Amateur operators shall ensure that their emissions do not occupy more than 2.8 kHz.”

47 CFR 97.303(h)(3)

Why not D? 3 kHz is wider than the 2.8 kHz the rule allows.

G1C04

Which of the following is required by the FCC rules when operating in the 60-meter band?

  1. A If you are using an antenna other than a dipole, you must keep a record of the gain of your antenna
  2. B You must keep a record of the date, time, frequency, power level, and stations worked
  3. C You must keep a record of all third-party traffic
  4. D You must keep a record of the manufacturer of your equipment and the antenna used
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. If you are using an antenna other than a dipole, you must keep a record of the gain of your antenna

Why A is right. A. On 60 meters, power is judged as radiated power compared with a dipole, so an operator using any other antenna must keep its gain on record.

The 60-meter limits are in effective radiated power (ERP): transmitter PEP times antenna gain compared with a half-wave dipole. A dipole counts as a gain of 1. With any other antenna, you must keep the maker's gain figure or your own calculation in your station records. Today the limits are 100 W ERP on the four channels and 9.15 W ERP in the 5.3515 to 5.3665 MHz segment.

“Licensees using other antennas must maintain in their station records either the antenna manufacturer's data on the antenna gain or calculations of the antenna gain.”

47 CFR 97.313(i)

Why not B? Part 97 does not require a full contact log on 60 meters.

G1C05

What is the limit for transmitter power on the 28 MHz band for a General Class control operator?

  1. A 100 watts PEP output
  2. B 1000 watts PEP output
  3. C 1500 watts PEP output
  4. D 2000 watts PEP output
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 1500 watts PEP output

Why C is right. C. A General operator on 10 meters falls under the overall 1.5 kW PEP ceiling, since no lower cap applies.

Part 97 caps all amateur stations at 1.5 kW (1500 W) PEP. On 28.0 to 28.5 MHz, a lower 200 W PEP cap applies, but only when the control operator is a Novice or Technician. A General control operator is not covered by that cap, so the full limit applies across 10 meters.

“No station may transmit with a transmitter power exceeding 1.5 kW PEP.”

47 CFR 97.313(b)

Why not A? 100 W is not a power limit for General operators on 10 meters.

G1C06

What is the limit for transmitter power on the 1.8 MHz band?

  1. A 200 watts PEP output
  2. B 1000 watts PEP output
  3. C 1200 watts PEP output
  4. D 1500 watts PEP output
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 1500 watts PEP output

Why D is right. D. 160 meters has no special power cap, so the overall limit of 1.5 kW PEP applies.

The 1.8 MHz band is 160 meters. Part 97 sets the general ceiling at 1.5 kW (1500 W) PEP and lists lower caps only for certain bands and license classes. 160 meters is not one of them, so the full limit applies, though you should still use the least power needed for the contact.

“No station may transmit with a transmitter power exceeding 1.5 kW PEP.”

47 CFR 97.313(b)

Why not A? 200 W is the 30-meter cap, not a 160-meter limit.

G1C07

What must be done before using a new digital protocol on the air?

  1. A Type-certify equipment to FCC standards
  2. B Obtain an experimental license from the FCC
  3. C Publicly document the technical characteristics of the protocol
  4. D Submit a rule-making proposal to the FCC describing the codes and methods of the technique
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Publicly document the technical characteristics of the protocol

Why C is right. C. Part 97 allows any digital technique whose technical details have been made public.

Amateurs may not send messages meant to hide their meaning. Publishing how a new digital code works lets anyone build a decoder and check what is being sent. That is the only step required: no FCC license, approval or rule change is needed before trying it on the air.

“may use any technique whose technical characteristics have been documented publicly”

47 CFR 97.309(a)(4)

Why not B? Amateurs do not need an experimental license to try a publicly documented code.

G1C11

What measurement is specified by FCC rules that regulate maximum power?

  1. A RMS output from the transmitter
  2. B RMS input to the antenna
  3. C PEP input to the antenna
  4. D PEP output from the transmitter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. PEP output from the transmitter

Why D is right. D. Part 97 states its power limits as peak envelope power measured at the transmitter's output.

Peak envelope power (PEP) is the average power over one radio cycle at the highest point of the signal. The rules measure it where the transmitter feeds the transmission line, not at the antenna. Losses in the feed line therefore do not raise the amount you may run.

“No station may transmit with a transmitter power output exceeding 200 W PEP”

47 CFR 97.313(c)

Why not C? The limit is measured at the transmitter output, not at the antenna.
