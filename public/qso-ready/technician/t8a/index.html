T8A: Basic characteristics of FM and SSB; Bandwidth of various modulation modes: CW, SSB, FM, fast-scan TV; Choice of emission type: selection of USB vs LSB, use of SSB for weak signal work, use of FM for VHF packet and repeaters

Group T8A of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T8A01

Which of the following is a form of amplitude modulation?

  1. A Spread spectrum
  2. B Packet radio
  3. C Single sideband
  4. D Phase shift keying (PSK)
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Single sideband

Why C is right. C. Single sideband is a kind of amplitude modulation.

A plain AM signal has a carrier plus two matching sidebands. Single sideband (SSB) keeps the same amplitude-based idea but removes the carrier and one of the two sidebands. What is left still carries your voice as changes in signal strength, so it belongs to the AM family.

Why not D? PSK changes the phase of the signal, not its strength.

Remember: SSB is AM with two of its three parts trimmed off.

T8A02

What type of modulation is commonly used for VHF packet radio transmissions?

  1. A FM or PM
  2. B SSB
  3. C AM
  4. D PSK
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. FM or PM

Why A is right. A. VHF packet radio is usually sent using FM or PM.

Most VHF packet stations feed audio tones from a modem into an ordinary FM radio. The tones stand for the ones and zeros of the data. Because almost every VHF radio already does FM, packet rides on top of FM or its close cousin, phase modulation.

Why not B? SSB is used for weak-signal voice and HF data, not for typical VHF packet.

T8A03

Which type of voice mode is often used for long-distance (weak signal) contacts on the VHF and UHF bands?

  1. A FM
  2. B DRM
  3. C SSB
  4. D PM
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. SSB

Why C is right. C. SSB is the voice mode of choice for weak-signal VHF and UHF contacts.

SSB packs all its power into a narrow slice of spectrum, about 3 kHz wide. A narrow signal lets the receiver use a narrow filter, which lets in less noise. That makes faint, far-away voices easier to hear than they would be on FM, which needs a much wider channel.

Why not A? FM is great for strong local signals through repeaters, but it drops out quickly when signals get weak.

T8A04

Which type of modulation is commonly used for VHF and UHF voice repeaters?

  1. A AM
  2. B SSB
  3. C PSK
  4. D FM or PM
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. FM or PM

Why D is right. D. VHF and UHF voice repeaters normally use FM or PM.

Repeaters handle mostly local, fairly strong signals. FM gives clear, quiet audio once the signal is strong enough, and it ignores many kinds of noise that change signal strength. Radios for repeater use are built around FM, or phase modulation, which sounds the same to the listener.

Why not B? SSB is mainly used for weak-signal work, not repeater channels.

T8A05

Which of the following signal types has the narrowest bandwidth?

  1. A FM voice
  2. B SSB voice
  3. C CW
  4. D Slow-scan TV
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. CW

Why C is right. C. CW has the narrowest bandwidth of these signals.

CW is Morse code sent by turning a single steady tone on and off. Because there is so little information changing, it needs only about 150 Hz of spectrum. SSB voice needs about 3 kHz, FM voice needs 10 to 15 kHz, and slow-scan TV needs room for picture tones.

Why not B? SSB voice is narrow for voice, but still far wider than a keyed Morse tone.

T8A06

Which sideband is normally used for 10-meter HF, VHF, and UHF single-sideband communications?

  1. A Upper sideband
  2. B Lower sideband
  3. C Suppressed sideband
  4. D Inverted sideband
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Upper sideband

Why A is right. A. Upper sideband is the usual choice on 10 meters, VHF and UHF.

Hams agree on one sideband per band so stations can hear each other without retuning. The common practice is upper sideband on 10 meters and above. If one station uses upper and the other uses lower, each will sound garbled to the other.

Why not C? A suppressed sideband is the one removed, so it carries nothing you can use.

Remember: Up high, use Upper.

T8A07

What is one characteristic of single sideband (SSB) compared to FM?

  1. A SSB signals are easier to tune in correctly
  2. B SSB signals are less susceptible to interference
  3. C SSB signals have narrower bandwidth
  4. D SSB signals are less susceptible to high SWR
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. SSB signals have narrower bandwidth

Why C is right. C. SSB uses a narrower bandwidth than FM.

An SSB voice signal fills about 3 kHz. An FM voice signal fills about 10 to 15 kHz. A narrower signal leaves room for more stations in the same band and lets in less noise at the receiver.

Why not A? SSB is harder to tune than FM, since a small tuning error makes voices sound high or low pitched.

T8A08

What is the approximate bandwidth of a typical single sideband (SSB) voice signal?

  1. A 1 kHz
  2. B 3 kHz
  3. C 6 kHz
  4. D 15 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 3 kHz

Why B is right. B. A typical SSB voice signal is about 3 kHz wide.

Speech that people can understand fits in tones from roughly 300 Hz up to about 3,000 Hz. SSB sends one copy of that audio range, shifted up to radio frequency, so the signal is about as wide as the speech itself, around 3 kHz.

Why not C? About 6 kHz is the width of full AM, which sends two sidebands.

T8A09

What is the approximate bandwidth of an FM voice signal on VHF repeaters?

  1. A Less than 500 Hz
  2. B About 150 kHz
  3. C Between 10 and 15 kHz
  4. D Between 50 and 125 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Between 10 and 15 kHz

Why C is right. C. An FM voice signal on a VHF repeater takes between 10 and 15 kHz.

In FM, your voice swings the frequency back and forth by a few kHz on each side of center. Add the audio tones on top of that swing, and the signal spreads over roughly 10 to 15 kHz. That is why repeater channels are spaced farther apart than SSB stations.

Why not D? 50 to 125 kHz is far wider than ham FM voice uses.

T8A10

What is the approximate bandwidth of AM fast-scan TV transmissions?

  1. A More than 10 MHz
  2. B About 6 MHz
  3. C About 3 MHz
  4. D About 1 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. About 6 MHz

Why B is right. B. AM fast-scan TV takes about 6 MHz.

Fast-scan TV sends full-motion pictures, which carry a huge amount of detail every second. Carrying all that detail needs a very wide signal, about 6 MHz, the same width as an analog broadcast TV channel. That is why hams send it mostly on 70 cm and higher bands, where there is room.

Why not D? 1 MHz is too narrow to carry moving analog pictures at normal quality.

T8A11

What is the approximate bandwidth required to transmit a CW signal?

  1. A 2.4 kHz
  2. B 150 Hz
  3. C 1000 Hz
  4. D 15 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 150 Hz

Why B is right. B. A CW signal needs about 150 Hz.

CW is a single tone switched on and off. The only reason it has any width is the on-off keying itself, which adds a small spread around the tone. At normal sending speeds that spread is about 150 Hz, the narrowest of the common ham modes.

Why not A? 2.4 kHz is roughly the width of a voice signal, far wider than Morse needs.

T8A12

Which of the following is a disadvantage of FM compared with single sideband?

  1. A Voice quality is poorer
  2. B Only one signal can be received at a time
  3. C FM signals are harder to tune
  4. D FM signals are more susceptible to high SWR
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Only one signal can be received at a time

Why B is right. B. With FM, you can receive only one signal at a time.

An FM receiver locks onto the strongest signal on the channel and pushes weaker ones out. This is called the capture effect. On SSB, two stations on the same frequency can both be heard at once, so you may still pick out the weaker one.

Why not C? FM signals are easier to tune than SSB, not harder.
