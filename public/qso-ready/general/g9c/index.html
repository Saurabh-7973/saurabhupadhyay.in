G9C: Directional antennas

Group G9C of the General pool (2023–2027) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G9C01

Which of the following would increase the bandwidth of a Yagi antenna?

  1. A Larger-diameter elements
  2. B Closer element spacing
  3. C Loading coils in series with the element
  4. D Tapered-diameter elements
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Larger-diameter elements

Why A is right. A. Thicker elements widen a Yagi's bandwidth.

Bandwidth is the range of frequencies over which the antenna keeps good SWR and pattern. A fatter conductor has lower Q, meaning its resonance is broader and less sharp. So larger-diameter elements let the antenna work well over a wider range.

Why not C? Loading coils make an element act longer but narrow the bandwidth.

G9C02

What is the approximate length of the driven element of a Yagi antenna?

  1. A 1/4 wavelength
  2. B 1/2 wavelength
  3. C 3/4 wavelength
  4. D 1 wavelength
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 1/2 wavelength

Why B is right. B. The driven element of a Yagi is about a half wavelength long.

The driven element is the one connected to the feed line. It is basically a half-wave dipole. The other elements, the reflector and directors, are not connected and work by picking up and re-radiating its signal.

Why not A? A quarter wavelength is the length of a ground-plane vertical, not a Yagi driven element.

G9C03

How do the lengths of a three-element Yagi reflector and director compare to that of the driven element?

  1. A The reflector is longer, and the director is shorter
  2. B The reflector is shorter, and the director is longer
  3. C They are all the same length
  4. D Relative length depends on the frequency of operation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The reflector is longer, and the director is shorter

Why A is right. A. A slightly long reflector and slightly short director re-radiate with timing that pushes energy toward the director side.

Small length differences change the timing of the current each element re-radiates. The reflector sits behind the driven element and the director in front, so the beam points toward the director.

Why not B? Those lengths are swapped; a longer element in front would aim the beam backward.

G9C04

How does antenna gain in dBi compare to gain stated in dBd for the same antenna?

  1. A Gain in dBi is 2.15 dB lower
  2. B Gain in dBi is 2.15 dB higher
  3. C Gain in dBd is 1.25 dBd lower
  4. D Gain in dBd is 1.25 dBd higher
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Gain in dBi is 2.15 dB higher

Why B is right. B. dBi is measured against an isotropic source, which is 2.15 dB weaker than the dipole used for dBd, so the dBi number is bigger.

dBi compares an antenna with an isotropic antenna, an imaginary source that radiates equally in all directions. dBd compares it with a half-wave dipole.

Why not A? The number in dBi is larger, because the isotropic reference is weaker than a dipole.

G9C05

What is the primary effect of increasing boom length and adding directors to a Yagi antenna?

  1. A Gain increases
  2. B Beamwidth increases
  3. C Front-to-back ratio decreases
  4. D Resonant frequency is lower
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Gain increases

Why A is right. A. A longer boom with more directors gives more gain.

Each added director helps focus energy into a narrower forward beam. A longer boom gives room to space them well. A narrower beam puts more power in the forward direction, which is what gain means.

Why not B? More directors make the beam narrower, not wider.

G9C07

What does “front-to-back ratio” mean in reference to a Yagi antenna?

  1. A The number of directors versus the number of reflectors
  2. B The relative position of the driven element with respect to the reflectors and directors
  3. C The power radiated in the major lobe compared to that in the opposite direction
  4. D The ratio of forward gain to dipole gain
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The power radiated in the major lobe compared to that in the opposite direction

Why C is right. C. A Yagi aims most power forward, and this ratio tells, in dB, how much less leaks out the back.

A high front-to-back ratio also means the antenna rejects signals and noise from behind.

Why not D? Forward gain compared with a dipole is gain in dBd, a different measure.

G9C08

What is meant by the “main lobe” of a directive antenna?

  1. A The magnitude of the maximum vertical angle of radiation
  2. B The point of maximum current in a radiating antenna element
  3. C The maximum voltage standing wave point on a radiating element
  4. D The direction of maximum radiated field strength from the antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The direction of maximum radiated field strength from the antenna

Why D is right. D. Of all the bulges in a pattern plot, the main lobe is the biggest, so it marks where the field is strongest.

A pattern plot shows lobes, which are bulges where the antenna sends more energy. The main lobe points the way the antenna is aimed. Smaller ones are called side lobes or back lobes.

Why not A? Elevation angle is a separate property; the main lobe is a direction, not an angle magnitude.

G9C09

In free space, how does the gain of two three-element, horizontally polarized Yagi antennas spaced vertically 1/2 wavelength apart typically compare to the gain of a single three-element Yagi?

  1. A Approximately 1.5 dB higher
  2. B Approximately 3 dB higher
  3. C Approximately 6 dB higher
  4. D Approximately 9 dB higher
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Approximately 3 dB higher

Why B is right. B. Two antennas fed in phase double the forward power, and doubling power is 3 dB.

Real arrays come close to this ideal when the antennas are spaced well.

gain increase in dB = 10 × log10(power ratio)
power ratio = 2 for two antennas
gain increase = 10 × log10(2) = 10 × 0.301
gain increase = 3.01 dB, about 3 dB
= 3 dB

Why not C? 6 dB would mean four times the power, which two antennas cannot give.

G9C10

Which of the following can be adjusted to optimize forward gain, front-to-back ratio, or SWR bandwidth of a Yagi antenna?

  1. A The physical length of the boom
  2. B The number of elements on the boom
  3. C The spacing of each element along the boom
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. Boom length, element count and element spacing can all be adjusted.

Designing a Yagi is a balancing act. Changing any of these three moves the trade-off between forward gain, front-to-back ratio and the range of frequencies with good SWR. Designers use computer models to find a good mix.

Why not C? Element spacing matters, but A and B matter too, so C alone is incomplete.

G9C11

What is a beta or hairpin match?

  1. A A shorted transmission line stub placed at the feed point of a Yagi antenna to provide impedance matching
  2. B A 1/4 wavelength section of 75-ohm coax in series with the feed point of a Yagi to provide impedance matching
  3. C A series capacitor selected to cancel the inductive reactance of a folded dipole antenna
  4. D A section of 300-ohm twin-lead transmission line used to match a folded dipole antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A shorted transmission line stub placed at the feed point of a Yagi antenna to provide impedance matching

Why A is right. A. A shorted stub acts as a small inductor across the feed point, raising the Yagi's low impedance to match the coax.

A Yagi's driven element usually has an impedance well below 50 ohms. The stub is two parallel wires shorted at the far end, and its U shape gives it the hairpin name.

Why not B? A section of 75-ohm coax is a different matching method, a quarter-wave transformer.

G9C12

Which of the following is a characteristic of using a gamma match with a Yagi antenna?

  1. A It does not require the driven element to be insulated from the boom
  2. B It does not require any inductors or capacitors
  3. C It is useful for matching multiband antennas
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It does not require the driven element to be insulated from the boom

Why A is right. A. The element's center sits at near-zero RF voltage, so it can be bolted straight to a metal boom while a rod taps the feed in off-center.

A gamma match feeds the element through a rod, with a capacitor to tune it. No insulator is needed at the center, which keeps the build sturdy.

Why not B? A gamma match normally includes a capacitor to cancel the rod's inductance.
