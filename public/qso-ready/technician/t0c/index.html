T0C: RF hazards: radiation exposure, proximity to antennas, recognized safe power levels, radiation types, duty cycle

Group T0C of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 13 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T0C01

What type of radiation are radio signals?

  1. A Gamma radiation
  2. B Ionizing radiation
  3. C Alpha radiation
  4. D Non-ionizing radiation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Non-ionizing radiation

Why D is right. D. Radio signals are non-ionizing radiation.

Ionizing radiation, such as X-rays and gamma rays, has enough energy to knock electrons off atoms and damage DNA. Radio waves carry far less energy in each tiny packet of energy, so they cannot do that. At high levels their main effect is heating.

Why not B? Radio waves do not have enough energy to ionize atoms.

T0C02

Which of the following bands has the lowest maximum permissible exposure for RF safety?

  1. A 3.5 MHz
  2. B 50 MHz
  3. C 440 MHz
  4. D 1296 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 50 MHz

Why B is right. B. 50 MHz has the lowest maximum permissible exposure of these bands.

Maximum permissible exposure is the safe limit for RF fields. The human body absorbs RF energy most strongly in the range of about 30 to 300 MHz, because its size is close to a resonant length there. Where the body absorbs more, the limit is set lower. 50 MHz falls in that range.

Why not D? At 1296 MHz the body absorbs less, so the limit is higher.

T0C03

How does the allowable power density for RF safety change if duty cycle changes from 100 percent to 50 percent?

  1. A It increases by a factor of 3
  2. B It decreases by 50 percent
  3. C It increases by a factor of 2
  4. D There is no adjustment allowed for lower duty cycle
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. It increases by a factor of 2

Why C is right. C. Going from 100 percent to 50 percent duty cycle doubles the allowable power density.

RF exposure is judged by average power over time. At 50 percent duty cycle the transmitter is on half the time, so the average is half. That means the peak power density can be twice as high and still give the same average exposure.

factor = 100% duty cycle / new duty cycle
factor = 100% / 50%
= 2

Why not B? A lower duty cycle raises the allowed level, it does not lower it.

T0C04

What factors affect the RF exposure of people near an amateur station antenna?

  1. A Frequency and power level of the RF field
  2. B Distance from the antenna to a person
  3. C Radiation pattern of the antenna
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

Frequency matters because the body absorbs some frequencies more. More power makes a stronger field. Fields get weaker quickly with distance. The antenna's pattern decides which directions get the most energy. Each factor changes how much RF a person nearby receives.

T0C05

Why do exposure limits vary with frequency?

  1. A Lower frequency RF fields have more energy than higher frequency fields
  2. B Lower frequency RF fields do not penetrate the human body
  3. C Higher frequency RF fields are transient in nature
  4. D The human body absorbs more RF energy at some frequencies than at others
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The human body absorbs more RF energy at some frequencies than at others

Why D is right. D. Exposure limits vary because the body absorbs more RF energy at some frequencies than at others.

The body acts a bit like an antenna. It soaks up the most energy at frequencies where its size fits the wavelength well. Limits are set lower at those frequencies and higher where the body absorbs less.

Why not A? Lower-frequency fields do not carry more energy. Absorption, not energy per wave, drives the limits.

T0C06

Which of the following is an acceptable method to determine whether your station complies with FCC RF exposure regulations?

  1. A By calculation based on FCC OET Bulletin 65
  2. B By calculation based on computer modeling
  3. C By measurement of field strength using calibrated equipment
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

You can check RF exposure in more than one accepted way. You can do the math using the methods in FCC OET Bulletin 65. You can use computer modeling of your antenna. Or you can measure the fields with calibrated equipment.

T0C07

What hazard is created by touching an antenna during a transmission?

  1. A Electrocution
  2. B RF burn to skin
  3. C Exposure to ionizing radiation
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. RF burn to skin

Why B is right. B. Touching an antenna during transmission can cause an RF burn to the skin.

An antenna carries strong RF current while you transmit. If you touch it, that current can flow into your skin at the point of contact and heat it, causing a painful burn. Never touch an antenna while it is in use.

Why not C? RF is non-ionizing radiation, so ionizing exposure is not the risk.

T0C08

Which of the following actions can reduce exposure to RF radiation?

  1. A Relocate antennas
  2. B Relocate the transmitter
  3. C Increase the duty cycle
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Relocate antennas

Why A is right. A. Relocating antennas can reduce RF exposure.

RF fields come from the antenna, not the radio. Moving the antenna higher or farther from where people spend time puts more distance between them and the strongest fields. Fields weaken quickly as distance grows.

Why not B? Moving the transmitter does not move the antenna, which is where the RF is radiated.

T0C09

How can you make sure your station stays in compliance with RF safety regulations?

  1. A By informing the FCC of any changes made in your station
  2. B By re-evaluating the station whenever an item in the transmitter or antenna system is changed
  3. C By making sure your antennas have low SWR
  4. D By using only Underwriter Laboratories approved transmitting equipment
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. By re-evaluating the station whenever an item in the transmitter or antenna system is changed

Why B is right. B. Re-evaluate your station whenever you change something in the transmitter or antenna system.

An RF exposure check applies to the station as it was when you checked it. More power, a new antenna or a new location can change the fields around you. A fresh evaluation after each change keeps your station within the limits.

Why not C? Low SWR means a good match. It does not show how strong the fields are near people.

T0C10

Why is duty cycle one of the factors used to determine safe RF radiation exposure levels?

  1. A It affects the average exposure to radiation
  2. B It affects the peak exposure to radiation
  3. C It takes into account the antenna feed line loss
  4. D It takes into account the thermal effects of the final amplifier
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It affects the average exposure to radiation

Why A is right. A. Duty cycle matters because it affects the average exposure.

Exposure limits are based on average power over a span of time. A transmitter that is on only part of the time puts out less average energy than one that is on all the time. Duty cycle tells you how much of the time it is on.

Why not B? Duty cycle changes the average, not the peak level while transmitting.

T0C11

What is the definition of duty cycle during the averaging time for RF exposure?

  1. A The difference between the lowest and highest power output of a transmitter
  2. B The difference between the PEP and the average power output of a transmitter
  3. C The percentage of time that a transmitter is transmitting
  4. D The percentage of time that a transmitter is not transmitting
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The percentage of time that a transmitter is transmitting

Why C is right. C. Duty cycle is the percentage of time a transmitter is transmitting.

During the averaging time, if your transmitter is on for half of it, the duty cycle is 50 percent. Modes like CW and SSB voice have pauses, so their duty cycle is lower than a mode that keeps a steady carrier.

Why not D? That would be the time the transmitter is off, the opposite of duty cycle.

T0C12

How does RF radiation differ from ionizing radiation (radioactivity)?

  1. A RF radiation does not have sufficient energy to cause chemical changes in cells and damage DNA
  2. B RF radiation can only be detected with an RF dosimeter
  3. C RF radiation is limited in range to a few feet
  4. D RF radiation is perfectly safe
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. RF radiation does not have sufficient energy to cause chemical changes in cells and damage DNA

Why A is right. A. Each tiny packet of RF energy is far too weak to break the chemical bonds in a cell, which is how ionizing radiation damages DNA.

Ionizing radiation, such as X-rays, carries enough energy in each packet to break bonds. RF's main effect at high levels is heating body tissue.

Why not D? RF is not perfectly safe. Strong fields can heat tissue, which is why limits exist.

T0C13

Who is responsible for ensuring that no person is exposed to RF energy above the FCC exposure limits?

  1. A The FCC
  2. B The station licensee
  3. C Anyone who is near an antenna
  4. D The local zoning board
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The station licensee

Why B is right. B. The station licensee must make sure no one is exposed above the FCC limits.

The person who holds the station license is in charge of the station. That includes checking the RF fields it creates and making sure people nearby stay within the limits.

Why not A? The FCC sets the limits, but it does not check each station.
