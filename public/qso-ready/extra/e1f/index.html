E1F: Miscellaneous rules: external RF power amplifiers; prohibited communications; spread spectrum; auxiliary stations; Canadian amateurs operating in the US; special temporary authority

Group E1F of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E1F01

On what frequencies are spread spectrum transmissions permitted?

  1. A Only on amateur frequencies above 50 MHz
  2. B Only on amateur frequencies above 222 MHz
  3. C Only on amateur frequencies above 420 MHz
  4. D Only on amateur frequencies above 144 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Only on amateur frequencies above 222 MHz

Why B is right. B. Spread spectrum (SS) is first listed for the 1.25 meter band at 222-225 MHz, and on every band above it.

Spread spectrum spreads a signal over a wide range of frequencies. The emission table lists SS for 222-225 MHz and for the UHF and higher bands. It does not list SS for 6 meters, 2 meters, or any HF band.

“222-225 MHz | RTTY, data, test MCW, phone, SS, image”

47 CFR 97.305(c)

Why not D? 2 meters, at 144-148 MHz, does not allow spread spectrum.

E1F02

What privileges are authorized in the US to persons holding an amateur service license granted by the government of Canada?

  1. A None, they must obtain a US license
  2. B Full privileges of the General class license on the 80-, 40-, 20-, 15-, and 10-meter bands
  3. C The operating terms and conditions of the Canadian amateur service license, not to exceed US Amateur Extra class license privileges
  4. D Full privileges, up to and including those of the Amateur Extra class license, on the 80-, 40-, 20-, 15-, and 10-meter bands
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The operating terms and conditions of the Canadian amateur service license, not to exceed US Amateur Extra class license privileges

Why C is right. C. A US-Canada convention and the rule let Canadians keep their own license terms here, with US Extra privileges as the ceiling.

Each country's amateurs may operate in the other without a second license. The Canadian follows the conditions of their own license and the US rules.

“The operating terms and conditions of the amateur service license issued by the Government of Canada … not to exceed the control operator privileges of an FCC-granted Amateur Extra Class operator license”

47 CFR 97.107(a)

Why not A? Canadians do not need a US license to operate here.

E1F03

Under what circumstances may a dealer sell an external RF power amplifier capable of operation below 144 MHz if it has not been granted FCC certification?

  1. A Gain is less than 23 dB when driven by power of 10 watts or less
  2. B The equipment dealer assembled it from a kit
  3. C It was manufactured and certificated in a country which has a reciprocal certification agreement with the FCC
  4. D The amplifier is constructed or modified by an amateur radio operator for use at an amateur station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The amplifier is constructed or modified by an amateur radio operator for use at an amateur station

Why D is right. D. The rule exempts amplifiers that amateurs build or modify for their own amateur use from the certification requirement.

Amplifiers made or imported for amateur use normally need FCC certification, and non-licensees may not build or modify units for below 144 MHz without it. A dealer assembling a kit is not an amateur building for their station.

“The amplifier is constructed or modified by an amateur radio operator for use at an amateur station.”

47 CFR 97.315(b)(1)

Why not B? A dealer is not an amateur building for their own station.

E1F04

Which of the following geographic descriptions approximately describes "Line A"?

  1. A A line roughly parallel to and south of the border between the US and Canada
  2. B A line roughly parallel to and west of the US Atlantic coastline
  3. C A line roughly parallel to and north of the border between the US and Mexico
  4. D A line roughly parallel to and east of the US Pacific coastline
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A line roughly parallel to and south of the border between the US and Canada

Why A is right. A. The rule traces Line A from Aberdeen, Washington, east across the northern states to Maine, keeping it a short way south of Canada.

Some frequency limits apply only to stations between Line A and the Canadian border.

“Line A. Begins at Aberdeen, WA”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(30)

Why not C? Line A is near Canada, not Mexico.

E1F05

Amateur stations may not transmit in which of the following frequency segments if they are located in the contiguous 48 states and north of Line A?

  1. A 440 MHz - 450 MHz
  2. B 53 MHz - 54 MHz
  3. C 222 MHz - 223 MHz
  4. D 420 MHz - 430 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 420 MHz - 430 MHz

Why D is right. D. The 70 cm rule bars stations located north of Line A from the 420-430 MHz segment.

The rest of the 70 cm band stays open to those stations. As background, the limit protects other users across the border.

“No amateur station shall transmit from north of Line A in the 420-430 MHz segment.”

47 CFR 97.303(m)(1)

Why not A? 440-450 MHz is not restricted by Line A.

E1F06

Under what circumstances might the FCC issue a Special Temporary Authority (STA) to an amateur station?

  1. A To provide for experimental amateur communications
  2. B To allow use of a special event call sign
  3. C To allow a VE group with less than three VEs to administer examinations in a remote, sparsely populated area
  4. D To allow a licensee who has passed an upgrade exam to operate with upgraded privileges while waiting for posting on the FCC database
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. To provide for experimental amateur communications

Why A is right. A. The FCC can grant a Special Temporary Authority so amateurs can run experiments that normal rules don't cover.

An STA is short-term permission to operate outside the usual rules, for a limited time and purpose. You don't need one to use upgraded privileges; the rules already allow that after passing.

“To conduct tests to determine necessary data for the preparation of an application for regular authorization”

47 CFR 1.931(b)(2)(iii)

Why not D? A passed upgrade already carries temporary privileges under the rules, with no STA needed.

E1F07

When may an amateur station send a message to a business?

  1. A When the pecuniary interest of the amateur or his or her employer is less than $25
  2. B When the pecuniary interest of the amateur or his or her employer is less than $50
  3. C At no time
  4. D When neither the amateur nor their employer has a pecuniary interest in the communications
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. When neither the amateur nor their employer has a pecuniary interest in the communications

Why D is right. D. The rule bans only messages in which the amateur or their employer has a money interest, so a message to a business is fine without one.

Pecuniary interest means a money interest. You may contact a business, for example to ask a hardware store about its hours. There is no dollar threshold.

“Communications in which the station licensee or control operator has a pecuniary interest, including communications on behalf of an employer”

47 CFR 97.113(a)(3)

Why not C? Such messages are allowed when no one involved has a money interest.

E1F08

Which of the following types of amateur station communications are prohibited?

  1. A Communications transmitted for hire or material compensation, except as otherwise provided in the rules
  2. B Communications that have political content, except as allowed by the Fairness Doctrine
  3. C Communications that have religious content
  4. D Communications in a language other than English
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Communications transmitted for hire or material compensation, except as otherwise provided in the rules

Why A is right. A. The amateur service is non-commercial, so the rule bans transmitting for hire or payment except where the rules allow.

A few exceptions exist, such as some classroom teaching. Political or religious topics are not banned, and no rule requires English.

“Communications for hire or for material compensation, direct or indirect, paid or promised, except as otherwise provided in these rules”

47 CFR 97.113(a)(2)

Why not D? No rule requires English for amateur communications.

E1F09

Which of the following cannot be transmitted over an amateur radio mesh network?

  1. A Third party traffic
  2. B Email
  3. C Messages encoded to obscure their meaning
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Messages encoded to obscure their meaning

Why C is right. C. Mesh nodes are ordinary amateur stations, so the rule against messages encoded to hide their meaning applies to them.

Third party traffic and email can be legal on a mesh network. Encryption is allowed only in narrow cases, such as commands to a satellite.

“messages encoded for the purpose of obscuring their meaning, except as otherwise provided herein”

47 CFR 97.113(a)(4)

Why not B? Email is allowed if it follows the other amateur rules.

E1F10

Who may be the control operator of an auxiliary station?

  1. A Any licensed amateur operator
  2. B Only Technician, General, Advanced, or Amateur Extra class operators
  3. C Only General, Advanced, or Amateur Extra class operators
  4. D Only Amateur Extra class operators
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Only Technician, General, Advanced, or Amateur Extra class operators

Why B is right. B. The auxiliary station rule names only Technician, General, Advanced and Extra holders, so Novices are left out.

An auxiliary station links parts of a system, such as a repeater control link.

“A holder of a Technician, General, Advanced or Amateur Extra Class operator license may be the control operator of an auxiliary station”

47 CFR 97.201(a)

Why not A? Novice licensees are left out, so not every licensee qualifies.

E1F11

Which of the following best describes one of the standards that must be met by an external RF power amplifier if it is to qualify for a grant of FCC certification?

  1. A It must produce full legal output when driven by not more than 5 watts of mean RF input power
  2. B It must have received an Underwriters Laboratory certification for electrical safety as well as having met IEEE standard 14.101(B)
  3. C It must exhibit a gain of less than 23 dB when driven by 10 watts or less
  4. D It must satisfy the FCC’s spurious emission standards when operated at the lesser of 1500 watts or its full output power
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It must satisfy the FCC’s spurious emission standards when operated at the lesser of 1500 watts or its full output power

Why D is right. D. Testing at the lesser of 1.5 kW PEP or full output proves the amplifier stays clean in real use, which the certification rule requires.

Other standards include no gain between 26 and 28 MHz and a gain of no more than 15 dB.

“when the amplifier is operated at the lesser of 1.5 kW PEP or its full output power”

47 CFR 97.317(a)(1)

Why not C? The current gain limit is 15 dB, not 23 dB.
