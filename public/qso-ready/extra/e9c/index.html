E9C: Practical wire antennas; folded dipoles; phased arrays; effects of ground near antennas

Group E9C of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 14 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E9C01

What type of radiation pattern is created by two 1/4-wavelength vertical antennas spaced 1/2-wavelength apart and fed 180 degrees out of phase?

  1. A Cardioid
  2. B Omni-directional
  3. C A figure-eight broadside to the axis of the array
  4. D A figure-eight oriented along the axis of the array
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. A figure-eight oriented along the axis of the array

Why D is right. D. Broadside the opposite feeds cancel, while along the line the half-wave travel delay brings them back in step, so the lobes point along the axis.

Picture a listener off to the side, the same distance from both verticals. The signals arrive at the same moment but are fed 180 degrees apart, so they cancel. Now picture a listener in line with the pair. One signal travels a half wavelength farther, which adds another 180 degrees of delay. Total shift is 360 degrees, so they add. That gives an end-fire figure-eight.

Why not C? A broadside figure-eight comes from in-phase feeding; with 180-degree feeding the broadside directions cancel.

E9C02

What type of radiation pattern is created by two 1/4-wavelength vertical antennas spaced 1/4-wavelength apart and fed 90 degrees out of phase?

  1. A Cardioid
  2. B A figure-eight end-fire along the axis of the array
  3. C A figure-eight broadside to the axis of the array
  4. D Omni-directional
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Cardioid

Why A is right. A. A quarter-wave spacing and a 90-degree feed shift add in one direction along the axis and cancel in the other, giving a cardioid.

In one direction along the line, the travel delay of 90 degrees matches the feed delay, so the two waves arrive in step and add. In the opposite direction, the delays stack to 180 degrees and cancel. Sideways, they are partly in step. The result is a heart-shaped pattern, a cardioid, with one deep null at the back.

Why not B? A figure-eight has two equal lobes, but here the waves cancel in one end-fire direction and add in the other.

E9C03

What type of radiation pattern is created by two 1/4-wavelength vertical antennas spaced 1/2-wavelength apart and fed in phase?

  1. A Omni-directional
  2. B Cardioid
  3. C A figure-eight broadside to the axis of the array
  4. D A figure-eight end-fire along the axis of the array
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A figure-eight broadside to the axis of the array

Why C is right. C. Fed in phase, the two signals reach a broadside listener together and add, but along the axis the half-wave delay cancels them.

Off to the side, both verticals are the same distance away. Their in-phase signals arrive together and reinforce each other. In line with the pair, one signal travels a half wavelength farther, a 180-degree delay, and the two cancel. So the lobes point broadside, at right angles to the line between the antennas.

Why not D? End-fire lobes need the 180-degree feed; in-phase feeding cancels along the axis instead.

E9C04

What happens to the radiation pattern of an unterminated long wire antenna as the wire length is increased?

  1. A Fewer lobes form with the major lobes increasing closer to broadside to the wire
  2. B Additional lobes form with major lobes increasingly aligned with the axis of the antenna
  3. C The elevation angle increases, and the front-to-rear ratio decreases
  4. D The elevation angle increases, while the front-to-rear ratio is unaffected
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Additional lobes form with major lobes increasingly aligned with the axis of the antenna

Why B is right. B. A longer wire holds more half-wave current sections, which split the pattern into more lobes that swing closer to the wire's line.

On an unterminated wire, current flips direction every half wavelength. Each added section changes how the fields combine, adding more lobes. As the wire grows to several wavelengths, the strongest lobes move toward the ends of the wire. A long wire therefore favors directions close to its own line, not broadside.

Why not A? Lengthening the wire adds lobes, not fewer, and the main lobes move away from broadside.

E9C05

What is the purpose of feeding an off-center-fed dipole (OCFD) between the center and one end instead of at the midpoint?

  1. A To create a similar feed point impedance on multiple bands
  2. B To suppress off-center lobes at higher frequencies
  3. C To resonate the antenna across a wider range of frequencies
  4. D To reduce common-mode current coupling on the feed line shield
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. To create a similar feed point impedance on multiple bands

Why A is right. A. Feeding a dipole away from its center picks a point where the impedance is about the same on several harmonic bands.

Along a wire, impedance changes with position: low near a current peak, high near a voltage peak. On each band the pattern of peaks shifts. At a point roughly a third of the way from one end, the impedance on several harmonically related bands lands near the same value, a few hundred ohms. One balun and one coax can then serve all those bands.

Why not D? An off-center feed tends to increase common-mode current on the coax, so reducing it is not the reason.

E9C06

What is the effect of adding a terminating resistor to a rhombic or long-wire antenna?

  1. A It reflects the standing waves on the antenna elements back to the transmitter
  2. B It changes the radiation pattern from bidirectional to unidirectional
  3. C It changes the radiation pattern from horizontal to vertical polarization
  4. D It decreases the ground loss
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. It changes the radiation pattern from bidirectional to unidirectional

Why B is right. B. The resistor soaks up the wave that reaches the far end, so no reflected wave radiates backward and the pattern points one way.

An unterminated long wire or rhombic carries a wave out to the end, where it bounces back. That returning wave radiates in the opposite direction, so the pattern has two main directions. A resistor matched to the antenna at the far end absorbs the forward wave instead. With only one wave on the wire, the pattern favors one direction.

Why not A? The resistor absorbs energy to stop reflections; it does not send standing waves back.

E9C07

What is the approximate feed point impedance at the center of a two-wire half-wave folded dipole antenna?

  1. A 300 ohms
  2. B 72 ohms
  3. C 50 ohms
  4. D 450 ohms
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 300 ohms

Why A is right. A. A two-wire folded dipole multiplies a plain dipole's resistance by 2 squared, so 4 × 73 ohms is about 292, close to 300 ohms.

A plain half-wave dipole in free space has a feed point resistance near 73 ohms. In a folded dipole, the same current flows in both wires, but only one is fed. The feed point sees only half the total current. Since power is current squared times resistance, half the current for the same power means four times the resistance. 300 ohms is the usual nominal figure, matching common 300-ohm twin-lead.

Z = N^2 × 73 ohms
N = 2 wires of equal size
Z = 2^2 × 73 = 4 × 73 ohms
Z = 292 ohms, about 300 ohms
= 292 ohms

Why not B? About 72 ohms is a plain single-wire dipole, not a folded one.

E9C08

What is a folded dipole antenna?

  1. A A dipole one-quarter wavelength long
  2. B A center-fed dipole with the ends folded down 90 degrees at the midpoint of each side
  3. C A half-wave dipole with an additional parallel wire connecting its two ends
  4. D A dipole configured to provide forward gain
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A half-wave dipole with an additional parallel wire connecting its two ends

Why C is right. C. Adding a second wire that joins the dipole's two ends splits the current between two conductors, and that parallel wire is what makes it folded.

The second wire runs close beside the first and connects to it at both tips. Only one wire is cut in the middle for the feed line. The extra wire raises the feed point impedance, to about 300 ohms for two equal wires. It can also widen the usable bandwidth a little.

Why not B? Bending the ends down makes a drooping or bent dipole; it does not add the parallel wire that defines a folded dipole.

E9C09

Which of the following describes a G5RV antenna?

  1. A A wire antenna center-fed through a specific length of open-wire line connected to a balun and coaxial feed line
  2. B A multi-band trap antenna
  3. C A phased array antenna consisting of multiple loops
  4. D A wide band dipole using shorted coaxial cable for the radiating elements and fed with a 4:1 balun
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A wire antenna center-fed through a specific length of open-wire line connected to a balun and coaxial feed line

Why A is right. A. The G5RV gets its multiband use from its feed: a set length of open-wire line acts as a matching section before a balun and coax.

The open-wire section acts as a matching section on some bands, moving the antenna's impedance toward a value the coax can handle. It works as a multiband antenna without traps. On most bands a tuner is still needed, because the match is only close on a few of them.

Why not B? A G5RV covers several bands without traps; its feed line section does the work instead.

E9C10

Which of the following describes a Zepp antenna?

  1. A A horizontal array capable of quickly changing the direction of maximum radiation by changing phasing lines
  2. B An end-fed half-wavelength dipole
  3. C An omni-directional antenna commonly used for satellite communications
  4. D A vertical array capable of quickly changing the direction of maximum radiation by changing phasing lines
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. An end-fed half-wavelength dipole

Why B is right. B. A Zepp is a half-wave dipole fed at one end, usually through open-wire line, since the end of a half-wave wire has high impedance.

The name comes from antennas trailed behind Zeppelin airships, where feeding at the end was practical. At the end of a half-wave wire, voltage is high and current is low, so the impedance is high. Open-wire line, often a quarter wave long, handles that high impedance and brings it to the station.

Why not C? A Zepp is a plain end-fed wire, not a special satellite antenna.

E9C11

How is the far-field elevation pattern of a vertically polarized antenna affected by being mounted over seawater versus soil?

  1. A Radiation at low angles decreases
  2. B Additional lobes appear at higher elevation angles
  3. C Separate elevation lobes will combine into a single lobe
  4. D Radiation at low angles increases
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Radiation at low angles increases

Why D is right. D. Seawater conducts far better than soil, so its reflections reinforce low-angle radiation from a vertical instead of absorbing it.

A vertical antenna's low-angle signal depends on the ground in front of it. Soil is a poor conductor, so much of that low-angle energy is lost or reflected out of phase. Seawater is a very good conductor, close to a perfect reflecting surface. The low-angle signal stays strong, which helps long-distance contacts. That is why verticals near the ocean work so well.

Why not A? Poor ground is what weakens low-angle radiation; seawater does the opposite.

E9C12

Which of the following describes an extended double Zepp antenna?

  1. A An end-fed full-wave dipole antenna
  2. B A center-fed 1.5-wavelength dipole antenna
  3. C A center-fed 1.25-wavelength dipole antenna
  4. D An end-fed 2-wavelength dipole antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A center-fed 1.25-wavelength dipole antenna

Why C is right. C. An extended double Zepp is center-fed, with each half stretched to 5/8 wavelength for a total of 1.25 wavelengths.

A double Zepp is two half-wave wires fed in the middle, a full wavelength in all. Stretching each half to 5/8 wavelength narrows the broadside lobes and adds gain, about 3 dB over a half-wave dipole. Making it much longer would break the main lobe into smaller ones.

Why not B? 1.5 wavelengths is longer than the extended double Zepp and would split its main lobes.

E9C13

How does the radiation pattern of a horizontally polarized antenna vary with increasing height above ground?

  1. A The takeoff angle of the lowest elevation lobe increases
  2. B The takeoff angle of the lowest elevation lobe decreases
  3. C The horizontal beamwidth increases
  4. D The horizontal beamwidth decreases
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The takeoff angle of the lowest elevation lobe decreases

Why B is right. B. Raising a horizontal antenna puts its ground reflection in step at lower angles, so the lowest lobe moves toward the horizon.

A horizontal antenna's pattern above ground comes from the direct wave combining with the wave reflected off the ground. The angle where they first add up depends on height in wavelengths. The higher the antenna, the lower that angle. A dipole a half wavelength up has its main lobe higher than one a full wavelength up.

Why not A? Height lowers the first lobe's angle; it does not raise it.

E9C14

How does the radiation pattern of a horizontally-polarized antenna mounted above a long slope compare with the same antenna mounted above flat ground?

  1. A The main lobe takeoff angle increases in the downhill direction
  2. B The main lobe takeoff angle decreases in the downhill direction
  3. C The horizontal beamwidth decreases in the downhill direction
  4. D The horizontal beamwidth increases in the uphill direction
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The main lobe takeoff angle decreases in the downhill direction

Why B is right. B. Facing downhill, the ground falls away, so the reflecting surface tilts and the main lobe aims lower toward the horizon.

The main lobe angle is set by how the direct and ground-reflected waves combine. On a slope, the reflection happens from tilted ground. In the downhill direction, that tilt lowers the angle of the main lobe by about the slope's angle. In the uphill direction the lobe aims higher. Hilltop stations with a long downhill run often do well on long-distance paths.

Why not A? The takeoff angle rises in the uphill direction, not downhill.
