E1C: Automatic and remote control; band-specific regulations; operating in and communicating with foreign countries; spurious emission standards; HF modulation index limit; band-specific rules

Group E1C of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E1C01

What is the maximum bandwidth for a data emission on 60 meters?

  1. A 60 Hz
  2. B 170 Hz
  3. C 1.5 kHz
  4. D 2.8 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 2.8 kHz

Why D is right. D. On 60 meters, no emission, including data, may be wider than 2.8 kHz.

The 60 meter rule caps every signal at 2.8 kHz, about the width of an SSB voice signal. Since February 2026, 60 meters also has a 5351.5 to 5366.5 kHz segment besides the four channels, and the 2.8 kHz limit covers all of it.

“For all 60 m spectrum, Amateur operators shall ensure that their emissions do not occupy more than 2.8 kHz.”

47 CFR 97.303(h)(3)

Why not C? 1.5 kHz is the carrier offset below channel center, not a bandwidth limit.

E1C02

Which of the following apply to communications transmitted to amateur stations in foreign countries?

  1. A Third party traffic must be limited to that intended for the exclusive use of government and non-Government Organization (NGOs) involved in emergency relief activities
  2. B All transmissions must be in English
  3. C Communications must be limited to those incidental to the purpose of the amateur service and remarks of a personal nature
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Communications must be limited to those incidental to the purpose of the amateur service and remarks of a personal nature

Why C is right. C. The rule limits messages to other countries to the purposes of amateur radio and personal remarks, so those are the only topics allowed.

Talking about your station, the contact, the weather or your family is fine. The rule says nothing about language, and third party traffic has its own separate rules.

“shall be limited to communications incidental to the purposes of the amateur service and to remarks of a personal character”

47 CFR 97.117

Why not B? No rule requires English for contacts with other countries.

E1C03

How long must an operator wait after filing a notification with the Utilities Technology Council (UTC) before operating on the 2200-meter or 630-meter band?

  1. A Operators must not operate until approval is received
  2. B Operators may operate after 30 days, providing they have not been told that their station is within 1 kilometer of PLC systems using those frequencies
  3. C Operators may not operate until a test signal has been transmitted in coordination with the local power company
  4. D Operations may commence immediately, and may continue unless interference is reported by the UTC
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Operators may operate after 30 days, providing they have not been told that their station is within 1 kilometer of PLC systems using those frequencies

Why B is right. B. The 30 day wait gives UTC time to check for power line carrier systems near you; if it raises no objection, you may start.

Power companies send power line carrier (PLC) signals over their wires to control equipment, some near 136 kHz and 475 kHz. UTC objects only if your antenna is within 1 km of one. No approval letter is needed.

“Amateur stations will be permitted to commence operations after the 30-day period unless UTC notifies the station that its fixed location is located within one kilometer of PLC systems”

47 CFR 97.303(g)(2)

Why not A? No approval is required; the 30 day wait without an objection is enough.

E1C04

What is an IARP?

  1. A A permit that allows US amateurs to operate in certain countries of the Americas
  2. B The internal amateur radio practices policy of the FCC
  3. C An indication of increased antenna reflected power
  4. D A forecast of intermittent aurora radio propagation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A permit that allows US amateurs to operate in certain countries of the Americas

Why A is right. A. An IARP is a permit under an Inter-American treaty that lets licensed amateurs operate in other member countries in the Americas.

IARP stands for International Amateur Radio Permit. Your home country issues it to you, and member countries accept it as permission to operate while visiting. It works much like the CEPT arrangement does in Europe.

“IARP (International Amateur Radio Permit). A document issued pursuant to the terms of the Inter-American Convention on an International Amateur Radio Permit”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(24)

Why not B? The FCC has no policy by that name; IARP is a travel permit.

E1C05

Under what situation may a station transmit third party communications while being automatically controlled?

  1. A Never
  2. B Only when transmitting RTTY or data emissions
  3. C Only when transmitting SSB or CW
  4. D On any mode approved by the National Telecommunication and Information Administration
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Only when transmitting RTTY or data emissions

Why B is right. B. The rule bars third party traffic under automatic control and makes an exception only for RTTY and data emissions.

Automatic control means no operator is at a control point while the station transmits. Automated digital message systems fit the exception. Voice third party traffic is fine under local or remote control.

“No station may transmit third party communications while being automatically controlled except a station transmitting a RTTY or data emission.”

47 CFR 97.115(c)

Why not A? RTTY and data are a stated exception, so it is not never.

E1C06

Which of the following is required in order to operate in accordance with CEPT rules in foreign countries where permitted?

  1. A You must identify in the official language of the country in which you are operating
  2. B The US embassy must approve of your operation
  3. C You must have a copy of FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048
  4. D You must append "/CEPT" to your call sign
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. You must have a copy of FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048

Why C is right. C. Officials in a host country need proof that a US license qualifies under CEPT, and that FCC public notice is the document that shows it.

In practice, visiting US hams carry the notice along with their license. You also put the host country's prefix before your call sign, as that country directs.

Why not D? You use the host country's prefix, not a /CEPT suffix.

E1C07

What notifications must be given before transmitting on the 630- or 2200-meter bands?

  1. A A special endorsement must be requested from the FCC
  2. B An environmental impact statement must be filed with the Department of the Interior
  3. C Operators must inform the FAA of their intent to operate, giving their call sign and distance to the nearest runway
  4. D Operators must inform the Utilities Technology Council (UTC) of their call sign and coordinates of the station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Operators must inform the Utilities Technology Council (UTC) of their call sign and coordinates of the station

Why D is right. D. UTC needs your call sign and antenna location so it can check whether you are within 1 km of a power line carrier system.

The rule calls the group the Utilities Telecom Council. Power line carrier systems use these frequencies to control power equipment. No FCC or FAA filing is involved.

“amateur operators shall notify the Utilities Telecom Council (UTC) of their intent to operate by submitting their call signs, intended band or bands of operation, and the coordinates of their antenna's fixed location”

47 CFR 97.303(g)(2)

Why not A? No special FCC endorsement is needed.

E1C08

What is the maximum permissible duration of a remotely controlled station’s transmissions if its control link malfunctions?

  1. A 30 seconds
  2. B 3 minutes
  3. C 5 minutes
  4. D 10 minutes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 3 minutes

Why B is right. B. A lost control link means no one can switch the transmitter off, so the rule requires a built-in cutoff within 3 minutes.

A timer or similar device makes sure a failed link can't leave the station on the air for long.

“Provisions are incorporated to limit transmission by the station to a period of no more than 3 minutes in the event of malfunction in the control link.”

47 CFR 97.213(b)

Why not D? 10 minutes is the station ID interval, not this limit.

E1C09

What is the highest modulation index permitted at the highest modulation frequency for angle modulation below 29.0 MHz?

  1. A 0.5
  2. B 1.0
  3. C 2.0
  4. D 3.0
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 1.0

Why B is right. B. The rule caps modulation index at 1, which keeps FM and PM signals narrow on crowded HF bands.

Modulation index is peak deviation divided by the modulating frequency. An index of 1 with 3 kHz audio means at most 3 kHz of deviation. The 29.0 MHz split comes from the 97.305(c) table: 29.0-29.7 MHz lists only standard (f)(2), not (f)(1).

“No angle-modulated emission may have a modulation index greater than 1 at the highest modulation frequency.”

47 CFR 97.307(f)(1)

Why not C? An index of 2 would allow twice the deviation the rule permits.

E1C10

What is the maximum mean power level for a spurious emission below 30 MHz with respect to the fundamental emission?

  1. A - 43 dB
  2. B - 53 dB
  3. C - 63 dB
  4. D - 73 dB
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. - 43 dB

Why A is right. A. Below 30 MHz the rule requires spurious emissions to be at least 43 dB weaker than the fundamental, for transmitters installed after January 1, 2003.

43 dB below means about 1/20,000 of the fundamental's mean power. Older transmitters have looser limits. Between 30 and 225 MHz the general figure is 60 dB, with some relief for transmitters of 25 W or less.

“transmitting on a frequency below 30 MHz must be at least 43 dB below the mean power of the fundamental emission”

47 CFR 97.307(d)

Why not C? 63 dB is stricter than the HF rule requires.

E1C11

Which of the following operating arrangements allows an FCC-licensed US citizen to operate in many European countries, and amateurs from many European countries to operate in the US?

  1. A CEPT
  2. B IARP
  3. C ITU reciprocal license
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. CEPT

Why A is right. A. Under CEPT, member countries accept each other's licenses for short visits, and US rules let visiting CEPT license holders operate here.

CEPT is a group of European telecom regulators. The European side, where US hams operate abroad, comes from CEPT's own recommendation, not Part 97. On the US side, the band tables give a visiting CEPT license holder the same privileges as an Extra.

“For a station having a control operator who has been granted an Amateur Extra Class operator license, who holds a CEPT radio amateur license”

47 CFR 97.301(b)

Why not B? IARP covers countries in the Americas, not Europe.

E1C12

In what portion of the 630-meter band are phone emissions permitted?

  1. A None
  2. B Only the top 3 kHz
  3. C Only the bottom 3 kHz
  4. D The entire band
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The entire band

Why D is right. D. The emission table lists phone and image for the whole 630 meter band, with no smaller segment.

The band is only 7 kHz wide and power is very low, so most activity is CW and data, but phone is allowed anywhere in it.

“630 m | Entire band | Phone, image | (f)(1), (2)”

47 CFR 97.305(c)

Why not A? The table authorizes phone on 630 meters.
