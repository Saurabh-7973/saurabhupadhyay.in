E3B: Transequatorial propagation; long-path propagation; ordinary and extraordinary waves; chordal hop; sporadic-E mechanisms; ground-wave propagation

Group E3B of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 13 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E3B01

Where is transequatorial propagation (TEP) most likely to occur?

  1. A Between points separated by 2,000 miles to 3,000 miles over a path perpendicular to the geomagnetic equator
  2. B Between points located 1,500 miles to 2,000 miles apart on the geomagnetic equator
  3. C Between points located at each other’s antipode
  4. D Through the region where the terminator crosses the geographic equator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Between points separated by 2,000 miles to 3,000 miles over a path perpendicular to the geomagnetic equator

Why A is right. A. TEP rides on dense ionization on both sides of the geomagnetic equator, so it links stations about 2,000 to 3,000 miles apart straight across it.

Transequatorial propagation (TEP) works best when the two stations sit about the same distance north and south of the geomagnetic equator.

Why not B? Both stations sit away from the geomagnetic equator, on opposite sides of it.

E3B02

What is the approximate maximum range for signals using transequatorial propagation?

  1. A 1,000 miles
  2. B 2,500 miles
  3. C 5,000 miles
  4. D 7,500 miles
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 5,000 miles

Why C is right. C. TEP joins the two ionization zones on either side of the geomagnetic equator, which can stretch a path to about 5,000 miles.

Typical TEP paths run 2,000 to 3,000 miles. In good conditions they can reach about 5,000 miles, far beyond normal VHF range.

Why not A? 1,000 miles is shorter than even the typical TEP range.

E3B03

At what time of day is transequatorial propagation most likely to occur?

  1. A Morning
  2. B Noon
  3. C Afternoon or early evening
  4. D Late at night
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Afternoon or early evening

Why C is right. C. Ionization near the geomagnetic equator builds through the day and peaks late, so TEP opens best in the afternoon or early evening.

Late at night the needed ionization often fades.

Why not D? Late at night, the needed ionization has often faded.

E3B04

What are “extraordinary” and “ordinary” waves?

  1. A Extraordinary waves exhibit rare long-skip propagation, compared to ordinary waves, which travel shorter distances
  2. B Independently propagating, elliptically polarized waves created in the ionosphere
  3. C Long-path and short-path waves
  4. D Refracted rays and reflected waves
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Independently propagating, elliptically polarized waves created in the ionosphere

Why B is right. B. The Earth's magnetic field splits a wave in the ionosphere into two elliptically polarized parts, ordinary and extraordinary, that travel separately.

The two parts bend differently and reach different heights. They can arrive at different times or places. This is part of why HF signals fade and change polarization.

Why not A? The names do not mean rare or common; they name two polarization parts.

E3B05

Which of the following paths is most likely to support long-distance propagation on 160 meters?

  1. A A path entirely in sunlight
  2. B Paths at high latitudes
  3. C A direct north-south path
  4. D A path entirely in darkness
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. A path entirely in darkness

Why D is right. D. The D layer, which absorbs 160 meter signals, forms only in sunlight, so a path that is all in darkness has the least loss.

If any part of the path is in daylight, the D layer there soaks up the signal.

Why not A? A daylight path has strong D layer absorption.

E3B06

On which of the following amateur bands is long-path propagation most frequent?

  1. A 160 meters and 80 meters
  2. B 40 meters and 20 meters
  3. C 10 meters and 6 meters
  4. D 6 meters and 2 meters
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 40 meters and 20 meters

Why B is right. B. A long path usually crosses both daylight and darkness, and 40 and 20 meters are the bands that can travel through both parts.

Long path means the signal goes the long way around the Earth, more than half way. Lower bands are absorbed in the daylight part; higher bands often fail in the dark part.

Why not C? 10 meters and 6 meters are open less often over such long paths.

E3B07

What effect does lowering a signal’s transmitted elevation angle have on ionospheric HF skip propagation?

  1. A Faraday rotation becomes stronger
  2. B The MUF decreases
  3. C The distance covered by each hop increases
  4. D The critical frequency increases
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The distance covered by each hop increases

Why C is right. C. A low-angle wave climbs slowly, so it reaches the ionosphere farther away and comes back down farther away.

A steep wave goes nearly straight up and comes down close by.

Why not D? The critical frequency depends on the ionosphere, not your antenna angle.

E3B08

How does the maximum range of ground-wave propagation change when the signal frequency is increased?

  1. A It stays the same
  2. B It increases
  3. C It decreases
  4. D It peaks at roughly 8 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. It decreases

Why C is right. C. The ground absorbs more energy as frequency rises, so a ground wave dies out sooner at higher frequencies.

This is why AM broadcast stations cover far more ground by ground wave than higher-frequency stations.

Why not B? Losses grow with frequency, so range drops.

E3B09

At what time of year is sporadic-E propagation most likely to occur?

  1. A Around the solstices, especially the summer solstice
  2. B Around the solstices, especially the winter solstice
  3. C Around the equinoxes, especially the spring equinox
  4. D Around the equinoxes, especially the fall equinox
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Around the solstices, especially the summer solstice

Why A is right. A. The patches of dense E-region ionization that make sporadic E form most often near the solstices, especially in early summer.

In the Northern Hemisphere, sporadic E peaks from May to August, with a smaller peak near the winter solstice.

Why not C? The equinoxes are a low time for sporadic E.

E3B10

What is the effect of chordal-hop propagation?

  1. A The signal experiences less loss compared to multi-hop propagation, which uses Earth as a reflector
  2. B The MUF for chordal-hop propagation is much lower than for normal skip propagation
  3. C Atmospheric noise is reduced in the direction of chordal-hop propagation
  4. D Signals travel faster along ionospheric chords
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The signal experiences less loss compared to multi-hop propagation, which uses Earth as a reflector

Why A is right. A. Chordal hop has less loss than normal multi-hop because it skips the lossy ground bounces.

In regular multi-hop, each hop returns to the ground and reflects back up, losing energy each time. In chordal hop, the signal stays in the ionosphere between refractions. Fewer ground hits means a stronger signal at the far end.

Why not D? All radio waves travel at about the same speed; the benefit is less loss.

E3B11

At what time of day is sporadic-E propagation most likely to occur?

  1. A Between midnight and sunrise
  2. B Between sunset and midnight
  3. C Between sunset and sunrise
  4. D Between sunrise and sunset
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Between sunrise and sunset

Why D is right. D. Sporadic E clouds form most often while the sun is up, so daytime is the most likely time.

Sporadic E can appear at any hour. It often peaks in late morning and again in early evening.

Why not A? Midnight to sunrise is the least likely time.

E3B12

What is chordal-hop propagation?

  1. A Propagation away from the great circle bearing between stations
  2. B Successive ionospheric refractions without an intermediate reflection from the ground
  3. C Propagation across the geomagnetic equator
  4. D Signals reflected back toward the transmitting station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Successive ionospheric refractions without an intermediate reflection from the ground

Why B is right. B. The term describes a signal that bends back toward Earth but rises again before touching the ground, skipping the ground bounces.

The signal travels along chords, straight lines that cut across the curve of the Earth, and comes down only at the far end.

Why not D? A signal returning to the sender is backscatter, not chordal hop.

E3B13

What type of polarization is supported by ground-wave propagation?

  1. A Vertical
  2. B Horizontal
  3. C Circular
  4. D Elliptical
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Vertical

Why A is right. A. The conducting ground shorts out a horizontal electric field near the surface, so only vertical polarization survives as a ground wave.

That is why AM broadcast stations use vertical towers.

Why not B? The ground shorts out horizontal fields near the surface.
