E3A: Electromagnetic Waves and Specialized Propagation: Earth-Moon-Earth (EME) communications; meteor scatter; microwave tropospheric and scatter propagation; auroral propagation; daily variation of ionospheric propagation; circular polarization

Group E3A of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 14 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E3A01

What is the approximate maximum separation measured along the surface of the Earth between two stations communicating by EME?

  1. A 2,000 miles, if the moon is at perigee
  2. B 2,000 miles, if the moon is at apogee
  3. C 5,000 miles, if the moon is at perigee
  4. D 12,000 miles, if the moon is “visible” by both stations
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 12,000 miles, if the moon is “visible” by both stations

Why D is right. D. EME works between any two stations that can both see the moon, which can be nearly half way around the Earth, about 12,000 miles.

Earth-Moon-Earth (EME) signals bounce off the moon. The moon is so far away that it can be above the horizon for two stations on nearly opposite sides of the planet at once. Half of Earth's circumference is about 12,450 miles, so 12,000 miles is the practical limit.

Why not A? 2,000 miles is far short; moon distance barely changes how far apart the two stations can be.

E3A02

What characterizes libration fading of an EME signal?

  1. A A slow change in the pitch of the CW signal
  2. B A fluttery, irregular fading
  3. C A gradual loss of signal as the sun rises
  4. D The returning echo is several hertz lower in frequency than the transmitted signal
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A fluttery, irregular fading

Why B is right. B. The moon rocks slightly, so echoes from many points on its rough surface keep shifting in path length and adding or canceling, which makes a flutter.

This rocking as seen from Earth is called libration. The fading is fast and irregular, unlike a steady Doppler shift.

Why not D? A steady frequency offset is Doppler shift, not libration fading.

E3A03

When scheduling EME contacts, which of these conditions will generally result in the least path loss?

  1. A When the Moon is at perigee
  2. B When the Moon is full
  3. C When the Moon is at apogee
  4. D When the MUF is above 30 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. When the Moon is at perigee

Why A is right. A. At perigee the moon is closest to Earth, so the round trip is shortest and path loss is lowest.

The moon's orbit is an ellipse. Perigee is the closest point and apogee the farthest. Because the signal must go out and back, loss rises fast with distance; perigee gives roughly 2 dB less loss than apogee.

Why not C? Apogee is the farthest point, so loss is highest then.

E3A04

In what direction does an electromagnetic wave travel?

  1. A It depends on the phase angle of the magnetic field
  2. B It travels parallel to the electric and magnetic fields
  3. C It depends on the phase angle of the electric field
  4. D It travels at a right angle to the electric and magnetic fields
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It travels at a right angle to the electric and magnetic fields

Why D is right. D. In a radio wave the electric field, the magnetic field and the direction of travel form three mutually perpendicular directions.

Picture the electric field pointing up, the magnetic field pointing sideways, and the wave moving straight ahead.

Why not B? The wave does not move along either field; it moves across both.

E3A05

How are the component fields of an electromagnetic wave oriented?

  1. A They are parallel
  2. B They are tangential
  3. C They are at right angles
  4. D They are 90 degrees out of phase
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. They are at right angles

Why C is right. C. A radio wave's changing electric field creates a magnetic field at a right angle to it, and the two stay perpendicular as the wave travels.

Both fields are also perpendicular to the direction the wave moves.

Why not D? In a free-space wave, the two fields rise and fall in step; they differ in direction, not phase.

E3A06

What should be done to continue a long-distance contact when the MUF for that path decreases due to darkness?

  1. A Switch to a higher frequency HF band
  2. B Switch to a lower frequency HF band
  3. C Change to an antenna with a higher takeoff angle
  4. D Change to an antenna with greater beam width
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Switch to a lower frequency HF band

Why B is right. B. When the MUF falls at night, move to a lower HF band that the ionosphere can still bend back to Earth.

The MUF (maximum usable frequency) is the highest frequency that will return to Earth on a given path. After dark the ionosphere weakens and the MUF drops. Signals above it pass into space. Moving down in frequency keeps you below the MUF.

Why not A? A higher band would be even further above the MUF.

E3A07

Atmospheric ducts capable of propagating microwave signals often form over what geographic feature?

  1. A Mountain ranges
  2. B Stratocumulus clouds
  3. C Large bodies of water
  4. D Nimbus clouds
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Large bodies of water

Why C is right. C. Water cools and moistens the air right above it, setting up the layer of warm, dry air over cool, moist air that forms a duct.

A duct traps signals between air layers and guides them along the surface. Large lakes and oceans form these layers often.

Why not A? Mountains block or scatter signals; they do not form ducts.

E3A08

When a meteor strikes the Earth’s atmosphere, a linear ionized region is formed at what region of the ionosphere?

  1. A The E region
  2. B The F1 region
  3. C The F2 region
  4. D The D region
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The E region

Why A is right. A. Meteors heat up and leave ionized trails where the air becomes dense enough, about 50 to 75 miles up, which is in the E region.

That trail height is below the F layers. A strong trail can reflect VHF signals for a moment.

Why not D? The D region is lower than where most meteor trails form.

E3A09

Which of the following frequency ranges is most suited for meteor-scatter communications?

  1. A 1.8 MHz - 1.9 MHz
  2. B 10 MHz - 14 MHz
  3. C 28 MHz - 148 MHz
  4. D 220 MHz - 450 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 28 MHz - 148 MHz

Why C is right. C. From 28 to 148 MHz, meteor trails give useful echoes while the regular ionosphere usually does not, and above that range trail echoes become much weaker and shorter.

The 10 meter, 6 meter and 2 meter bands fit this range, with 6 meters often the best.

Why not D? At 220 to 450 MHz, meteor trail echoes are much weaker and shorter.

E3A10

What determines the speed of electromagnetic waves through a medium?

  1. A Resistance and reactance
  2. B Evanescence
  3. C Birefringence
  4. D The index of refraction
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The index of refraction

Why D is right. D. A wave's speed in a medium equals the speed of light in a vacuum divided by the medium's index of refraction.

A higher index means slower travel. Changes in this index are also what bend waves, as in the troposphere.

Why not A? Resistance and reactance describe circuits, not wave speed in a medium.

E3A11

What is a typical range for tropospheric duct propagation of microwave signals?

  1. A 10 miles to 50 miles
  2. B 100 miles to 300 miles
  3. C 1,200 miles
  4. D 2,500 miles
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 100 miles to 300 miles

Why B is right. B. A duct can guide signals well past the horizon but usually spans only a few hundred miles, so 100 to 300 miles is typical.

Rare, very long ducts, such as over the Pacific, go much farther, but those are not typical.

Why not A? 10 to 50 miles is within normal line-of-sight range.

E3A12

What is most likely to result in auroral propagation?

  1. A Meteor showers
  2. B Quiet geomagnetic conditions
  3. C Severe geomagnetic storms
  4. D Extreme low-pressure areas in polar regions
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Severe geomagnetic storms

Why C is right. C. A severe geomagnetic storm drives the aurora that makes auroral propagation possible.

During a storm, charged particles pour into the upper atmosphere near the poles. They ionize the air, creating auroral zones. VHF signals aimed toward the aurora can bounce off those zones.

Why not B? Quiet magnetic conditions produce little aurora.

E3A13

Which of these emission modes is best for auroral propagation?

  1. A CW
  2. B SSB
  3. C FM
  4. D RTTY
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. CW

Why A is right. A. CW works best via aurora because it stays readable even when the signal is badly distorted.

The aurora moves fast, so reflected signals pick up a rough, buzzing sound. Voice becomes hard to understand. A CW tone, even when rough, can still be read as on and off.

Why not C? FM voice is badly distorted by auroral flutter.

E3A14

What are circularly polarized electromagnetic waves?

  1. A Waves with an electric field bent into a circular shape
  2. B Waves with rotating electric and magnetic fields
  3. C Waves that circle Earth
  4. D Waves produced by a loop antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Waves with rotating electric and magnetic fields

Why B is right. B. In circular polarization the field direction turns one full revolution each cycle, so the fields rotate as the wave moves.

In a linear wave, the electric field stays in one plane. Satellites often use circular waves to avoid fading.

Why not D? A loop antenna can be linearly polarized; circular describes how the field turns.
