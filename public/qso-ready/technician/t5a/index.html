T5A: Current and voltage: terminology and units, conductors and insulators, alternating and direct current

Group T5A of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T5A01

Electrical current is measured in which of the following units?

  1. A Volts
  2. B Watts
  3. C Ohms
  4. D Amperes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Amperes

Why D is right. D. Amperes.

Current is how much electric charge moves past a point each second. Its unit is the ampere, often shortened to amp. One ampere is a lot of charge passing every second.

Why not A? Volts measure electrical pressure, the push, not the flow itself.

T5A02

Electrical power is measured in which of the following units?

  1. A Volts
  2. B Watts
  3. C Watt-hours
  4. D Amperes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Watts

Why B is right. B. Watts.

Power is how fast energy is used or delivered, and it is measured in watts. A 100-watt light uses energy twice as fast as a 50-watt light.

Why not C? Watt-hours measure total energy used over time, not the rate.

T5A03

What is the term for the flow of electrons in an electric circuit?

  1. A Voltage
  2. B Amperes per second
  3. C Volts per second
  4. D Current
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Current

Why D is right. D. Current.

When electrons move through a wire, that movement is called current. Voltage is the push that causes it, and current is the resulting flow. Current is measured in amperes.

Why not A? Voltage is the push that drives the flow, not the flow itself.

T5A04

What term describes the number of times per second that an alternating current makes a complete cycle?

  1. A Pulse rate
  2. B Wave number
  3. C Frequency
  4. D Wavelength
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Frequency

Why C is right. C. Frequency.

Alternating current swings one way, then the other, and back again. One full swing is a cycle. The number of cycles in one second is the frequency, measured in hertz.

Why not D? Wavelength is the physical distance one cycle covers in space, not a count per second.

T5A05

A difference in which of the following causes electron flow?

  1. A Voltage
  2. B Ampere-hours
  3. C Capacitance
  4. D Inductance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Voltage

Why A is right. A. Voltage.

Electrons move when there is a difference in electrical pressure between two points, like water flowing from high to low. That difference is voltage. With no voltage difference, there is no steady flow.

Why not C? Capacitance is the ability to store charge, not the cause of electron flow.

T5A06

What is the unit of frequency?

  1. A Hertz
  2. B Henry
  3. C Farad
  4. D Epicycles per second
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Hertz

Why A is right. A. Hertz.

Frequency counts cycles each second, and one cycle per second is one hertz. Radio frequencies are so high that we usually talk in kilohertz, megahertz or gigahertz.

Why not B? The henry is the unit of inductance.

T5A07

Why are metals generally good conductors of electricity?

  1. A They have relatively high density
  2. B They have many free electrons
  3. C They have many free protons
  4. D They have relatively high Young’s modulus values
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. They have many free electrons

Why B is right. B. They have many free electrons.

In a metal, some electrons are loosely held and can drift from atom to atom. When a voltage is applied, these free electrons move easily, which is what makes a good conductor.

Why not C? Protons are locked in the nucleus; current in metal is carried by electrons.

T5A08

Which of the following is a good electrical insulator?

  1. A Sea water
  2. B Glass
  3. C Stainless steel
  4. D Graphite
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Glass

Why B is right. B. Glass.

An insulator has very few free electrons, so current can hardly flow through it. Glass holds its electrons tightly, so it blocks current well and is used on antenna and power line insulators.

Why not A? Sea water contains dissolved salt, which lets current flow.

T5A09

Which of the following describes alternating current?

  1. A Current that alternates between a positive direction and zero
  2. B Current that alternates between a negative direction and zero
  3. C Current that alternates between positive and negative directions
  4. D All these answers are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Current that alternates between positive and negative directions

Why C is right. C. Current that alternates between positive and negative directions.

Alternating current flows one way, slows to zero, then reverses and flows the other way, over and over. The reversal is the key feature. Current that only rises and falls in one direction is still direct current.

Why not A? Current that only goes positive and back to zero never reverses, so it is not alternating.

T5A10

Which term describes the rate at which electrical energy is used?

  1. A Resistance
  2. B Current
  3. C Power
  4. D Voltage
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Power

Why C is right. C. Power.

Power is the rate at which electrical energy is used, measured in watts. Energy is the total amount; power tells how fast that amount is being spent.

Why not B? Current is the flow of charge, not the rate of using energy.

T5A11

What type of current flow is opposed by resistance?

  1. A Direct current
  2. B Alternating current
  3. C RF current
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

Resistance opposes the flow of every kind of current. Direct current, alternating current and RF current are all moving electrons, and a resistor slows each of them and turns some energy into heat.
