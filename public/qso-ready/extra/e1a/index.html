E1A: Frequency privileges; signal frequency range; automatic message forwarding; stations aboard ships or aircraft; power restriction on 630- and 2200-meter bands

Group E1A of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E1A01

Why is it not legal to transmit a 3 kHz bandwidth USB signal with a carrier frequency of 14.348 MHz?

  1. A USB is not used on 20-meter phone
  2. B The lower 1 kHz of the signal is outside the 20-meter band
  3. C 14.348 MHz is outside the 20-meter band
  4. D The upper 1 kHz of the signal is outside the 20-meter band
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The upper 1 kHz of the signal is outside the 20-meter band

Why D is right. D. An upper sideband signal spreads upward from its carrier, so a 3 kHz wide signal at 14.348 MHz reaches 14.351 MHz, 1 kHz past the 14.350 MHz band edge.

Your display shows the carrier frequency, but the voice energy of USB sits above it. The whole signal, not only the carrier, must stay inside the band. On 20 meters the top edge is 14.350 MHz, so a 3 kHz USB signal needs a carrier no higher than 14.347 MHz.

top of signal = carrier + bandwidth
14.348 MHz + 0.003 MHz = 14.351 MHz
14.351 MHz - 14.350 MHz = 1 kHz over
= 1 kHz

“Emissions resulting from modulation must be confined to the band or segment available to the control operator.”

47 CFR 97.307(b)

Why not B? On USB nothing extends below the carrier, so the lower part is not the problem.

E1A02

When using a transceiver that displays the carrier frequency of phone signals, which of the following displayed frequencies represents the lowest frequency at which a properly adjusted LSB emission will be totally within the band?

  1. A The exact lower band edge
  2. B 300 Hz above the lower band edge
  3. C 1 kHz above the lower band edge
  4. D 3 kHz above the lower band edge
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 3 kHz above the lower band edge

Why D is right. D. A lower sideband voice signal spreads about 3 kHz below its carrier, so the carrier must be at least 3 kHz above the lower edge.

With LSB, the displayed frequency is the top of your signal and the voice energy sits below it. If the display reads exactly the band edge, almost the whole signal is outside the band. Setting the carrier 3 kHz up keeps every part of a normal voice signal inside.

lowest carrier = lower edge + signal width below the carrier
LSB voice extends about 3 kHz below the carrier
carrier >= lower edge + 3 kHz
= 3 kHz

“Emissions resulting from modulation must be confined to the band or segment available to the control operator.”

47 CFR 97.307(b)

Why not A? At the exact edge, nearly all of an LSB signal lies below the band.

E1A03

What is the highest legal carrier frequency on the 20-meter band for transmitting a 2.8 kHz wide USB data signal?

  1. A 14.0708 MHz
  2. B 14.1002 MHz
  3. C 14.1472 MHz
  4. D 14.3490 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 14.1472 MHz

Why C is right. C. Data on 20 meters must stay at or below 14.150 MHz, and a USB signal spreads 2.8 kHz above its carrier, so the highest carrier is 14.1472 MHz.

The 20 meter RTTY and data segment runs from 14.00 to 14.15 MHz. With USB, the signal sits above the carrier. Subtract the signal width from the top of the segment to find the highest carrier that keeps the whole signal inside.

highest carrier = segment top - bandwidth
14.150 MHz - 0.0028 MHz = 14.1472 MHz
= 14.1472 MHz

“20 m | 14.00-14.15 MHz | RTTY, data | (f)(3)”

47 CFR 97.305(c)

Why not D? 14.3490 MHz is in the phone segment, where data is not authorized.

E1A04

May an Extra class operator answer the CQ of a station on 3.601 MHz LSB phone?

  1. A Yes, the entire signal will be inside the SSB allocation for Extra class operators
  2. B Yes, the displayed frequency is within the 75-meter phone band segment
  3. C No, the sideband components will extend beyond the edge of the phone band segment
  4. D No, US stations are not permitted to use phone emissions below 3.610 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. No, the sideband components will extend beyond the edge of the phone band segment

Why C is right. C. On LSB the signal reaches about 3 kHz below 3.601 MHz, down to about 3.598 MHz, which is below the 3.600 MHz start of the phone segment.

For an Extra class operator, phone on 75 meters begins at 3.600 MHz. Being able to transmit on the displayed frequency is not enough; the whole sideband must fit. To answer an LSB station near the edge, its carrier would need to be at least 3.603 MHz.

“75 m | 3.600-3.800 | 3.600-4.000 | 3.600-3.900 | (a)”

47 CFR 97.301(b)

Why not B? The display shows the carrier, but the rest of an LSB signal sits below it.

E1A05

Who must be in physical control of the station apparatus of an amateur station aboard any vessel or craft that is documented or registered in the United States?

  1. A Only a person with an FCC Marine Radio license grant
  2. B Only a person named in an amateur station license grant
  3. C Any person holding an FCC issued amateur license or who is authorized for alien reciprocal operation
  4. D Any person named in an amateur station license grant or a person holding an unrestricted Radiotelephone Operator Permit
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Any person holding an FCC issued amateur license or who is authorized for alien reciprocal operation

Why C is right. C. The rule allows a person named in an amateur license grant, or a person authorized for alien reciprocal operation, to be in control.

Amateur operation aboard a US-registered vessel or craft falls under FCC amateur rules. So the same people who may control a station on land may control it there: anyone with an FCC amateur license, or a foreign amateur allowed to operate in the US by a reciprocal arrangement.

“under the physical control of a person named in an amateur station license grant on the ULS consolidated license database or a person authorized for alien reciprocal operation”

47 CFR 97.5(a)

Why not D? A commercial radiotelephone permit does not authorize amateur operation.

E1A06

What is the required transmit frequency of a CW signal for channelized 60 meter operation?

  1. A At the lowest frequency of the channel
  2. B At the center frequency of the channel
  3. C At the highest frequency of the channel
  4. D On any frequency where the signal’s sidebands are within the channel
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. At the center frequency of the channel

Why B is right. B. For CW on a 60 meter channel, the rule sets the carrier on the channel's center frequency.

Phone and data stations may set their carrier 1.5 kHz below center so a USB signal is centered in the channel. CW is narrow and needs no offset, so it goes right on center. Since February 2026 this rule sits in 97.303(h)(3), which also opens a 5351.5 to 5366.5 kHz segment; the CW channel rule did not change.

“For CW emissions (emission designator 150HA1A), the carrier frequency is set to the center frequency.”

47 CFR 97.303(h)(3)

Why not D? CW on a channel must be on center, not anywhere inside it.

E1A07

What is the maximum power permitted on the 2200-meter band?

  1. A 50 watts PEP (peak envelope power)
  2. B 100 watts PEP (peak envelope power)
  3. C 1 watt EIRP (equivalent isotropic radiated power)
  4. D 5 watts EIRP (equivalent isotropic radiated power)
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 1 watt EIRP (equivalent isotropic radiated power)

Why C is right. C. The 2200 meter rule caps the power leaving the antenna at 1 W EIRP, even though it lets the transmitter itself run up to 1.5 kW PEP.

EIRP (equivalent isotropic radiated power) compares what your antenna radiates with an ideal antenna that radiates equally in all directions. At 136 kHz practical antennas are very inefficient, so a big transmitter still radiates very little. The radiated limit is the one that counts.

“No station may transmit in the 135.7-137.8 kHz (2200 m) band with a transmitter power exceeding 1.5 kW PEP or a radiated power exceeding 1 W EIRP.”

47 CFR 97.313(k)

Why not D? 5 watts EIRP is the 630 meter limit.

E1A08

If a station in a message forwarding system inadvertently forwards a message that is in violation of FCC rules, who is primarily accountable for the rules violation?

  1. A The control operator of the packet bulletin board station
  2. B The control operator of the originating station
  3. C The control operators of all the stations in the system
  4. D The control operators of all the stations in the system not authenticating the source from which they accept communications
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The control operator of the originating station

Why B is right. B. Forwarding stations cannot screen every message they relay, so the rule puts primary accountability on the control operator of the station that originated it.

In a message forwarding system, messages pass automatically through many stations. Forwarding stations are not held accountable for violations they relay without knowing, but they must stop passing a bad message once they learn of it.

“the control operator of the station originating a message is primarily accountable for any violation of the rules in this part contained in the message”

47 CFR 97.219(b)

Why not A? The bulletin board station only relays; the originator is primarily accountable.

E1A09

Except in some parts of Alaska, what is the maximum power permitted on the 630-meter band?

  1. A 50 watts PEP (peak envelope power)
  2. B 100 watts PEP (peak envelope power)
  3. C 1 watt EIRP (equivalent isotropic radiated power)
  4. D 5 watts EIRP (equivalent isotropic radiated power)
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 5 watts EIRP (equivalent isotropic radiated power)

Why D is right. D. The 630 meter rule limits radiated power to 5 W EIRP everywhere except the part of Alaska near Russia, where the cap is 1 W EIRP.

EIRP compares your radiated power with an ideal antenna that radiates equally in all directions. The rule also caps transmitter output at 500 W PEP. The lower Alaska limit applies within 800 km of the Russian Federation.

“No station may transmit in the 472-479 kHz (630 m) band with a transmitter power exceeding 500 W PEP or a radiated power exceeding 5 W EIRP”

47 CFR 97.313(l)

Why not C? 1 watt EIRP is the limit only in that part of Alaska, and on 2200 meters.

E1A10

If an amateur station is installed aboard a ship or aircraft, what condition must be met before the station is operated?

  1. A Its operation must be approved by the master of the ship or the pilot in command of the aircraft
  2. B The amateur station operator must agree not to transmit when the main radio of the ship or aircraft is in use
  3. C The amateur station must have a power supply that is completely independent of the main ship or aircraft power supply
  4. D The amateur station must operate only in specific segments of the amateur service HF and VHF bands
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Its operation must be approved by the master of the ship or the pilot in command of the aircraft

Why A is right. A. The master or pilot in command is responsible for the safety of the craft, so the rule requires that person's approval of the station's installation and operation.

The amateur station must also be separate from and independent of the craft's other radio equipment, and it must not interfere with that equipment.

“The installation and operation of an amateur station on a ship or aircraft must be approved by the master of the ship or pilot in command of the aircraft.”

47 CFR 97.11(a)

Why not C? The rule requires separate radio apparatus, not a separate power supply.

E1A11

What licensing is required when operating an amateur station aboard a US-registered vessel in international waters?

  1. A Any amateur license with an FCC Marine or Aircraft endorsement
  2. B Any FCC-issued amateur license
  3. C Only General class or higher amateur licenses
  4. D An unrestricted Radiotelephone Operator Permit
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Any FCC-issued amateur license

Why B is right. B. Amateur rules cover any vessel documented or registered in the US, wherever it sails, so the license you already hold works there.

You operate with the privileges of your own license class. No special endorsement or commercial permit is needed.

“Within 50 km of the Earth's surface and aboard any vessel or craft that is documented or registered in the United States”

47 CFR 97.5(a)(2)

Why not C? No minimum class is required; each class uses its own privileges.
