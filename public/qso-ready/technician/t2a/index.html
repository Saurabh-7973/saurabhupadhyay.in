T2A: Station operation: choosing an operating frequency, calling another station, test transmissions; Band plans: calling frequencies, repeater offsets

Group T2A of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T2A01

What is a common repeater frequency offset in the 2-meter band?

  1. A Plus or minus 5 MHz
  2. B Plus or minus 600 kHz
  3. C Plus or minus 500 kHz
  4. D Plus or minus 1 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Plus or minus 600 kHz

Why B is right. B. On 2 meters, repeaters usually listen and transmit 600 kHz apart.

A repeater hears you on one frequency and retransmits on another. On the 2-meter band the usual gap is 600 kHz, either above or below the output. A 146.940 MHz repeater, for example, would listen on 146.340 MHz. This is a voluntary band-plan convention, not an FCC rule.

Why not A? A 5 MHz split is the usual gap on the 70-centimeter band, not 2 meters.

Remember: Higher band, bigger gap: 2 meters uses 600 kHz, 70 centimeters uses 5 MHz.

T2A02

What is the national calling frequency for FM simplex operations in the 2-meter band?

  1. A 146.520 MHz
  2. B 145.000 MHz
  3. C 432.100 MHz
  4. D 446.000 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 146.520 MHz

Why A is right. A. 146.520 MHz is the national FM simplex calling frequency on 2 meters.

A calling frequency is a shared meeting spot. You make a call there, and once you reach someone you both move to another simplex frequency so the channel stays open for others. This is a voluntary band-plan convention, not an FCC rule.

Why not C? 432.100 MHz is in the 70-centimeter band, not the 2-meter band.

T2A03

What is a common repeater frequency offset in the 70-centimeter band?

  1. A Plus or minus 5 MHz
  2. B Plus or minus 600 kHz
  3. C Plus or minus 500 kHz
  4. D Plus or minus 1 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Plus or minus 5 MHz

Why A is right. A. On 70 centimeters, repeaters usually listen and transmit 5 MHz apart.

The 70-centimeter band is much wider than 2 meters, so its repeaters use a larger split between input and output. The common gap is 5 MHz, above or below the output frequency. This is a voluntary band-plan convention, not an FCC rule.

Why not B? 600 kHz is the usual gap on the 2-meter band.

T2A04

What is an appropriate way to call another station on a repeater if you know the other station's call sign?

  1. A Say "break, break," then the other station’s call sign, followed by your call sign
  2. B Say the station's call sign, then identify with your call sign
  3. C Say "CQ" three times, then the other station's call sign, followed by your call sign
  4. D Wait for the station to call CQ, then answer with your call sign
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Say the station's call sign, then identify with your call sign

Why B is right. B. Their call sign first gets that station's attention, and your call sign next tells them who is calling, with no extra words to tie up the repeater.

No extra words are needed on a repeater. Station identification is still required every 10 minutes and at the end of the contact.

Why not A? "Break, break" signals an urgent interruption, so it would alarm listeners for a routine call.

T2A05

How should you respond to a station calling CQ?

  1. A Transmit "CQ" followed by the other station’s call sign
  2. B Transmit your call sign followed by the other station’s call sign
  3. C Transmit the other station’s call sign followed by your call sign
  4. D Transmit a signal report followed by your call sign
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Transmit the other station’s call sign followed by your call sign

Why C is right. C. Send their call sign first, then yours.

The station calling CQ is listening for its own call sign. Hearing it first tells that operator you are answering them. Your own call sign then tells them who you are.

Why not A? Sending CQ would be a new general call, not an answer to theirs.

Remember: Answer like a letter: address the envelope to them, then sign your name.

T2A06

What is an effective way to seek a call from any phone station when not using a repeater?

  1. A Sign your call sign once, followed by the words “listening for a call;” if there is no answer, change the frequency and repeat
  2. B Say “QTC,” followed by “this is” and your call sign; if there is no answer, change the frequency and repeat
  3. C Transmit an unmodulated carrier for approximately 10 seconds, followed by “this is” and your call sign, and pause to listen; repeat as necessary
  4. D Repeat “CQ” a few times, followed by “this is,” and your call sign, then pause to listen; repeat as necessary
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Repeat “CQ” a few times, followed by “this is,” and your call sign, then pause to listen; repeat as necessary

Why D is right. D. Say "CQ" a few times, then "this is" and your call sign, then listen.

CQ means you would like to talk with any station. Repeating it a few times gives tuning listeners a chance to find you. Pausing to listen lets them reply. If no one answers, you can call again.

Why not C? An unmodulated carrier carries no message and only blocks the frequency.

T2A07

What does the term "repeater offset" mean?

  1. A The difference between a repeater’s transmit and receive frequencies
  2. B The repeater has a time delay to prevent interference
  3. C The approximately half-second delay to allow for the “squelch tail” to be removed
  4. D The fee charged by the repeater owner or club to provide for maintenance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The difference between a repeater’s transmit and receive frequencies

Why A is right. A. Repeater offset is the gap between a repeater's transmit and receive frequencies.

A repeater cannot listen and talk on the same frequency at the same moment without hearing itself. So it receives on one frequency and transmits on another. The difference between those two is the offset, which you set in your radio.

Why not D? Offset is a frequency difference, not money.

T2A08

What is the meaning of the procedural signal “CQ”?

  1. A A shortened form of the term “Contest QSO”
  2. B “Communication Quality,” used to indicate transmitted audio clarity
  3. C Only the called station should transmit
  4. D Calling any station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Calling any station

Why D is right. D. CQ means you are calling any station.

CQ is a general call. It invites anyone who hears you to answer. It is used on voice, Morse code and digital modes alike.

Why not C? CQ does the opposite: it invites any station to reply, not one station only.

T2A09

Which of the following is a customary way to indicate a station is listening on a repeater and looking for a contact?

  1. A “CQ CQ” followed by the repeater’s call sign
  2. B The station’s call sign followed by the word “listening”
  3. C The repeater’s call sign followed by the station’s call sign
  4. D “QSY” followed by your call sign
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The station’s call sign followed by the word “listening”

Why B is right. B. Give your call sign followed by the word "listening.".

Repeaters are quiet, local and shared. A short note with your call sign and "listening" tells others you are free to talk. It is quicker than a long CQ and ties up the repeater less.

Why not D? QSY means changing frequency, not waiting for a contact.

T2A10

What is a band plan, beyond the privileges established by the FCC?

  1. A A voluntary guideline for using different modes or activities within an amateur band
  2. B A list of operating schedules
  3. C A list of available net frequencies
  4. D A plan devised by a club to indicate frequency band usage
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A voluntary guideline for using different modes or activities within an amateur band

Why A is right. A. A band plan is a voluntary guide for which modes and activities go where in a band.

The FCC sets which frequencies and modes each license class may use. Within those limits, amateurs agree on where to put things such as Morse code, voice, digital modes and repeaters. Following the plan cuts down on interference, but the plan itself is not an FCC rule.

Why not D? A band plan is a wide, shared agreement, not one club's local plan.

T2A11

What term describes an amateur station that is transmitting and receiving on the same frequency?

  1. A Full duplex
  2. B Diplex
  3. C Simplex
  4. D Multiplex
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Simplex

Why C is right. C. Simplex means sending and receiving on the same frequency.

In simplex, both stations use one frequency and take turns talking. No repeater is involved. Full duplex, in contrast, means sending and receiving at the same time, usually on two frequencies.

Why not A? Full duplex means sending and receiving at the same time, which needs separate frequencies.
