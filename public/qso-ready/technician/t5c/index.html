T5C: Capacitance and inductance terminology and units; Radio frequency definition and units; Impedance definition and units; Calculating power

Group T5C of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T5C01

What describes the ability to store energy in an electric field?

  1. A Inductance
  2. B Resistance
  3. C Frequency
  4. D Capacitance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Capacitance

Why D is right. D. Capacitance.

A capacitor is two conductors separated by an insulator. When voltage is applied, charge builds up on the plates and energy is held in the electric field between them. That ability is capacitance.

Why not A? Inductance stores energy in a magnetic field, not an electric field.

T5C02

What is the unit of capacitance?

  1. A Farad
  2. B Ohm
  3. C Volt
  4. D Henry
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Farad

Why A is right. A. Farad.

Capacitance is measured in farads. One farad is very large, so radio parts are usually rated in microfarads or picofarads.

Why not D? The henry is the unit of inductance.

T5C03

What describes the ability to store energy in a magnetic field?

  1. A Admittance
  2. B Capacitance
  3. C Resistance
  4. D Inductance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Inductance

Why D is right. D. Inductance.

Current through a coil of wire creates a magnetic field around it, and energy is held in that field. The coil's ability to store energy this way is called inductance.

Why not B? Capacitance stores energy in an electric field, not a magnetic one.

T5C04

What is the unit of inductance?

  1. A Coulomb
  2. B Farad
  3. C Henry
  4. D Ohm
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Henry

Why C is right. C. Henry.

Inductance is measured in henrys. Coils in radio circuits are often rated in millihenrys or microhenrys.

Why not B? The farad is the unit of capacitance.

T5C05

What is the unit of impedance?

  1. A Volt
  2. B Ampere
  3. C Coulomb
  4. D Ohm
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Ohm

Why D is right. D. Ohm.

Impedance is the total opposition to alternating current, combining resistance and reactance (the opposition from capacitors and coils that changes with frequency). Since it opposes current the way resistance does, it uses the same unit, the ohm.

Why not A? The volt measures electrical pressure, not opposition to current.

T5C06

What is the abbreviation for kilohertz?

  1. A kHz
  2. B khz
  3. C KHz
  4. D KHZ
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. kHz

Why A is right. A. kHz.

The metric prefix for kilo is a lowercase k. Hertz is named after a person, so its symbol is a capital H followed by a lowercase z. Put together, that is kHz.

Why not C? A capital K is not the metric symbol for kilo.

T5C07

What is the abbreviation for megahertz?

  1. A mHz
  2. B mHZ
  3. C Mhz
  4. D MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. MHz

Why D is right. D. MHz.

Mega uses a capital M, because a lowercase m means milli, one thousandth. Hertz is written Hz, with a capital H and lowercase z. So megahertz is MHz.

Why not A? mHz with a lowercase m would mean millihertz, a billion times smaller than megahertz.

T5C08

What formula is used to calculate electrical power (P) in a DC circuit?

  1. A P = I x E
  2. B P = E / I
  3. C P = I² x E
  4. D P = I / E
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. P = I x E

Why A is right. A. P = I x E.

Power is current times voltage. Voltage tells how much energy each bit of charge carries, and current tells how much charge flows each second. Multiply them and you get energy per second, which is power in watts.

Why not B? Dividing voltage by current gives resistance, not power.

T5C09

How much power is delivered by a voltage of 13.8 volts DC and a current of 10 amperes?

  1. A 138 watts
  2. B 0.7 watts
  3. C 23.8 watts
  4. D 3.8 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 138 watts

Why A is right. A. 138 watts.

Multiply voltage by current to find power in a DC circuit.

P = E × I
P = 13.8 V × 10 A = 138 W
= 138 watts

Why not C? 23.8 watts adds the numbers instead of multiplying them.

T5C10

How much power is delivered by a voltage of 12 volts DC and a current of 2.5 amperes?

  1. A 4.8 watts
  2. B 30 watts
  3. C 14.5 watts
  4. D 0.208 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Multiply voltage by current to find power in a DC circuit.

P = E × I
P = 12 V × 2.5 A = 30 W
= 30 watts

T5C11

How much current is required to deliver 120 watts at a voltage of 12 volts DC?

  1. A 0.1 amperes
  2. B 10 amperes
  3. C 12 amperes
  4. D 132 amperes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 10 amperes

Why B is right. B. 10 amperes.

Power equals voltage times current, so current equals power divided by voltage.

I = P / E
I = 120 W / 12 V = 10 A
= 10 amperes

Why not D? 132 amperes adds the numbers instead of dividing.

T5C12

What is impedance?

  1. A The opposition to AC current flow
  2. B The inverse of resistance
  3. C The inverse of reactance
  4. D The power handling capability of a component
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The opposition to AC current flow

Why A is right. A. The opposition to AC current flow.

Impedance is the total opposition a circuit gives to alternating current. It includes plain resistance plus reactance, the extra opposition from capacitors and inductors that changes with frequency. It is measured in ohms.

Why not B? The inverse of resistance is conductance, not impedance.
