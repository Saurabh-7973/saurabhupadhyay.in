G1B: Antenna structure limitations; good engineering and good amateur practice; beacon operation; prohibited transmissions; retransmitting radio signals

Group G1B of the General pool (2023–2027) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G1B01

What is the maximum height above ground for an antenna structure not near a public use airport without requiring notification to the FAA and registration with the FCC?

  1. A 50 feet
  2. B 100 feet
  3. C 200 feet
  4. D 250 feet
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 200 feet

Why C is right. C. The FAA notice and FCC registration rule starts above 60.96 meters, which is 200 feet, for sites away from public-use airports.

Tall structures can be a hazard to aircraft. Part 97 points antenna owners to the FCC's antenna structure rules when a structure is more than 200 feet above ground, or is near a public-use airport. Near an airport, a much shorter structure can need notice, so the 200-foot figure applies only away from airports.

“Owners of certain antenna structures more than 60.96 meters (200 feet) above ground level at the site or located near or at a public use airport must notify the Federal Aviation Administration”

47 CFR 97.15(a)

Why not B? 100 feet is below the height where the rule begins away from airports.

G1B02

With which of the following conditions must beacon stations comply?

  1. A No more than one beacon station may transmit in the same band from the same station location
  2. B The frequency must be coordinated with the National Beacon Organization
  3. C The frequency must be posted on the internet or published in a national periodical
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. No more than one beacon station may transmit in the same band from the same station location

Why A is right. A. Part 97 limits a beacon to one channel per band at a time from any one station location.

A beacon is a station that sends a steady signal so others can check propagation. To save band space, the rules let a station location run only one beacon channel in each band at once. The other choices describe a coordinating group and a publishing rule that Part 97 does not contain.

“A beacon must not concurrently transmit on more than 1 channel in the same amateur service frequency band, from the same station location.”

47 CFR 97.203(b)

Why not B? Part 97 names no national group that must coordinate beacon frequencies.

G1B03

Which of the following is a purpose of a beacon station as identified in the FCC rules?

  1. A Observation of propagation and reception
  2. B Automatic identification of repeaters
  3. C Transmission of bulletins of general interest to amateur radio licensees
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Observation of propagation and reception

Why A is right. A. The Part 97 definition says a beacon's job is to let operators watch how signals travel and how well they are received.

Listening to a beacon of known location and power tells you whether a band is open to that area right now. That is why the rules define a beacon by its purpose: observing propagation and reception, or related experiments. Repeater IDs and news bulletins are other kinds of transmissions.

“Beacon. An amateur station transmitting communications for the purposes of observation of propagation and reception or other related experimental activities.”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(9)

Why not C? Bulletins are a separate kind of one-way transmission, not a beacon's purpose.

G1B04

Which of the following transmissions is permitted for all amateur stations?

  1. A Unidentified transmissions of less than 10 seconds duration for test purposes only
  2. B Automatic retransmission of other amateur signals by any amateur station
  3. C Occasional retransmission of weather and propagation forecast information from US government stations
  4. D Encrypted messages, if not intended to facilitate a criminal act
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Occasional retransmission of weather and propagation forecast information from US government stations

Why C is right. C. Part 97 makes an exception for US government weather and propagation forecasts, as long as you relay them only now and then.

Amateurs generally may not rebroadcast signals from other kinds of radio stations. One exception is public weather and propagation forecasts from US government stations. Even these may be passed along only occasionally, as part of normal amateur operating, not on a regular schedule.

“except propagation and weather forecast information intended for use by the general public and originated from United States Government stations”

47 CFR 97.113(c)

Why not A? Part 97 bans unidentified signals outright; there is no short-test exception.

G1B05

Which of the following one-way transmissions are permitted?

  1. A Unidentified test transmissions of less than 10 seconds in duration
  2. B Transmissions to assist with learning the International Morse code
  3. C Regular transmissions offering equipment for sale, if intended for amateur radio use
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Transmissions to assist with learning the International Morse code

Why B is right. B. Part 97 lists Morse code practice among the one-way transmissions an amateur station may make.

Most amateur contacts are two-way. Part 97 lists a few one-way transmissions that are allowed, such as brief adjustments, emergency messages, information bulletins, and sending code to help people learn or practice Morse. Selling gear on a regular basis is not on the list, and unidentified signals are never allowed.

“Transmissions necessary to assisting persons learning, or improving proficiency in, the international Morse code”

47 CFR 97.111(b)(5)

Why not C? Regular sales offers are not among the allowed one-way transmissions.

G1B06

Under what conditions are state and local governments permitted to regulate amateur radio antenna structures?

  1. A Under no circumstances, FCC rules take priority
  2. B At any time and to any extent necessary to accomplish a legitimate purpose of the state or local entity, provided that proper filings are made with the FCC
  3. C Only when such structures exceed 50 feet in height and are clearly visible 1,000 feet from the structure
  4. D Amateur Service communications must be reasonably accommodated, and regulations must constitute the minimum practical to accommodate a legitimate purpose of the state or local entity
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Amateur Service communications must be reasonably accommodated, and regulations must constitute the minimum practical to accommodate a legitimate purpose of the state or local entity

Why D is right. D. Local rules must make reasonable room for amateur antennas and use only the least regulation that meets the local goal.

Towns can regulate antennas for reasons like safety, but Part 97, based on the FCC order known as PRB-1, sets limits. A local rule may not block amateur communication. It must reasonably accommodate it and be the minimum practical regulation for the town's legitimate purpose. No height number or FCC filing is involved.

“it must reasonably accommodate such communications and must constitute the minimum practicable regulation to accomplish the state or local authority's legitimate purpose”

47 CFR 97.15(b)

Why not A? Local governments may regulate antennas; they must only use a light hand.

G1B07

What are the restrictions on the use of abbreviations or procedural signals in the amateur service?

  1. A Only “Q” signals are permitted
  2. B They may be used if they do not obscure the meaning of a message
  3. C They are not permitted
  4. D They are limited to those expressly listed in Part 97 of the FCC rules
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. They may be used if they do not obscure the meaning of a message

Why B is right. B. Part 97 bans messages coded to hide their meaning, so abbreviations that everyone understands are fine.

Q signals, prosigns and short forms like 73 are widely known, so they do not hide anything. The rule targets messages encoded for the purpose of obscuring their meaning. Part 97 has no list of approved abbreviations; the test is whether the meaning stays clear.

“messages encoded for the purpose of obscuring their meaning”

47 CFR 97.113(a)(4)

Why not D? Part 97 contains no list of approved abbreviations.

G1B08

When is it permissible to communicate with amateur stations in countries outside the areas administered by the Federal Communications Commission?

  1. A Only when the foreign country has a formal third-party agreement filed with the FCC
  2. B When the contact is with amateurs in any country except those whose administrations have notified the ITU that they object to such communications
  3. C Only when the contact is with amateurs licensed by a country which is a member of the United Nations, or by a territory possessed by such a country
  4. D Only when the contact is with amateurs licensed by a country which is a member of the International Amateur Radio Union, or by a territory possessed by such a country
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. When the contact is with amateurs in any country except those whose administrations have notified the ITU that they object to such communications

Why B is right. B. US amateurs may talk with amateurs in any country unless that country has told the ITU it objects.

Amateur contacts across borders are the normal case, not the exception. The only countries off limits are those whose governments have notified the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) that they object to such contacts. Third-party agreements matter only when you pass messages for people who are not licensed amateurs.

“except those in any country whose administration has notified the ITU that it objects to such communications”

47 CFR 97.111(a)(1)

Why not A? Third-party agreements cover messages for non-amateurs, not ordinary amateur contacts.

G1B09

On what HF frequencies are automatically controlled beacons permitted?

  1. A On any frequency if power is less than 1 watt
  2. B On any frequency if transmissions are in Morse code
  3. C 21.08 MHz to 21.09 MHz
  4. D 28.20 MHz to 28.30 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 28.20 MHz to 28.30 MHz

Why D is right. D. The only HF segment where a beacon may run under automatic control is 28.20 to 28.30 MHz on 10 meters.

Automatic control means the station runs with no operator at the controls. Part 97 lists the segments where beacons may do that. Among the HF bands, only 28.20 to 28.30 MHz appears; the rest of the list is on 6 meters and higher. Elsewhere, a beacon needs a control operator.

“A beacon may be automatically controlled while it is transmitting on the 28.20-28.30 MHz”

47 CFR 97.203(d)

Why not C? 21.08 to 21.09 MHz is not on the list of automatic beacon segments.

G1B10

What is the power limit for beacon stations?

  1. A 10 watts PEP output
  2. B 20 watts PEP output
  3. C 100 watts PEP output
  4. D 200 watts PEP output
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 100 watts PEP output

Why C is right. C. Part 97 caps beacon transmitter power at 100 W.

A beacon runs for long periods, often unattended, so the rules keep its power moderate. The beacon rule sets the limit at 100 W. Higher power would add little for checking propagation and would raise the chance of interference.

“The transmitter power of a beacon must not exceed 100 W.”

47 CFR 97.203(c)

Why not D? 200 W is the 30-meter power limit, not the beacon limit.

G1B11

Who or what determines “good engineering and good amateur practice,” as applied to the operation of an amateur station in all respects not covered by the Part 97 rules?

  1. A The FCC
  2. B The control operator
  3. C The IEEE
  4. D The ITU
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The FCC

Why A is right. A. The standard is part of the FCC's own rules, so the FCC is the one that judges whether an operation met it.

Part 97 cannot cover every situation. Where it is silent, stations must follow good engineering and good amateur practice. Because this is an FCC rule, the FCC decides whether a station fell short, for example when it reviews a complaint. Other groups publish standards, but they do not enforce Part 97.

“In all respects not specifically covered by FCC Rules each amateur station must be operated in accordance with good engineering and good amateur practice.”

47 CFR 97.101(a)

Why not B? A control operator must follow the standard but does not set it.
