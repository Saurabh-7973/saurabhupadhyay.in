E4D: Receiver performance characteristics: dynamic range; intermodulation and cross-modulation interference; third-order intercept; desensitization; preselector; sensitivity; link margin

Group E4D of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 13 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E4D01

What is meant by the blocking dynamic range of a receiver?

  1. A The difference in dB between the noise floor and the level of an incoming signal that will cause 1 dB of gain compression
  2. B The minimum difference in dB between the levels of two FM signals that will cause one signal to block the other
  3. C The difference in dB between the noise floor and the third-order intercept point
  4. D The minimum difference in dB between two signals which produce third-order intermodulation products greater than the noise floor
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The difference in dB between the noise floor and the level of an incoming signal that will cause 1 dB of gain compression

Why A is right. A. Blocking dynamic range runs from the noise floor up to the signal strong enough to cut the receiver's gain by 1 dB.

Gain compression means a strong signal pushes an amplifier toward its limit, so its gain for everything else drops. Weak signals then fade when a strong station nearby transmits. The larger the blocking dynamic range in dB, the stronger the nearby signals the receiver can tolerate.

Why not D? D describes the threshold for intermodulation products, which defines a different measure: two-tone dynamic range.

E4D02

Which of the following describes problems caused by poor dynamic range in a receiver?

  1. A Spurious signals caused by cross modulation and desensitization from strong adjacent signals
  2. B Oscillator instability requiring frequent retuning and loss of ability to recover the opposite sideband
  3. C Poor weak signal reception caused by insufficient local oscillator injection
  4. D Oscillator instability and severe audio distortion of all but the strongest received signals
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Spurious signals caused by cross modulation and desensitization from strong adjacent signals

Why A is right. A. A receiver with poor dynamic range is overwhelmed by strong nearby signals, which cause cross modulation and desensitization.

Dynamic range is the span of signal strengths a receiver handles without distortion. When a strong signal goes beyond that span, nonlinear stages can copy its modulation onto other signals (cross modulation) or reduce the gain for weak signals (desensitization). Both get worse as the strong signal gets stronger and closer.

Why not C? Low oscillator injection is a mixer drive problem, not a dynamic range problem.

E4D03

What creates intermodulation interference between two repeaters in close proximity?

  1. A The output signals cause feedback in the final amplifier of one or both transmitters
  2. B The output signals mix in the final amplifier of one or both transmitters
  3. C The input frequencies are harmonically related
  4. D The output frequencies are harmonically related
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The output signals mix in the final amplifier of one or both transmitters

Why B is right. B. When one repeater's signal gets into the other's transmitter, the two signals mix in that nonlinear final amplifier and create new frequencies.

Antennas close together couple each other's signals back into the final amplifiers. A final amplifier is not perfectly linear, so it acts as a mixer. Products such as 2f1 - f2 can land on a third frequency, perhaps another repeater's input, and get radiated.

Why not D? Mixing happens with any two frequencies; they do not need to be harmonically related.

E4D04

Which of the following is used to reduce or eliminate intermodulation interference in a repeater caused by a nearby transmitter?

  1. A A band-pass filter in the feed line between the transmitter and receiver
  2. B A properly terminated circulator at the output of the repeater’s transmitter
  3. C Utilizing a Class C final amplifier
  4. D Utilizing a Class D final amplifier
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A properly terminated circulator at the output of the repeater’s transmitter

Why B is right. B. A circulator passes the transmitter's signal to the antenna but sends signals coming back from the antenna into a load, so they never reach the final amplifier.

A circulator is a three-port device built around a magnetized ferrite. Signals travel only one way around it: transmitter to antenna, and antenna to the third port. With a matched load on that third port, energy from a nearby transmitter is absorbed instead of mixing in the final amplifier.

Why not C? A Class C amplifier is very nonlinear, so it mixes signals readily.

E4D06

What is the term for the reduction in receiver sensitivity caused by a strong signal near the received frequency?

  1. A Reciprocal mixing
  2. B Quieting
  3. C Desensitization
  4. D Cross modulation interference
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Desensitization

Why C is right. C. Desensitization is the loss of sensitivity when a strong nearby signal pushes the receiver's front end toward overload.

The strong signal may be outside your passband, so you do not hear it. But it drives early stages into compression, and their gain for the weak signal you want drops. The weak station fades or disappears while the strong one transmits.

Why not D? Cross modulation copies the strong signal's modulation onto the wanted one; here the problem is lost sensitivity.

E4D07

Which of the following reduces the likelihood of receiver desensitization?

  1. A Insert attenuation before the first RF stage
  2. B Raise the receiver’s IF frequency
  3. C Increase the receiver’s front-end gain
  4. D Switch from fast AGC to slow AGC
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Insert attenuation before the first RF stage

Why A is right. A. An attenuator ahead of the first RF stage lowers the strong signal so the amplifier no longer overloads.

Desensitization happens when a strong signal drives the first stages near their limit. Adding attenuation, or turning off a preamp, cuts every signal by the same amount, and the strong one drops below the overload point. On busy HF bands the outside noise is usually high enough that weak signals stay readable.

Why not C? More front-end gain makes overload worse, not better.

E4D08

What causes intermodulation in an electronic circuit?

  1. A Negative feedback
  2. B Lack of neutralization
  3. C Nonlinear circuits or devices
  4. D Positive feedback
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Nonlinear circuits or devices

Why C is right. C. Intermodulation is created when two or more signals pass through a nonlinear device, which mixes them and makes new frequencies.

In a linear circuit the output is a scaled copy of the input, and signals pass through without affecting each other. A nonlinear circuit, such as an overdriven amplifier or a diode, bends the waveform. That bending creates sums and differences of the inputs, such as 2f1 - f2, which are intermodulation products.

Why not A? Negative feedback makes an amplifier more linear, so it reduces intermodulation.

E4D09

What is the purpose of the preselector in a communications receiver?

  1. A To store frequencies that are often used
  2. B To provide broadband attenuation before the first RF stage to prevent intermodulation
  3. C To increase the rejection of signals outside the band being received
  4. D To allow selection of the optimum RF amplifier device
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. To increase the rejection of signals outside the band being received

Why C is right. C. A preselector is a tuned filter at the receiver's input that weakens signals outside the band you are receiving.

By passing only the band you want, it keeps strong out-of-band signals from reaching the RF amplifier and mixer. That reduces overload, intermodulation and image responses. Some preselectors follow the tuning dial; others are fixed band filters.

Why not B? Broadband attenuation cuts all signals equally; a preselector is selective.

E4D10

What does a third-order intercept level of 40 dBm mean with respect to receiver performance?

  1. A Signals less than 40 dBm will not generate audible third-order intermodulation products
  2. B The receiver can tolerate signals up to 40 dB above the noise floor without producing third-order intermodulation products
  3. C A pair of 40 dBm input signals will theoretically generate a third-order intermodulation product that has the same output amplitude as either of the input signals
  4. D A pair of 1 mW input signals will produce a third-order intermodulation product that is 40 dB stronger than the input signal
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A pair of 40 dBm input signals will theoretically generate a third-order intermodulation product that has the same output amplitude as either of the input signals

Why C is right. C. The intercept point is the input level where the third-order product would grow to equal the wanted signals; here that is 40 dBm per tone.

When two input tones each rise by 1 dB, the third-order intermodulation products rise by 3 dB. Plot both lines and they eventually meet; that meeting point is the third-order intercept. Real receivers overload before reaching it, so it is a theoretical figure. A higher intercept means better resistance to intermodulation.

Why not A? Third-order products appear far below 40 dBm; the intercept is an extended-line point, not a threshold.

E4D11

Why are odd-order intermodulation products, created within a receiver, of particular interest compared to other products?

  1. A Odd-order products of two signals in the band being received are also likely to be within the band
  2. B Odd-order products are more likely to overload the IF filters
  3. C Odd-order products are an indication of poor image rejection
  4. D Odd-order intermodulation produces three products for every input signal within the band of interest
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Odd-order products of two signals in the band being received are also likely to be within the band

Why A is right. A. Odd-order products such as 2f1 - f2 fall close to the original signals, so they often land inside the band you are receiving.

Take signals at 14.200 and 14.210 MHz. Second-order products land at 0.010 MHz and 28.410 MHz, far away and easy to filter. Third-order products land at 14.190 and 14.220 MHz, right in the same band, where filters cannot remove them. That is why receiver specs focus on odd orders.

Why not D? Each pair of signals gives two nearby third-order products, not three per signal.

E4D12

What is the link margin in a system with a transmit power level of 10 W (+40 dBm), a system antenna gain of 10 dBi, a cable loss of 3 dB, a path loss of 136 dB, a receiver minimum discernable signal of -103 dBm, and a required signal-to-noise ratio of 6 dB?

  1. A -8dB
  2. B -14dB
  3. C +8dB
  4. D +14dB
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. +8dB

Why C is right. C. The signal arrives 8 dB above the level needed for the required signal-to-noise ratio, so the margin is positive.

Link margin is how much stronger the received signal is than the minimum you need. Add the gains to the transmit power and subtract the losses to get the received level. The minimum needed is the MDS plus the required signal-to-noise ratio. A positive margin means the link works with room to spare.

Margin = received level - (MDS + required SNR)
Received = 40 dBm + 10 dB - 3 dB - 136 dB = -89 dBm
Needed = -103 dBm + 6 dB = -97 dBm
Margin = -89 dBm - (-97 dBm) = 8 dB
= +8 dB

Why not A? -8 dB reverses the subtraction; the signal is above what is needed, so the margin is positive.

E4D13

What is the received signal level with a transmit power of 10 W (+40 dBm), a transmit antenna gain of 6 dBi, a receive antenna gain of 3 dBi, and a path loss of 100 dB?

  1. A -51 dBm
  2. B -54 dBm
  3. C -57 dBm
  4. D -60 dBm
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. -51 dBm

Why A is right. A. Adding both antenna gains to +40 dBm and subtracting 100 dB of path loss gives -51 dBm.

Working in decibels turns multiplying into adding. dBm is power compared with 1 milliwatt, so +40 dBm is 10 W. Gains add dB and losses subtract dB.

Received level = transmit power + transmit gain + receive gain - path loss
40 dBm + 6 dB + 3 dB - 100 dB
= -51 dBm
= -51 dBm

Why not B? -54 dBm leaves out the 3 dB receive antenna gain.

E4D14

What power level does a receiver minimum discernible signal of -100 dBm represent?

  1. A 100 microwatts
  2. B 0.1 microwatt
  3. C 0.001 microwatts
  4. D 0.1 picowatts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 0.1 picowatts

Why D is right. D. -100 dBm is 100 dB below 1 milliwatt, which is 10^-13 W, or 0.1 picowatt.

dBm compares power with 1 milliwatt. Each 10 dB is a factor of 10, so -100 dBm means dividing 1 mW by 10 ten times. A picowatt is 10^-12 W, so 10^-13 W is 0.1 picowatt.

P = 1 mW × 10^(dBm / 10)
P = 1 mW × 10^(-100 / 10) = 1 mW × 10^-10
= 10^-13 W = 0.1 picowatts
= 0.1 picowatts

Why not C? 0.001 microwatts is 1 nanowatt, which is -60 dBm.
