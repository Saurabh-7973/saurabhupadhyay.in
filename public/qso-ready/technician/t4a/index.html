T4A: Station setup: connecting a microphone, RF power meter, a power source, a computer, digital equipment, an SWR meter; bonding; Mobile radio installation

Group T4A of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T4A01

Which of the following is an appropriate power supply rating for a typical 50-watt output mobile FM transceiver?

  1. A 24.0 volts at 4 amperes
  2. B 13.8 volts at 4 amperes
  3. C 24.0 volts at 12 amperes
  4. D 13.8 volts at 12 amperes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 13.8 volts at 12 amperes

Why D is right. D. 13.8 volts at 12 amperes.

Mobile radios are built to run from a car's electrical system, which sits near 13.8 volts while the engine charges the battery. A transmitter wastes much of its input power as heat, so a 50-watt mobile radio typically draws around 10 amperes while transmitting. The supply has to deliver at least that much current; 12 amperes covers it.

Why not B? 4 amperes at 13.8 volts is only about 55 watts of input, far less than a 50-watt transmitter draws.

T4A02

Which of the following should be considered when selecting an accessory SWR meter?

  1. A The frequency and power level at which the measurements will be made
  2. B The distance that the meter will be located from the antenna
  3. C The maximum SWR anticipated on the transmission line
  4. D The ability of the meter to compensate for a poor transmission line match to the antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The frequency and power level at which the measurements will be made

Why A is right. A. The frequency and power level at which the measurements will be made.

Every SWR meter is designed for a certain range of frequencies and a maximum power. Outside that range its readings drift, and too much power can damage it. Pick one that covers the bands you use and handles your transmitter's output.

Why not D? A meter only measures the match; it cannot fix or compensate for a poor one.

T4A03

Why are short, heavy-gauge wires used for a transceiver’s DC power connection?

  1. A To minimize voltage drop when transmitting
  2. B To provide a close match to the power supply output impedance
  3. C To avoid RF interference
  4. D To minimize radiative losses in the power cable
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. To minimize voltage drop when transmitting

Why A is right. A. To minimize voltage drop when transmitting.

Every wire has a little resistance, and a voltage drop equals current times resistance. When you transmit, the radio draws many amperes, so even a small resistance steals volts before they reach the radio. Shorter and thicker wire has less resistance, so more of the supply voltage arrives at the radio.

Why not C? The power lead carries DC; its size is chosen for resistance, not RF interference.

Remember: Big current through thin wire means lost volts.

T4A04

How are the audio input and output of a transceiver connected in a station configured to operate using FT8?

  1. A To a computer running a terminal program and connected to a terminal node controller unit
  2. B To the audio output and input of a computer running FT8 software
  3. C To an FT8 conversion unit, a keyboard, and a computer monitor
  4. D To a computer connected to the FT8converter.com website
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. To the audio output and input of a computer running FT8 software

Why B is right. B. To the audio output and input of a computer running FT8 software.

FT8 is made of audio tones. The FT8 software turns your message into tones that go into the radio's audio input to be transmitted. Received audio from the radio goes into the computer, where the software decodes the tones. No extra converter box or website is needed.

Why not A? A terminal program with a TNC is a packet radio setup, not FT8.

T4A05

Where should an RF power meter be installed?

  1. A In the feed line, between the transmitter and antenna
  2. B At the power supply output
  3. C In parallel with the push-to-talk line and the antenna
  4. D In the power supply cable, as close as possible to the radio
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. In the feed line, between the transmitter and antenna

Why A is right. A. In the feed line, between the transmitter and antenna.

An RF power meter measures the radio-frequency energy leaving the transmitter. That energy travels only in the feed line, the cable from the radio to the antenna. So the meter must be placed in that cable, where the RF actually flows.

Why not D? The power supply cable carries DC, not the RF output the meter is built to measure.

T4A06

What signals are used in a computer-radio interface for digital mode operation?

  1. A Receive and transmit mode, status, and location
  2. B Antenna and RF power
  3. C Receive audio, transmit audio, and transmitter keying
  4. D NMEA GPS location and DC power
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Receive audio, transmit audio, and transmitter keying

Why C is right. C. Receive audio, transmit audio, and transmitter keying.

For digital modes, the computer needs three things from the radio link. It hears received audio to decode signals. It sends transmit audio carrying the encoded signal. It also needs a way to key the transmitter, switching it on to send and off to listen.

Why not B? The antenna and RF power stay at the radio; the computer handles only audio and keying.

T4A07

Which of the following is one of the connections required between a computer and a transceiver to operate digital modes?

  1. A Computer “line out” to transceiver push-to-talk
  2. B Computer “line in” to transceiver push-to-talk
  3. C Computer “line in” to transceiver speaker connector
  4. D Computer “line out” to transceiver speaker connector
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Computer “line in” to transceiver speaker connector

Why C is right. C. Computer “line in” to transceiver speaker connector.

The radio's speaker connector carries received audio out of the radio. The computer's line in is an input that accepts audio. Connecting the two lets the software hear and decode incoming signals. Audio flows out of one jack and into the other.

Why not D? Line out and the speaker jack are both outputs, so connecting them sends audio nowhere useful.

Remember: Match an output to an input: speaker out goes to line in.

T4A08

Which of the following conductors is preferred for bonding at RF?

  1. A Copper braid removed from coaxial cable
  2. B Copper-clad steel wire
  3. C Twisted-pair cable
  4. D Flat copper strap
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Flat copper strap

Why D is right. D. Flat copper strap.

At radio frequencies, current flows mostly on the outer surface of a conductor. A flat strap has a lot of surface and low inductance (little opposition to fast-changing current), so RF passes through it easily between pieces of equipment. Bonding ties station gear together so it all sits at the same RF potential.

Why not A? Braid taken from coax tends to corrode and loosen, so its connection degrades over time.

T4A09

How can you determine the length of time that equipment can be powered from a battery?

  1. A Divide the watt-hour rating of the battery by the peak power consumption of the equipment
  2. B Divide the battery ampere-hour rating by the average current draw of the equipment
  3. C Multiply the watts per hour consumed by the equipment by the battery power rating
  4. D Multiply the square of the current rating of the battery by the input resistance of the equipment
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Divide the battery ampere-hour rating by the average current draw of the equipment

Why B is right. B. Divide the battery ampere-hour rating by the average current draw of the equipment.

An ampere-hour rating tells how much current a battery can supply for how long. Dividing it by the current your equipment draws gives hours of run time. Use the average draw, because a radio pulls more when transmitting and less when listening. For example, 20 ampere-hours at an average of 2 amperes gives about 10 hours.

Why not A? Using peak power assumes the radio always draws its maximum, which underestimates run time.

T4A10

What function does a digital mode hotspot perform for nearby transceivers?

  1. A Communication with a digital voice or data network
  2. B FT8 digital communications via AFSK using a smartphone connected to the internet
  3. C RTTY encoding and decoding without a computer
  4. D High-speed digital communications for meteor scatter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Communication with a digital voice or data network

Why A is right. A. Communication with a digital voice or data network.

A hotspot is a small, low-power radio linked to the internet. Your handheld talks to the hotspot over the air, and the hotspot passes your digital voice or data on to a network such as DMR or D-STAR. It gives you network access where no repeater is in range.

Why not C? RTTY coding is not what a hotspot does; it relays digital voice and data to a network.

T4A11

Where should the negative power return of a mobile transceiver be connected in a vehicle?

  1. A At the 12-volt battery chassis ground
  2. B To the shell of the power connector
  3. C To any metal part of the vehicle
  4. D Through the transceiver’s mounting bracket
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. At the 12-volt battery chassis ground

Why A is right. A. At the 12-volt battery chassis ground.

Connecting the negative lead where the battery's negative meets the chassis gives the radio a solid, low-resistance return path. Random metal parts may have paint, rust or loose joints that add resistance and noise. A direct connection at the battery ground avoids those problems.

Why not C? Any metal part may connect poorly to the battery, adding resistance and noise.

T4A12

What is an electronic keyer?

  1. A A device for switching antennas from transmit to receive
  2. B A device for voice activated switching from receive to transmit
  3. C A device that assists in manual sending of Morse code
  4. D An interlock to prevent unauthorized use of a radio
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A device that assists in manual sending of Morse code

Why C is right. C. A device that assists in manual sending of Morse code.

With an electronic keyer, you press a paddle and the keyer makes the dots and dashes for you. Holding one side sends a string of evenly timed dots, the other side sends dashes. You still form each character by hand, but the timing comes out clean and steady.

Why not B? Voice activated switching is VOX, which has nothing to do with Morse code.
