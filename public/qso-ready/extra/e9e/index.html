E9E: Impedance matching: matching antennas to feed lines; phasing lines; power dividers

Group E9E of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 10 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E9E01

Which matching system for Yagi antennas requires the driven element to be insulated from the boom?

  1. A Gamma
  2. B Beta or hairpin
  3. C Shunt-fed
  4. D T-match
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Beta or hairpin

Why B is right. B. A beta or hairpin match feeds a driven element split in the middle, so the two halves must be insulated from the metal boom.

In a hairpin match, the coax connects across a gap at the center of the driven element. A U-shaped wire, the hairpin, bridges that gap and acts as a small inductor. If the element ran unbroken through a metal boom, the boom would short the two halves together. Gamma and T matches tap a solid element instead, so it can be bolted to the boom.

Why not A? A gamma match feeds a continuous element at its grounded center, so the element can touch the boom.

E9E02

What antenna matching system matches coaxial cable to an antenna by connecting the shield to the center of the antenna and the conductor a fraction of a wavelength to one side?

  1. A Gamma match
  2. B Delta match
  3. C T-match
  4. D Stub match
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Gamma match

Why A is right. A. A gamma match bonds the coax shield to the element's center and runs the center conductor to a tap partway along one side.

The center of a half-wave element has zero voltage, so the shield can attach there along with the boom. A rod parallel to one half carries the center conductor out to a tap point. Moving the tap outward raises the impedance it sees, so you can slide it to match 50-ohm coax. A series capacitor tunes out the rod's inductance.

Why not C? A T-match uses two rods, one on each side, and is fed with balanced line, not one coax tap on one side.

E9E03

What matching system uses a short length of transmission line connected in parallel with the feed line at or near the feed point?

  1. A Gamma match
  2. B Delta match
  3. C T-match
  4. D Stub match
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Stub match

Why D is right. D. A stub match places a short section of line across the feed line near the antenna to cancel the mismatch there.

A short or open length of line acts like a capacitor or an inductor, depending on its length. Placed in parallel with the feed line at the right spot, it cancels the reactance there. What remains matches the feed line's impedance. The stub's length and position can be found with a Smith chart.

Why not B? A delta match fans the feed line wires out to tap points on the element; it uses no parallel line section.

E9E04

What is the purpose of the series capacitor in a gamma match?

  1. A To provide DC isolation between the feed line and the antenna
  2. B To cancel unwanted inductive reactance
  3. C To provide a rejection notch that prevents the radiation of harmonics
  4. D To transform the antenna impedance to a higher value
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. To cancel unwanted inductive reactance

Why B is right. B. The gamma rod adds inductive reactance in series with the feed, and the capacitor adds an equal opposite reactance to cancel it.

The gamma rod and the element section beside it form a short piece of line that acts like an inductor. That inductance would leave a mismatch even when the tap is at the right resistance. A series capacitor's reactance has the opposite sign. Adjusting it until the two cancel leaves a purely resistive match.

Why not A? The shield already connects to the element, so the capacitor is not there for DC isolation.

E9E05

What Yagi driven element feed point impedance is required to use a beta or hairpin matching system?

  1. A Capacitive (driven element electrically shorter than 1/2 wavelength)
  2. B Inductive (driven element electrically longer than 1/2 wavelength)
  3. C Purely resistive
  4. D Purely reactive
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Capacitive (driven element electrically shorter than 1/2 wavelength)

Why A is right. A. The hairpin is an inductor across the feed point, so it needs a driven element tuned short, whose feed point looks capacitive.

A Yagi's driven element often has a feed point resistance below 50 ohms. Shortening it a little adds capacitive reactance. The hairpin's inductance, placed in parallel, combines with that capacitance like an L-network. The pair steps the low resistance up toward 50 ohms and cancels the reactance.

Why not B? An inductive element would add to the hairpin's inductance instead of combining with it to match.

E9E06

Which of these transmission line impedances would be suitable for constructing a quarter-wave Q-section for matching a 100-ohm feed point impedance to a 50-ohm transmission line?

  1. A 50 ohms
  2. B 62 ohms
  3. C 75 ohms
  4. D 90 ohms
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 75 ohms

Why C is right. C. A quarter-wave matching line needs an impedance equal to the square root of 100 times 50, about 70.7 ohms, and 75-ohm line is closest.

A quarter-wave section transforms a load: the impedance seen at its input is its own impedance squared, divided by the load. To make 100 ohms look like 50 ohms, solve for the line impedance. The ideal value is not a standard cable, but common 75-ohm coax is close enough to give a low SWR.

Zq = square root of (Zin × Zload)
Zq = square root of (50 × 100) ohms
Zq = square root of 5000 = 70.7 ohms
nearest choice: 75 ohms
= 70.7 ohms

Why not B? 62 ohms is farther from the ideal 70.7 ohms than 75 ohms is.

E9E07

What parameter describes the interaction of a load and transmission line?

  1. A Characteristic impedance
  2. B Reflection coefficient
  3. C Velocity factor
  4. D Dielectric constant
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Reflection coefficient

Why B is right. B. The reflection coefficient tells how much of the wave on a line bounces back from the load, which depends on both of them.

When a load's impedance differs from the line's characteristic impedance, part of the wave reflects. The reflection coefficient gives the size and phase of that reflection. Zero means a perfect match; 1 means everything reflects, as with an open or short. SWR can be calculated from it.

Why not A? Characteristic impedance belongs to the line alone; it does not involve the load.

E9E08

What is a use for a Wilkinson divider?

  1. A To divide the operating frequency of a transmitter signal so it can be used on a lower frequency band
  2. B To feed high-impedance antennas from a low-impedance source
  3. C To divide power equally between two 50-ohm loads while maintaining 50-ohm input impedance
  4. D To divide the frequency of the input to a counter to increase its frequency range
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. To divide power equally between two 50-ohm loads while maintaining 50-ohm input impedance

Why C is right. C. Each quarter-wave arm turns a 50-ohm load into 100 ohms, and two 100-ohm arms in parallel give 50 ohms, so power splits evenly with a matched input.

A common form uses two quarter-wave line sections, one to each output, plus a resistor joining the outputs. The resistor carries no current when both loads match, but absorbs power if one output is mismatched, which keeps the outputs isolated. Hams use it to feed two antennas in a stack or array.

Why not A? A Wilkinson divider splits power, not frequency; frequency division uses a different circuit.

E9E09

Which of the following is used to shunt feed a grounded tower at its base?

  1. A Double-bazooka match
  2. B Beta or hairpin match
  3. C Gamma match
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Gamma match

Why C is right. C. A gamma match taps the tower partway up while its base stays grounded, so it can shunt-feed a grounded tower.

A tower bolted to ground has near-zero impedance at its base, so you cannot feed it there directly. A wire running parallel to the tower connects to it some distance up, where the impedance is higher. A series capacitor at the bottom tunes out the wire's inductance. That is the gamma match idea applied to a vertical.

Why not B? A hairpin match needs a split, insulated feed point, but a grounded tower has no gap to bridge.

E9E11

What is the purpose of using multiple driven elements connected through phasing lines?

  1. A To control the antenna’s radiation pattern
  2. B To prevent harmonic radiation from the transmitter
  3. C To allow single-band antennas to operate on other bands
  4. D To create a low-angle radiation pattern
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. To control the antenna’s radiation pattern

Why A is right. A. Phasing lines set the timing between driven elements, and that timing decides where their signals add or cancel.

Each phasing line delays the signal by an amount set by its electrical length. Feeding elements with different delays steers the main lobe and places nulls. Changing or switching the lines can turn a pattern from broadside to end-fire, or reverse its direction, without moving the antennas.

Why not D? Phasing can shape the pattern in many ways; a low angle is not its general purpose.
