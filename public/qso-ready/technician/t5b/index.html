T5B: Math for electronics: conversion of electrical units, decibels

Group T5B of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 13 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T5B01

How many milliamperes is 1.5 amperes?

  1. A 0.0000015 milliamperes
  2. B 0.0015 milliamperes
  3. C 1500 milliamperes
  4. D 1,500,000 milliamperes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 1500 milliamperes

Why C is right. C. 1500 milliamperes.

Milli means one thousandth, or 10^-3. So one ampere equals 1000 milliamperes. To change amperes into milliamperes, multiply by 1000.

mA = A × 1000
1.5 A × 1000 = 1500 mA
= 1500 milliamperes

Why not B? 0.0015 divides by 1000 instead of multiplying, which goes the wrong way.

Remember: Smaller unit, bigger number.

T5B02

Which is equal to 1,500,000 hertz?

  1. A 1500 kHz
  2. B 1500 MHz
  3. C 15 GHz
  4. D 150 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 1500 kHz

Why A is right. A. 1500 kHz.

Kilo means one thousand, or 10^3. One kilohertz is 1000 hertz. To change hertz into kilohertz, divide by 1000.

kHz = Hz ÷ 1000
1,500,000 Hz ÷ 1000 = 1500 kHz
= 1500 kHz

Why not B? 1500 MHz would be 1,500,000,000 hertz, a thousand times too large.

T5B03

Which is equal to one kilovolt?

  1. A One one-thousandth of a volt
  2. B One hundred volts
  3. C One thousand volts
  4. D One million volts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. One thousand volts

Why C is right. C. One thousand volts.

Kilo means one thousand, or 10^3. A kilovolt is 1000 volts, the same way a kilometer is 1000 meters.

1 kV = 1 × 10^3 V
1 × 1000 V = 1000 V
= 1000 volts

Why not A? One one-thousandth of a volt is a millivolt, not a kilovolt.

T5B04

Which is equal to one microvolt?

  1. A One one-millionth of a volt
  2. B One million volts
  3. C One thousand kilovolts
  4. D One one-thousandth of a volt
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. One one-millionth of a volt

Why A is right. A. One one-millionth of a volt.

Micro means one millionth, or 10^-6. A microvolt is a very small voltage, about the size of a weak radio signal at a receiver's input.

1 μV = 1 × 10^-6 V
1 × 0.000001 V = 0.000001 V
= 0.000001 volts

Why not D? One one-thousandth of a volt is a millivolt, which is 1000 times larger.

T5B05

Which is equal to 500 milliwatts?

  1. A 5 watts
  2. B 0.5 watts
  3. C 500,000 watts
  4. D 500,000,000 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 0.5 watts

Why B is right. B. 0.5 watts.

Milli means one thousandth, or 10^-3. So 1000 milliwatts make one watt. To change milliwatts to watts, divide by 1000.

W = mW ÷ 1000
500 mW ÷ 1000 = 0.5 W
= 0.5 watts

Why not A? 5 watts would be 5000 milliwatts, ten times too large.

T5B06

Which is equal to 3000 milliamperes?

  1. A 0.003 amperes
  2. B 0.3 amperes
  3. C 3,000,000 amperes
  4. D 3 amperes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 3 amperes

Why D is right. D. 3 amperes.

Milli means one thousandth, or 10^-3, so 1000 milliamperes make one ampere. Divide milliamperes by 1000 to get amperes.

A = mA ÷ 1000
3000 mA ÷ 1000 = 3 A
= 3 amperes

Why not A? 0.003 amperes divides by 1000 twice; that would be 3 milliamperes.

T5B07

Which is equal to 3.525 MHz?

  1. A 0.003525 kHz
  2. B 35.25 kHz
  3. C 3525 kHz
  4. D 3,525,000 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 3525 kHz

Why C is right. C. 3525 kHz.

Mega means one million, or 10^6, and kilo means one thousand, or 10^3. So one megahertz equals 1000 kilohertz. Multiply megahertz by 1000 to get kilohertz.

kHz = MHz × 1000
3.525 MHz × 1000 = 3525 kHz
= 3525 kHz

Why not B? 35.25 kHz multiplies by 10 instead of 1000.

T5B08

Which is equal to 1,000,000 picofarads?

  1. A 0.001 microfarads
  2. B 1 microfarad
  3. C 1000 microfarads
  4. D 1,000,000,000 microfarads
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 1 microfarad

Why B is right. B. 1 microfarad.

Pico means 10^-12 and micro means 10^-6. The gap between them is 10^6, so one million picofarads make one microfarad. Divide picofarads by 1,000,000 to get microfarads.

μF = pF ÷ 1,000,000
1,000,000 pF × 10^-12 = 10^-6 F
10^-6 F = 1 μF
= 1 microfarad

Why not A? 0.001 microfarads is 1000 picofarads, a thousand times too small.

T5B09

Which decibel value most closely represents a power increase from 5 watts to 10 watts?

  1. A 2 dB
  2. B 3 dB
  3. C 5 dB
  4. D 10 dB
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 3 dB

Why B is right. B. 3 dB.

Decibels compare two powers using dB = 10 × log10(P2 / P1). Going from 5 to 10 watts doubles the power, a ratio of 2. Since log10(2) is about 0.301, doubling is about 3 dB, the closest choice.

dB = 10 × log10(P2 / P1)
10 × log10(10 W / 5 W)
10 × log10(2) = 10 × 0.301 = 3.01
= 3 dB

Why not D? 10 dB is a ratio of 10, such as 5 watts to 50 watts.

T5B10

Which decibel value most closely represents a power decrease from 12 watts to 3 watts?

  1. A -1 dB
  2. B -3 dB
  3. C -6 dB
  4. D -9 dB
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. -6 dB

Why C is right. C. -6 dB.

Use dB = 10 × log10(P2 / P1). Going from 12 to 3 watts keeps one quarter of the power. A quarter is half of a half, and each halving is about -3 dB, so the total is about -6 dB.

dB = 10 × log10(P2 / P1)
10 × log10(3 W / 12 W)
10 × log10(0.25) = 10 × (-0.602) = -6.02
= -6 dB

Why not B? -3 dB is one halving, which would leave 6 watts, not 3.

T5B11

Which decibel value represents a power increase from 20 watts to 200 watts?

  1. A 10 dB
  2. B 12 dB
  3. C 18 dB
  4. D 28 dB
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 10 dB

Why A is right. A. 10 dB.

Use dB = 10 × log10(P2 / P1). Going from 20 to 200 watts multiplies the power by 10. Since log10(10) is exactly 1, the change is exactly 10 dB.

dB = 10 × log10(P2 / P1)
10 × log10(200 W / 20 W)
10 × log10(10) = 10 × 1 = 10
= 10 dB

Why not C? 18 dB would be a ratio of about 63, far more than 10 times.

T5B12

Which is equal to 28400 kHz?

  1. A 28.400 kHz
  2. B 2.800 MHz
  3. C 284.00 MHz
  4. D 28.400 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 28.400 MHz

Why D is right. D. 28.400 MHz.

Kilo is 10^3 and mega is 10^6, so 1000 kilohertz make one megahertz. To change kilohertz to megahertz, divide by 1000, which moves the decimal point three places left.

MHz = kHz ÷ 1000
28400 kHz ÷ 1000 = 28.400 MHz
= 28.400 MHz

Why not C? 284.00 MHz moves the decimal only two places, giving a value ten times too large.

T5B13

Which is equal to 2425 MHz?

  1. A 0.002425 GHz
  2. B 24.25 GHz
  3. C 2.425 GHz
  4. D 242.5 GHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 2.425 GHz

Why C is right. C. 2.425 GHz.

Mega is 10^6 and giga is 10^9, so 1000 megahertz make one gigahertz. Divide megahertz by 1000 to get gigahertz.

GHz = MHz ÷ 1000
2425 MHz ÷ 1000 = 2.425 GHz
= 2.425 GHz

Why not B? 24.25 GHz divides by 100 instead of 1000.
