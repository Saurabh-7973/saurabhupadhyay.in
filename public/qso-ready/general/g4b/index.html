G4B: Tests and test equipment

Group G4B of the General pool (2023–2027) has 13 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G4B01

What item of test equipment contains horizontal and vertical channel amplifiers?

  1. A An ohmmeter
  2. B A signal generator
  3. C An ammeter
  4. D An oscilloscope
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. An oscilloscope

Why D is right. D. An oscilloscope uses one amplifier for each axis of its display.

An oscilloscope draws a graph of a signal. The vertical channel amplifies the signal's voltage and moves the trace up and down. The horizontal channel sweeps the trace across the screen over time. Each channel needs its own amplifier so it can be scaled separately.

Why not B? A signal generator makes test signals; it does not display them.

G4B02

Which of the following is an advantage of an oscilloscope versus a digital voltmeter?

  1. A An oscilloscope uses less power
  2. B Complex impedances can be easily measured
  3. C Greater precision
  4. D Complex waveforms can be measured
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Complex waveforms can be measured

Why D is right. D. An oscilloscope shows the shape of a waveform, not only one reading.

A digital voltmeter gives you a single number. An oscilloscope draws the signal over time, so you can see its shape, peaks, distortion and timing. For complex waveforms, the shape is often what you need to know.

Why not C? A digital voltmeter usually reads a voltage to more digits than you can read off a scope screen.

G4B03

Which of the following is the best instrument to use for checking the keying waveform of a CW transmitter?

  1. A An oscilloscope
  2. B A field strength meter
  3. C A sidetone monitor
  4. D A wavemeter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. An oscilloscope

Why A is right. A. An oscilloscope shows how each CW element rises and falls over time.

The keying waveform is the shape of each dot and dash as the carrier turns on and off. If the edges are too sharp, the signal spreads out and causes clicks on nearby frequencies. Only an instrument that draws the signal over time can show those edges.

Why not B? A field strength meter shows relative signal level, not how it changes during each element.

G4B04

What signal source is connected to the vertical input of an oscilloscope when checking the RF envelope pattern of a transmitted signal?

  1. A The local oscillator of the transmitter
  2. B An external RF oscillator
  3. C The transmitter balanced mixer output
  4. D The attenuated RF output of the transmitter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The attenuated RF output of the transmitter

Why D is right. D. The envelope exists only on the real transmitted signal, so you take that signal and cut it down to a level the scope input can handle.

The envelope is the outline traced by the peaks of the RF signal as modulation changes it. The sample goes to the scope's vertical input, which moves the trace up and down.

Why not A? The local oscillator is an internal signal, not the transmitted output.

G4B05

Why do voltmeters have high input impedance?

  1. A It improves the frequency response
  2. B It allows for higher voltages to be safely measured
  3. C It improves the resolution of the readings
  4. D It decreases the loading on circuits being measured
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It decreases the loading on circuits being measured

Why D is right. D. High input impedance means the meter draws very little current from the circuit.

A voltmeter connects across the part you are measuring, so it adds a path for current. If the meter drew much current, it would change the very voltage it is reading. This is called loading. A high input impedance, often millions of ohms, keeps that effect tiny.

Why not B? Safely measuring high voltage depends on the meter's insulation and ratings.

G4B06

What is an advantage of a digital multimeter as compared to an analog multimeter?

  1. A Better for measuring computer circuits
  2. B Less prone to overload
  3. C Higher precision
  4. D Faster response
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Higher precision

Why C is right. C. A digital multimeter gives a reading to more digits than you can read from a needle.

An analog meter shows a needle on a printed scale, and you estimate where it points. A digital meter shows a number, often to three or four digits. That makes it more precise for reading a steady value.

Why not D? A needle moves smoothly and often shows changes faster to the eye than a digital display that updates a few times a second.

G4B07

What signals are used to conduct a two-tone test?

  1. A Two audio signals of the same frequency shifted 90 degrees
  2. B Two non-harmonically related audio signals
  3. C Two swept frequency tones
  4. D Two audio frequency range square wave signals of equal amplitude
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Two non-harmonically related audio signals

Why B is right. B. If one tone were a multiple of the other, distortion products would land right on the tones and hide, so the tones are chosen without that relationship.

Harmonically related means one frequency is a whole-number multiple of the other. You feed the two audio tones into an SSB transmitter. A clean transmitter puts out only those two tones, shifted up to RF, so any extra signals you see are distortion.

Why not A? Two tones at the same frequency are really one tone, so there would be nothing to compare.

G4B08

What transmitter performance parameter does a two-tone test analyze?

  1. A Linearity
  2. B Percentage of suppression of the carrier and undesired sideband for SSB
  3. C Percentage of frequency modulation
  4. D Percentage of carrier phase shift
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Linearity

Why A is right. A. The two-tone test checks linearity.

A linear amplifier's output is a scaled-up copy of its input. If it is not linear, two input tones mix and create extra frequencies, called intermodulation products. Those products widen your signal and cause interference. The test shows how large they are.

Why not C? An SSB transmitter does not use frequency modulation, so that is not what this test measures.

G4B09

When is an analog multimeter preferred to a digital multimeter?

  1. A When testing logic circuits
  2. B When high precision is desired
  3. C When measuring the frequency of an oscillator
  4. D When adjusting circuits for maximum or minimum values
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. When adjusting circuits for maximum or minimum values

Why D is right. D. A needle sweeps smoothly, so you can watch it rise, stop and fall as you tune, while digits only flicker.

When you adjust a circuit for the highest or lowest reading, you are watching the trend, not the exact value. The moving needle shows that trend at a glance.

Why not B? Higher precision is an advantage of digital meters.

G4B10

Which of the following can be determined with a directional wattmeter?

  1. A Standing wave ratio
  2. B Antenna front-to-back ratio
  3. C RF interference
  4. D Radio wave propagation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Standing wave ratio

Why A is right. A. The meter reads forward power and reflected power separately, and SWR can be worked out from those two readings.

Forward power travels toward the antenna, and reflected power comes back. SWR, the standing wave ratio, tells how well the antenna system matches the feed line. More reflected power means a higher SWR.

Why not B? Front-to-back ratio requires measuring the antenna's signal in two directions out in the field.

G4B11

Which of the following must be connected to an antenna analyzer when it is being used for SWR measurements?

  1. A Receiver
  2. B Transmitter
  3. C Antenna and feed line
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Antenna and feed line

Why C is right. C. The analyzer makes its own small test signal, so the only thing you attach is the load you want to measure.

It sends that signal into whatever is connected and measures what comes back. To see the SWR of your antenna system, you connect the feed line with the antenna on its far end. No radio is needed.

Why not B? A transmitter is not needed, and its power could damage the analyzer.

G4B12

What effect can strong signals from nearby transmitters have on an antenna analyzer?

  1. A Desensitization which can cause intermodulation products which interfere with impedance readings
  2. B Received power that interferes with SWR readings
  3. C Generation of harmonics which interfere with frequency readings
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Received power that interferes with SWR readings

Why B is right. B. The antenna picks up nearby transmitters, and that outside power reaches the analyzer's detector along with its own weak test signal.

An analyzer works with a very low-power test signal. Even modest outside signals add to what it measures, so the SWR reading comes out wrong.

Why not D? The problem is extra received power at the detector, not intermodulation or harmonics, so A and C fail and D cannot be right.

G4B13

Which of the following can be measured with an antenna analyzer?

  1. A Front-to-back ratio of an antenna
  2. B Power output from a transmitter
  3. C Impedance of coaxial cable
  4. D Gain of a directional antenna
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Impedance of coaxial cable

Why C is right. C. The analyzer reads impedance at its connector, and with the right far-end tests that reading reveals the cable's own impedance.

Measure the coax once with its far end open and once shorted. The cable's characteristic impedance is the square root of the open reading times the shorted reading. Another way: put a variable resistor at the far end and adjust it until the reading stops changing with frequency. That resistance equals the cable's impedance.

Why not B? An analyzer is a low-power test source, not a meter built to accept a transmitter's output.
