E9D: Yagi antennas; parabolic reflectors; feed point impedance and loading of electrically short antennas; antenna Q; RF grounding

Group E9D of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E9D01

How much does the gain of an ideal parabolic reflector antenna increase when the operating frequency is doubled?

  1. A 2 dB
  2. B 3 dB
  3. C 4 dB
  4. D 6 dB
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 6 dB

Why D is right. D. A dish's gain grows with the square of its size in wavelengths, so doubling frequency multiplies gain by 4, which is 6 dB.

Gain of an ideal dish depends on its area measured in square wavelengths. Doubling the frequency halves the wavelength. The same dish then measures twice as wide in wavelengths, and its area in square wavelengths becomes four times larger. A power ratio of 4 is about 6 dB.

gain change (dB) = 10 log10((f2 / f1)^2)
f2 / f1 = 2
10 log10(2^2) = 10 log10(4) = 6.02 dB, about 6 dB
= 6.02 dB

Why not B? 3 dB is a doubling of gain, but gain grows with frequency squared, so it quadruples.

E9D02

How can two linearly polarized Yagi antennas be used to produce circular polarization?

  1. A Stack two Yagis to form an array with the respective elements in parallel planes fed 90 degrees out of phase
  2. B Stack two Yagis to form an array with the respective elements in parallel planes fed in phase
  3. C Arrange two Yagis on the same axis and perpendicular to each other with the driven elements at the same point on the boom and fed 90 degrees out of phase
  4. D Arrange two Yagis collinear to each other with the driven elements fed 180 degrees out of phase
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Arrange two Yagis on the same axis and perpendicular to each other with the driven elements at the same point on the boom and fed 90 degrees out of phase

Why C is right. C. Two Yagis at right angles on one boom, fed a quarter cycle apart, combine their fields into one that rotates, which is circular polarization.

One Yagi might be horizontal and the other vertical. Their driven elements sit at the same spot on the boom, so their waves start together. Feeding one 90 degrees behind the other makes the combined electric field turn in a circle as the wave travels. Satellite stations use this so the signal holds up as the spacecraft tumbles.

Why not A? Yagis with parallel elements share one polarization, so phasing them cannot produce a rotating field.

E9D03

What is the most efficient location for a loading coil on an electrically short whip?

  1. A Near the center of the vertical radiator
  2. B As low as possible on the vertical radiator
  3. C At a voltage maximum
  4. D At a voltage null
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Near the center of the vertical radiator

Why A is right. A. A coil near the middle keeps current high along the lower half while keeping the coil small, the best balance of radiation and loss.

A short whip radiates mainly where its current is high. A coil at the base leaves the whole whip above it with tapering current. Moving the coil up keeps full current on the section below, raising radiation resistance. But higher up, the coil needs more turns, which add loss. Near the center is the usual best tradeoff.

Why not B? A base coil is easy to build but leaves low current on the whip, so it radiates less efficiently.

E9D04

Why should antenna loading coils have a high ratio of reactance to resistance?

  1. A To swamp out harmonics
  2. B To lower the radiation angle
  3. C To maximize efficiency
  4. D To minimize the Q
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. To maximize efficiency

Why C is right. C. A coil with a high ratio of reactance to resistance has little loss resistance, so less power turns into heat in the coil.

The ratio of reactance to resistance is the coil's Q. A loading coil sits where current is high, so any resistance in it burns power. A short antenna already has low radiation resistance, so even a few ohms of coil loss can waste much of the power. Thick wire and good spacing give a high-Q, efficient coil.

Why not D? A high ratio of reactance to resistance is high Q, the opposite of minimizing Q.

E9D05

Approximately how long is a Yagi’s driven element?

  1. A 234 divided by frequency in MHz
  2. B 1005 divided by frequency in MHz
  3. C 1/4 wavelength
  4. D 1/2 wavelength
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 1/2 wavelength

Why D is right. D. The driven element is the fed part of a Yagi, and it works as a resonant half-wave dipole, so it is about 1/2 wavelength.

A Yagi has one fed element plus parasitic elements that are not wired to the feed line. The fed element is a dipole sized near resonance, about a half wavelength. Nearby elements change its impedance a little, so its exact length is trimmed during tuning.

Why not C? A quarter wavelength fits a vertical over a ground plane, not a Yagi's dipole driven element.

E9D06

What happens to SWR bandwidth when one or more loading coils are used to resonate an electrically short antenna?

  1. A It is increased
  2. B It is decreased
  3. C It is unchanged if the loading coil is located at the feed point
  4. D It is unchanged if the loading coil is located at a voltage maximum point
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. It is decreased

Why B is right. B. A loaded short antenna stores much more energy than it radiates, so its Q is high and its SWR rises quickly away from resonance.

A short antenna has large capacitive reactance and small radiation resistance. The loading coil cancels the reactance at one frequency. Move off that frequency and the leftover reactance grows fast compared with the small resistance. The SWR climbs quickly, so the usable range shrinks. Coil position does not avoid this.

Why not A? Loading raises Q, which narrows bandwidth instead of widening it.

E9D07

What is an advantage of top loading an electrically short HF vertical antenna?

  1. A Lower Q
  2. B Greater structural strength
  3. C Higher losses
  4. D Improved radiation efficiency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Improved radiation efficiency

Why D is right. D. A top hat keeps current high all the way up the short vertical, which raises its radiation resistance and its efficiency.

Top loading adds capacitance at the top, often wires or a disk called a capacity hat. Current can now flow into that capacitance instead of dropping to zero at the tip. More of the vertical carries strong current, so it radiates more. It also needs a smaller loading coil, or none, which cuts coil loss.

Why not C? Top loading cuts losses relative to radiated power, so higher losses is the opposite of the advantage.

E9D08

What happens as the Q of an antenna increases?

  1. A SWR bandwidth increases
  2. B SWR bandwidth decreases
  3. C Gain is reduced
  4. D More common-mode current is present on the feed line
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. SWR bandwidth decreases

Why B is right. B. A higher Q means sharper resonance, so the impedance changes faster with frequency and the SWR bandwidth gets narrower.

Q compares stored energy with energy lost or radiated each cycle. A high-Q antenna acts like a sharply tuned circuit. It matches well right at resonance, but its reactance grows quickly as you tune away. That makes the range of frequencies with acceptable SWR smaller.

Why not A? SWR bandwidth widens as Q drops, not as it rises.

E9D09

What is the function of a loading coil in an electrically short antenna?

  1. A To increase the SWR bandwidth by increasing net reactance
  2. B To lower the losses
  3. C To lower the Q
  4. D To resonate the antenna by cancelling the capacitive reactance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. To resonate the antenna by cancelling the capacitive reactance

Why D is right. D. A short antenna looks capacitive, and the coil adds equal inductive reactance so the two cancel and the antenna resonates.

An antenna shorter than resonant length has a feed point impedance with capacitive reactance. That reactance blocks power from flowing in. A series inductor adds an equal amount of inductive reactance. They cancel, leaving mainly resistance, and the antenna is resonant at the chosen frequency.

Why not B? A loading coil adds its own resistance, so it raises losses slightly rather than lowering them.

E9D10

How does radiation resistance of a base-fed whip antenna change below its resonant frequency?

  1. A Radiation resistance increases
  2. B Radiation resistance decreases
  3. C Radiation resistance becomes imaginary
  4. D Radiation resistance does not depend on frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Radiation resistance decreases

Why B is right. B. Below resonance the whip is shorter in wavelengths, and radiation resistance drops quickly as electrical length shrinks.

A quarter-wave whip over a good ground has about 36 ohms of radiation resistance. Lower the frequency and the same whip becomes a smaller fraction of a wavelength. Radiation resistance falls roughly with the square of that electrical length. A whip one-eighth wavelength long has only about 6 ohms.

Why not C? Radiation resistance is always a real resistance; the imaginary part of impedance is the reactance.

E9D11

Why do most two-element Yagis with normal spacing have a reflector instead of a director?

  1. A Lower SWR
  2. B Higher receiving directivity factor
  3. C Greater front-to-side
  4. D Higher gain
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Higher gain

Why D is right. D. At normal element spacing, a driven element plus reflector gives slightly more forward gain than a driven element plus director.

In a two-element beam, the parasitic element's current phase sets which way the beam points and how much gain it has. With typical spacing near 0.1 to 0.2 wavelength, the reflector layout comes out a bit ahead in gain. That is why most two-element Yagis use a reflector behind the driven element.

Why not C? Front-to-side ratio is not the main reason; the reflector is chosen for forward gain.

E9D12

What is the purpose of making a Yagi’s parasitic elements either longer or shorter than resonance?

  1. A Wind torque cancellation
  2. B Mechanical balance
  3. C Control of phase shift
  4. D Minimize losses
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Control of phase shift

Why C is right. C. Detuning a parasitic element sets the phase of its induced current, which decides whether it pushes the beam forward or backward.

A parasitic element is not fed; current is induced in it by the driven element. Made slightly long, it acts inductive and its current lags, so it works as a reflector. Made slightly short, it acts capacitive and its current leads, so it works as a director. The right phase makes signals add toward the front.

Why not D? Element losses depend mainly on conductor material and size; the length is detuned on purpose to set phase.
