T0B: Antenna safety: tower safety and grounding, installing antennas, antenna supports

Group T0B of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T0B01

Which of the following is good practice when installing ground wires on a tower for lightning protection?

  1. A Put a drip loop in the ground connection to prevent water damage to the ground system
  2. B Make sure all ground wire bends are right angles
  3. C Ensure that connections are short and direct
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Ensure that connections are short and direct

Why C is right. C. Keep lightning ground connections short and direct.

Lightning is a fast, powerful surge. Long or winding wires resist that surge more, so the current may jump to other paths, such as your house wiring. Short, straight runs give the surge the easiest path to ground.

Why not B? Sharp right-angle bends make it harder for the surge to follow the wire.

T0B02

What is required when climbing an antenna tower?

  1. A Have sufficient training on safe tower climbing techniques
  2. B Use appropriate tie-off to the tower at all times
  3. C Always wear an approved climbing harness
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

Tower climbing is dangerous work. You need training in safe climbing methods. You need an approved climbing harness, and you must stay tied off to the tower at all times, so a slip does not become a fall.

T0B03

Under what circumstances is it safe to climb a tower without a helper or observer?

  1. A When no electrical work is being performed
  2. B When no mechanical work is being performed
  3. C When the work being done is not more than 20 feet above the ground
  4. D Never
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Never

Why D is right. D. It is never safe to climb a tower without a helper or observer.

If something goes wrong on a tower, you may not be able to call for help. A helper on the ground can watch for trouble, pass up tools and get help fast. That is true for any job and any height.

Why not C? A fall from 20 feet can still cause serious injury.

T0B04

Which of the following is an important safety precaution to observe when putting up an antenna tower?

  1. A Wear a ground strap connected to your wrist at all times
  2. B Insulate the base of the tower to avoid lightning strikes
  3. C Look for and stay clear of any overhead electrical wires
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Look for and stay clear of any overhead electrical wires

Why C is right. C. Look for and stay clear of overhead electrical wires when putting up a tower.

Towers and antennas are long metal objects. If one tips over or touches a power line while you are holding it, the current can kill you. Check overhead before you start, and plan so nothing can reach the lines.

Why not B? Insulating the tower base does not stop lightning. Towers should be well grounded.

T0B05

What is the purpose of a safety wire through a turnbuckle used to tension guy lines?

  1. A Secure the guy line if the turnbuckle breaks
  2. B Prevent loosening of the turnbuckle from vibration
  3. C Provide a ground path for lightning strikes
  4. D Provide an ability to measure for proper tensioning
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Prevent loosening of the turnbuckle from vibration

Why B is right. B. A safety wire stops the turnbuckle from loosening from vibration.

A turnbuckle sets the tension on a guy line by turning a threaded body. Wind shakes the guy line over time and can slowly unscrew it. A wire looped through the turnbuckle locks it so it cannot turn.

Why not A? The safety wire locks the threads. It is not strong enough to hold the guy line if the turnbuckle breaks.

T0B06

What is the minimum safe distance from a power line to allow when installing an antenna?

  1. A Add the height of the antenna to the height of the power line and multiply by a factor of 1.5
  2. B The height of the power line above ground
  3. C 1/2 wavelength at the operating frequency
  4. D Enough so that if the antenna falls, no part of it can come within 10 feet of the power wires
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Enough so that if the antenna falls, no part of it can come within 10 feet of the power wires

Why D is right. D. Place the antenna so that if it falls, no part of it can come within 10 feet of the power wires.

Antennas and masts can fall in storms or during installation. Measure the full length of the antenna and mast, and place them so a fall in any direction leaves at least 10 feet between it and the power lines.

Why not C? Half a wavelength depends on frequency and has nothing to do with how far a falling antenna can reach.

T0B07

Which of the following is an important safety rule to remember when using a crank-up tower?

  1. A This type of tower must never be painted
  2. B This type of tower must never be grounded
  3. C This type of tower must not be climbed unless it is retracted, or mechanical safety locking devices have been installed
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. This type of tower must not be climbed unless it is retracted, or mechanical safety locking devices have been installed

Why C is right. C. Do not climb a crank-up tower unless it is retracted or mechanical safety locks are installed.

A crank-up tower is held up by cables and a winch. If a cable slips or breaks, the sections can drop and crush anything between them. Climb only when the tower is fully down, or when locking devices hold the sections in place.

Why not B? Crank-up towers need to be grounded like any other tower.

T0B08

Which is a proper grounding method for a tower?

  1. A A single four-foot ground rod, driven into the ground no more than 12 inches from the base
  2. B A ferrite-core RF choke connected between the tower and ground
  3. C A connection between the tower base and a cold-water pipe
  4. D Separate eight-foot ground rods for each tower leg, bonded to the tower and each other
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Separate eight-foot ground rods for each tower leg, bonded to the tower and each other

Why D is right. D. Use separate eight-foot ground rods for each tower leg, bonded to the tower and each other.

A good tower ground spreads lightning current into the earth through several deep paths. One rod at each leg gives more contact with the soil. Bonding them all together keeps them at the same voltage.

Why not C? A cold-water pipe may have plastic sections and is not a dependable lightning ground.

T0B09

Why should you avoid attaching an antenna to a utility pole?

  1. A The antenna will not work properly because of induced voltages
  2. B The antenna may unbalance the power transformer, causing power fluctuations
  3. C The antenna could contact high-voltage power lines
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The antenna could contact high-voltage power lines

Why C is right. C. An antenna on a utility pole could contact high-voltage power lines.

Utility poles carry power lines that can be thousands of volts. Working on the pole, or having the antenna fall or move, could put you or the antenna in contact with those lines. That can be deadly.

Why not A? The concern is safety from touching high voltage, not how well the antenna works.

T0B10

Which of the following is true when installing grounding conductors used for lightning protection?

  1. A Use only non-insulated wire
  2. B Wires must be carefully routed with precise right-angle bends
  3. C Sharp bends must be avoided
  4. D Common grounds must be avoided
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Sharp bends must be avoided

Why C is right. C. Sharp bends must be avoided in lightning grounding conductors.

A lightning surge rises very fast. Sharp bends make it harder for the current to stay on the wire, so it may jump across to something nearby. Gentle curves and straight runs keep the surge on its intended path to ground.

Why not D? Grounds should be bonded together into a common system, not kept apart.

T0B11

Which of the following establishes grounding requirements for an amateur radio tower or antenna?

  1. A FCC Part 97 rules
  2. B Local electrical codes
  3. C FAA tower lighting regulations
  4. D UL recommended practices
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Local electrical codes

Why B is right. B. Local electrical codes set grounding requirements for an amateur tower or antenna.

Grounding is part of building and electrical safety, which is handled by local codes. These codes say what kind of rods, wire and connections you need. Check with your local building department before installing a tower.

Why not A? Part 97 covers how amateur stations operate, not how towers are grounded.
