T1F: Station identification; Repeaters; Third party communications; Club stations; FCC inspection

Group T1F of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T1F01

When must the station licensee make the station and the station records available for inspection?

  1. A At any time ten days after notification by the FCC of such an inspection
  2. B At any time upon request by an FCC representative
  3. C At any time after a written notification by the FCC of such inspection
  4. D Only when presented with a valid warrant by an FCC official or government agent
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. At any time upon request by an FCC representative

Why B is right. B. At any time upon request by an FCC representative.

If an FCC representative asks, the station licensee must let them inspect the station and its records. The rule sets no waiting period and needs no written notice or warrant.

“The station licensee must make the station and the station records available for inspection upon request by an FCC representative.”

47 CFR 97.103(c)

Why not A? The rule has no ten-day waiting period.

T1F02

How often must you identify with your FCC-assigned call sign when using tactical call signs such as “Race Headquarters”?

  1. A Never, the tactical call is sufficient
  2. B Once every hour
  3. C At least every 10 minutes during and at the end of a communication
  4. D At the end of every transmission
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. At least every 10 minutes during and at the end of a communication

Why C is right. C. At least every 10 minutes during and at the end of a communication.

A tactical call like "Race Headquarters" tells people your job at an event. It does not replace your FCC call sign. You still give your assigned call sign at least every 10 minutes and at the end of each communication.

“must transmit its assigned call sign on its transmitting channel at the end of each communication, and at least every 10 minutes during a communication”

47 CFR 97.119(a)

Why not A? A tactical name is not an FCC call sign, so it cannot replace one.

T1F03

When are you required to transmit your assigned call sign?

  1. A At the beginning of each contact, and every 10 minutes thereafter
  2. B At least once during each transmission
  3. C At least every 15 minutes during and at the end of a communication
  4. D At least every 10 minutes during and at the end of a communication
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. At least every 10 minutes during and at the end of a communication

Why D is right. D. At least every 10 minutes during and at the end of a communication.

Two times matter: during a long contact, at least every 10 minutes, and once more when you finish. You do not have to say it at the start or in every single transmission.

“must transmit its assigned call sign on its transmitting channel at the end of each communication, and at least every 10 minutes during a communication”

47 CFR 97.119(a)

Why not C? The interval is 10 minutes, not 15.

Remember: Ten minutes and at the end.

T1F04

What language must you use for identification when using a phone emission?

  1. A Any language recognized by the United Nations
  2. B Any language recognized by the ITU
  3. C English
  4. D English, French, or Spanish
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. English

Why C is right. C. English.

When you give your call sign by voice, you must say it in English. You may speak other languages in the rest of the contact, but the call sign itself must be in English.

“By a phone emission in the English language.”

47 CFR 97.119(b)(2)

Why not D? French and Spanish are not allowed for the call sign itself.

T1F05

What method of call sign identification is required for a station transmitting phone signals?

  1. A Send the call sign followed by the indicator RPT
  2. B Send the call sign using a CW or phone emission
  3. C Send the call sign followed by the indicator R
  4. D Send the call sign using only a phone emission
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Send the call sign using a CW or phone emission

Why B is right. B. Send the call sign using a CW or phone emission.

Part 97 lists several ways to send your call sign, including CW (Morse) and phone (voice in English). A voice station may use either one. Many repeaters, for example, send their call sign in Morse.

“The call sign must be transmitted with an emission authorized for the transmitting channel in one of the following ways: (1) By a CW emission.”

47 CFR 97.119(b)

Why not D? Voice is allowed, but the rule does not make it the only choice.

T1F06

Which of the following self-assigned indicators are acceptable when using a phone transmission?

  1. A KL7CC stroke W3
  2. B KL7CC slant W3
  3. C KL7CC slash W3
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

An indicator like /W3 adds information, such as where you are. It must be set off from the call sign by the slant mark (/) or by any word that means the slant mark. Stroke, slant and slash all mean the same mark, so any of them works on voice.

“Each indicator must be separated from the call sign by the slant mark (/) or by any suitable word that denotes the slant mark.”

47 CFR 97.119(c)

T1F07

Which of the following restrictions apply when a non-licensed person speaks to a foreign amateur radio station via a station under the control of an FCC-licensed amateur radio operator?

  1. A The person must be a U.S. citizen
  2. B The foreign station must be in a country with which the U.S. has a third-party agreement
  3. C The licensed control operator must do the station identification
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The foreign station must be in a country with which the U.S. has a third-party agreement

Why B is right. B. The foreign station must be in a country with which the U.S. has a third-party agreement.

Here the third party is an unlicensed person speaking over your station. You may send their messages abroad only to countries that have an agreement with the United States allowing it, or in an emergency. The person does not need to be a U.S. citizen, and the station identifies as usual.

“No station shall transmit messages for a third party to any station within the jurisdiction of any foreign government whose administration has not made such an arrangement.”

47 CFR 97.115(a)(2)

Why not A? Citizenship of the third party is not a requirement.

T1F08

What is the definition of third-party communications?

  1. A A message from a control operator to another amateur station control operator on behalf of another person
  2. B Amateur radio communications where three stations are in communications with one another
  3. C Operation when the transmitting equipment is licensed to a person other than the control operator
  4. D Temporary authorization for an unlicensed person to transmit on the amateur bands for technical experiments
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A message from a control operator to another amateur station control operator on behalf of another person

Why A is right. A. A message from a control operator to another amateur station control operator on behalf of another person.

Count the parties: you (the first party) send a message to another ham (the second party) for someone else (the third party). That someone else is often an unlicensed friend or family member.

“A message from the control operator (first party) of an amateur station to another amateur station control operator (second party) on behalf of another person (third party).”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(47)

Why not B? Three stations on the air at once is a group contact, not third-party traffic.

Remember: First party sends, second party gets, third party is who it is for.

T1F09

What type of amateur station simultaneously retransmits the signal of another amateur station on a different channel or channels?

  1. A Beacon station
  2. B Remote control station
  3. C Repeater station
  4. D Message forwarding station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Repeater station

Why C is right. C. Repeater station.

A repeater listens on one channel and sends the same signal out on another channel at the same time. Placed high up, it lets handheld radios reach much farther.

“Repeater. An amateur station that simultaneously retransmits the transmission of another amateur station on a different channel or channels.”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(40)

Why not A? A beacon sends its own signal; it does not relay others.

T1F10

Who is accountable if a repeater inadvertently retransmits communications that violate the FCC rules?

  1. A The control operator of the originating station
  2. B The control operator of the repeater
  3. C The owner of the repeater
  4. D Both the originating station and the repeater owner
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The control operator of the originating station

Why A is right. A. The control operator of the originating station.

A repeater relays whatever comes in, often with no one watching. If it passes along a rule-breaking message by accident, its control operator is not held accountable. The blame stays with the person who sent the message.

“The control operator of a repeater that retransmits inadvertently communications that violate the rules in this part is not accountable for the violative communications.”

47 CFR 97.205(g)

Why not B? The rule says the repeater's control operator is not accountable for accidental relays.

T1F11

Which of the following is a requirement for the issuance of a club station license grant?

  1. A The trustee must have an Amateur Extra Class operator license grant
  2. B The club must have at least four members
  3. C The club must be registered with the American Radio Relay League (ARRL)
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The club must have at least four members

Why B is right. B. The club must have at least four members.

To get a club station license, the club needs at least four people, a name, a document of organization, management, and a main purpose tied to amateur radio. The trustee needs only an operator/primary station license, of any class.

“The club must be composed of at least four persons”

47 CFR 97.5(b)(2)

Why not A? The trustee needs an operator license, but not the Amateur Extra class.
