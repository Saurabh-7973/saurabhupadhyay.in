T1C: Licensing: classes, sequential and vanity call sign systems, places where the Amateur Radio Service is regulated by the FCC, name and address on FCC license database, term, renewal, grace period, maintaining mailing address; International communications

Group T1C of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T1C01

For which classes of amateur radio licenses does the FCC currently issue new licenses?

  1. A Novice, Technician, General, Amateur Extra
  2. B Technician, Technician Plus, General, Amateur Extra
  3. C Novice, Technician Plus, General, Advanced
  4. D Technician, General, Amateur Extra
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Technician, General, Amateur Extra

Why D is right. D. Technician, General, Amateur Extra.

Five license classes still exist on paper: Novice, Technician, General, Advanced and Amateur Extra. But the FCC no longer issues new Novice or Advanced licenses. That leaves Technician, General and Amateur Extra. Technician Plus is no longer a class at all.

“No new license grant will be issued for a Novice or Advanced Class operator/primary station.”

47 CFR 97.17(a)

Why not A? Novice licenses are no longer issued to new applicants.

T1C02

Who may select a desired call sign under the vanity call sign rules?

  1. A Only a licensed amateur with a General or Amateur Extra Class license
  2. B Only a licensed amateur with an Amateur Extra Class license
  3. C Only a licensed amateur who has been licensed continuously for more than 10 years
  4. D Any licensed amateur
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Any licensed amateur

Why D is right. D. Any licensed amateur.

A vanity call sign is one you pick instead of the one the FCC assigns in order. Anyone named on an operator/primary station license may apply. Your class limits which call sign formats you can choose, but not whether you can apply.

“The person named in an operator/primary station license grant… is eligible to make application for modification of the license grant… to show a call sign selected by the vanity call sign system”

47 CFR 97.19(a)

Why not C? There is no rule requiring 10 years of licensing to get a vanity call sign.

T1C03

What types of international communications are FCC-licensed amateur radio stations permitted to make?

  1. A Communications incidental to the purposes of the Amateur Radio Service and remarks of a personal character
  2. B Communications incidental to conducting business or remarks of a personal nature
  3. C Only communications incidental to contest exchanges; all other communications are prohibited
  4. D Any communications that would be permitted by an international broadcast station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Communications incidental to the purposes of the Amateur Radio Service and remarks of a personal character

Why A is right. A. Communications incidental to the purposes of the Amateur Radio Service and remarks of a personal character.

When you talk to a station in another country, the rule limits you to two kinds of content: things related to amateur radio itself, and personal remarks. Business talk falls outside both. Acting like a broadcast station falls outside both too, and broadcasting is banned for hams anyway.

“shall be limited to communications incidental to the purposes of the amateur service and to remarks of a personal character”

47 CFR 97.117

Why not B? Business communications are not allowed on international contacts.

Remember: Overseas: talk about ham radio or about yourself, never business.

T1C04

What may happen if the FCC is unable to reach you by email?

  1. A Fine and suspension of operator license
  2. B Revocation of the station license or suspension of the operator license
  3. C Revocation of access to the license record in the FCC system
  4. D Nothing; there is no such requirement
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Revocation of the station license or suspension of the operator license

Why B is right. B. Revocation of the station license or suspension of the operator license.

The FCC writes to you by email, so your record must have a working email address. If FCC mail bounces because you gave a wrong address, the FCC may revoke your station license or suspend your operator license. Keep your email current in the FCC system.

“Revocation of the station license or suspension of the operator license may result when correspondence from the FCC is returned as undeliverable because the grantee failed to provide the correct email address.”

47 CFR 97.23

Why not D? The rule does require a correct email address.

T1C05

Which of the following is a valid Group D call sign format for Technician class?

  1. A KF1XXX
  2. B KA1X
  3. C W1XX
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. KF1XXX

Why A is right. A. KF1XXX.

The FCC's sequential call sign system, which is FCC practice rather than Part 97 text, sorts call signs into groups. Group D uses a 2-by-3 pattern: two letters, one digit, then three letters, with K or W first. KF1XXX fits. KA1X (2-by-1) and W1XX (1-by-2) are Group A formats, for Amateur Extra holders.

Why not C? W1XX has one letter before the digit and two after, which is a Group A format.

Remember: Count letters around the digit: 2 then 3 means Group D.

T1C06

Which of the following statements is true about using your amateur radio license when operating aboard a vessel or craft in international waters?

  1. A Amateur operation is prohibited in international waters
  2. B You may only operate during maritime emergencies
  3. C You need special FCC authorization for maritime mobile operation
  4. D You may operate from a US-documented vessel with the master's permission
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. You may operate from a US-documented vessel with the master's permission

Why D is right. D. You may operate from a US-documented vessel with the master's permission.

FCC rules apply aboard any ship or aircraft documented or registered in the United States, even far out at sea. You may use your normal license there. You also need the approval of the ship's master (captain) or the aircraft's pilot in command. No extra FCC permit is required.

“The installation and operation of an amateur station on a ship or aircraft must be approved by the master of the ship or pilot in command of the aircraft.”

47 CFR 97.11(a)

Why not C? No special FCC authorization is needed; your regular license covers it.

T1C07

How long before the expiration date may an amateur radio license renewal be requested?

  1. A 30 days
  2. B 90 days
  3. C 6 months
  4. D 1 year
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 90 days

Why B is right. B. 90 days.

Amateur licenses fall under the FCC's general rules for wireless licenses. Those rules say you may file to renew no sooner than 90 days before the expiration date, and no later than the expiration date itself.

“must be filed no later than the expiration date of the authorization, and no sooner than 90 days prior to the expiration date”

47 CFR 1.949(a)

Why not D? A year ahead is too early; filing opens 90 days before expiration.

T1C08

What is the normal term for an FCC-issued amateur radio license?

  1. A Five years
  2. B Eight years
  3. C Ten years
  4. D Life
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Ten years

Why C is right. C. Ten years.

An amateur license is normally granted for 10 years. You can renew it near the end of each term, so you can keep it for life as long as you renew.

“An amateur service license is normally granted for a 10-year term.”

47 CFR 97.25

Why not D? A license lasts for life only if you keep renewing it every term.

T1C09

What is the grace period for renewal if an amateur license expires?

  1. A Two years
  2. B Three years
  3. C Five years
  4. D Ten years
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Two years

Why A is right. A. Two years.

If your license expires, you have a 2-year grace period to apply for renewal. During that time you can renew without taking an exam. After 2 years, the license is gone, and you would have to pass at least the Technician exam again to get a new one.

“may apply to the FCC for renewal of the license grant for another term during a 2 year filing grace period”

47 CFR 97.21(b)

Why not C? Five years is longer than the grace period in the rule.

T1C10

How soon after passing the examination for your first amateur radio license may you transmit on the amateur radio bands?

  1. A Immediately on receiving your Certificate of Successful Completion of Examination (CSCE)
  2. B As soon as your operator/station license grant appears on the ARRL website
  3. C As soon as your operator/station license grant appears in the FCC’s license database
  4. D As soon as you receive your license in the mail from the FCC
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. As soon as your operator/station license grant appears in the FCC’s license database

Why C is right. C. As soon as your operator/station license grant appears in the FCC's license database.

Passing the exam is not the same as having a license. Your first license takes effect when the FCC posts your grant in its ULS database. Once it shows there, you may transmit. A first-time licensee cannot operate on the test certificate alone.

“The station apparatus must be under the physical control of a person named in an amateur station license grant on the ULS consolidated license database”

47 CFR 97.5(a)

Why not A? The CSCE proves you passed, but for a first license you must wait for the FCC grant.

T1C11

If your license has expired and is still within the allowable grace period, may you continue to transmit on the amateur radio bands?

  1. A Yes, for up to two years
  2. B Yes, as soon as you apply for renewal
  3. C Yes, for up to one year
  4. D No, you must wait until the license has been renewed
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. No, you must wait until the license has been renewed

Why D is right. D. No, you must wait until the license has been renewed.

The grace period only gives you time to file a renewal. It does not let you transmit. Until the FCC actually renews the license, you hold no operating privileges.

“Unless and until the license grant is renewed, no privileges in this part are conferred.”

47 CFR 97.21(b)

Why not A? The 2 years are for applying to renew, not for operating.

Remember: Grace period means paperwork time, not air time.
