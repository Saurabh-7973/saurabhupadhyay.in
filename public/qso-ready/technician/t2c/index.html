T2C: Public service: emergency operations, applicability of FCC rules, RACES and ARES, net and traffic procedures, operating restrictions during emergencies, use of phonetics in message handling

Group T2C of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T2C01

When do the FCC Part 97 Amateur Radio Service rules NOT apply to the operation of an amateur station?

  1. A When operating under RACES rules
  2. B When operating under FEMA rules
  3. C When operating under ARES rules
  4. D FCC rules always apply
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. FCC rules always apply

Why D is right. D. FCC rules always apply.

RACES and ARES are emergency programs that amateurs may join, and FEMA is a federal agency. None of them replaces Part 97. The station licensee stays responsible for running the station under FCC rules at all times.

“The station licensee is responsible for the proper operation of the station in accordance with the FCC Rules.”

47 CFR 97.103(a)

Why not A? RACES operation is itself defined inside Part 97, so those rules still apply.

T2C02

Which of the following are typical duties of a Net Control Station?

  1. A Choose the regular net meeting time and frequency
  2. B Ensure that all stations checking into the net are properly licensed for operation on the net frequency
  3. C Call the net to order and direct communications between stations checking in
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Call the net to order and direct communications between stations checking in

Why C is right. C. Net control calls the net to order and directs stations that check in.

The net control station runs the session on the air. It opens the net, takes check-ins and tells stations when to talk. It does not usually set the schedule, and it is not its job to check licenses.

Why not D? Choices A and B are not typical net control duties, so not all are correct.

T2C03

What technique is used to ensure that voice messages containing unusual words are received correctly?

  1. A Send the words by voice and Morse code
  2. B Use the Q-code “QSR” to ask the receiving station to repeat the words back
  3. C Spell the words using a standard phonetic alphabet
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Spell the words using a standard phonetic alphabet

Why C is right. C. Spell unusual words with a standard phonetic alphabet.

Many letters sound alike over the air, such as B, D and E. A phonetic alphabet replaces each letter with a full word that is hard to mistake, so the receiving station can write the word down correctly.

Why not B? QSR is not a standard way to request a repeat.

T2C04

What is RACES?

  1. A An emergency organization combining amateur radio and citizens band operators and frequencies
  2. B An international radio experimentation society
  3. C A radio contest held in a short period, sometimes called a “sprint”
  4. D An FCC Part 97 amateur radio service for civil defense communications during national emergencies
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. An FCC Part 97 amateur radio service for civil defense communications during national emergencies

Why D is right. D. RACES is a Part 97 amateur service for civil defense communications.

RACES stands for Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service. It uses amateur stations to carry civil defense messages during emergencies. Part 97 defines it, so its stations work under amateur rules.

“RACES (radio amateur civil emergency service). A radio service using amateur stations for civil defense communications”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(38)

Why not A? RACES uses amateur stations only; citizens band is a separate service.

T2C05

What does the term “traffic” refer to in net operation?

  1. A Formal messages exchanged by net stations
  2. B The number of stations checking in and out of a net
  3. C Operation by mobile or portable stations
  4. D A count of the number of activations of the net each month
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Formal messages exchanged by net stations

Why A is right. A. Traffic means formal messages passed between net stations.

Nets that handle traffic relay written messages from station to station. Each message follows a set format so it can be passed along without errors.

Why not B? A count of check-ins is not traffic; traffic is the messages themselves.

T2C06

What is the Amateur Radio Emergency Service (ARES)?

  1. A A group of licensed amateurs who have voluntarily registered their qualifications and equipment for communications duty in the public service
  2. B A group of licensed amateurs who are members of the military and who voluntarily agreed to provide message handling services in the case of an emergency
  3. C A training program that provides licensing courses for those interested in obtaining an amateur license to use during emergencies
  4. D A training program that certifies amateur operators for membership in the Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A group of licensed amateurs who have voluntarily registered their qualifications and equipment for communications duty in the public service

Why A is right. A. ARES is a group of licensed amateurs who volunteer their skills and equipment for public service communications.

Members sign up ahead of time, listing what they can do and what gear they have. When an emergency or public event needs radio help, leaders know whom to call.

Why not B? ARES members are not required to be in the military.

T2C07

Which of the following is standard practice when you participate in a net?

  1. A When first responding to the net control station, transmit your call sign, name, and address as in the FCC database
  2. B Record the time of each of your transmissions
  3. C Unless you are reporting an emergency, transmit only when directed by the net control station
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Unless you are reporting an emergency, transmit only when directed by the net control station

Why C is right. C. Unless you have an emergency, talk only when net control calls on you.

A net works like a meeting with one chairperson. Waiting for net control keeps stations from talking over each other. An emergency report is the exception and may break in.

Why not A? Giving your full address on the air is not standard net practice.

T2C08

Which of the following relays messages using email addresses based on amateur callsigns?

  1. A Winlink
  2. B FT8
  3. C PSK31
  4. D AMTOR
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Winlink

Why A is right. A. Winlink relays email using addresses built from amateur call signs.

Winlink is a radio email system. Your address uses your call sign, and messages pass over radio links to stations that connect to the internet. This helps when normal internet service is down.

Why not B? FT8 sends short contact exchanges, not email.

T2C09

Are amateur station control operators ever permitted to operate outside the frequency privileges of their license class?

  1. A No
  2. B Yes, but only when part of a FEMA emergency plan
  3. C Yes, but only when part of a RACES emergency plan
  4. D Yes, but only in situations involving the immediate safety of human life or protection of property
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Yes, but only in situations involving the immediate safety of human life or protection of property

Why D is right. D. Yes, but only when human life or property is in immediate danger.

Normally you must stay within your license privileges. When normal communication systems are not available and there is an immediate threat to life or property, the rules let an amateur station use any radio means it has to get the message through.

“No provision of these rules prevents the use by an amateur station of any means of radiocommunication at its disposal to provide essential communication needs in connection with the immediate safety of human life”

47 CFR 97.403

Why not C? RACES membership does not create this permission; the danger itself does.

T2C10

What information is contained in the preamble of a formal traffic message?

  1. A The email address of the originating station
  2. B The address of the intended recipient
  3. C The telephone number of the addressee
  4. D Information needed to track the message
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Information needed to track the message

Why D is right. D. The preamble holds the information used to track the message.

The preamble is the opening part of a formal message. It includes items such as a message number and other handling details. With these, anyone who relays the message can keep track of it.

Why not B? The recipient's address is its own section after the preamble.

T2C11

What is meant by “check” in a radiogram header?

  1. A The number of words or word equivalents in the text portion of the message
  2. B The call sign of the originating station
  3. C A list of stations that have relayed the message
  4. D A box on the message form that indicates that the message was received and/or relayed
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The number of words or word equivalents in the text portion of the message

Why A is right. A. The check is the count of words in the message text.

The receiving station counts the words it copied and compares that count to the check. If the two numbers differ, part of the message may be missing or added.

Why not C? Relay stations are not listed in the check.

Remember: Check the count.

T2C12

Which of the following requires certification by a civil defense agency?

  1. A ARES
  2. B RACES
  3. C MARS
  4. D SKYWARN
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. RACES

Why B is right. B. RACES requires certification by a civil defense agency.

To transmit in RACES, a station must be registered with a civil defense organization. Its control operator must be certified as enrolled with that organization.

“No station may transmit in RACES unless it is an FCC-licensed primary, club, or military recreation station and it is certified by a civil defense organization as registered with that organization.”

47 CFR 97.407(a)

Why not A? ARES is a volunteer group and needs no civil defense certification.
