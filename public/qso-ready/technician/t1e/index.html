T1E: Control operator: eligibility, designating, privileges, duties, location, required; Control point; Control types: automatic, remote

Group T1E of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T1E01

When may an amateur station transmit without a control operator?

  1. A When using automatic control, such as in the case of a repeater
  2. B When the station licensee is away and another licensed amateur is using the station
  3. C When the transmitting station is an auxiliary station
  4. D Never
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Never

Why D is right. D. Never.

Every amateur station needs a control operator whenever it transmits. Even a repeater under automatic control still has a control operator who is responsible for it, though that person need not be at a control point. Auxiliary stations need one too.

“When transmitting, each amateur station must have a control operator.”

47 CFR 97.7

Why not A? Automatic control changes where the operator must be, not whether one is needed.

T1E02

Who may be the control operator of a station communicating through an amateur satellite or space station?

  1. A Only an Amateur Extra Class operator
  2. B A General class or higher licensee with a satellite operator certification
  3. C Only an Amateur Extra Class operator who is also an AMSAT member
  4. D Any amateur allowed to transmit on the satellite uplink frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Any amateur allowed to transmit on the satellite uplink frequency

Why D is right. D. Any amateur allowed to transmit on the satellite uplink frequency.

The uplink is the frequency you send on to reach the satellite. A station on the ground working a satellite is an Earth station, and a holder of any license class may control one, within that class's privileges. So if your class lets you transmit on the uplink frequency, you may work the satellite. No special certificate or club membership is needed.

“A holder of any class operator license may be the control operator of an Earth station, subject to the privileges of the class of operator license held by the control operator.”

47 CFR 97.209(a)

Why not B? There is no satellite operator certificate in Part 97.

T1E03

Who must designate the station control operator?

  1. A The station licensee
  2. B The FCC
  3. C The frequency coordinator
  4. D Any licensed operator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The station licensee

Why A is right. A. The station licensee.

The person who holds the station license chooses who will be the control operator. If no one else is named in the station records, the FCC assumes the licensee is the control operator.

“The station licensee must designate the station control operator.”

47 CFR 97.103(b)

Why not C? A frequency coordinator suggests repeater channels; it does not pick operators.

T1E04

What determines the transmitting frequency privileges of an amateur station?

  1. A The frequency authorized by the frequency coordinator
  2. B The frequencies printed on the license grant
  3. C The highest class of operator license held by anyone on the premises
  4. D The class of operator license held by the control operator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The class of operator license held by the control operator

Why D is right. D. The class of operator license held by the control operator.

The control operator is the person responsible for what the station sends. Which frequencies the station may use depends on that person's license class. A Technician at the controls has Technician privileges, even at an Extra holder's station.

“A station may only be operated in the manner and to the extent permitted by the privileges authorized for the class of operator license held by the control operator.”

47 CFR 97.105(b)

Why not C? Who else is in the room does not matter; only the control operator's class counts.

Remember: The person at the controls sets the band limits.

T1E05

What is an amateur station’s control point?

  1. A The location of the station’s transmitting antenna
  2. B The location of the station’s transmitting apparatus
  3. C The location at which the control operator function is performed
  4. D The mailing address of the station licensee
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The location at which the control operator function is performed

Why C is right. C. The location at which the control operator function is performed.

The control point is where the control operator is when they run the station. It may be the same place as the radio, or far away for a remotely controlled station. It is not the antenna site or the mailing address.

“Control point. The location at which the control operator function is performed.”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(14)

Why not B? The radio can be in one place while the control point is in another.

T1E06

When, other than during an emergency, may a Technician class licensee be the control operator of a station operating in an Amateur Extra Class band segment?

  1. A At no time
  2. B When designated as the control operator by an Amateur Extra Class licensee
  3. C As part of a multi-operator contest team
  4. D When using a club station whose trustee holds an Amateur Extra Class license
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. At no time

Why A is right. A. At no time.

Band privileges follow the control operator's own license. Part 97 lists certain segments only for Amateur Extra control operators. A Technician cannot gain those privileges by being designated, joining a team or using a club station. Outside an emergency, the answer is never.

“For a station having a control operator who has been granted an Amateur Extra Class operator license”

47 CFR 97.301

Why not B? Being designated by an Extra holder does not give you Extra privileges.

T1E07

When the control operator is not the station licensee, who is responsible for the proper operation of the station?

  1. A All licensed amateurs who are present at the operation
  2. B Only the station licensee
  3. C Only the control operator
  4. D The control operator and the station licensee
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The control operator and the station licensee

Why D is right. D. The control operator and the station licensee.

When a different ham operates your station, both of you answer for what it sends. The rule says the station licensee and the control operator are equally responsible.

“When the control operator is a different amateur operator than the station licensee, both persons are equally responsible for proper operation of the station.”

47 CFR 97.103(a)

Why not B? The licensee does not carry the load alone; the operator shares it.

Remember: Two names, one shared duty.

T1E08

Which of the following is an example of automatic control?

  1. A Repeater operation
  2. B Controlling a station over the internet
  3. C Using a computer or other device to send CW automatically
  4. D Using a computer or other device to identify automatically
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Repeater operation

Why A is right. A. Repeater operation.

Automatic control means the station follows the rules through devices and procedures, with no control operator at a control point. A repeater that runs on its own around the clock is the classic case, and Part 97 allows repeaters to be automatically controlled.

“A repeater may be automatically controlled.”

47 CFR 97.205(d)

Why not B? Running a station over the internet is remote control, with an operator at the control point.

T1E09

Which amateur stations may be remotely controlled?

  1. A Only repeater stations
  2. B Only automatically controlled stations
  3. C Only digital stations
  4. D Any station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Any station

Why D is right. D. Any station.

Remote control means the control operator runs the station from a distance through a control link. Part 97 allows it for any station, as long as the control operator is at the control point.

“When a station is being remotely controlled, the control operator must be at the control point. Any station may be remotely controlled.”

47 CFR 97.109(c)

Why not A? Remote control is not limited to repeaters.

T1E10

Which of the following is an example of remote control as defined in Part 97?

  1. A A software defined radio (SDR)
  2. B Operating the station over the internet
  3. C Controlling a model aircraft, boat, or car by amateur radio
  4. D Earth-Moon-Earth (EME) communication
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Operating the station over the internet

Why B is right. B. Operating the station over the internet.

With remote control, the control operator is not at the radio but adjusts it through a control link. The internet can serve as that link, letting you run your home station from far away.

“Remote control. The use of a control operator who indirectly manipulates the operating adjustments in the station through a control link”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(39)

Why not C? Steering a model craft is telecommand of the model, not remote control of an amateur station.

T1E11

What is a control operator as defined in Part 97?

  1. A The person speaking or otherwise communicating messages over an amateur station
  2. B The person who is the licensee of an amateur station
  3. C An amateur operator identified in the FCC database as responsible for transmissions and FCC rules compliance at a station license location
  4. D An amateur operator designated by the licensee of a station to be responsible for transmissions and FCC rules compliance at that station
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. An amateur operator designated by the licensee of a station to be responsible for transmissions and FCC rules compliance at that station

Why D is right. D. An amateur operator designated by the licensee of a station to be responsible for transmissions and FCC rules compliance at that station.

The control operator is the ham the station licensee picks to be responsible for the station's transmissions and for following the rules. It is not whoever happens to be talking, and it is not a role listed in the FCC database.

“Control operator. An amateur operator designated by the licensee of a station to be responsible for the transmissions from that station to assure compliance with the FCC Rules.”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(13)

Why not A? A guest speaking on the air is not the control operator.
