T1A: Purpose and permissible use of the Amateur Radio Service; Operator/primary station license grant; Meanings of basic terms used in FCC rules; Interference; RACES rules; Phonetics; Frequency Coordinator; Beacon

Group T1A of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T1A01

Which of the following is part of the Basis and Purpose of the Amateur Radio Service?

  1. A Providing personal radio communications for as many citizens as possible
  2. B Providing communications for international contesting
  3. C Advancing skills in the technical and communication phases of the radio art
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Advancing skills in the technical and communication phases of the radio art

Why C is right. C. Advancing skills in the technical and communication phases of the radio art.

Part 97 opens with five principles that explain why the amateur service exists. One of them is to build skill in both the technical side (how radios work) and the communication side (how to operate well). Personal chat for the whole public and contest traffic are not listed as purposes.

“rules which provide for advancing skills in both the communication and technical phases of the art”

47 CFR 97.1(c)

Why not A? Serving as many citizens as possible describes a public radio service, not the amateur purpose.

Remember: Ham radio is a school: you learn the tech and the talk.

T1A02

Which agency regulates and enforces the rules for the Amateur Radio Service in the United States?

  1. A ARRL
  2. B Homeland Security
  3. C The FCC
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The FCC

Why C is right. C. The FCC.

Part 97, the amateur rulebook, sits in Title 47 of the Code of Federal Regulations, which holds the rules of the Federal Communications Commission. The FCC grants licenses and enforces the rules, for example by inspecting stations. The ARRL is a national membership association, and Homeland Security does not run the amateur service.

“The station licensee must make the station and the station records available for inspection upon request by an FCC representative.”

47 CFR 97.103(c)

Why not A? The ARRL is a member organization; it cannot make or enforce federal rules.

T1A03

What do the FCC rules state regarding the use of a phonetic alphabet for station identification in the Amateur Radio Service?

  1. A It is required when transmitting emergency messages
  2. B It is encouraged when using phone emissions
  3. C It is required when in contact with foreign stations
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. It is encouraged when using phone emissions

Why B is right. B. It is encouraged when using phone emissions.

When you give your call sign by voice, the rule requires English and suggests a phonetic alphabet so letters like B, D and E are not confused. The word for phonetics in the rule is "encouraged," not "required." No rule makes phonetics mandatory for emergencies or foreign contacts.

“By a phone emission in the English language. Use of a phonetic alphabet as an aid for correct station identification is encouraged”

47 CFR 97.119(b)(2)

Why not C? Phonetics help with foreign stations, but the rule never requires them.

Remember: Phonetics are a good habit, not a law.

T1A04

How do you receive official notification of your new license and call sign after passing the exam?

  1. A Email from the FCC with a link to download the license grant
  2. B USPS First-Class Mail from the FCC
  3. C Email from the lead volunteer examiner
  4. D USPS First-Class Mail from the lead volunteer examiner
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Email from the FCC with a link to download the license grant

Why A is right. A. Email from the FCC with a link to download the license grant.

After you pass, the examiners send your application to their VEC (Volunteer Examiner Coordinator), which files it with the FCC. Only the FCC grants the license. The FCC currently tells new licensees by email and includes a link to download the official license. That is why your record must have a working email address under 97.23.

“the administering VEs must submit the application document to the coordinating VEC according to the coordinating VEC's instructions”

47 CFR 97.509(m)

Why not C? The examiners send your paperwork to their VEC, which files it with the FCC; only the FCC grants the license.

T1A05

What proves that the FCC has issued an operator/primary license grant?

  1. A A printed copy of the certificate of successful completion of examination
  2. B An email notification from the NCVEC granting the license
  3. C The license appears in the FCC ULS database
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The license appears in the FCC ULS database

Why C is right. C. The license appears in the FCC ULS database.

ULS is the FCC's Universal Licensing System, its online record of licenses. Part 97 ties your right to be a control operator to your grant being on that database. A test certificate shows you passed, but the license itself exists only once the FCC posts it in ULS.

“For whom an amateur operator/primary station license grant appears on the ULS consolidated licensee database”

47 CFR 97.7(a)

Why not B? NCVEC is a council of examiner groups; it does not grant licenses.

Remember: If ULS doesn't list it, you don't have it yet.

T1A06

On which of the following HF frequencies can automatically controlled amateur propagation beacons be found?

  1. A Anywhere in the Technician portion of 10 meters
  2. B Anywhere, if they are registered with the FCC
  3. C In a specific portion of each of the HF Technician bands
  4. D On ten meters, between 28.200 MHz and 28.300 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. On ten meters, between 28.200 MHz and 28.300 MHz

Why D is right. D. On ten meters, between 28.200 MHz and 28.300 MHz.

A beacon sends a steady signal so others can check radio conditions. On HF, the rule allows a beacon to run under automatic control only in the 28.20-28.30 MHz segment of the 10-meter band. Other listed segments are on VHF and UHF.

“A beacon may be automatically controlled while it is transmitting on the 28.20-28.30 MHz”

47 CFR 97.203(d)

Why not A? The rule names one narrow segment, not all of the Technician part of 10 meters.

T1A07

What is the FCC Part 97 definition of a space station?

  1. A Any satellite orbiting Earth
  2. B A manned satellite orbiting Earth
  3. C An amateur station located more than 50 km above Earth's surface
  4. D An amateur station using amateur radio satellites for relay of signals
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. An amateur station located more than 50 km above Earth's surface

Why C is right. C. An amateur station located more than 50 km above Earth's surface.

Part 97 defines a space station by height alone: any amateur station more than 50 km above the Earth. It does not matter whether a crew is on board or whether it orbits. A ground station that talks through a satellite is still an Earth station, not a space station.

“Space station. An amateur station located more than 50 km above the Earth's surface.”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(41)

Why not D? A station that uses a satellite to relay is on the ground, so it is not a space station.

Remember: Space starts at 50 km for ham rules.

T1A08

Who recommends transmit/receive channels for repeater and auxiliary stations?

  1. A A Frequency Spectrum Manager appointed by the FCC
  2. B A Volunteer Frequency Coordinator recognized by local amateurs
  3. C An FCC Regional Field Office
  4. D The International Telecommunication Union
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A Volunteer Frequency Coordinator recognized by local amateurs

Why B is right. B. A Volunteer Frequency Coordinator recognized by local amateurs.

Repeaters in the same area could jam each other if they picked channels at random. A frequency coordinator is a group recognized by local amateurs whose stations could be repeaters or auxiliary stations. It suggests transmit and receive channels and other settings to keep interference low. It is not an FCC office.

“An entity, recognized in a local or regional area by amateur operators whose stations are eligible to be auxiliary or repeater stations, that recommends transmit/receive channels”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(22)

Why not A? The FCC does not appoint frequency coordinators; local amateurs recognize them.

T1A09

Who selects a Frequency Coordinator?

  1. A The FCC Office of Spectrum Management and Coordination Policy
  2. B The local chapter of the Office of National Council of Independent Frequency Coordinators
  3. C Amateur operators in a local or regional area whose stations are eligible to be repeater or auxiliary stations
  4. D The FCC Regional Field Office
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Amateur operators in a local or regional area whose stations are eligible to be repeater or auxiliary stations

Why C is right. C. Section 97.3(a)(22) defines a frequency coordinator as an entity recognized by the local or regional amateurs whose stations could be repeaters or auxiliary stations, so those amateurs pick it.

A frequency coordinator gets its standing from the people it serves. The definition says it is recognized by local or regional amateurs whose stations could be repeaters or auxiliary stations. No federal office or national body picks it.

“An entity, recognized in a local or regional area by amateur operators whose stations are eligible to be auxiliary or repeater stations”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(22)

Why not D? FCC field offices handle enforcement; they do not choose coordinators.

T1A10

Besides an FCC-issued amateur operator license, what is required to be the control operator of a Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service (RACES) Station?

  1. A A written recommendation by the local ARRL Emergency Coordinator
  2. B Membership in the Amateur Radio Emergency Service (ARES)
  3. C Certification of current enrollment by a civil defense organization
  4. D Nothing
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Certification of current enrollment by a civil defense organization

Why C is right. C. Certification of current enrollment by a civil defense organization.

RACES is the amateur side of civil defense emergency work. To be the control operator of a station in RACES, you need two things: an FCC amateur license and certification from a civil defense organization that you are enrolled with it. ARES membership or an ARRL letter does not meet this rule.

“unless that person holds a FCC-issued amateur operator license and is certified by a civil defense organization as enrolled in that organization”

47 CFR 97.407(a)

Why not B? ARES is a separate volunteer program; joining it is not what RACES requires.

T1A11

Which of the following is prohibited?

  1. A International communications on VHF bands and higher frequencies
  2. B Willful or malicious interference
  3. C Third-party traffic using digital modes
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Willful or malicious interference

Why B is right. B. Willful or malicious interference.

Interfering on purpose with any radio signal is banned outright. That covers jamming, blocking a repeater or playing noise over a contact. International contacts on VHF and third-party traffic on digital modes are both allowed when other rules are met.

“No amateur operator shall willfully or maliciously interfere with or cause interference to any radio communication or signal.”

47 CFR 97.101(d)

Why not A? International contacts are allowed on VHF and higher bands.
