G1E: Control categories; repeater regulations; third-party rules; ITU regions; automatically controlled digital station

Group G1E of the General pool (2023–2027) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G1E01

Which of the following would disqualify a third party from participating in sending a message via an amateur station?

  1. A The third party’s amateur license has been revoked and not reinstated
  2. B The third party is not a US citizen
  3. C The third party is speaking in a language other than English
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The third party’s amateur license has been revoked and not reinstated

Why A is right. A. Part 97 keeps people who lost their amateur license by revocation, and have not been relicensed, from getting back on the air as a third party.

A third party is someone who is not the control operator, such as a guest speaking at your microphone. Part 97 bars some people from this: those whose amateur licenses were revoked, suspended or surrendered under enforcement and not restored. Citizenship and language do not matter.

“The third party is not a prior amateur service licensee whose license was revoked or not renewed after hearing and re-licensing has not taken place”

47 CFR 97.115(b)(2)

Why not B? Third parties do not have to be US citizens.

G1E02

When may a 10-meter repeater retransmit the 2-meter signal from a station that has a Technician class control operator?

  1. A Under no circumstances
  2. B Only if the station on 10-meters is operating under a Special Temporary Authorization allowing such retransmission
  3. C Only during an FCC-declared general state of communications emergency
  4. D Only if the 10-meter repeater control operator holds at least a General class license
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Only if the 10-meter repeater control operator holds at least a General class license

Why D is right. D. A repeater's control operator must have privileges on its transmit frequency, and 10-meter repeaters sit where Technicians have no privileges.

Repeaters on 10 meters may only use 29.5 to 29.7 MHz. Technicians have no privileges there; their 10-meter privileges stop at 28.5 MHz. Any licensee may control a repeater, but only within their own privileges. So a 10-meter repeater relaying a Technician's 2-meter signal needs a control operator of General class or higher.

“may be the control operator of a repeater, subject to the privileges of the class of operator license held”

47 CFR 97.205(a)

Why not A? The relay is allowed when the repeater's own control operator holds General or higher.

G1E03

What is required to conduct communications with a digital station operating under automatic control outside the automatic control band segments?

  1. A The station initiating the contact must be under local or remote control
  2. B The interrogating transmission must be made by another automatically controlled station
  3. C No third-party traffic may be transmitted
  4. D The control operator of the interrogating station must hold an Amateur Extra class license
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The station initiating the contact must be under local or remote control

Why A is right. A. Outside the listed segments, an automatic digital station may transmit only in reply to a station under local or remote control.

Automatic control means no person is at the controls. Part 97 lists band segments where automatic digital stations may run freely. Elsewhere, such a station may transmit only when a station with a human control operator, under local or remote control, calls it first, and its signal must be 500 Hz wide or less.

“The station is responding to interrogation by a station under local or remote control”

47 CFR 97.221(c)(1)

Why not B? Another automatic station cannot start the contact outside the listed segments.

G1E04

Which of the following conditions require a licensed amateur radio operator to take specific steps to avoid harmful interference to other users or facilities?

  1. A When operating within one mile of an FCC Monitoring Station
  2. B When using a band where the Amateur Service is secondary
  3. C When a station is transmitting spread spectrum emissions
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. Each listed case carries its own Part 97 duty to avoid causing harmful interference.

Within about 1600 m (1 mile) of an FCC monitoring facility, you must protect it from interference. On bands where amateurs are secondary, you must not interfere with primary users. Spread spectrum signals, which spread energy over a wide range of frequencies, must not interfere with other authorized emissions.

“A station within 1600 m (1 mile) of an FCC monitoring facility must protect that facility from harmful interference.”

47 CFR 97.13(b)

G1E05

What are the restrictions on messages sent to a third party in a country with which there is a Third-Party Agreement?

  1. A They must relate to emergencies or disaster relief
  2. B They must be for other licensed amateurs
  3. C They must relate to amateur radio, or remarks of a personal character, or messages relating to emergencies or disaster relief
  4. D The message must be limited to no longer than 1 minute in duration and the name of the third party must be recorded in the station log
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. They must relate to amateur radio, or remarks of a personal character, or messages relating to emergencies or disaster relief

Why C is right. C. International amateur messages must be about amateur radio or personal remarks, and emergency or relief traffic is also allowed.

When you pass a message for a non-amateur to a country with a third-party agreement, it is limited to the same topics as other international contacts: amateur radio matters and personal remarks. Emergency and disaster relief messages may go to any foreign station. Business messages are not allowed, and there is no time limit or log rule.

“limited to communications incidental to the purposes of the amateur service and to remarks of a personal character”

47 CFR 97.117

Why not A? Messages are not limited to emergencies; personal remarks are fine too.

G1E06

The frequency allocations of which ITU region apply to radio amateurs operating in North and South America?

  1. A Region 4
  2. B Region 3
  3. C Region 2
  4. D Region 1
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Region 2

Why C is right. C. The ITU divides the world into three regions, and North and South America together form Region 2.

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) sets frequency allocations by region. Region 1 is mainly Europe, Africa and the Middle East; Region 2 is the Americas; Region 3 is most of Asia and the Pacific. The Part 97 band tables list frequencies for each region, and a station uses the column for the region where it is located.

“The following transmitting frequency bands are available to an amateur station located within 50 km of the Earth's surface, within the specified ITU Region”

47 CFR 97.301

Why not A? There is no Region 4; the ITU uses three regions.

G1E07

In what part of the 2.4 GHz band may an amateur station communicate with non-licensed Wi-Fi stations?

  1. A Anywhere in the band
  2. B Channels 1 through 4
  3. C Channels 42 through 45
  4. D No part
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. No part

Why D is right. D. Amateur stations may exchange messages only with amateur stations and a few listed services, and unlicensed Wi-Fi devices are not among them.

Part 97 lists who an amateur station may talk with: other amateur stations, plus a few listed services in emergencies, RACES, or with FCC approval. Unlicensed Wi-Fi devices fall in none of those. So even though amateurs share part of the 2.4 GHz band with Wi-Fi, they may not link with it.

“Transmissions necessary to exchange messages with other stations in the amateur service”

47 CFR 97.111(a)(1)

Why not B? No Wi-Fi channel is open to two-way amateur contact.

G1E08

What is the maximum PEP output allowed for spread spectrum transmissions?

  1. A 100 milliwatts
  2. B 10 watts
  3. C 100 watts
  4. D 1500 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 10 watts

Why B is right. B. Part 97 caps spread spectrum transmissions at 10 W PEP output.

Spread spectrum (SS) spreads a signal over a wide range of frequencies, so it can add low-level noise to many other users at once. To limit that, the rules hold SS transmitter output to 10 W PEP (peak envelope power).

“No station may transmit with a transmitter output exceeding 10 W PEP when the station is transmitting a SS emission type.”

47 CFR 97.313(j)

Why not D? 1500 W is the general limit; spread spectrum has a much lower cap.

G1E10

Why should an amateur operator normally avoid transmitting on 14.100, 18.110, 21.150, 24.930 and 28.200 MHz?

  1. A A system of propagation beacon stations operates on those frequencies
  2. B A system of automatic digital stations operates on those frequencies
  3. C These frequencies are set aside for emergency operations
  4. D These frequencies are set aside for bulletins from the FCC
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A system of propagation beacon stations operates on those frequencies

Why A is right. A. These five frequencies carry an international network of propagation beacons, which you should leave clear.

A worldwide set of beacons takes turns sending on 14.100, 18.110, 21.150, 24.930 and 28.200 MHz. Operators listen to them to see which paths are open. Part 97 does not reserve these frequencies, but it asks operators to cooperate in sharing the bands. Transmitting on top of the beacons spoils them for everyone.

“Each station licensee and each control operator must cooperate in selecting transmitting channels and in making the most effective use of the amateur service frequencies.”

47 CFR 97.101(b)

Why not C? No amateur frequency is set aside for emergencies by these numbers.

G1E11

On what bands may automatically controlled stations transmitting RTTY or data emissions communicate with other automatically controlled digital stations?

  1. A On any band segment where digital operation is permitted
  2. B Anywhere in the non-phone segments of the 10-meter or shorter wavelength bands
  3. C Only in the non-phone Extra Class segments of the bands
  4. D Anywhere in the 6-meter or shorter wavelength bands, and in limited segments of some of the HF bands
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Anywhere in the 6-meter or shorter wavelength bands, and in limited segments of some of the HF bands

Why D is right. D. Part 97 lets automatic digital stations work freely on 6 meters and up, but on HF only in a short list of narrow segments.

Automatic control means no operator is at the controls. On 6 meters and every higher band, automatic RTTY and data stations may work each other anywhere those emissions are allowed. On HF, Part 97 names small segments, such as 14.0950 to 14.0995 MHz and 28.120 to 28.189 MHz. Outside them, an automatic station may only answer a station with a human operator.

“A station may be automatically controlled while transmitting a RTTY or data emission on the 6 m or shorter wavelength bands”

47 CFR 97.221(b)

Why not B? On 10 meters, automatic operation is limited to one small segment, not the whole non-phone part.

G1E12

When may third-party messages be transmitted via remote control?

  1. A Under any circumstances in which third party messages are permitted by FCC rules
  2. B Under no circumstances except for emergencies
  3. C Only when the message is intended for licensed radio amateurs
  4. D Only when the message is intended for third parties in areas where licensing is controlled by the FCC
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Under any circumstances in which third party messages are permitted by FCC rules

Why A is right. A. Remote control is not automatic control, so the usual third-party rules apply with no extra limits.

Under remote control, a control operator runs the station from a distance and is still in charge. Part 97 restricts third-party messages only on stations under automatic control, which have no operator present. A remotely controlled station may pass third-party traffic wherever the normal third-party rules allow it.

“No station may transmit third party communications while being automatically controlled except a station transmitting a RTTY or data emission.”

47 CFR 97.115(c)

Why not B? Third-party traffic by remote control is not limited to emergencies.
