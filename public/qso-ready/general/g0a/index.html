G0A: RF safety principles, rules, and guidelines; routine station evaluation

Group G0A of the General pool (2023–2027) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G0A01

What is one way that RF energy can affect human body tissue?

  1. A It heats body tissue
  2. B It causes radiation poisoning
  3. C It causes the blood count to reach a dangerously low level
  4. D It cools body tissue
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It heats body tissue

Why A is right. A. RF fields push the water molecules in tissue back and forth, and that motion becomes heat.

Body tissue is mostly water, so the effect is like a microwave oven. Parts of the body with poor blood flow, such as the eyes, are less able to carry the heat away.

Why not B? RF is non-ionizing, so it does not cause radiation poisoning the way X-rays or nuclear radiation can.

G0A02

Which of the following is used to determine RF exposure from a transmitted signal?

  1. A Its duty cycle
  2. B Its frequency
  3. C Its power density
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. Duty cycle, frequency and power density all go into judging RF exposure.

Power density is how much RF power passes through an area, such as milliwatts per square centimeter. Frequency matters because the body absorbs some frequencies more than others, so the safe limits change with frequency. Duty cycle matters because exposure is averaged over time, and a signal that is on less often delivers less.

Why not C? Power density counts, but A and B count too, so C alone is incomplete.

G0A03

How can you determine that your station complies with FCC RF exposure regulations?

  1. A By calculation based on FCC OET Bulletin 65
  2. B By calculation based on computer modeling
  3. C By measurement of field strength using calibrated equipment
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. Calculation, computer modeling or calibrated measurement can each show you comply.

The rules make you, the licensee, responsible for meeting the exposure limits. They point to FCC OET Bulletin 65 for methods. You can calculate exposure with its formulas, model your antenna on a computer, or measure the field with calibrated equipment.

“The licensee shall ensure compliance with the Commission's radio frequency exposure requirements”

47 CFR 97.13(c)(1)

Why not A? Calculation from OET Bulletin 65 works, but B and C also work, so A alone is incomplete.

G0A04

What does “time averaging” mean when evaluating RF radiation exposure?

  1. A The average amount of power developed by the transmitter over a specific 24-hour period
  2. B The average time it takes RF radiation to have any long-term effect on the body
  3. C The total time of the exposure
  4. D The total RF exposure averaged over a certain period
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The total RF exposure averaged over a certain period

Why D is right. D. The limits judge exposure averaged across a set window, so short strong bursts count only by their share of that window.

The window is 6 minutes for controlled settings and 30 minutes for the general public. A burst of high power can be acceptable if the average over that window stays under the limit.

Why not C? Total exposure time alone ignores how strong the field was.

G0A05

What must you do if an evaluation of your station shows that the RF energy radiated by your station exceeds permissible limits for possible human absorption?

  1. A Take action to prevent human exposure to the excessive RF fields
  2. B File an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS-97) with the FCC
  3. C Secure written permission from your neighbors to operate above the controlled MPE limits
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Take action to prevent human exposure to the excessive RF fields

Why A is right. A. Section 97.13(c)(2) puts the duty on you: when fields could exceed the limits where people can go, you must act to stop that exposure.

Ways to do that include lowering power, raising or moving the antenna, or keeping people out of that area.

“the licensee must take action to prevent human exposure to such RF electromagnetic fields”

47 CFR 97.13(c)(2)

Why not C? A neighbor's permission does not change the limits; the rule requires action.

G0A06

What must you do if your station fails to meet the FCC RF exposure exemption criteria?

  1. A Perform an RF exposure evaluation in accordance with FCC OET Bulletin 65
  2. B Contact the FCC for permission to transmit
  3. C Perform an RF exposure evaluation in accordance with World Meteorological Organization guidelines
  4. D Use an FCC-approved band-pass filter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Perform an RF exposure evaluation in accordance with FCC OET Bulletin 65

Why A is right. A. Missing the exemption only means you cannot skip the detailed check, and 97.13(c)(1) names OET Bulletin 65 as the method for it.

The FCC exemption criteria let some low-power setups skip a detailed evaluation. If your station does not qualify, you evaluate it yourself. Supplement B of the bulletin covers amateur stations.

“Appropriate methodologies and guidance for evaluating amateur radio service operation is described in the Office of Engineering and Technology (OET) Bulletin 65, Supplement B.”

47 CFR 97.13(c)(1)

Why not B? You do not need FCC permission to transmit; you need to evaluate and fix any problem yourself.

G0A07

What is the effect of modulation duty cycle on RF exposure?

  1. A A lower duty cycle permits greater power levels to be transmitted
  2. B A higher duty cycle permits greater power levels to be transmitted
  3. C Low duty cycle transmitters are exempt from RF exposure evaluation requirements
  4. D High duty cycle transmitters are exempt from RF exposure requirements
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A lower duty cycle permits greater power levels to be transmitted

Why A is right. A. Limits apply to power averaged over time, so a mode that is on less of the time can use more peak power for the same average.

Duty cycle is the share of time the transmitter is putting out power. A steady carrier has a high duty cycle; voice with pauses has a lower one.

Why not C? Low duty cycle lowers your average exposure, but it does not exempt you from evaluating.

G0A08

Which of the following steps must an amateur operator take to ensure compliance with RF safety regulations?

  1. A Post a copy of FCC Part 97.13 in the station
  2. B Notify neighbors within a 100-foot radius of the antenna of the existence of the station and power levels
  3. C Perform a routine RF exposure evaluation and prevent access to any identified high exposure areas
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Perform a routine RF exposure evaluation and prevent access to any identified high exposure areas

Why C is right. C. Section 97.13(c)(2) links two steps: evaluate your station, and where fields could exceed the limits in reachable areas, keep people out.

First you evaluate your station. If fields could be too strong where people can reach, you might fence off the area, move the antenna or lower power.

“If the routine environmental evaluation indicates that the RF electromagnetic fields could exceed the limits … in accessible areas, the licensee must take action to prevent human exposure”

47 CFR 97.13(c)(2)

Why not A? No rule requires posting a copy of the regulations in your station.

G0A09

What type of instrument can be used to accurately measure an RF field strength?

  1. A A receiver with digital signal processing (DSP) noise reduction
  2. B A calibrated field strength meter with a calibrated antenna
  3. C An SWR meter with a peak-reading function
  4. D An oscilloscope with a high-stability crystal marker generator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A calibrated field strength meter with a calibrated antenna

Why B is right. B. A true field reading needs both the sensing antenna and the meter calibrated; anything less gives only a rough comparison.

Calibration means the instrument has been checked against a known standard. Then its reading is a true value, such as volts per meter.

Why not C? An SWR meter measures power moving along a feed line, not the field in the air around you.

G0A10

What should be done if evaluation shows that a neighbor might experience more than the allowable limit of RF exposure from the main lobe of a directional antenna?

  1. A Change to a non-polarized antenna with higher gain
  2. B Use an antenna with a higher front-to-back ratio
  3. C Take precautions to ensure that the antenna cannot be pointed in their direction when they are present
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Take precautions to ensure that the antenna cannot be pointed in their direction when they are present

Why C is right. C. The main lobe is where the power is concentrated, so the fix is to keep it from pointing at the neighbor while they are there.

One way is to limit the rotator's range so the beam cannot swing toward the neighbor's home when they are there.

Why not A? Higher gain would concentrate more power, making the problem worse.

G0A11

What precaution should be taken if you install an indoor transmitting antenna?

  1. A Locate the antenna close to your operating position to minimize feed-line radiation
  2. B Position the antenna along the edge of a wall to reduce parasitic radiation
  3. C Make sure that MPE limits are not exceeded in occupied areas
  4. D Make sure the antenna is properly shielded
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Make sure that MPE limits are not exceeded in occupied areas

Why C is right. C. An indoor antenna puts its strongest fields in rooms where people live, so the exposure limits must be checked there.

MPE stands for maximum permissible exposure, the limit set for RF fields. Evaluate the fields in rooms people use, and lower power or move the antenna if needed.

Why not D? Shielding an antenna would stop it from radiating at all.

G0A12

What stations are subject to the FCC rules on RF exposure?

  1. A All commercial stations; amateur radio stations are exempt
  2. B Only stations with antennas lower than one wavelength above the ground
  3. C Only stations transmitting more than 500 watts PEP
  4. D All stations with a time-averaged transmission of more than one milliwatt
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All stations with a time-averaged transmission of more than one milliwatt

Why D is right. D. The exemption that applies at any distance, 1.1307(b)(3)(i)(A), stops at 1 mW time-averaged, so stronger stations fall under the rules.

The FCC exempts a single RF source only in limited cases. Amateur stations are not exempt as a class, and nearly every transmitter is well above 1 mW.

“The available maximum time-averaged power is no more than 1 mW, regardless of separation distance.”

47 CFR 1.1307(b)(3)(i)(A)

Why not A? Amateur stations are covered by the same RF exposure rules.
