E5B: Time constants and phase relationships: RL and RC time constants; phase angle in reactive circuits and components; admittance and susceptance

Group E5B of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E5B01

What is the term for the time required for the capacitor in an RC circuit to be charged to 63.2% of the applied voltage or to discharge to 36.8% of its initial voltage?

  1. A An exponential rate of one
  2. B One time constant
  3. C One exponential period
  4. D A time factor of one
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. One time constant

Why B is right. B. The time to charge to 63.2%, or discharge to 36.8%, is defined as one time constant.

For a resistor and capacitor, one time constant is R × C in seconds, with R in ohms and C in farads. In each time constant the capacitor covers 63.2% of the remaining gap to its final voltage. After about five time constants it is treated as fully charged or discharged.

Why not C? Exponential period is not a standard name for this interval.

E5B02

What letter is commonly used to represent susceptance?

  1. A G
  2. B X
  3. C Y
  4. D B
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. B

Why D is right. D. Susceptance is written with the letter B.

Admittance (Y) is the reciprocal of impedance: how easily a circuit passes AC. Like impedance it has two parts. The real part is conductance (G) and the imaginary part is susceptance (B), so Y = G + jB.

Why not B? X stands for reactance, the imaginary part of impedance.

E5B03

How is impedance in polar form converted to an equivalent admittance?

  1. A Take the reciprocal of the angle and change the sign of the magnitude
  2. B Take the reciprocal of the magnitude and change the sign of the angle
  3. C Take the square root of the magnitude and add 180 degrees to the angle
  4. D Square the magnitude and subtract 90 degrees from the angle
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Take the reciprocal of the magnitude and change the sign of the angle

Why B is right. B. Admittance is 1 divided by impedance, and dividing 1 by a polar number inverts its magnitude and flips the sign of its angle.

In polar form an impedance is a magnitude and an angle, such as 50 ohms at +30 degrees. Its reciprocal is 1/50 = 0.02 siemens at -30 degrees. Siemens is the unit of admittance.

Why not A? An angle has no useful reciprocal; the reciprocal applies to the magnitude.

E5B04

What is the time constant of a circuit having two 220-microfarad capacitors and two 1-megohm resistors, all in parallel?

  1. A 55 seconds
  2. B 110 seconds
  3. C 440 seconds
  4. D 220 seconds
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 220 seconds

Why D is right. D. Two equal resistors in parallel give 0.5 megohm, parallel capacitors add to 440 microfarads, and their product is 220 seconds.

A time constant is R × C. Two equal resistors in parallel have half the resistance of one. Capacitors in parallel add. Multiply the combined values, in ohms and farads, to get seconds.

T = R × C
R = (1 megohm × 1 megohm) / (1 megohm + 1 megohm) = 0.5 megohm = 500,000 ohms
C = 220 μF + 220 μF = 440 μF = 0.000440 F
T = 500,000 ohms × 0.000440 F = 220 s
= 220 seconds

Why not C? 440 seconds uses one 1-megohm resistor instead of two in parallel.

E5B05

What is the effect on the magnitude of pure reactance when it is converted to susceptance?

  1. A It is unchanged
  2. B The sign is reversed
  3. C It is shifted by 90 degrees
  4. D It is replaced by its reciprocal
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It is replaced by its reciprocal

Why D is right. D. Susceptance is the reciprocal of reactance, so its magnitude becomes 1 divided by the reactance.

Reactance (X) tells how much a coil or capacitor opposes AC; susceptance (B) tells how easily it passes AC. For a pure reactance, the size of B is 1/X. A 100-ohm reactance becomes 0.01 siemens of susceptance.

Why not B? The sign does flip, but the question asks about magnitude, which is inverted.

E5B06

What is susceptance?

  1. A The magnetic impedance of a circuit
  2. B The ratio of magnetic field to electric field
  3. C The imaginary part of admittance
  4. D A measure of the efficiency of a transformer
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The imaginary part of admittance

Why C is right. C. Susceptance is the imaginary part of admittance, the part due to inductors and capacitors.

Admittance is the reciprocal of impedance and is written Y = G + jB. G, conductance, is the real part from resistance. B, susceptance, is the imaginary part from reactance. Both are measured in siemens.

Why not A? Susceptance comes from capacitors as well as inductors, so it is not a magnetic quantity.

E5B07

What is the phase angle between the voltage across and the current through a series RLC circuit if XC is 500 ohms, R is 1 kilohm, and XL is 250 ohms?

  1. A 68.2 degrees with the voltage leading the current
  2. B 14.0 degrees with the voltage leading the current
  3. C 14.0 degrees with the voltage lagging the current
  4. D 68.2 degrees with the voltage lagging the current
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 14.0 degrees with the voltage lagging the current

Why C is right. C. Capacitive reactance is the larger, so the circuit acts capacitive, and the net reactance over resistance gives 14.0 degrees with voltage lagging.

In a series circuit the reactances subtract: 250 - 500 = -250 ohms, a net capacitive reactance. The phase angle is the angle whose tangent is net reactance divided by resistance. A negative angle means voltage lags current, as in a capacitor.

Phase angle = arctan((XL - XC) / R)
(250 ohms - 500 ohms) / 1,000 ohms = -0.25
arctan(-0.25) = -14.0 degrees
Negative angle: capacitive, so voltage lags current
= -14.0 degrees

Why not B? The size is right, but with more capacitive reactance the voltage lags rather than leads.

E5B08

What is the phase angle between the voltage across and the current through a series RLC circuit if XC is 300 ohms, R is 100 ohms, and XL is 100 ohms?

  1. A 63 degrees with the voltage lagging the current
  2. B 63 degrees with the voltage leading the current
  3. C 27 degrees with the voltage leading the current
  4. D 27 degrees with the voltage lagging the current
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 63 degrees with the voltage lagging the current

Why A is right. A. The net reactance is 200 ohms capacitive, twice the resistance, giving about 63 degrees with voltage lagging.

Subtract the reactances: 100 - 300 = -200 ohms, so capacitance wins. The tangent of the phase angle is net reactance over resistance. Because the net reactance is capacitive, current peaks first and voltage lags.

Phase angle = arctan((XL - XC) / R)
(100 ohms - 300 ohms) / 100 ohms = -2.0
arctan(-2.0) = -63.4 degrees, about -63 degrees
Negative angle: capacitive, so voltage lags current
= -63 degrees

Why not D? 27 degrees comes from flipping the ratio to resistance over reactance.

E5B09

What is the relationship between the AC current through a capacitor and the voltage across a capacitor?

  1. A Voltage and current are in phase
  2. B Voltage and current are 180 degrees out of phase
  3. C Voltage leads current by 90 degrees
  4. D Current leads voltage by 90 degrees
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Current leads voltage by 90 degrees

Why D is right. D. In a capacitor, current leads voltage by 90 degrees, because current must flow first to build up the charge that makes the voltage.

Capacitor current is greatest when the voltage is changing fastest, which is as the voltage crosses zero. The voltage peaks a quarter cycle later, when the current has fallen to zero. A quarter cycle is 90 degrees, so current leads.

Why not C? Voltage leading by 90 degrees describes an inductor.

E5B10

What is the relationship between the AC current through an inductor and the voltage across an inductor?

  1. A Voltage leads current by 90 degrees
  2. B Current leads voltage by 90 degrees
  3. C Voltage and current are 180 degrees out of phase
  4. D Voltage and current are in phase
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Voltage leads current by 90 degrees

Why A is right. A. In an inductor, voltage leads current by 90 degrees, because the coil opposes any change in current.

Inductor voltage is greatest when the current is changing fastest, which is as the current crosses zero. The current reaches its peak a quarter cycle later. So the voltage wave runs 90 degrees ahead of the current.

Why not B? Current leading voltage describes a capacitor.

E5B11

What is the phase angle between the voltage across and the current through a series RLC circuit if XC is 25 ohms, R is 100 ohms, and XL is 75 ohms?

  1. A 27 degrees with the voltage lagging the current
  2. B 27 degrees with the voltage leading the current
  3. C 63 degrees with the voltage lagging the current
  4. D 63 degrees with the voltage leading the current
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 27 degrees with the voltage leading the current

Why B is right. B. The net reactance is 50 ohms inductive, half the resistance, giving about 27 degrees with voltage leading.

Subtract capacitive from inductive reactance: 75 - 25 = +50 ohms. A positive result means the circuit acts inductive, so voltage leads current. The tangent of the angle is 50 / 100 = 0.5.

Phase angle = arctan((XL - XC) / R)
(75 ohms - 25 ohms) / 100 ohms = 0.5
arctan(0.5) = 26.6 degrees, about 27 degrees
Positive angle: inductive, so voltage leads current
= 27 degrees

Why not D? 63 degrees comes from inverting the ratio to resistance over reactance.

E5B12

What is admittance?

  1. A The inverse of impedance
  2. B The term for the gain of a field effect transistor
  3. C The inverse of reactance
  4. D The term for the on-impedance of a field effect transistor
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The inverse of impedance

Why A is right. A. Admittance is the reciprocal of impedance: it tells how easily a circuit lets AC flow.

Impedance, in ohms, measures opposition to AC. Admittance, Y = 1/Z, measures the opposite and is given in siemens. It is handy for parallel circuits, because the admittances of parallel branches add directly.

Why not C? The reciprocal of reactance alone is susceptance, only one part of admittance.
