G2C: CW operating procedures and procedural signals; Q signals; full break-in

Group G2C of the General pool (2023–2027) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G2C01

Which of the following describes full break-in CW operation (QSK)?

  1. A Breaking stations send the Morse code prosign “BK”
  2. B Automatic keyers, instead of hand keys, are used to send Morse code
  3. C An operator must activate a manual send/receive switch before and after every transmission
  4. D Transmitting stations can receive between code characters and elements
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Transmitting stations can receive between code characters and elements

Why D is right. D. With QSK, the radio switches back to receive in every tiny gap of your own sending.

In full break-in, transmit-receive switching is fast enough that you hear the band between each dot and dash. The other station can interrupt you mid-word by sending, and you will notice right away. Semi break-in, by contrast, waits for a longer pause in your sending before it switches to receive.

Why not A? BK is a prosign for handing over the turn, not a way of switching the radio.

G2C02

What should you do if a CW station sends “QRS?”

  1. A Send slower
  2. B Change frequency
  3. C Increase your power
  4. D Repeat everything twice
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Send slower

Why A is right. A. QRS is the Q signal about sending speed, and the other operator is asking you to slow down.

Q signals are three-letter codes that begin with Q. QRS means send more slowly. Strictly, adding a question mark turns it into an offer to slow down, but on the air it is almost always meant as a request. Slow your sending to a pace the other operator can copy.

Why not B? Asking for a frequency change is QSY, not QRS.

G2C03

What does it mean when a CW operator sends “KN” at the end of a transmission?

  1. A No US stations should call
  2. B Operating full break-in
  3. C Listening only for a specific station or stations
  4. D Closing station now
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Listening only for a specific station or stations

Why C is right. C. KN hands the turn to the station or stations already in the contact and asks everyone else not to break in.

At the end of a CW transmission, K alone invites any station to answer. KN, sent as one joined character, says go ahead, but only the station I am working. Use it when you do not want new callers to jump in.

Why not D? Closing a station is signaled with SK and CL, not KN.

G2C04

What does the Q signal “QRL?” mean?

  1. A “Will you keep the frequency clear?”
  2. B “Are you operating full break-in?” or “Can you operate full break-in?”
  3. C “Are you listening only for a specific station?”
  4. D “Are you busy?” or “Is this frequency in use?”
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. “Are you busy?” or “Is this frequency in use?”

Why D is right. D. QRL? checks whether someone is already using the frequency before you transmit.

Before calling CQ on CW, listen, then send QRL? and your call sign. If anyone replies, the frequency is taken and you should move. Without a question mark, QRL tells others that you are busy on that frequency.

Why not B? Full break-in is asked about with QSK, not QRL.

G2C05

What is the best speed to use when answering a CQ in Morse code?

  1. A The fastest speed at which you are comfortable copying, but no slower than the CQ
  2. B The fastest speed at which you are comfortable copying, but no faster than the CQ
  3. C At the standard calling speed of 10 wpm
  4. D At the standard calling speed of 5 wpm
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The fastest speed at which you are comfortable copying, but no faster than the CQ

Why B is right. B. Answer at your own comfortable pace, but do not outrun the calling station, since its speed shows what it can copy.

The speed of a CQ is a hint about the caller's skill. If you reply faster, the caller may not copy you. Slower is fine. You also need to copy the reply, so stay at a speed you are comfortable with.

Why not A? Sending faster than the CQ may go beyond what the caller can copy.

G2C06

What does the term “zero beat” mean in CW operation?

  1. A Matching the speed of the transmitting station
  2. B Operating split to avoid interference on frequency
  3. C Sending without error
  4. D Matching the transmit frequency to the frequency of a received signal
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Matching the transmit frequency to the frequency of a received signal

Why D is right. D. Zero beat means tuning until your transmit frequency lands exactly on the station you hear.

When two signals mix in your receiver, you hear a tone at the difference between their frequencies. As you tune closer, that tone drops in pitch until it disappears; that point is zero beat. Zero beating a CW station puts your reply where it is listening. Many radios have a button to help.

Why not A? Matching speed is about sending pace, not frequency.

G2C07

When sending CW, what does a “C” mean when added to the RST report?

  1. A Chirpy or unstable signal
  2. B Report was read from an S meter rather than estimated
  3. C 100 percent copy
  4. D Key clicks
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Chirpy or unstable signal

Why A is right. A. A C added to an RST report warns that the signal chirps, meaning its pitch shifts as it is keyed.

RST stands for readability, strength and tone. A letter added after the numbers flags a fault in the signal. C means chirp: the transmitter's frequency slides each time it keys, often from a weak power supply or an unstable oscillator. It tells the other operator something needs fixing.

Why not D? Key clicks are flagged with a different letter.

G2C08

What prosign is sent to indicate the end of a formal message when using CW?

  1. A SK
  2. B BK
  3. C AR
  4. D KN
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. AR

Why C is right. C. AR marks the end of a formal message in CW.

CW prosigns are pairs of letters sent as one character with no gap. AR ends a message. SK ends the whole contact. KN hands the turn to only the station you are working. BK is a quick hand-back during fast exchanges.

Why not A? SK closes out the contact, not a single message.

G2C09

What does the Q signal “QSL” mean?

  1. A Send slower
  2. B We have already confirmed the contact
  3. C I have received and understood
  4. D We have worked before
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. I have received and understood

Why C is right. C. QSL is a receipt: it confirms that the other station's last transmission came through.

Q signals are three-letter codes that start with Q. QSL acknowledges receipt. Cards that confirm a contact are called QSL cards because they serve the same purpose on paper. When asked QSL?, the other operator wants to know if you got the message.

Why not B? QSL cards take their name from the signal, but the signal itself confirms receipt, not a past contact.

G2C10

What does the Q signal “QRN” mean?

  1. A Send more slowly
  2. B Stop sending
  3. C Zero beat my signal
  4. D I am troubled by static
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. I am troubled by static

Why D is right. D. QRN reports trouble from static, the natural noise from sources such as lightning.

Q signals give short ways to describe band conditions. QRN refers to static, the crackle and crashes from storms and other natural sources. Interference from other stations is a different signal, QRM. Sending QRN tells the other station why you may miss parts of its message.

Why not B? Asking someone to stop sending is QRT, not QRN.

G2C11

What does the Q signal “QRV” mean?

  1. A You are sending too fast
  2. B There is interference on the frequency
  3. C I am quitting for the day
  4. D I am ready to receive
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. I am ready to receive

Why D is right. D. QRV says you are ready to copy, or ready to operate.

As a statement, QRV means you are ready. With a question mark, it asks whether the other station is ready. Net operators and others use it to show they are set up and active on a band or frequency.

Why not A? A complaint about speed is answered with QRS, not QRV.
