T7B: Symptoms, causes, and cures of common transmitter and receiver problems: overload and overdrive, distortion, interference and consumer electronics, RF feedback

Group T7B of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T7B01

What can you do if you are told your FM handheld or mobile transceiver is over-deviating?

  1. A Talk louder into the microphone
  2. B Let the transceiver cool off
  3. C Change to a higher power level
  4. D Talk farther away from the microphone
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Talk farther away from the microphone

Why D is right. D. Talk farther away from the microphone.

In FM, louder audio swings the signal's frequency further. Too much swing, called over-deviation, makes your signal wide and distorted. Backing away from the microphone lowers the audio level and the deviation.

Why not A? Talking louder increases deviation and makes the problem worse.

T7B02

What would cause a broadcast AM or FM radio to receive an amateur radio transmission unintentionally?

  1. A The receiver is unable to reject strong signals outside the AM or FM band
  2. B The microphone gain of the transmitter is turned up too high
  3. C The audio amplifier of the transmitter is overloaded
  4. D The deviation of an FM transmitter is set too low
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The receiver is unable to reject strong signals outside the AM or FM band

Why A is right. A. The receiver is unable to reject strong signals outside the AM or FM band.

Many home radios have weak filtering at their input. A strong nearby amateur signal can get in even though it is on a different frequency. The fault is in the receiver's design, not the transmitter.

Why not B? Microphone gain changes your audio, not whether a different receiver picks you up.

T7B03

Which of the following can cause radio frequency interference?

  1. A Fundamental overload
  2. B Harmonics
  3. C Spurious emissions
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

Interference can come from a strong signal swamping a nearby receiver (fundamental overload), from harmonics at multiples of your frequency, or from other unwanted spurious outputs. Each is a real cause.

T7B04

Which of the following might be the cause of low RF power output from a solid-state transceiver?

  1. A Poor amplifier noise figure
  2. B Poor amplifier linearity
  3. C Low SWR
  4. D High SWR
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. High SWR

Why D is right. D. High SWR.

High SWR means power is reflecting back from a poorly matched antenna system. Many solid-state radios sense this and cut their output to protect themselves, so you see low power.

Why not C? Low SWR means a good match, which lets the radio deliver full power.

T7B05

Which of the following might reduce interference by an amateur station to a non-amateur over-the-air radio receiver?

  1. A Block the amateur signal with a filter at the antenna input of the affected receiver
  2. B Block the interfering signal with a filter on the amateur transmitter
  3. C Switch the transmitter from FM to SSB
  4. D Switch the transmitter to a narrow-band mode
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Block the amateur signal with a filter at the antenna input of the affected receiver

Why A is right. A. Block the amateur signal with a filter at the antenna input of the affected receiver.

When a clean amateur signal overloads a nearby receiver, the fix is at the receiver. A filter on its antenna input passes the channels it should hear and blocks the strong amateur signal.

Why not B? When your own clean signal is what overloads the receiver, a transmitter filter can't remove it without blocking your signal too.

T7B06

Which of the following actions should you take if a neighbor tells you that your station’s transmissions are interfering with their radio or TV reception?

  1. A Make sure that your station is functioning properly and that it does not cause interference to your own radio or television when it is tuned to the same channel
  2. B Immediately turn off your transmitter and contact the nearest FCC office for assistance
  3. C Install a harmonic doubler on the output of your transmitter and tune it until the interference is eliminated
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Make sure that your station is functioning properly and that it does not cause interference to your own radio or television when it is tuned to the same channel

Why A is right. A. Make sure that your station is functioning properly and that it does not cause interference to your own radio or television when it is tuned to the same channel.

Start at home. If your own TV or radio on the same channel works fine while you transmit, your station is probably clean, and the problem likely lies in the neighbor's equipment.

Why not C? A harmonic doubler would create more harmonics, not fewer.

T7B07

Which of the following can reduce interference to a 2-meter band transceiver from a nearby commercial FM station?

  1. A Installing an RF preamplifier
  2. B Using double-shielded coaxial cable
  3. C Installing bypass capacitors on the microphone cable
  4. D Installing a band-reject filter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Installing a band-reject filter

Why D is right. D. Installing a band-reject filter.

A band-reject filter blocks a chosen range of frequencies and passes the rest. Set to block the FM broadcast band, it keeps the strong commercial station out of your 2-meter receiver.

Why not A? A preamplifier would boost the strong FM signal too and make overload worse.

T7B08

What should you do if something in a neighbor’s home is causing harmful interference to your amateur station?

  1. A Work with your neighbor to identify the offending device
  2. B Politely inform your neighbor that FCC rules prohibit the use of devices that cause interference
  3. C Make sure your station meets the standards of good amateur practice
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

Work with your neighbor to find the device, kindly let them know the rules on interference, and make sure your own station is in good order. Calm, friendly steps solve most problems.

T7B09

What should be the first step to resolve non-fiber optic cable TV interference caused by your amateur radio transmission?

  1. A Add a low-pass filter to the TV antenna input
  2. B Add a high-pass filter to the TV antenna input
  3. C Add a preamplifier to the TV antenna input
  4. D Be sure all TV feed line coaxial connectors are installed properly
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Be sure all TV feed line coaxial connectors are installed properly

Why D is right. D. Be sure all TV feed line coaxial connectors are installed properly.

Cable TV is a closed system, so your signal should not get in. When it does, a loose or poor connector is a common way in. Checking the connectors comes before adding filters.

Why not C? A preamplifier would boost the unwanted signal along with the TV signal.

T7B10

What might be a problem if you receive a report that your audio signal through an FM repeater is distorted or unintelligible?

  1. A Your transmitter is slightly off frequency
  2. B You are speaking too loudly or too close to the microphone
  3. C You are in a bad location
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

Each choice degrades your audio in its own way. Off frequency, part of your signal falls outside the repeater receiver's filter and gets clipped. Too loud or too close to the microphone, you over-deviate, so peaks splatter and distort. From a poor location, a weak signal arrives noisy or fluttering as you move.

T7B11

Which of the following can eliminate distorted voice transmissions?

  1. A Adding extra feedline to the antenna to lower SWR
  2. B Turning the radio on and off to reset the computer-controlled circuitry
  3. C Adding a clip-on ferrite “choke” to the microphone cable to prevent the transmitted signal from feeding back into the transmitter
  4. D Turning the squelch control fully clockwise to prevent the transmitted signal from triggering the squelch circuit
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Adding a clip-on ferrite “choke” to the microphone cable to prevent the transmitted signal from feeding back into the transmitter

Why C is right. C. Adding a clip-on ferrite “choke” to the microphone cable to prevent the transmitted signal from feeding back into the transmitter.

Your own RF can travel back along the microphone cable and mix into the audio, causing distortion. A ferrite choke clipped on the cable blocks RF on the cable while letting the audio pass.

Why not B? Restarting the radio does not stop RF from getting into the audio.
