G4D: Speech processors; S meters; sideband operation near band edges

Group G4D of the General pool (2023–2027) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G4D01

What is the purpose of a speech processor in a transceiver?

  1. A Increase the apparent loudness of transmitted voice signals
  2. B Increase transmitter bass response for more natural-sounding SSB signals
  3. C Prevent distortion of voice signals
  4. D Decrease high-frequency voice output to prevent out-of-band operation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Increase the apparent loudness of transmitted voice signals

Why A is right. A. It lifts the quieter parts of your speech closer to the peaks, so more of your voice is near full strength and listeners hear a stronger signal.

Voice has loud peaks and quieter parts between them. The transmitter's peak power stays the same with a processor; only the quieter parts come up.

Why not C? A processor can add distortion if set too high; it does not prevent it.

G4D02

How does a speech processor affect a single sideband phone signal?

  1. A It increases peak power
  2. B It increases average power
  3. C It reduces harmonic distortion
  4. D It reduces intermodulation distortion
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. It increases average power

Why B is right. B. The peaks stay at the transmitter's limit while the quieter syllables are raised, so the average power goes up.

An SSB transmitter's peak power is set by its design and your power setting. A processor squeezes the voice's range of loudness, filling in more of the space below the peaks.

Why not A? Peak power stays at the transmitter's limit; the processor fills in below the peaks.

G4D03

What is the effect of an incorrectly adjusted speech processor?

  1. A Distorted speech
  2. B Excess intermodulation products
  3. C Excessive background noise
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All three problems can come from a speech processor set wrong.

Too much processing distorts the voice. It can also overdrive later stages and create intermodulation products, extra signals that spread onto nearby frequencies. It raises background sounds, like a fan, as well as your voice. Set the processor with care and get on-air reports.

G4D04

What does an S meter measure?

  1. A Carrier suppression
  2. B Impedance
  3. C Received signal strength
  4. D Transmitter power output
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Received signal strength

Why C is right. C. An S meter shows the strength of the signal you are receiving.

The S meter reads in S units from S1 to S9, then in decibels above S9 for stronger signals. It gives a quick way to compare signals and to share signal reports.

Why not D? Transmitter power is measured with a wattmeter, not an S meter.

G4D05

How does a signal that reads 20 dB over S9 compare to one that reads S9 on a receiver, assuming a properly calibrated S meter?

  1. A It is 10 times less powerful
  2. B It is 20 times less powerful
  3. C It is 20 times more powerful
  4. D It is 100 times more powerful
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It is 100 times more powerful

Why D is right. D. 20 dB is a power ratio of 100, so the signal is 100 times stronger.

Decibels, or dB, compare two powers. Every 10 dB means the power is multiplied by 10. So 20 dB is 10 times 10, which is 100. A reading of 20 dB over S9 means 100 times the power of an S9 signal.

power ratio = 10^(dB / 10)
power ratio = 10^(20 / 10)
power ratio = 10^2 = 100
= 100 times

Why not C? Decibels are not a simple count; 20 dB is a factor of 100, not 20.

G4D06

How much change in signal strength is typically represented by one S unit?

  1. A 6 dB
  2. B 12 dB
  3. C 15 dB
  4. D 18 dB
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 6 dB

Why A is right. A. One S unit is typically 6 dB.

A calibrated S meter changes by one S unit for every 6 dB change in signal. A 6 dB change is about four times the power. Many radios are not calibrated exactly, so treat readings as rough.

Why not B? 12 dB would be two S units on a calibrated meter.

G4D07

How much must the power output of a transmitter be raised to change the S meter reading on a distant receiver from S8 to S9?

  1. A Approximately 1.5 times
  2. B Approximately 2 times
  3. C Approximately 4 times
  4. D Approximately 8 times
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Approximately 4 times

Why C is right. C. Going up one S unit means about 4 times the power.

From S8 to S9 is one S unit, which is 6 dB on a calibrated meter. Convert dB to a power ratio by raising 10 to the dB divided by 10. That gives about 4, so you would need to raise your power about four times.

power ratio = 10^(dB / 10)
S8 to S9 = 1 S unit = 6 dB
power ratio = 10^(6 / 10) = 10^0.6
power ratio = 3.98, about 4
= 4 times

Why not B? Doubling the power is only about 3 dB, half an S unit.

G4D08

What frequency range is occupied by a 3 kHz LSB signal when the displayed carrier frequency is set to 7.178 MHz?

  1. A 7.178 MHz to 7.181 MHz
  2. B 7.178 MHz to 7.184 MHz
  3. C 7.175 MHz to 7.178 MHz
  4. D 7.1765 MHz to 7.1795 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 7.175 MHz to 7.178 MHz

Why C is right. C. Lower sideband sits below the displayed carrier, so it runs from 7.175 MHz to 7.178 MHz.

The displayed frequency is where the suppressed carrier would be. A lower sideband signal extends below that point by its bandwidth. Here the bandwidth is 3 kHz, which is 0.003 MHz, so subtract it from 7.178 MHz to find the bottom edge.

lower edge = carrier - bandwidth
lower edge = 7.178 MHz - 0.003 MHz = 7.175 MHz
upper edge = carrier = 7.178 MHz
= 7.175 MHz to 7.178 MHz

Why not A? That range lies above the carrier, where an upper sideband signal would be.

G4D09

What frequency range is occupied by a 3 kHz USB signal with the displayed carrier frequency set to 14.347 MHz?

  1. A 14.347 MHz to 14.647 MHz
  2. B 14.347 MHz to 14.350 MHz
  3. C 14.344 MHz to 14.347 MHz
  4. D 14.3455 MHz to 14.3485 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 14.347 MHz to 14.350 MHz

Why B is right. B. Upper sideband sits above the displayed carrier, so it runs from 14.347 MHz to 14.350 MHz.

The displayed frequency marks the suppressed carrier. An upper sideband signal extends above that point by its bandwidth. Add 3 kHz, which is 0.003 MHz, to 14.347 MHz to find the top edge.

upper edge = carrier + bandwidth
lower edge = carrier = 14.347 MHz
upper edge = 14.347 MHz + 0.003 MHz = 14.350 MHz
= 14.347 MHz to 14.350 MHz

Why not C? That range lies below the carrier, where a lower sideband signal would be.

G4D10

How close to the lower edge of a band’s phone segment should your displayed carrier frequency be when using 3 kHz wide LSB?

  1. A At least 3 kHz above the edge of the segment
  2. B At least 3 kHz below the edge of the segment
  3. C At least 1 kHz below the edge of the segment
  4. D At least 1 kHz above the edge of the segment
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. At least 3 kHz above the edge of the segment

Why A is right. A. Keep the carrier at least 3 kHz above the lower edge so the whole signal stays inside.

A lower sideband signal extends below the displayed carrier frequency. If it is 3 kHz wide, its bottom edge is 3 kHz under the display. To keep that bottom edge inside the phone segment, set the display at least 3 kHz above the segment's lower edge.

Why not B? Below the edge, the entire signal would be outside the segment.

G4D11

How close to the upper edge of a band’s phone segment should your displayed carrier frequency be when using 3 kHz wide USB?

  1. A At least 3 kHz above the edge of the band
  2. B At least 3 kHz below the edge of the band
  3. C At least 1 kHz above the edge of the segment
  4. D At least 1 kHz below the edge of the segment
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. At least 3 kHz below the edge of the band

Why B is right. B. Keep the carrier at least 3 kHz below the upper edge so the whole signal stays inside.

An upper sideband signal extends above the displayed carrier frequency. If it is 3 kHz wide, its top edge is 3 kHz over the display. To keep that top edge inside the band, set the display at least 3 kHz below the upper edge.

Why not A? Above the edge, the entire signal would be out of the band.
