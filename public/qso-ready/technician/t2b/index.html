T2B: VHF/UHF operating practices: FM repeater, simplex, reverse splits; Access tones: CTCSS, DTMF; DMR operation; Resolving operating problems; Q signals

Group T2B of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 14 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T2B01

What is the purpose of the reverse function on a VHF/UHF transceiver?

  1. A To reduce power output
  2. B To increase power output
  3. C To listen on a repeater’s input frequency
  4. D To listen on a repeater’s output frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. To listen on a repeater’s input frequency

Why C is right. C. Reverse lets you listen on the repeater's input frequency.

The reverse button swaps your radio's transmit and receive frequencies. You then hear what stations send to the repeater. If you can hear the other station directly this way, you can move to simplex and free the repeater.

Why not D? You already hear the repeater's output in normal mode; reverse moves you to the input.

T2B02

What term describes the use of a sub-audible tone transmitted along with normal voice audio to open the squelch of a receiver?

  1. A Carrier squelch
  2. B Tone burst
  3. C DTMF
  4. D CTCSS
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. CTCSS

Why D is right. D. CTCSS is a low tone sent with your voice to open a receiver's squelch.

CTCSS stands for Continuous Tone-Coded Squelch System. It adds a steady low tone, below the voice range the radio passes, that receivers filter out of the speaker audio. A repeater set for that tone opens only when it hears it, so it ignores other signals on the same frequency.

Why not B? A tone burst is a short tone at the start of a transmission, not one that runs the whole time.

T2B03

Which of the following describes a linked repeater network?

  1. A A network of repeaters in which signals received by one repeater are transmitted by all the repeaters in the network
  2. B A single repeater with more than one receiver
  3. C Multiple repeaters with the same control operator
  4. D A system of repeaters linked by APRS
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A network of repeaters in which signals received by one repeater are transmitted by all the repeaters in the network

Why A is right. A. In a linked network, whatever one repeater hears, all of them transmit.

Repeaters can be tied together by radio links or the internet. When you talk into one, your signal comes out of every repeater in the network. This lets you reach people far beyond one repeater's range.

Why not D? APRS is a position and data reporting system, not the thing that defines a linked network.

T2B04

Which of the following could be the reason you are unable to access a repeater whose output you can hear?

  1. A Improper transceiver offset
  2. B You are using the wrong CTCSS tone
  3. C You are using the wrong DCS code
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

Hearing a repeater only proves your receive side is right. To get in, your radio must also transmit on the correct input, set by the offset. Many repeaters also require a specific CTCSS tone or DCS code. Any one of these set wrong keeps the repeater from responding.

T2B05

Which of the following would cause your FM transmission audio to drop out on voice peaks?

  1. A Your repeater offset is inverted
  2. B Your FM deviation is too low
  3. C You are talking too loudly
  4. D Your transmit power is too high
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. You are talking too loudly

Why C is right. C. Talking too loudly can make FM audio drop out on voice peaks.

In FM, louder audio swings the signal farther from its center frequency. This swing is called deviation. Too much deviation pushes the signal outside the receiver's passband, so the loudest parts of your voice break up or vanish. Speak at a normal level with the microphone a few inches away.

Why not B? Deviation that is too low makes you sound quiet, not choppy on peaks.

T2B06

What type of signaling to a repeater uses two simultaneous audio tones?

  1. A DTMF
  2. B CTCSS
  3. C GMRS
  4. D D-STAR
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. DTMF

Why A is right. A. DTMF uses two tones at the same time.

DTMF stands for Dual-Tone Multi-Frequency. Each key on the keypad sends a pair of tones together, the same system a touch-tone phone uses. Repeaters use these codes for control functions.

Why not B? CTCSS is a single low tone, not a pair.

Remember: Dual-Tone is right there in the name.

T2B07

How can you join a digital repeater’s “talkgroup”?

  1. A Register with the local Frequency Coordinator
  2. B Register with the digital repeater control operator
  3. C Program your radio with the group’s ID or code
  4. D Program your radio with the DTMF tone used by the talkgroup
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Program your radio with the group’s ID or code

Why C is right. C. Program the talkgroup's ID or code into your radio.

On a digital repeater system, a talkgroup is picked by a number. When your radio is set to that ID, your transmissions go to that group and you hear the group's traffic.

Why not D? DTMF tones are an analog control method; talkgroups are chosen by a digital ID.

T2B08

Which of the following applies when two stations transmitting on the same frequency interfere with each other?

  1. A The stations should negotiate continued use of the frequency
  2. B Both stations should choose another frequency to avoid conflict
  3. C Whichever station was on the frequency first has preemptive rights to the frequency
  4. D Use subaudible tones so both stations can share the frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The stations should negotiate continued use of the frequency

Why A is right. A. The two stations should work out how to share or settle the frequency.

No one owns an amateur frequency. Being there first is a courtesy, not a legal right. The rules require operators to cooperate, so the stations should talk it over and agree on a solution.

“Each station licensee and each control operator must cooperate in selecting transmitting channels … No frequency will be assigned for the exclusive use of any station.”

47 CFR 97.101(b)

Why not C? Being first earns courtesy, but the rules give no station exclusive use.

T2B09

Why are simplex channels designated in the VHF/UHF band plans?

  1. A So stations within range of each other can communicate without tying up a repeater
  2. B For contest operation
  3. C For working DX only
  4. D So stations with simple transmitters can access the repeater without automated offset
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. So stations within range of each other can communicate without tying up a repeater

Why A is right. A. Simplex channels let nearby stations talk without tying up a repeater.

If two stations can hear each other directly, they do not need a repeater. Moving to a simplex channel leaves the repeater free for people who need its wider coverage. This is a voluntary band-plan convention, not an FCC rule.

Why not D? Simplex channels are not repeater channels at all; no repeater is used.

T2B10

Which Q signal indicates that you are receiving interference from other stations?

  1. A QRM
  2. B QRN
  3. C QTH
  4. D QSB
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. QRM

Why A is right. A. QRM means interference from other stations.

Q signals are three-letter codes that start with Q. QRM means interference from other stations, such as signals on or near your frequency.

Why not B? QRN refers to natural noise, such as static from lightning.

T2B11

Which Q signal indicates that you are changing frequency?

  1. A QRU
  2. B QSY
  3. C QSL
  4. D QRZ
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. QSY

Why B is right. B. QSY means you are changing frequency.

Operators often say QSY when moving a conversation to a new frequency, for example from a calling frequency to a quieter one.

Why not C? QSL is a confirmation that you received something.

T2B12

What is the digital color code used on DMR repeater systems?

  1. A An access code which must be programmed into a DMR transmitter to access a specific repeater
  2. B A code which automatically programs the repeater offset to a frequency chosen by the user
  3. C A code which identifies the specific CODEC being used so that the repeater can properly decode the audio
  4. D A code transmitted by the repeater to indicate health and status of the equipment
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. An access code which must be programmed into a DMR transmitter to access a specific repeater

Why A is right. A. The color code is an access code your DMR radio must match to use a repeater.

DMR is a digital voice mode. Each DMR repeater has a color code, a number your radio must be set to. If the number does not match, the repeater ignores you. It works a bit like a CTCSS tone does on analog FM.

Why not B? The color code has nothing to do with setting the offset.

T2B13

What is the purpose of a squelch function?

  1. A Reduce a CW transmitter's key clicks
  2. B Mute the receiver audio when a signal is not present
  3. C Eliminate parasitic oscillations in an RF amplifier
  4. D Reduce interference from impulse noise
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Mute the receiver audio when a signal is not present

Why B is right. B. Squelch mutes the speaker when no signal is present.

Without squelch, an FM receiver plays loud hiss between transmissions. Squelch keeps the audio off until a signal strong enough to open it arrives.

Why not D? Reducing impulse noise is the job of a noise blanker, not squelch.

T2B14

Which of the following is a “talkgroup”?

  1. A A group of amateur radio operators who share email on a specific amateur radio topic
  2. B An identifier used by DMR to organize radio traffic so that those who want to hear the group aren’t bothered by other radio traffic
  3. C The members of a net organized for amateur radio operators with a specific common interest
  4. D A method of automatically alerting all group members to the frequency where any member is transmitting
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. An identifier used by DMR to organize radio traffic so that those who want to hear the group aren’t bothered by other radio traffic

Why B is right. B. A talkgroup is a DMR identifier that sorts radio traffic into groups.

Many conversations can share one DMR network. Each talkgroup has its own ID. Your radio passes along only the talkgroup you have selected, so you do not hear everyone else's traffic.

Why not C? A net is a scheduled on-air gathering, not a digital routing ID.
