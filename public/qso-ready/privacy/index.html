Privacy Policy

QSO Ready (Android app com.saurabh7973.hamexam) · Effective 3 November 2026 Developer: Saurabh Upadhyay · Contact: hello@saurabhupadhyay.in

QSO Ready helps you study for the US amateur radio license exams and learn Morse code. It has no account and no ads, and it never asks for your name, email or location. This policy explains, in plain words, exactly what leaves your phone and why.

What stays on your phone

Everything you do in the app is stored only on your device: the license classes you chose, your exam date, every answer, your scores and practice-exam history, review schedules, missed and flagged questions, your pass-chance estimate, Morse progress and settings, and your reminder time. We never receive any of it.

If Android backup is on, your phone also saves this progress in your own Google account backup, so it comes back on a new phone. That backup is managed by Google under your Google account; we can't see it.

What leaves your phone

The app uses three services. Each one receives only what is listed here, always over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection.

1. Google Analytics for Firebase (optional usage counts)

  • What: anonymous counts of what happens in the app: for example, that a practice exam was started or finished, which question pool it used, a score band such as "80-89", whether a Morse lesson was passed, which calculator was used, and whether a purchase succeeded. Firebase also adds a random app-instance ID, your app and Android version, device model, language, and an approximate country, region or city that Google works out from your IP address.
  • Never sent: your answers, question text or question numbers, anything you type, your exam date, call signs, or the advertising ID (the app has no advertising ID permission).
  • Your choice: the "Share usage counts and crash reports" switch is on the last setup screen and in Settings. It starts off on every phone, and nothing is sent unless you turn it on. Until you finish setup, nothing is sent. When it's off, no usage counts are sent at all. You can change it any time.
  • Why: to see which features help people pass and fix what doesn't.

2. Firebase Crashlytics (crash reports)

  • What: when the app crashes or an internal error happens, a report with the technical error details, your app and Android version, device model and state (such as free memory), a random Crashlytics installation ID and the time.
  • Never sent: anything you typed or answered.
  • Your choice: the same "Share usage counts and crash reports" switch controls crash reports. Until you finish setup, and whenever the switch is off, no crash reports are sent. Google keeps them for 90 days.

3. RevenueCat and Google Play (only for the Pro unlock)

  • What: to check whether Pro is unlocked, and when you buy or restore it, RevenueCat receives a random app user ID made by the app, the Google Play purchase token, order number and product, purchase time, price and country, your device type and Android version, and, as with any internet request, your IP address. RevenueCat processes this for us only to verify and restore your purchase.
  • Never sent to us or RevenueCat: your payment details. Payment happens inside Google Play under Google's privacy policy.

Google Play rating card

At a good moment (for example after you tap "I passed!"), the app may ask Google Play to show its rating card. Your rating goes to Google Play; the app is not told whether or how you rated.

Email you choose to send

"Send feedback" and "Report an error" open your own email app with a draft to hello@saurabhupadhyay.in. A feedback draft starts with your app version, Android version and phone model; an error report includes the question id, the pool, and the content and app versions. You can edit or delete any of it, and nothing is sent unless you press Send. We use your email only to reply and delete it when the issue is closed.

Content updates

When content updates are switched on, the app checks a public file server (jsDelivr, or GitHub as a fallback) at most once a day for corrected questions or explanations. The request carries no ID or personal data; like any download, the server sees your IP address.

Links

Links such as this policy open in your browser, which handles them under its own policy.

What we don't do

No account or sign-in. No ads and no advertising ID. No sale or sharing of data. No location, microphone, camera, contacts or file permissions. Google and RevenueCat process the data above as our service providers, not for their own advertising.

Children

QSO Ready is meant for people aged 13 and over. It is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. If you think a child under 13 has sent us information, email us and we'll delete it.

Deleting your data

  1. Everything on your phone: uninstall QSO Ready, or go to Android Settings → Apps → QSO Ready → Storage → Clear storage. This deletes your progress and the app's random IDs. To delete your Android backup copy, manage backups in your Google account settings.
  2. Usage counts (Firebase Analytics): turn off "Share usage counts and crash reports" in Settings to stop them. Because they carry no name or account, we can't look them up by person; they are deleted automatically after 2 months. If you send us a request, we'll delete whatever we can identify.
  3. Crash reports: deleted automatically after 90 days.
  4. Purchase records (RevenueCat): email hello@saurabhupadhyay.in with your Google Play order number (it starts with "GPA." and is in your Google Play receipt). We'll delete that record from RevenueCat within 30 days and confirm. Your purchase stays valid with Google Play and can be restored. Google Play keeps its own record of the purchase; for refunds or that record, use Google Play.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date above and the app's store listing before the change takes effect.
