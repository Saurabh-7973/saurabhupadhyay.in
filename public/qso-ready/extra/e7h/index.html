E7H: Oscillators and signal sources: types of oscillators; synthesizers and phase-locked loops; direct digital synthesizers; stabilizing thermal drift; microphonics; high-accuracy oscillators

Group E7H of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 13 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E7H01

What are three common oscillator circuits?

  1. A Taft, Pierce, and negative feedback
  2. B Pierce, Fenner, and Beane
  3. C Taft, Hartley, and Pierce
  4. D Colpitts, Hartley, and Pierce
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Colpitts, Hartley, and Pierce

Why D is right. D. Colpitts, Hartley and Pierce are standard oscillator designs, each named for its inventor and set apart by how it feeds energy back.

An oscillator needs positive feedback: part of the output returned to the input in phase. A Colpitts takes its feedback from a pair of capacitors, a Hartley from a tapped coil, and a Pierce from a quartz crystal. The other names in the choices are not standard oscillator types.

Why not C? Taft is not an oscillator circuit, so C fails even though it includes Hartley and Pierce.

E7H02

What is a microphonic?

  1. A An IC used for amplifying microphone signals
  2. B Distortion caused by RF pickup on the microphone cable
  3. C Changes in oscillator frequency caused by mechanical vibration
  4. D Excess loading of the microphone by an oscillator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Changes in oscillator frequency caused by mechanical vibration

Why C is right. C. A microphonic is a frequency change caused when vibration physically moves oscillator parts and shifts their capacitance or inductance.

Tapping the radio or a loud sound nearby can shake coil turns or capacitor plates slightly. The tuned circuit's values change, so the frequency changes. The signal then wobbles in step with the vibration, which can be heard as a ringing or warble.

Why not B? RF picked up on a microphone cable is feedback or interference, not a mechanical effect on the oscillator.

E7H03

What is a phase-locked loop?

  1. A An electronic servo loop consisting of a ratio detector, reactance modulator, and voltage-controlled oscillator
  2. B An electronic circuit also known as a monostable multivibrator
  3. C An electronic servo loop consisting of a phase detector, a low-pass filter, a voltage-controlled oscillator, and a stable reference oscillator
  4. D An electronic circuit consisting of a precision push-pull amplifier with a differential phase input
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. An electronic servo loop consisting of a phase detector, a low-pass filter, a voltage-controlled oscillator, and a stable reference oscillator

Why C is right. C. A PLL compares a controllable oscillator's phase with a stable reference and steers it until the two stay locked.

The phase detector puts out a voltage that depends on the phase difference between the two signals. The low-pass filter smooths that voltage, which then tunes the voltage-controlled oscillator, or VCO. Once locked, the VCO is as stable as the reference.

Why not A? A ratio detector and reactance modulator are FM parts, not the elements of a phase-locked loop.

E7H04

How is positive feedback supplied in a Colpitts oscillator?

  1. A Through a tapped coil
  2. B Through link coupling
  3. C Through a capacitive divider
  4. D Through a neutralizing capacitor
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Through a capacitive divider

Why C is right. C. A Colpitts oscillator takes its feedback from the point between two capacitors in series across the tuned circuit.

The two capacitors split the tuned circuit's voltage, much like a voltage divider. The point between them feeds part of the signal back to the amplifier's input. The ratio of the two capacitors sets how much feedback the circuit gets.

Why not A? Feedback through a tapped coil is the Hartley method.

E7H05

How is positive feedback supplied in a Pierce oscillator?

  1. A Through a tapped coil
  2. B Through link coupling
  3. C Through a neutralizing capacitor
  4. D Through a quartz crystal
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Through a quartz crystal

Why D is right. D. In a Pierce oscillator a quartz crystal sits in the feedback path and passes energy only very near its resonant frequency.

The crystal connects between the amplifier's output and input. Along with two small capacitors to ground, it gives the phase shift needed for oscillation. Because quartz resonates very sharply, the frequency is very stable.

Why not A? A tapped coil gives feedback in a Hartley oscillator.

E7H06

Which of these functions can be performed by a phase-locked loop?

  1. A Wide-band AF and RF power amplification
  2. B Frequency synthesis and FM demodulation
  3. C Photovoltaic conversion and optical coupling
  4. D Comparison of two digital input signals and digital pulse counting
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Frequency synthesis and FM demodulation

Why B is right. B. A PLL can generate many stable frequencies from one reference, and its control voltage follows FM, so it can also demodulate.

Place a frequency divider between the VCO and the phase detector, and the loop locks the VCO to a multiple of the reference; changing the divider changes the output frequency. When the loop locks to an FM signal, its control voltage rises and falls with the modulation, recovering the audio.

Why not D? Counting pulses and comparing digital words are jobs for counters and logic, not a phase-locked loop.

E7H07

How can an oscillator’s microphonic responses be reduced?

  1. A Use NP0 capacitors
  2. B Reduce noise on the oscillator’s power supply
  3. C Increase the gain
  4. D Mechanically isolate the oscillator circuitry from its enclosure
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Mechanically isolate the oscillator circuitry from its enclosure

Why D is right. D. Mechanically isolating the oscillator keeps vibration from reaching its parts, so their values don't shift.

Shock mounts, rubber spacers and solid construction keep vibration from flexing coils and capacitors. Fixing coil windings in place with cement also helps. Electrical changes do not fix what is a mechanical problem.

Why not A? NP0 capacitors fight drift caused by temperature, not vibration.

E7H08

Which of the following components can be used to reduce thermal drift in crystal oscillators?

  1. A NP0 capacitors
  2. B Toroidal inductors
  3. C Wirewound resistors
  4. D Non-inductive resistors
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. NP0 capacitors

Why A is right. A. NP0 capacitors keep nearly the same value as temperature changes, so they don't pull the oscillator's frequency as the circuit warms.

NP0, also written C0G, means negative-positive-zero: its temperature coefficient is near zero. Many ordinary ceramic capacitors change value with heat, which retunes the oscillator. Using NP0 parts in frequency-setting spots keeps drift small.

Why not B? Toroidal inductors are chosen for their contained magnetic field, not for temperature stability.

E7H09

What type of frequency synthesizer circuit uses a phase accumulator, lookup table, digital-to-analog converter, and a low-pass anti-alias filter?

  1. A A direct digital synthesizer
  2. B A hybrid synthesizer
  3. C A phase-locked loop synthesizer
  4. D A direct conversion synthesizer
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A direct digital synthesizer

Why A is right. A. A direct digital synthesizer steps through phase, looks up the matching amplitude, converts it to voltage and filters the result into a clean wave.

The phase accumulator adds a set number every clock cycle, stepping around the wave's cycle. The lookup table turns each phase into a sample value. The digital-to-analog converter outputs those values as voltages, and the low-pass filter removes clock-related images. A bigger step size gives a higher frequency.

Why not C? A phase-locked loop uses a phase detector and VCO, not a lookup table.

E7H10

What information is contained in the lookup table of a direct digital synthesizer (DDS)?

  1. A The phase relationship between a reference oscillator and the output waveform
  2. B Amplitude values that represent the desired waveform
  3. C The phase relationship between a voltage-controlled oscillator and the output waveform
  4. D Frequently used receiver and transmitter frequencies
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Amplitude values that represent the desired waveform

Why B is right. B. The DDS lookup table holds amplitude samples of one cycle of the wanted waveform, usually a sine wave.

Each phase value from the accumulator points to a table entry, and that entry is the wave's height at that point in the cycle. The digital-to-analog converter turns these numbers into voltages, building the wave one sample at a time.

Why not D? Stored operating frequencies belong to a radio's memory channels, not to the waveform table.

E7H11

What are the major spectral impurity components of direct digital synthesizers?

  1. A Broadband noise
  2. B Digital conversion noise
  3. C Spurious signals at discrete frequencies
  4. D Harmonics of the local oscillator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Spurious signals at discrete frequencies

Why C is right. C. A DDS mainly produces spurs: separate unwanted tones caused by rounding of phase and amplitude values and by converter errors.

A DDS builds its output from a limited number of steps. Dropping phase bits and rounding amplitudes create small errors that repeat. Repeating errors show up as distinct tones at fixed frequencies, not as a smooth noise floor. Their size and spacing depend on the frequency chosen.

Why not A? The errors repeat, so they land at discrete frequencies instead of spreading as broadband noise.

E7H12

Which of the following ensures that a crystal oscillator operates on the frequency specified by the crystal manufacturer?

  1. A Provide the crystal with a specified parallel inductance
  2. B Provide the crystal with a specified parallel capacitance
  3. C Bias the crystal at a specified voltage
  4. D Bias the crystal at a specified current
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Provide the crystal with a specified parallel capacitance

Why B is right. B. A crystal's exact frequency depends on the capacitance across it, so the maker specifies the parallel load capacitance it was cut for.

In many oscillators the crystal works with circuit capacitance in parallel. Change that capacitance and the frequency shifts slightly. Matching the specified load, such as 20 pF, puts the oscillator on its marked frequency.

Why not A? The maker specifies a load capacitance, not an inductance, for parallel-mode crystals.

E7H13

Which of the following is a technique for providing highly accurate and stable oscillators needed for microwave transmission and reception?

  1. A Use a GPS signal reference
  2. B Use a rubidium stabilized reference oscillator
  3. C Use a temperature-controlled high Q dielectric resonator
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. GPS references, rubidium standards and temperature-controlled dielectric resonators each give the stability microwave work needs.

At 10 GHz, an error of one part per million is 10 kHz, enough to miss a narrow signal. GPS-disciplined oscillators steer to the atomic clocks on the satellites. Rubidium standards use an atomic resonance. Temperature-controlled high-Q dielectric resonators hold their frequency steady by holding temperature steady.
