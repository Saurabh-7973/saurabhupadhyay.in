T3A: Radio wave characteristics: how a radio signal travels, fading, multipath, polarization, wavelength vs absorption; Antenna orientation

Group T3A of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T3A01

Why do VHF signal strengths sometimes vary greatly when the antenna is moved only a few feet?

  1. A The signal path encounters different concentrations of water vapor
  2. B VHF ionospheric propagation is very sensitive to path length
  3. C Multipath propagation cancels or reinforces signals
  4. D The Doppler effect causes slight frequency shifts which result in changes in signal strength
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Multipath propagation cancels or reinforces signals

Why C is right. C. Multipath signals add up or cancel depending on where the antenna is.

VHF signals bounce off buildings, hills and vehicles, so several copies reach you by different routes. At some spots the copies arrive in step and get stronger. A few feet away they may arrive out of step and weaken each other.

Why not B? VHF signals usually travel by line of sight, not by the ionosphere.

T3A02

How does vegetation affect UHF and microwave signals?

  1. A Causes knife-edge diffraction, distorting voice peaks
  2. B Absorbs signals, leading to poor reception of weak signals
  3. C Amplifies signals, improving reception of weak signals
  4. D Has no effect
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Absorbs signals, leading to poor reception of weak signals

Why B is right. B. Vegetation absorbs UHF and microwave signals, which hurts weak-signal reception.

Leaves and branches hold a lot of water. Water soaks up energy at UHF and microwave frequencies, so signals passing through trees come out weaker.

Why not C? Plants cannot add energy to a signal, so they cannot amplify it.

T3A03

What antenna polarization is normally used for long-distance CW and SSB contacts on the VHF and UHF bands?

  1. A Right-hand circular
  2. B Left-hand circular
  3. C Horizontal
  4. D Vertical
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Horizontal

Why C is right. C. Horizontal polarization is the norm for long-distance CW and SSB on VHF and UHF.

For weak-signal work, most operators use horizontal antennas. If you match their polarization, you avoid a large signal loss. FM and repeaters mostly use vertical, which is why the answer depends on the mode.

Why not D? Vertical is common for FM and repeater use, not for long-distance CW and SSB.

T3A04

What is the effect of antenna cross-polarization over a line-of-sight VHF or UHF path?

  1. A Modulation sidebands might become inverted
  2. B Received signal strength is reduced
  3. C Signals have an echo effect
  4. D Nothing significant will happen
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Received signal strength is reduced

Why B is right. B. Cross-polarized antennas receive a much weaker signal.

On a direct VHF or UHF path, the wave keeps its polarization. If one antenna is vertical and the other horizontal, the receiving antenna picks up only a small part of the signal.

Why not D? The loss can be large, so the effect is significant.

T3A05

When using a directional antenna, how might your station be able to communicate with a distant repeater if buildings or obstructions are blocking the direct line of sight path?

  1. A Change from vertical to horizontal polarization
  2. B Try to find a path that reflects signals to the repeater
  3. C Try the long path
  4. D Increase the antenna SWR
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Try to find a path that reflects signals to the repeater

Why B is right. B. Look for a path that bounces your signal to the repeater.

With a directional antenna you can aim at a building or hill that reflects radio waves. The bounced signal may reach the repeater even when the straight path is blocked.

Why not D? Raising the SWR wastes power and does not help you reach the repeater.

T3A06

What is the meaning of the term “picket fencing”?

  1. A Alternating transmissions during a net operation
  2. B Rapid flutter on mobile signals due to multipath propagation
  3. C A type of ground system used with vertical antennas
  4. D Interference from cable TV in the form of carriers at fixed intervals across the band
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Rapid flutter on mobile signals due to multipath propagation

Why B is right. B. Picket fencing is a fast flutter on mobile signals caused by multipath.

As a vehicle moves, it passes through spots where reflected signals add up and spots where they cancel. The result is a quick, choppy flutter in the audio.

Why not A? Taking turns on a net has nothing to do with this term.

Remember: Picture driving past a fence: open gap, post, open gap, post.

T3A07

What weather condition might decrease range at microwave frequencies?

  1. A High winds
  2. B Low barometric pressure
  3. C Precipitation
  4. D Colder temperatures
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Precipitation

Why C is right. C. Precipitation can shorten range at microwave frequencies.

Raindrops and other water in the air absorb and scatter energy at microwave frequencies. Heavy rain can noticeably weaken a microwave signal.

Why not A? Wind does not change how radio waves pass through the air.

T3A08

What is a likely cause of irregular fading of signals propagated by the ionosphere?

  1. A Frequency shift due to Faraday rotation
  2. B Interference from thunderstorms
  3. C Intermodulation distortion
  4. D Random combining of signals arriving via different paths
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Random combining of signals arriving via different paths

Why D is right. D. Signals arriving by different paths combine at random.

Signals from the ionosphere may reach you after one, two or more bounces. The ionosphere keeps shifting, so the copies drift in and out of step. Their sum rises and falls in an irregular way, which you hear as fading.

Why not B? Thunderstorms cause static crashes, not irregular fading.

T3A09

Which of the following results from the fact that signals propagated by the ionosphere are elliptically polarized?

  1. A Digital modes are unusable
  2. B Either vertically or horizontally polarized antennas may be used for transmission or reception
  3. C FM voice is unusable
  4. D Both the transmitting and receiving antennas must be of the same polarization
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Either vertically or horizontally polarized antennas may be used for transmission or reception

Why B is right. B. Either a vertical or a horizontal antenna can be used.

After passing through the ionosphere, a signal's polarization twists and keeps changing, so it is elliptical. It has some vertical and some horizontal part, so neither antenna orientation loses out for long.

Why not D? Matching polarization matters on direct line-of-sight paths, not on ionospheric ones.

T3A10

What effect does multi-path propagation have on data transmissions?

  1. A Transmission rates must be increased by a factor equal to the number of separate paths observed
  2. B Transmission rates must be decreased by a factor equal to the number of separate paths observed
  3. C No significant changes will occur if the signals are transmitted using FM
  4. D Error rates are likely to increase
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Error rates are likely to increase

Why D is right. D. Multipath makes data errors more likely.

Copies of a data signal arrive at slightly different times. The delayed copies smear into the next bits and confuse the receiver, so more bits are decoded wrong.

Why not A? Raising the data rate makes the timing smear worse, not better.

T3A11

Which region of the atmosphere can reflect HF radio waves?

  1. A The stratosphere
  2. B The troposphere
  3. C The ionosphere
  4. D The electrosphere
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The ionosphere

Why C is right. C. The ionosphere can reflect HF radio waves.

The ionosphere is a high layer of air charged by sunlight. It bends HF waves back toward Earth, which lets them travel far beyond the horizon.

Why not B? The troposphere is the lowest layer, where weather happens; it does not reflect HF.

T3A12

What effect does fog or rain have on 10-meter and 6-meter band signals?

  1. A Absorption
  2. B Little effect
  3. C Deflection
  4. D Increased range
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Little effect

Why B is right. B. Fog and rain have little effect on 10- and 6-meter signals.

At these lower frequencies the wavelength is several meters long, far bigger than water drops. The drops barely interact with the waves, so rain and fog do little. That changes at microwave frequencies.

Why not A? Absorption by rain is a problem at microwave frequencies, not at 10 or 6 meters.
