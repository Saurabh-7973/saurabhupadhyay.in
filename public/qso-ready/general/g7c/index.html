G7C: Transceiver design; filters; oscillators; digital signal processing (DSP)

Group G7C of the General pool (2023–2027) has 14 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G7C01

What circuit is used to select one of the sidebands from a balanced modulator?

  1. A Carrier oscillator
  2. B Filter
  3. C IF amplifier
  4. D RF amplifier
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Filter

Why B is right. B. A filter picks out one sideband from the balanced modulator's output.

A balanced modulator makes two sidebands, one above and one below the suppressed carrier frequency. To get single sideband, a narrow filter passes the wanted sideband and blocks the other. Crystal filters are often used because their edges are very steep.

Why not A? The carrier oscillator feeds the modulator; it cannot remove a sideband.

G7C02

What output is produced by a balanced modulator?

  1. A Frequency modulated RF
  2. B Audio with equalized frequency response
  3. C Audio extracted from the modulation signal
  4. D Double-sideband modulated RF
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Double-sideband modulated RF

Why D is right. D. A balanced modulator puts out double-sideband RF with the carrier suppressed.

It mixes audio with an RF carrier and then cancels the carrier itself. What remains are the upper and lower sidebands. A filter later removes one of them to make SSB.

Why not A? Frequency modulation comes from changing an oscillator's frequency, not from a balanced modulator.

G7C03

What is one reason to use an impedance matching transformer at a transmitter output?

  1. A To minimize transmitter power output
  2. B To present the desired impedance to the transmitter and feed line
  3. C To reduce power supply ripple
  4. D To minimize radiation resistance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. To present the desired impedance to the transmitter and feed line

Why B is right. B. A transmitter delivers full power safely only into the load it was designed for, so the transformer converts the antenna system's impedance to that value.

Many transmitters are designed for 50 ohms. With the impedance matched, power moves from transmitter to feed line with little reflected back.

Why not A? The goal is to deliver power well, not to reduce output.

G7C04

How is a product detector used?

  1. A Used in test gear to detect spurious mixing products
  2. B Used in a transmitter to perform frequency multiplication
  3. C Used in an FM receiver to filter out unwanted sidebands
  4. D Used in a single sideband receiver to extract the modulated signal
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Used in a single sideband receiver to extract the modulated signal

Why D is right. D. SSB arrives with no carrier, so the detector mixes it with a locally made carrier to get the audio back.

A plain AM detector needs a carrier in the signal, so it cannot decode SSB. The product detector's carrier comes from an oscillator in the receiver, and the difference between the two is the original audio.

Why not C? FM receivers use discriminators or similar detectors, not a product detector to filter sidebands.

G7C05

Which of the following is characteristic of a direct digital synthesizer (DDS)?

  1. A Extremely narrow tuning range
  2. B Relatively high-power output
  3. C Pure sine wave output
  4. D Variable output frequency with the stability of a crystal oscillator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Variable output frequency with the stability of a crystal oscillator

Why D is right. D. The frequency is set by numbers stepped out by a crystal clock, so changing the numbers tunes it while the crystal keeps it stable.

A direct digital synthesizer, or DDS, builds a waveform one sample at a time from a table of numbers. It can tune in small steps across a wide range.

Why not C? A DDS output has small steps and spurious signals, so it is not a pure sine wave.

G7C06

Which of the following is an advantage of a digital signal processing (DSP) filter compared to an analog filter?

  1. A A wide range of filter bandwidths and shapes can be created
  2. B Fewer digital components are required
  3. C Mixing products are greatly reduced
  4. D The DSP filter is much more effective at VHF frequencies
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A wide range of filter bandwidths and shapes can be created

Why A is right. A. The filter is math run on digitized samples, so a new width or shape only needs different math, not new parts.

Doing the same with analog filters would need separate sets of coils, capacitors or crystals for each width and shape.

Why not B? Fewer digital parts is not the advantage; the gain is flexibility.

G7C07

What term specifies a filter’s attenuation inside its passband?

  1. A Insertion loss
  2. B Return loss
  3. C Q
  4. D Ultimate rejection
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Insertion loss

Why A is right. A. Insertion loss is how much a filter weakens signals it is meant to pass.

The passband is the range of frequencies a filter should let through. No real filter is perfect, so even those signals come out a little weaker. That loss, usually given in dB, is the insertion loss.

Why not D? Ultimate rejection describes signals outside the passband, not inside it.

G7C08

Which parameter affects receiver sensitivity?

  1. A Input amplifier gain
  2. B Demodulator stage bandwidth
  3. C Input amplifier noise figure
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. Gain, demodulator bandwidth and noise figure all affect sensitivity.

Sensitivity is the weakest signal a receiver can detect over its own noise. A quieter first amplifier, measured by noise figure, adds less noise. Enough gain lifts weak signals for later stages. A narrower bandwidth lets in less noise.

Why not C? Noise figure matters, but A and B also matter, so C alone is incomplete.

G7C09

What is the phase difference between the I and Q RF signals that software-defined radio (SDR) equipment uses for modulation and demodulation?

  1. A Zero
  2. B 90 degrees
  3. C 180 degrees
  4. D 45 degrees
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 90 degrees

Why B is right. B. The I and Q signals are 90 degrees apart.

I stands for in-phase and Q for quadrature. Quadrature means a quarter of a cycle, which is 90 degrees. With two copies a quarter cycle apart, the SDR can track both the amplitude and the phase of a signal.

Why not C? 180 degrees would make Q a flipped copy of I, which adds no new information.

G7C10

What is an advantage of using I-Q modulation with software-defined radios (SDRs)?

  1. A The need for high resolution analog-to-digital converters is eliminated
  2. B All types of modulation can be created with appropriate processing
  3. C Minimum detectible signal level is reduced
  4. D Automatic conversion of the signal from digital to analog
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. All types of modulation can be created with appropriate processing

Why B is right. B. I and Q together describe a signal's amplitude and phase at every instant, and every modulation type is some pattern of amplitude and phase.

So the same SDR hardware can produce or decode AM, FM, SSB or digital modes by running different software.

Why not A? SDRs still need good analog-to-digital converters to sample the signal.

G7C11

Which of these functions is performed by software in a software-defined radio (SDR)?

  1. A Filtering
  2. B Detection
  3. C Modulation
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. Filtering, detection and modulation can all be done in SDR software.

In a software-defined radio, the signal is turned into numbers early in the chain. After that, a processor handles jobs that used to need separate circuits. It can filter, it can detect to recover audio or data, and it can modulate for transmitting.

Why not A? Filtering is done in software, but so are B and C, so A alone is incomplete.

G7C12

What is the frequency above which a low-pass filter’s output power is less than half the input power?

  1. A Notch frequency
  2. B Neper frequency
  3. C Cutoff frequency
  4. D Rolloff frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Cutoff frequency

Why C is right. C. The cutoff frequency is where a low-pass filter's output falls below half power.

A low-pass filter passes low frequencies and blocks higher ones. The cutoff is the point where the output power is half the input, which is 3 dB down. Above it, the output keeps dropping.

Why not D? Rolloff describes how fast the output drops past cutoff, not the point itself.

G7C13

What term specifies a filter’s maximum ability to reject signals outside its passband?

  1. A Notch depth
  2. B Rolloff
  3. C Insertion loss
  4. D Ultimate rejection
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Ultimate rejection

Why D is right. D. Ultimate rejection is the most a filter can weaken signals outside its passband.

Far from the passband, a filter's attenuation stops increasing and levels off. That floor, given in dB, is the ultimate rejection. A higher number means strong unwanted signals are blocked better.

Why not C? Insertion loss is the loss for signals inside the passband.

G7C14

The bandwidth of a band-pass filter is measured between what two frequencies?

  1. A Upper and lower half-power
  2. B Cutoff and rolloff
  3. C Pole and zero
  4. D Image and harmonic
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Upper and lower half-power

Why A is right. A. The edges of a band-pass filter are defined where output power has fallen to half (3 dB down) on each side of center.

A band-pass filter passes a range of frequencies. On each side, the output drops as you tune away from the center. The space between the two half-power points is the bandwidth.

Why not B? Cutoff and rolloff are not two frequencies; rolloff describes how steeply the output falls.
