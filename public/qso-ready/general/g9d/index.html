G9D: Specialized antenna types and applications

Group G9D of the General pool (2023–2027) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G9D01

Which of the following antenna types will be most effective as a near vertical incidence skywave (NVIS) antenna for short-skip communications on 40 meters during the day?

  1. A A horizontal dipole placed between 1/10 and 1/4 wavelength above the ground
  2. B A vertical antenna placed between 1/4 and 1/2 wavelength above the ground
  3. C A horizontal dipole placed at approximately 1/2 wavelength above the ground
  4. D A vertical dipole placed at approximately 1/2 wavelength above the ground
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A horizontal dipole placed between 1/10 and 1/4 wavelength above the ground

Why A is right. A. A low horizontal dipole's ground reflection sends most energy nearly straight up, which is what NVIS needs.

NVIS stands for near vertical incidence skywave. The signal goes almost straight up and is bent back down by the ionosphere, covering a few hundred miles. A height of 1/10 to 1/4 wavelength keeps the energy aimed high.

Why not C? At a half wavelength, more energy goes out at lower angles, toward farther contacts.

G9D02

What is the feed point impedance of an end-fed half-wave antenna?

  1. A Very low
  2. B Approximately 50 ohms
  3. C Approximately 300 ohms
  4. D Very high
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Very high

Why D is right. D. An end-fed half-wave antenna has a very high feed point impedance.

On a half-wave wire, current is near zero at the ends and voltage is at its peak. Impedance is voltage divided by current, so at the end it is very high, often a few thousand ohms. A matching transformer is needed to connect it to 50-ohm coax.

Why not B? About 50 ohms is closer to a center-fed dipole, not an end-fed one.

G9D03

In which direction is the maximum radiation from a VHF/UHF “halo” antenna?

  1. A Broadside to the plane of the halo
  2. B Opposite the feed point
  3. C Omnidirectional in the plane of the halo
  4. D On the same side as the feed point
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Omnidirectional in the plane of the halo

Why C is right. C. A halo is a half-wave dipole bent into a flat ring, so it has no favored side around its own plane.

A halo is usually mounted flat and level. That gives horizontal polarization with a roughly round pattern around the horizon, useful for mobile SSB on VHF.

Why not A? Broadside means straight up and down for a level halo, where it radiates least.

G9D04

What is the primary function of antenna traps?

  1. A To enable multiband operation
  2. B To notch spurious frequencies
  3. C To provide balanced feed point impedance
  4. D To prevent out-of-band operation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. To enable multiband operation

Why A is right. A. Each trap blocks current on its own band, cutting the wire to the right length there, while passing current on lower bands.

A trap is a coil and capacitor tuned to resonate at one frequency. On that band, the wire beyond the trap is cut off and the antenna acts shorter. On lower bands the whole wire is used.

Why not B? A trap shapes the antenna's working length; it is not a filter for spurious signals.

G9D05

What is an advantage of vertically stacking horizontally polarized Yagi antennas?

  1. A It allows quick selection of vertical or horizontal polarization
  2. B It allows simultaneous vertical and horizontal polarization
  3. C It narrows the main lobe in azimuth
  4. D It narrows the main lobe in elevation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. It narrows the main lobe in elevation

Why D is right. D. Signals from two antennas at different heights add fully only near one vertical angle, squeezing the beam into a narrower range of elevation.

Elevation is the angle above the horizon. A narrower elevation beam puts more energy toward the horizon, which raises gain. The width in compass direction stays about the same.

Why not C? Stacking vertically narrows the vertical beam; side-by-side stacking would narrow it in azimuth.

G9D06

Which of the following is an advantage of a log-periodic antenna?

  1. A Wide bandwidth
  2. B Higher gain per element than a Yagi antenna
  3. C Harmonic suppression
  4. D Polarization diversity
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Wide bandwidth

Why A is right. A. A log-periodic antenna works over a wide range of frequencies.

Different elements on the boom are active at different frequencies. As frequency changes, the active region shifts along the boom. So one antenna can cover several bands with fairly steady gain and SWR.

Why not B? Per element, a log-periodic has less gain than a Yagi, since only some elements are active at a time.

G9D07

Which of the following describes a log-periodic antenna?

  1. A Element length and spacing vary logarithmically along the boom
  2. B Impedance varies periodically as a function of frequency
  3. C Gain varies logarithmically as a function of frequency
  4. D SWR varies periodically as a function of boom length
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Element length and spacing vary logarithmically along the boom

Why A is right. A. In a log-periodic, element lengths and spacing change by a constant ratio along the boom.

The longest element is at the back and the shortest at the front. Each one is a fixed fraction of the one before, which is a logarithmic pattern. This scaled design is what gives the antenna its wide frequency range.

Why not C? Gain stays fairly steady across the frequency range; it does not vary logarithmically.

G9D08

How does a “screwdriver” mobile antenna adjust its feed point impedance?

  1. A By varying its body capacitance
  2. B By varying the base loading inductance
  3. C By extending and retracting the whip
  4. D By deploying a capacitance hat
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. By varying the base loading inductance

Why B is right. B. A motor runs a contact along the base coil, changing how many turns are in use and so the inductance that resonates the short whip.

A short mobile whip needs added inductance to resonate on HF. Retuning the coil this way sets the antenna to the operating frequency.

Why not C? The whip's length stays fixed; tuning is done in the coil.

G9D09

What is the primary use of a Beverage antenna?

  1. A Directional receiving for MF and low HF bands
  2. B Directional transmitting for low HF bands
  3. C Portable direction finding at higher HF frequencies
  4. D Portable direction finding at lower HF frequencies
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Directional receiving for MF and low HF bands

Why A is right. A. A long, low wire aimed at the wanted signals hears well from that way and rejects noise from others, but wastes most transmit power.

A Beverage is often one wavelength or more long and strung a few feet above the ground. It is a receiving antenna, used for MF and the low HF bands.

Why not B? A Beverage is too lossy to be a good transmitting antenna.

G9D10

In which direction or directions does an electrically small loop (less than 1/10 wavelength in circumference) have nulls in its radiation pattern?

  1. A In the plane of the loop
  2. B Broadside to the loop
  3. C Broadside and in the plane of the loop
  4. D Electrically small loops are omnidirectional
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Broadside to the loop

Why B is right. B. Through the loop's face, signals reach opposite sides of the small loop together and cancel.

Hold a small loop like a hoop in front of you. It receives best along the line of the hoop's edges, in its plane. Broadside means straight through the hoop's face, at right angles to its plane, where the nulls are.

Why not A? The plane of the loop is where it is strongest, not where its nulls are.

G9D11

Which of the following is a disadvantage of multiband antennas?

  1. A They present low impedance on all design frequencies
  2. B They must be used with an antenna tuner
  3. C They must be fed with open wire line
  4. D They have poor harmonic rejection
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. They have poor harmonic rejection

Why D is right. D. An antenna built to work on several bands may also work well on a harmonic, so it radiates that harmonic instead of helping block it.

A harmonic is an unwanted signal at a whole-number multiple of the operating frequency. The transmitter's own filters then have to do all the work of suppressing it.

Why not B? Many multiband antennas work without a tuner on their design bands.

G9D12

What is the common name of a dipole with a single central support?

  1. A Inverted V
  2. B Inverted L
  3. C Sloper
  4. D Lazy H
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Inverted V

Why A is right. A. A dipole hung from one central support with its ends sloping down is an inverted V.

The center sits on a mast or tree and each half slopes toward the ground. Seen from the side, the wire forms an upside-down letter V. It needs only one tall support.

Why not C? A sloper is a single wire or dipole slanted from a support to the ground on one side.
