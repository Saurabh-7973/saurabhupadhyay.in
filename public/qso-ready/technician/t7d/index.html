T7D: Using basic test instruments: voltmeter, ammeter, and ohmmeter; Soldering

Group T7D of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T7D01

Which instrument would you use to measure electric potential?

  1. A An ammeter
  2. B A voltmeter
  3. C A potentiometer
  4. D An ohmmeter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A voltmeter

Why B is right. B. A voltmeter.

Electric potential is another name for voltage, and a voltmeter measures voltage between two points.

Why not C? A potentiometer is an adjustable resistor, not a measuring tool, despite its name.

T7D02

How is a voltmeter connected to a component to measure applied voltage?

  1. A In series
  2. B In parallel
  3. C In quadrature
  4. D In phase
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. In parallel

Why B is right. B. In parallel.

To measure voltage, touch the meter's leads to both sides of the part. The meter then sees the same voltage the part does. That side-by-side hookup is called parallel.

Why not A? In series, current would have to flow through the meter, which is how you measure current.

T7D03

When configured to measure current, how is a multimeter connected to a component?

  1. A In series
  2. B In parallel
  3. C In quadrature
  4. D In phase
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. In series

Why A is right. A. In series.

To measure current, open the circuit and insert the meter so all the current flows through it. That in-line hookup is called series.

Why not B? Placing a meter set for current in parallel can short the circuit and blow the meter's fuse.

T7D04

Which instrument is used to measure electric current?

  1. A An ohmmeter
  2. B An electrometer
  3. C A voltmeter
  4. D An ammeter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. An ammeter

Why D is right. D. An ammeter.

Current is measured in amperes, and the meter that measures it is an ammeter. It is connected in series so the current passes through it.

Why not C? A voltmeter measures voltage, not current.

T7D05

How does an ohmmeter measure the resistance of a circuit or component?

  1. A By applying a small current and measuring the resulting voltage
  2. B By placing a variable resistor in parallel with the circuit
  3. C By placing a variable resistor in series with the circuit
  4. D By applying a variable voltage and measuring the resulting current change
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. By applying a small current and measuring the resulting voltage

Why A is right. A. By applying a small current and measuring the resulting voltage.

An ohmmeter sends a small current from its own battery through the part and measures the voltage across it. Resistance equals voltage divided by current, so the meter can show ohms.

Why not D? The meter doesn't sweep a variable voltage; it works from a fixed battery and a known current.

T7D06

Which of the following can damage a multimeter?

  1. A Attempting to measure resistance using the voltage setting
  2. B Failing to connect one of the probes to ground
  3. C Attempting to measure voltage when using the resistance setting
  4. D Not allowing it to warm up properly
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Attempting to measure voltage when using the resistance setting

Why C is right. C. Attempting to measure voltage when using the resistance setting.

On the resistance setting, the meter puts out its own small current and expects an unpowered part. Touching the leads to a live voltage can push too much current into the meter and damage it.

Why not A? The voltage setting draws very little current, so it will not be harmed by a resistor.

T7D07

Which of the following measurements are made using a multimeter?

  1. A Signal strength and noise
  2. B Impedance and reactance
  3. C Voltage and resistance
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Voltage and resistance

Why C is right. C. Voltage and resistance.

A typical multimeter measures voltage, current and resistance, all with one tool. Voltage and resistance are two of its basic jobs.

Why not B? Impedance and reactance need special instruments like an antenna analyzer.

T7D08

Which of the following types of solder should not be used for radio and electronic applications?

  1. A Acid-core solder
  2. B Lead-tin solder
  3. C Rosin-core solder
  4. D Tin-copper solder
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Acid-core solder

Why A is right. A. Acid-core solder.

Acid-core solder is made for plumbing. The acid left behind keeps eating away at wires and circuit boards. Electronics use rosin-core solder, whose leftover is not corrosive.

Why not C? Rosin-core solder is the right type for electronics.

T7D09

What is the characteristic appearance of a cold tin-lead solder joint?

  1. A Dark black spots
  2. B A bright or shiny surface
  3. C A rough or lumpy surface
  4. D A greenish tinge
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A rough or lumpy surface

Why C is right. C. A rough or lumpy surface.

A cold joint happens when the solder does not get hot enough to flow smoothly. It sets dull, rough or lumpy and may not grip the wire well. A good joint is smooth.

Why not B? A bright, smooth surface is the mark of a good joint.

T7D10

What reading indicates that an ohmmeter is connected across a large, discharged capacitor?

  1. A Increasing resistance with time
  2. B Decreasing resistance with time
  3. C Steady full-scale reading
  4. D Alternating between open and short circuit
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Increasing resistance with time

Why A is right. A. Increasing resistance with time.

An empty capacitor at first lets the ohmmeter's current flow freely, so it reads low. As the capacitor charges, current drops and the reading climbs. A large capacitor makes this slow enough to watch.

Why not B? A falling reading would mean current is increasing, the opposite of charging.

T7D11

Which of the following precautions should be taken when measuring in-circuit resistance with an ohmmeter?

  1. A Ensure that the applied voltages are correct
  2. B Ensure that the circuit is not powered
  3. C Ensure that the circuit is grounded
  4. D Ensure that the circuit is operating at the correct frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Ensure that the circuit is not powered

Why B is right. B. Ensure that the circuit is not powered.

An ohmmeter uses its own small current. Power in the circuit would upset the reading and could damage the meter. Turn the circuit off before measuring.

Why not C? Grounding the circuit does not remove the voltage that causes the problem.
