E8A: Fourier analysis; RMS measurements; average RF power and peak envelope power (PEP); analog/digital conversion

Group E8A of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E8A01

What technique shows that a square wave is made up of a sine wave and its odd harmonics?

  1. A Fourier analysis
  2. B Vector analysis
  3. C Numerical analysis
  4. D Differential analysis
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Fourier analysis

Why A is right. A. Fourier analysis breaks any repeating waveform into a sum of sine waves at the fundamental and its multiples.

A square wave looks nothing like a sine wave, yet it can be rebuilt from one. Add a sine at the base frequency, then smaller sines at 3, 5, 7 times that frequency, and the sum sharpens into a square shape. Fourier analysis is the math that finds which harmonics are present and how strong each one is.

Why not B? Vector analysis adds quantities with size and direction; it does not split a waveform into harmonics.

E8A02

Which of the following is a type of analog-to-digital conversion?

  1. A Successive approximation
  2. B Harmonic regeneration
  3. C Level shifting
  4. D Phase reversal
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Successive approximation

Why A is right. A. A successive-approximation converter finds the digital value by testing one bit at a time, from the largest to the smallest.

The converter guesses the top bit, compares a test voltage with the input, and keeps or clears that bit. It then moves to the next bit down. After as many steps as there are bits, the code matches the input voltage. It is a common, moderate-speed way to turn an analog voltage into a number.

Why not C? Level shifting moves a voltage up or down to suit another circuit, but it does not produce a digital code.

E8A03

Which of the following describes a signal in the time domain?

  1. A Power at intervals of phase
  2. B Amplitude at different times
  3. C Frequency at different times
  4. D Discrete impulses in time order
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Amplitude at different times

Why B is right. B. The time domain plots how a signal's strength changes from moment to moment, so it shows amplitude against time.

An oscilloscope shows the time domain: the horizontal axis is time and the vertical axis is voltage. A spectrum analyzer shows the other view, the frequency domain, with amplitude plotted against frequency. Both views describe the same signal, and Fourier analysis converts one into the other.

Why not C? Frequency is the horizontal axis of the frequency domain, not something plotted over time in the time domain.

E8A04

What is “dither” with respect to analog-to-digital converters?

  1. A An abnormal condition where the converter cannot settle on a value to represent the signal
  2. B A small amount of noise added to the input signal to reduce quantization noise
  3. C An error caused by irregular quantization step size
  4. D A method of decimation by randomly skipping samples
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A small amount of noise added to the input signal to reduce quantization noise

Why B is right. B. Dither is a small, deliberate noise added before conversion so rounding errors become random instead of tied to the signal.

A converter rounds each sample to the nearest step, called quantization. With a weak, steady input, that rounding error repeats with the signal and shows up as false tones. Adding a little random noise breaks up the pattern. The error spreads out as a smooth, low noise floor, and small signals below one step can still be detected after averaging.

Why not D? Skipping samples is decimation, which lowers the sample rate; dither is noise added to the input.

E8A05

What is the benefit of making voltage measurements with a true-RMS calculating meter?

  1. A An inverse Fourier transform can be used
  2. B The signal’s RMS noise factor is also calculated
  3. C The calculated RMS value can be converted directly into phasor form
  4. D RMS is measured for both sinusoidal and non-sinusoidal signals
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. RMS is measured for both sinusoidal and non-sinusoidal signals

Why D is right. D. A true-RMS meter computes the heating value from the actual waveform, so it reads correctly for any wave shape.

RMS (root mean square) voltage is the DC voltage that would heat a resistor the same amount. Cheaper meters measure the average or peak and scale it by a factor that is correct only for a pure sine wave. On square waves, pulses or distorted signals those meters read wrong. A true-RMS meter squares, averages and takes the square root of the real signal.

Why not A? An inverse Fourier transform rebuilds a time signal from its spectrum; it has nothing to do with how the meter reads RMS.

E8A06

What is the approximate ratio of PEP-to-average power in an unprocessed single-sideband phone signal?

  1. A 2.5 to 1
  2. B 25 to 1
  3. C 1 to 1
  4. D 13 to 1
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 2.5 to 1

Why A is right. A. Speech has brief loud peaks and many quiet moments, so its average power is well below its peak, about 2.5 to 1.

PEP (peak envelope power) is the power at the highest point of the signal envelope. Average power counts the quiet gaps between syllables and the soft parts of words. For normal voice on SSB without a speech processor, the peak is roughly two and a half times the average. A speech processor raises the average and narrows that ratio.

Why not C? A 1 to 1 ratio fits a steady carrier, such as a held CW key, not a voice signal that rises and falls.

E8A07

What determines the PEP-to-average power ratio of an unprocessed single-sideband phone signal?

  1. A The frequency of the modulating signal
  2. B Speech characteristics
  3. C The degree of carrier suppression
  4. D Amplifier gain
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Speech characteristics

Why B is right. B. In SSB the output follows the voice, so the way a person talks, with peaks and pauses, sets the peak-to-average ratio.

An SSB transmitter sends no carrier, so with no audio there is no output. Every bit of RF comes from the voice. A speaker with sharp peaks and long pauses gives a high ratio; a smooth, steady voice gives a lower one. The pitch of the voice does not change the ratio, and neither does amplifier gain, which scales peak and average equally.

Why not D? Amplifier gain raises peak and average power by the same factor, so their ratio stays the same.

E8A08

Why are direct or flash conversion analog-to-digital converters used for a software defined radio?

  1. A Very low power consumption decreases frequency drift
  2. B Immunity to out-of-sequence coding reduces spurious responses
  3. C Very high speed allows digitizing high frequencies
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Very high speed allows digitizing high frequencies

Why C is right. C. A flash converter compares the input against every level at once, so it can sample fast enough to digitize RF directly.

A flash converter uses a bank of comparators, one for each possible level, all working at the same instant. The result is ready in a single step instead of many. That speed lets a software defined radio sample signals at high frequencies. The cost is many parts and high power use, which is why option A does not fit.

Why not A? Flash converters use a lot of power because of their many comparators, so low power is not their benefit.

E8A09

How many different input levels can be encoded by an analog-to-digital converter with 8-bit resolution?

  1. A 8
  2. B 8 multiplied by the gain of the input amplifier
  3. C 256 divided by the gain of the input amplifier
  4. D 256
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 256

Why D is right. D. Each added bit doubles the number of codes, and 8 bits give 2 to the 8th power, which is 256 levels.

One bit has two values, 0 or 1. Two bits have four combinations, three bits have eight, and so on. The count is 2 raised to the number of bits. The input amplifier changes which voltage maps to which code, but it does not change how many codes exist.

levels = 2^n
n = 8 bits
levels = 2^8 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2
= 256

Why not A? 8 is the number of bits, not the number of distinct codes those bits can form.

E8A10

What is the purpose of a low-pass filter used at the output of a digital-to-analog converter?

  1. A Lower the input bandwidth to increase the effective resolution
  2. B Improve accuracy by removing out-of-sequence codes from the input
  3. C Remove spurious sampling artifacts from the output signal
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Remove spurious sampling artifacts from the output signal

Why C is right. C. A DAC output moves in steps at the sample rate, and the low-pass filter removes those high-frequency artifacts.

A digital-to-analog converter (DAC) holds each value until the next sample, so its output is a staircase. The sharp steps create unwanted copies of the signal near the sample rate and its multiples. A low-pass filter after the DAC passes the wanted signal and blocks those higher copies, which smooths the staircase into a clean wave.

Why not A? The filter sits at the output of the DAC, so it cannot change the bandwidth of anything going into the converter.

E8A11

Which of the following is a measure of the quality of an analog-to-digital converter?

  1. A Total harmonic distortion
  2. B Peak envelope power
  3. C Reciprocal mixing
  4. D Power factor
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Total harmonic distortion

Why A is right. A. Total harmonic distortion shows how much a converter's rounding and nonlinearity add false harmonics to a clean input.

Feed a pure sine wave into an analog-to-digital converter and look at the digital output's spectrum. A perfect converter shows only that one tone. Real converters add harmonics because their steps are not perfectly even. Total harmonic distortion (THD) adds those up and compares them with the tone, so a lower THD means a better converter.

Why not B? Peak envelope power describes a transmitter's output, not the accuracy of a converter.
