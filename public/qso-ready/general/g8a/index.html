G8A: Carriers and modulation: AM, FM, and single sideband; modulation envelope; digital modulation; overmodulation; link budgets and link margins

Group G8A of the General pool (2023–2027) has 14 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G8A01

How is direct binary FSK modulation generated?

  1. A By keying an FM transmitter with a sub-audible tone
  2. B By changing an oscillator’s frequency directly with a digital control signal
  3. C By using a transceiver’s computer data interface protocol to change frequencies
  4. D By reconfiguring the CW keying input to act as a tone generator
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. By changing an oscillator’s frequency directly with a digital control signal

Why B is right. B. 'Direct' means the data line itself sets the oscillator's frequency, with no audio tones in between.

FSK stands for frequency shift keying. In the direct method, the oscillator moves to one frequency for a 1 and another for a 0. Other methods feed audio tones into an SSB or FM transmitter instead.

Why not A? A sub-audible tone keys a repeater's access, not the data bits of an FSK signal.

G8A02

What is the name of the process that changes the phase angle of an RF signal to convey information?

  1. A Phase convolution
  2. B Phase modulation
  3. C Phase transformation
  4. D Phase inversion
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Phase modulation

Why B is right. B. Modulation is the name for varying a carrier to carry information, and the word in front names what varies, so varying phase is phase modulation.

Phase is where a wave is in its cycle at a given moment. Phase modulation shifts that timing a little ahead or behind in step with the audio or data. The receiver reads those shifts to recover the message.

Why not D? Phase inversion names one 180-degree flip, not the general process of varying phase to carry information.

G8A03

What is the name of the process that changes the instantaneous frequency of an RF wave to convey information?

  1. A Frequency convolution
  2. B Frequency transformation
  3. C Frequency conversion
  4. D Frequency modulation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Frequency modulation

Why D is right. D. Changing the instantaneous frequency to carry information is frequency modulation.

In FM, the carrier's frequency moves up and down around its center in step with the audio. Louder audio moves it farther. The amplitude stays the same, so the receiver reads only the frequency changes.

Why not C? Frequency conversion moves a whole signal to a new frequency in a mixer; it carries no message by itself.

G8A04

What emission is produced by a reactance modulator connected to a transmitter RF amplifier stage?

  1. A Multiplex modulation
  2. B Phase modulation
  3. C Amplitude modulation
  4. D Pulse modulation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Phase modulation

Why B is right. B. A reactance modulator on an RF amplifier stage produces phase modulation.

A reactance modulator acts like a capacitor or coil whose value follows the audio. On an amplifier stage, that changing reactance shifts the timing, or phase, of the RF passing through. If it were placed on the oscillator instead, it would shift frequency and make FM.

Why not C? The reactance changes timing, not the strength of the signal, so it is not AM.

G8A05

What type of modulation varies the instantaneous power level of the RF signal?

  1. A Power modulation
  2. B Phase modulation
  3. C Frequency modulation
  4. D Amplitude modulation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Amplitude modulation

Why D is right. D. Amplitude modulation varies the signal's instantaneous power level.

In AM, the strength of the RF wave rises and falls with the audio. A stronger moment means more power at that instant. Phase and frequency modulation keep the amplitude steady.

Why not A? Power modulation is not a standard name for a modulation type.

G8A06

Which of the following is characteristic of QPSK31?

  1. A It is sideband sensitive
  2. B Its encoding provides error correction
  3. C Its bandwidth is approximately the same as BPSK31
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. QPSK31 needs the correct sideband, uses error correction, and is about as wide as BPSK31.

QPSK31 uses four phase states instead of two. Reversing sideband flips the phase direction, so both stations must use the same sideband. The extra states carry error-correcting code, not extra speed. It sends 31.25 symbols per second, the same as BPSK31, so its width is about the same.

Why not B? Error correction is true, but A and C are also true, so B alone is incomplete.

G8A07

Which of the following phone emissions uses the narrowest bandwidth?

  1. A Single sideband
  2. B Vestigial sideband
  3. C Phase modulation
  4. D Frequency modulation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Single sideband

Why A is right. A. Single sideband uses the narrowest bandwidth of these voice modes.

SSB sends only one sideband and no carrier, so a voice signal needs about 3 kHz. Vestigial sideband keeps part of the second sideband, so it is wider. FM and phase modulation spread the voice over a much wider range.

Why not B? Vestigial sideband keeps a piece of the other sideband, making it wider than SSB.

G8A08

Which of the following is an effect of overmodulation?

  1. A Insufficient audio
  2. B Insufficient bandwidth
  3. C Frequency drift
  4. D Excessive bandwidth
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Excessive bandwidth

Why D is right. D. Overmodulation makes the signal take up too much bandwidth.

Overmodulation happens when the audio drives the transmitter past what it can follow cleanly. The waveform gets clipped and distorted. Distortion creates new frequencies, called splatter, that spread out and interfere with nearby stations.

Why not B? Overmodulation makes the signal too wide, not too narrow.

G8A09

What type of modulation is used by FT8?

  1. A 8-tone frequency shift keying
  2. B Vestigial sideband
  3. C Amplitude compressed AM
  4. D 8-bit direct sequence spread spectrum
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 8-tone frequency shift keying

Why A is right. A. Each FT8 symbol is one of eight audio tones, so the data rides on which tone is sent.

FT8 is a weak-signal digital mode, and switching among tones is a form of frequency shift keying. The signal is about 50 Hz wide.

Why not D? FT8 is not spread spectrum; it stays in a narrow slice of the band.

G8A10

What is meant by the term “flat-topping,” when referring to an amplitude-modulated phone signal?

  1. A Signal distortion caused by insufficient collector current
  2. B The transmitter’s automatic level control (ALC) is properly adjusted
  3. C Signal distortion caused by excessive drive or speech levels
  4. D The transmitter’s carrier is properly suppressed
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Signal distortion caused by excessive drive or speech levels

Why C is right. C. Too much drive asks the amplifier for more peak power than it can give, so the waveform peaks are cut off flat.

That clipping is distortion. It comes from too much drive or speech level, makes the signal sound rough, and spreads it wider than it should be.

Why not B? A properly set ALC helps prevent flat-topping; it does not describe it.

G8A11

What is the modulation envelope of an AM signal?

  1. A The waveform created by connecting the peak values of the modulated signal
  2. B The carrier frequency that contains the signal
  3. C Spurious signals that envelop nearby frequencies
  4. D The bandwidth of the modulated signal
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The waveform created by connecting the peak values of the modulated signal

Why A is right. A. Joining the RF peaks traces a slow outline that rises and falls with the audio, and that outline is the envelope.

On a scope, an AM signal's RF wave rises and falls in strength. The shape traced over the peaks copies the audio that modulated it.

Why not B? The carrier is the RF wave inside the envelope, not the envelope itself.

G8A12

What is QPSK modulation?

  1. A Modulation using quasi-parallel to serial conversion to reduce bandwidth
  2. B Modulation using quadra-pole sideband keying to generate spread spectrum signals
  3. C Modulation using Fast Fourier Transforms to generate frequencies at the first, second, third, and fourth harmonics of the carrier frequency to improve noise immunity
  4. D Modulation in which digital data is transmitted using 0-, 90-, 180- and 270-degrees phase shift to represent pairs of bits
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Modulation in which digital data is transmitted using 0-, 90-, 180- and 270-degrees phase shift to represent pairs of bits

Why D is right. D. Two bits have four combinations, and four phases a quarter cycle apart give each combination its own phase.

Q stands for quadrature: the four phases sit a quarter cycle, 90 degrees, apart, at 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees. The bit pairs are 00, 01, 10 and 11, so every symbol carries two bits.

Why not C? QPSK does not use harmonics of the carrier; it changes the phase of one carrier.

G8A13

What is a link budget?

  1. A The financial costs associated with operating a radio link
  2. B The sum of antenna gains minus system losses
  3. C The sum of transmit power and antenna gains minus system losses as seen at the receiver
  4. D The difference between transmit power and receiver sensitivity
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The sum of transmit power and antenna gains minus system losses as seen at the receiver

Why C is right. C. To predict what arrives, you start with transmitter power, add both antenna gains, and subtract every loss along the way.

A link budget is usually worked in dB, so gains and losses add and subtract. Losses include feed line loss and path loss. The result is the expected signal at the receiver.

Why not B? B leaves out the transmit power, so it cannot give a received signal level.

G8A14

What is link margin?

  1. A The opposite of fade margin
  2. B The difference between received power level and minimum required signal level at the input to the receiver
  3. C Transmit power minus receiver sensitivity
  4. D Receiver sensitivity plus 3 dB
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The difference between received power level and minimum required signal level at the input to the receiver

Why B is right. B. Margin is the cushion: how many dB the signal can fade before it drops below what the receiver needs.

Suppose the receiver needs a certain signal level to work well. If the signal arriving is 10 dB stronger than that, the link margin is 10 dB.

Why not C? Transmit power minus sensitivity ignores the path and antenna losses between stations.
