T1D: Authorized and prohibited transmissions; Sale of equipment

Group T1D of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T1D01

With which countries are FCC-licensed amateur radio stations prohibited from exchanging communications?

  1. A Any country whose administration has notified the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) that it objects to such communications
  2. B Any country whose administration has notified the American Radio Relay League (ARRL) that it objects to such communications
  3. C Any country banned from such communications by the International Amateur Radio Union (IARU)
  4. D Any country banned from making such communications by the American Radio Relay League (ARRL)
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Any country whose administration has notified the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) that it objects to such communications

Why A is right. A. Any country whose administration has notified the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) that it objects to such communications.

The ITU is the United Nations agency for radio and telecom. A country can tell the ITU that it objects to its amateurs talking with stations abroad. FCC-licensed hams may not exchange messages with stations in such a country. Private groups like the ARRL or IARU have no power to ban contacts.

“except those in any country whose administration has notified the ITU that it objects to such communications”

47 CFR 97.111(a)(1)

Why not C? The IARU is a federation of amateur societies; it cannot ban contacts.

T1D02

Under which of the following circumstances are one-way transmissions by an amateur station prohibited?

  1. A Announcements of upcoming ham radio operating events
  2. B Broadcasting
  3. C International Morse Code Practice
  4. D Telecommand or transmissions of telemetry
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Broadcasting

Why B is right. B. Broadcasting.

Most amateur traffic is two-way. Some one-way sending is allowed, such as Morse practice, bulletins, telecommand and telemetry. Broadcasting, meaning sending to the general public, is never allowed for amateur stations.

“An amateur station shall not engage in any form of broadcasting”

47 CFR 97.113(b)

Why not C? Morse code practice is on the list of allowed one-way transmissions.

T1D03

When is it permissible to transmit messages encoded to obscure their meaning?

  1. A Only when using a remote station during a contest
  2. B Only when transmitting certain approved digital codes
  3. C Only when transmitting control commands to space stations or model craft
  4. D Never
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Only when transmitting control commands to space stations or model craft

Why C is right. C. Only when transmitting control commands to space stations or model craft.

Messages meant to hide their meaning are banned, with narrow exceptions. A telecommand station may use secret codes when sending commands to a satellite, so no one else can take control of it. Control signals for model craft are not treated as hidden codes either.

“A telecommand station may transmit special codes intended to obscure the meaning of telecommand messages to the station in space operation.”

47 CFR 97.211(b)

Why not D? "Never" is wrong because the rules allow codes for commanding a space station.

T1D04

Under what conditions is an amateur station authorized to transmit music using a phone emission?

  1. A When incidental to an authorized retransmission of manned spacecraft communications
  2. B When the music produces no spurious emissions
  3. C When transmissions are limited to less than three minutes per hour
  4. D When the music is transmitted above 1280 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. When incidental to an authorized retransmission of manned spacecraft communications

Why A is right. A. When incidental to an authorized retransmission of manned spacecraft communications.

Music over phone is banned on amateur bands with one exception. With NASA's prior approval, hams may now and then retransmit talk between a crewed spacecraft and its Earth stations. If music is part of that audio, it may go out too.

“communications, including incidental music, originating on United States Government frequencies between a manned spacecraft and its associated Earth stations”

47 CFR 97.113(c)

Why not D? No frequency, including above 1280 MHz, makes music over phone legal.

T1D05

When may amateur radio operators use their stations to notify other amateurs of the availability of equipment for sale or trade?

  1. A Never
  2. B When the equipment is not the personal property of either the station licensee, or the control operator, or their close relatives
  3. C When no profit is made on the sale
  4. D When selling amateur radio equipment and not on a regular basis
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. When selling amateur radio equipment and not on a regular basis

Why D is right. D. When selling amateur radio equipment and not on a regular basis.

You may tell other hams on the air that you have radio gear for sale or trade. Two limits apply: it must be equipment normally used in an amateur station, and you may not do it on a regular basis, like a store would.

“An amateur operator may notify other amateur operators of the availability for sale or trade of apparatus normally used in an amateur station, provided that such activity is not conducted on a regular basis.”

47 CFR 97.113(a)(3)(ii)

Why not C? Making a profit is not the test; the gear type and how often you do it are.

T1D06

What, if any, are the restrictions concerning transmission of language that may be considered indecent or obscene?

  1. A The FCC maintains a list of words that are not permitted to be used on amateur frequencies
  2. B Any such language is prohibited
  3. C The International Telecommunications Union (ITU) maintains a list of words that are not permitted to be used on amateur frequencies
  4. D There is no such prohibition
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Any such language is prohibited

Why B is right. B. Any such language is prohibited.

Part 97 lists obscene or indecent words or language among the things you may not transmit. The rule sets no word list. Any language of that kind is banned on amateur frequencies.

“obscene or indecent words or language”

47 CFR 97.113(a)(4)

Why not A? The FCC does not publish a list of banned words for amateurs.

T1D07

Which of the following is an example of an auxiliary station?

  1. A A station sending one-way transmissions between a remote repeater receiver and the main repeater transmitter
  2. B A backup radio for emergency use in case the main station radio fails
  3. C A station used in the Military Auxiliary Radio System for linking government and amateur stations in a digital network
  4. D A second station utilized in multi-operator contest stations
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A station sending one-way transmissions between a remote repeater receiver and the main repeater transmitter

Why A is right. A. A station sending one-way transmissions between a remote repeater receiver and the main repeater transmitter.

An auxiliary station is a helper link inside an amateur system. A common case is a receiver on a far hilltop that passes signals one way to the main repeater transmitter. The rules let auxiliary stations send one-way and retransmit other amateur signals.

“An auxiliary station may transmit one-way communications.”

47 CFR 97.201(e)

Why not B? A spare radio is backup gear, not a separate kind of station.

T1D08

In which of the following circumstances may the control operator of an amateur station receive compensation for operating that station?

  1. A When the communication is related to the sale of amateur equipment by the control operator's employer
  2. B When the communication is a part of classroom instruction at an educational institution
  3. C When the communication is made to obtain emergency information for a local broadcast station
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. When the communication is a part of classroom instruction at an educational institution

Why B is right. B. When the communication is a part of classroom instruction at an educational institution.

Hams normally may not be paid for operating. One exception covers teachers: if a teacher uses an amateur station as part of a class at a school, the pay from that teaching job is allowed.

“A control operator may accept compensation as an incident of a teaching position during periods of time when an amateur station is used by that teacher as a part of classroom instruction at an educational institution.”

47 CFR 97.113(a)(3)(iii)

Why not A? Helping an employer sell gear is business use, which is not allowed.

T1D09

When may amateur stations transmit information in support of broadcasting, program production, or news gathering, assuming no other means is available?

  1. A When such communications are directly related to the immediate safety of human life or protection of property
  2. B When broadcasting communications to or from the space shuttle
  3. C Where non-commercial programming is gathered and supplied exclusively to the National Public Radio network
  4. D Never
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. When such communications are directly related to the immediate safety of human life or protection of property

Why A is right. A. When such communications are directly related to the immediate safety of human life or protection of property.

Hams may not help with broadcasting or news gathering. The one exception: when lives or property are at immediate risk and no other way to communicate is available, amateurs may pass that information to broadcasters to share with the public.

“except that communications directly related to the immediate safety of human life or the protection of property may be provided by amateur stations to broadcasters”

47 CFR 97.113(b)

Why not D? "Never" ignores the safety-of-life exception in the rule.

T1D10

How does the FCC define broadcasting for the Amateur Radio Service?

  1. A Two-way transmissions by amateur stations
  2. B Any transmission made by the licensed station
  3. C Transmission of messages directed only to amateur operators
  4. D Transmissions intended for reception by the general public
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Transmissions intended for reception by the general public

Why D is right. D. Transmissions intended for reception by the general public.

Under Part 97, broadcasting means sending something meant for the general public to receive, either directly or through a relay. Amateur messages are meant for other hams, which is why broadcasting is not allowed.

“Broadcasting. Transmissions intended for reception by the general public, either direct or relayed.”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(10)

Why not C? Messages meant only for hams are the opposite of broadcasting.

T1D11

When may an amateur station transmit without identifying on the air?

  1. A When the transmissions are of a brief nature to make station adjustments
  2. B When the transmissions are unmodulated
  3. C When the transmitted power level is below 0.1 watt
  4. D When transmitting signals to control model craft
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. When transmitting signals to control model craft

Why D is right. D. When transmitting signals to control model craft.

Signals that steer a model plane, boat or car do not need an on-air call sign. Instead, a label with the call sign and the licensee's name and address must be fixed to the transmitter. Brief tuning or low-power signals still need identification.

“The station identification procedure is not required for transmissions directed only to the model craft, provided that a label indicating the station call sign and the station licensee's name and address is affixed”

47 CFR 97.215(a)

Why not C? Low power does not excuse you from giving your call sign.

T1D12

Which of the following is required when making on-the-air test transmissions?

  1. A Identify the transmitting station
  2. B Conduct tests only between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. local time
  3. C Notify the FCC of the transmissions
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Identify the transmitting station

Why A is right. A. Identify the transmitting station.

A test transmission is still a transmission. You must give your call sign the same way as for any other signal. There is no rule about test hours and no need to tell the FCC first.

“No station may transmit unidentified communications or signals”

47 CFR 97.119(a)

Why not C? The FCC does not need notice before you send test signals.
