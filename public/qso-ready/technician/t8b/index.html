T8B: Amateur satellite operation: Doppler shift, basic orbits, operating protocols, modulation mode selection, transmitter power considerations, telemetry, satellite tracking programs, beacons, uplink and downlink mode definitions, spin fading, definition of “LEO”, setting uplink power

Group T8B of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T8B01

What telemetry information is typically transmitted by satellite beacons?

  1. A The signal strength of received signals
  2. B Time of day accurate to plus or minus 1/10 second
  3. C Health and status of the satellite
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Health and status of the satellite

Why C is right. C. Satellite beacons usually send health and status information.

Telemetry is data a spacecraft sends about itself. Typical items are battery voltage, solar panel current and temperatures. Ground stations decode it to check that the satellite is working well.

Why not A? The satellite does not report how strong each user's received signal is.

T8B02

What is the impact of using excessive effective radiated power on a satellite uplink?

  1. A Possibility of commanding the satellite to an improper mode
  2. B Blocking access by other users
  3. C Overloading the satellite batteries
  4. D Possibility of rebooting the satellite control computer
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Blocking access by other users

Why B is right. B. Too much uplink power can block other users.

A satellite has limited transmit power, shared by everyone using it at once. If your uplink signal is too strong, the satellite spends more of its power repeating you. Other stations get less, and some may be pushed out entirely. Use only the power needed to be heard.

Why not C? The satellite does not charge its batteries from your signal, so you cannot overload them this way.

T8B03

Which of the following are provided by satellite tracking programs?

  1. A Maps showing the real-time position of the satellite track over Earth
  2. B The time, azimuth, and elevation of the start, maximum altitude, and end of a pass
  3. C The apparent frequency of the satellite transmission, including effects of Doppler shift
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

A tracking program uses orbit data to work out where a satellite is and where it will be. From that it can draw the satellite's path on a map, list when a pass starts, peaks and ends with antenna pointing angles, and estimate the frequency shift you will hear as the satellite moves.

T8B04

What mode of transmission is commonly used by amateur radio satellites?

  1. A SSB
  2. B FM
  3. C CW/data
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. All these choices are correct.

Amateur satellites carry many types of signals. Some repeat FM voice like a repeater in orbit. Others repeat a band of signals so many SSB and CW contacts can happen at once. Many also send data or telemetry.

T8B05

What is a satellite beacon?

  1. A The primary transmit antenna on the satellite
  2. B An indicator light that shows where to point your antenna
  3. C A reflective surface on the satellite
  4. D A transmission from a satellite that contains status information
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. A transmission from a satellite that contains status information

Why D is right. D. A satellite beacon is a transmission that contains status information.

A beacon is a signal the satellite sends on its own, without anyone talking through it. It often carries telemetry about the spacecraft. Hams also use the beacon to find the satellite, check its signal strength and adjust their tuning.

Why not B? A beacon is a radio signal, not a light.

T8B06

Which of the following are inputs to a satellite tracking program?

  1. A The satellite transmitted power
  2. B The Keplerian elements
  3. C The last observed time of zero Doppler shift
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The Keplerian elements

Why B is right. B. The Keplerian elements are fed into a tracking program.

Keplerian elements are a short set of numbers that describe a satellite's orbit, such as its shape, its tilt and where the satellite was at a set time. A tracking program uses these numbers, plus your location and the time, to predict passes. Update them often, since orbits slowly change.

Why not A? The satellite's transmit power does not tell the program where the satellite is.

T8B07

What is Doppler shift in reference to satellite communications?

  1. A A change in the satellite orbit
  2. B A mode where the satellite receives signals on one band and transmits on another
  3. C An observed change in signal frequency caused by relative motion between the satellite and Earth station
  4. D A special digital communications mode for some satellites
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. An observed change in signal frequency caused by relative motion between the satellite and Earth station

Why C is right. C. Doppler shift is a change in the received frequency caused by motion between the satellite and your station.

When a satellite moves toward you, its signal arrives a little higher in frequency. As it moves away, the frequency drops. You hear the same effect when a siren passes you. Satellite operators retune during a pass to follow this shift.

Why not A? Doppler shift changes the frequency you hear, not the satellite's orbit.

T8B08

What does it mean if a satellite is operating in U/V mode?

  1. A The satellite uplink is in the 15-meter band and the downlink is in the 10-meter band
  2. B The satellite uplink is in the 70-centimeter band and the downlink is in the 2-meter band
  3. C The satellite operates using ultraviolet frequencies
  4. D The satellite frequencies are usually variable
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The satellite uplink is in the 70-centimeter band and the downlink is in the 2-meter band

Why B is right. B. U/V mode means a 70-centimeter uplink and a 2-meter downlink.

Satellite modes are named by two letters: the uplink band first, then the downlink band. U stands for UHF, which includes 70 centimeters (about 435 MHz). V stands for VHF, which includes 2 meters (about 145 MHz). So you transmit on 70 cm and listen on 2 m.

Why not C? U and V name radio bands, not ultraviolet light.

Remember: First letter up, second letter down.

T8B09

What causes spin fading of satellite signals?

  1. A Circular polarized noise interference radiated from the sun
  2. B Rotation of the satellite and its antennas
  3. C Doppler shift of the received signal
  4. D Interfering signals within the satellite uplink band
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Rotation of the satellite and its antennas

Why B is right. B. Spin fading comes from the satellite and its antennas rotating.

Many small satellites slowly spin or tumble. As they turn, their antennas point toward you and then away. The signal you hear rises and falls in a regular rhythm because of that rotation.

Why not C? Doppler shift moves the frequency, but it does not make the signal fade in and out.

T8B10

What does the term LEO mean in reference to communication satellites?

  1. A Low Energy Orbit, which conserves battery power
  2. B Low Elevation Orbit, which appears close to the horizon from the earth station
  3. C Low Equilibrium Orbit, which has a slightly unstable period
  4. D Low Earth Orbit, which has a period of around 100 minutes
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Low Earth Orbit, which has a period of around 100 minutes

Why D is right. D. LEO means Low Earth Orbit, with a period of around 100 minutes.

Satellites in low Earth orbit circle only a few hundred kilometers up. At that height they must move very fast to stay in orbit, so they go around Earth in roughly 90 to 100 minutes. From the ground, a pass lasts only minutes.

Why not B? The E in LEO stands for Earth, not elevation.

T8B11

Who is permitted to receive telemetry from an amateur radio satellite?

  1. A Anyone
  2. B Only the satellite control operator
  3. C Only the control operator or a licensed radio amateur who has received the encryption key from the control operator
  4. D Only a licensed radio amateur who has received the encryption key from AMSAT
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Anyone

Why A is right. A. Anyone may receive telemetry from an amateur satellite.

Amateur radio is an open service. Its signals are meant to be heard, and telemetry from amateur satellites is open for anyone to listen to and decode. Students and listeners around the world collect this data to help track how the satellite is doing.

Why not C? No encryption key is needed to receive amateur satellite telemetry.

T8B12

Which of the following is a way to determine whether your satellite uplink power into a linear transponder satellite is neither too low nor too high?

  1. A Check your signal strength report in the telemetry data
  2. B Listen for distortion on your downlink signal
  3. C Your signal strength on the downlink should be about the same as the beacon
  4. D Compare your signal to others on the downlink using an internet SDR receiver
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Your signal strength on the downlink should be about the same as the beacon

Why C is right. C. Your downlink signal should be about as strong as the beacon.

On a linear transponder, many signals share the satellite's limited power. The beacon gives you a steady reference level. If your own signal coming back down is about as strong as the beacon, your uplink power is about right. Much stronger means you are taking more than your share.

Why not B? Distortion can have other causes, so it is not a reliable way to judge your power level.
