T7C: Antenna and transmission line measurements and troubleshooting: measuring SWR, effects of high SWR, causes of feed line failures; Basic coaxial cable characteristics; Use of dummy loads when testing

Group T7C of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T7C01

What is the primary purpose of a dummy load?

  1. A To prevent transmitting signals over the air when making tests
  2. B To prevent over-modulation of a transmitter
  3. C To improve the efficiency of an antenna
  4. D To improve the signal-to-noise ratio of a receiver
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. To prevent transmitting signals over the air when making tests

Why A is right. A. To prevent transmitting signals over the air when making tests.

A dummy load takes the place of the antenna and turns the transmitter's power into heat. You can test and adjust the radio without putting a signal on the air and causing interference.

Why not C? A dummy load replaces the antenna; it does nothing to improve it.

T7C02

Which of the following is used to determine if an antenna is resonant at the desired operating frequency?

  1. A A VTVM
  2. B An antenna analyzer
  3. C A Q meter
  4. D A frequency counter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. An antenna analyzer

Why B is right. B. An antenna analyzer.

An antenna analyzer sends a small signal into the antenna and measures how it responds across a range of frequencies. It shows where the antenna is resonant and how well it matches.

Why not D? A frequency counter only measures the frequency of a signal it receives.

T7C03

What does a typical RF dummy load consist of?

  1. A A low-voltage power supply and an AC relay
  2. B A 50-ohm non-inductive resistor mounted on a heat sink
  3. C A low-voltage power supply and a DC relay
  4. D A 50-ohm inductive reactance mounted in a shielded enclosure
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A 50-ohm non-inductive resistor mounted on a heat sink

Why B is right. B. A 50-ohm non-inductive resistor mounted on a heat sink.

A dummy load must look like a perfect 50-ohm antenna to the radio, so it uses a 50-ohm resistor built with almost no inductance. The heat sink gets rid of the heat from the transmitter's power.

Why not D? Inductive reactance would make a poor match and absorb little power.

T7C04

What reading on an SWR meter indicates a perfect impedance match between the antenna and the feed line?

  1. A 50:50
  2. B Zero
  3. C 1:1
  4. D Full Scale
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 1:1

Why C is right. C. 1:1.

SWR compares the highest and lowest voltage along the feed line. With a perfect match, there is no reflected power, the voltage is even all along the line, and the ratio is 1 to 1.

Why not B? SWR can never go below 1:1, so zero is not a possible reading.

T7C05

Why do most solid-state transmitters reduce output power as SWR increases beyond a certain level?

  1. A To protect the RF output amplifier transistors
  2. B To comply with FCC rules on spectral purity
  3. C Because power supplies cannot supply enough current at high SWR
  4. D To lower the SWR on the transmission line
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. To protect the RF output amplifier transistors

Why A is right. A. To protect the RF output amplifier transistors.

High SWR sends power back into the transmitter, which can overheat or overstress its output transistors. The radio lowers its power to keep them safe.

Why not D? Cutting power does not change the match, so the SWR stays the same.

T7C06

What does an SWR reading of 4:1 indicate?

  1. A Loss of -4 dB
  2. B Good impedance match
  3. C Gain of +4 dB
  4. D Impedance mismatch
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Impedance mismatch

Why D is right. D. Impedance mismatch.

Any SWR above 1:1 means some power is reflected because the load does not match the line. A reading of 4:1 shows a large mismatch.

Why not B? A good match reads close to 1:1.

T7C07

What happens to power lost in a feed line?

  1. A It increases the SWR
  2. B It is radiated as harmonics
  3. C It is converted into heat
  4. D It distorts the signal
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. It is converted into heat

Why C is right. C. It is converted into heat.

Energy cannot vanish. Power lost in a feed line warms the wire and the insulation inside the cable.

Why not B? Harmonics are made by nonlinear circuits, not by ordinary cable loss.

T7C08

Which instrument can be used to determine SWR?

  1. A Voltmeter
  2. B Ohmmeter
  3. C Iambic pentameter
  4. D Directional wattmeter
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Directional wattmeter

Why D is right. D. Directional wattmeter.

A directional wattmeter measures power going toward the antenna and power reflected back. Comparing those two numbers gives the SWR.

Why not A? A plain voltmeter cannot tell forward power from reflected power.

T7C09

Which of the following causes failure of coaxial cables?

  1. A Moisture contamination
  2. B Solder flux contamination
  3. C Rapid fluctuation in transmitter output power
  4. D Operation at 100% duty cycle for an extended period
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Moisture contamination

Why A is right. A. Moisture contamination.

Water that gets inside coax raises its loss and corrodes the conductors over time. Sealed connectors and an undamaged jacket keep moisture out.

Why not D? Normal operation within ratings does not ruin coax.

T7C10

Why should the outer jacket of coaxial cable be resistant to ultraviolet light?

  1. A Ultraviolet light can increase the resistance of the conductors
  2. B Ultraviolet light can increase losses in the cable’s jacket
  3. C Ultraviolet and RF signals can mix, causing interference
  4. D Ultraviolet light can damage the jacket and allow water to enter the cable
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Ultraviolet light can damage the jacket and allow water to enter the cable

Why D is right. D. Ultraviolet light can damage the jacket and allow water to enter the cable.

Sunlight's ultraviolet rays break down many plastics, making a jacket crack. Cracks let water in, which harms the cable inside.

Why not C? Light and radio signals do not mix to make interference.

T7C11

What is an advantage of foam-dielectric versus solid-dielectric coaxial cable?

  1. A It is more resistant to moisture contamination
  2. B It has higher voltage breakdown
  3. C It has less loss per foot
  4. D It has a better impedance match to 50 ohms
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. It has less loss per foot

Why C is right. C. It has less loss per foot.

Foam insulation is mostly gas bubbles, which waste less signal energy than solid plastic. So foam coax loses less signal over the same length.

Why not A? Foam can soak up water more easily, so it is less moisture resistant.
