E1B: Station restrictions and special operations: restrictions on station location; general operating restrictions; spurious emissions; antenna structure restrictions; RACES operations

Group E1B of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E1B01

Which of the following constitutes a spurious emission?

  1. A An amateur station transmission made without the proper call sign identification
  2. B A signal transmitted to prevent its detection by any station other than the intended recipient
  3. C Any transmitted signal that unintentionally interferes with another licensed radio station and whose levels exceed 40 dB below the fundamental power level
  4. D An emission outside the signal’s necessary bandwidth that can be reduced or eliminated without affecting the information transmitted
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. An emission outside the signal’s necessary bandwidth that can be reduced or eliminated without affecting the information transmitted

Why D is right. D. The rule defines a spurious emission by where it falls and whether it can be reduced, not by whether it causes interference.

Harmonics and other unwanted products from a transmitter are examples. They lie outside the necessary bandwidth and carry nothing useful, so a filter or better adjustment can cut them without losing information.

“Spurious emission. An emission, or frequencies outside the necessary bandwidth of a transmission, the level of which may be reduced without affecting the information being transmitted.”

47 CFR 97.3(a)(43)

Why not C? The definition is not based on causing interference or on a 40 dB figure.

E1B02

Which of the following is an acceptable bandwidth for digital voice or slow-scan TV transmissions made on the HF amateur bands?

  1. A 3 kHz
  2. B 10 kHz
  3. C 15 kHz
  4. D 20 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 3 kHz

Why A is right. A. On HF, these signals may be no wider than a normal voice signal of the same modulation type, and an SSB voice signal is about 3 kHz wide.

The rule uses a communications quality phone signal as the yardstick for bandwidth. A clear single sideband voice signal fills about 3 kHz. Digital voice and slow-scan TV signals sent through an SSB transmitter should fit in about that same space.

“No non-phone emission shall exceed the bandwidth of a communications quality phone emission of the same modulation type.”

47 CFR 97.307(f)(2)

Why not D? 20 kHz is far wider than a communications quality voice signal on HF.

E1B03

Within what distance must an amateur station protect an FCC monitoring facility from harmful interference?

  1. A 1 mile
  2. B 3 miles
  3. C 10 miles
  4. D 30 miles
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 1 mile

Why A is right. A. A nearby transmitter can overload a monitoring station's sensitive receivers, so the rule sets a protection distance of 1600 m, which is 1 mile.

FCC monitoring stations listen for signals across the spectrum. If an amateur station that close causes harmful interference, the FCC can restrict its operation.

“A station within 1600 m (1 mile) of an FCC monitoring facility must protect that facility from harmful interference.”

47 CFR 97.13(b)

Why not B? The rule sets the distance at 1 mile, not 3.

E1B04

What must the control operator of a repeater operating in the 70-centimeter band do if a radiolocation system experiences interference from that repeater?

  1. A Reduce the repeater antenna HAAT (Height Above Average Terrain)
  2. B File an FAA NOTAM (Notice to Air Missions) with the repeater system's ERP, call sign, and six-character grid locator
  3. C Cease operation or make changes to the repeater that mitigate the interference
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Cease operation or make changes to the repeater that mitigate the interference

Why C is right. C. On 70 cm, amateurs must not cause harmful interference to government radiolocation, so the repeater must stop or be changed so the interference ends.

Radiolocation means radar and similar systems. On the 70 cm band, amateurs are secondary to US Government radiolocation stations. A secondary user must both accept interference from them and avoid causing it. The fix can be anything that ends the interference, including going off the air.

“Amateur stations transmitting in the 70 cm band … must not cause harmful interference to, and must accept interference from, stations authorized by the United States Government in the radiolocation service.”

47 CFR 97.303(b)

Why not B? No rule calls for an FAA notice to fix interference to radar.

E1B05

What is the National Radio Quiet Zone?

  1. A An area surrounding the FCC monitoring station in Laurel, Maryland
  2. B An area in New Mexico surrounding the White Sands Test Area
  3. C An area surrounding the National Radio Astronomy Observatory
  4. D An area in Florida surrounding Cape Canaveral
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. An area surrounding the National Radio Astronomy Observatory

Why C is right. C. The Quiet Zone exists to keep radio noise away from the sensitive radio telescopes of the National Radio Astronomy Observatory at Green Bank, West Virginia.

Radio telescopes hunt for very faint signals from space. The rules mark the zone as a coordinate box covering parts of Maryland, Virginia and West Virginia. A beacon or repeater planned inside it must first notify the observatory.

“National Radio Astronomy Observatory at Green Bank, Pocahontas County, WV”

47 CFR 97.203(e)

Why not A? The FCC monitoring station in Maryland has its own 1 mile protection rule, not the Quiet Zone.

E1B06

Which of the following additional rules apply if you are erecting an amateur station antenna structure at a site at or near a public use airport?

  1. A You may have to notify the Federal Aviation Administration and register it with the FCC as required by Part 17 of the FCC rules
  2. B You may have to enter the height above ground in meters, and the latitude and longitude in degrees, minutes, and seconds on the FAA website
  3. C You must file an Environmental Impact Statement with the EPA before construction begins
  4. D You must obtain a construction permit from the airport zoning authority per Part 119 of the FAA regulations
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. You may have to notify the Federal Aviation Administration and register it with the FCC as required by Part 17 of the FCC rules

Why A is right. A. Tall structures near airports can endanger aircraft, so the rule may require FAA notice and FCC registration under Part 17.

Part 17 sets out when a structure must be studied by the FAA and registered with the FCC. The same rule applies to any structure over 200 feet.

“located near or at a public use airport must notify the Federal Aviation Administration and register with the Commission as required by part 17 of this chapter”

47 CFR 97.15(a)

Why not C? No EPA filing is required for an amateur antenna near an airport.

E1B07

To what type of regulations does PRB-1 apply?

  1. A Homeowners associations
  2. B FAA tower height limits
  3. C State and local zoning
  4. D Use of wireless devices in vehicles
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. State and local zoning

Why C is right. C. The rule written from PRB-1 limits what state and local governments may do to amateur antennas, which is where zoning rules come from.

As background, PRB-1 is an FCC order from 1985. It says state and local rules must not prevent amateur communications. It does not cover private agreements, such as many homeowners association rules.

“State and local regulation of a station antenna structure must not preclude amateur service communications.”

47 CFR 97.15(b)

Why not A? PRB-1 is about government regulation, not private homeowners association rules.

E1B08

What limitations may the FCC place on an amateur station if its signal causes interference to domestic broadcast reception, assuming that the receivers involved are of good engineering design?

  1. A The amateur station must cease operation
  2. B The amateur station must cease operation on all frequencies below 30 MHz
  3. C The amateur station must cease operation on all frequencies above 30 MHz
  4. D The amateur station must avoid transmitting during certain hours on frequencies that cause the interference
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The amateur station must avoid transmitting during certain hours on frequencies that cause the interference

Why D is right. D. When the receivers are well designed, the rule does not shut the station down; it bars only certain hours on the frequencies that cause the trouble.

The quiet hours are 8 p.m. to 10:30 p.m. local time, plus 10:30 a.m. to 1 p.m. on Sunday. They apply only after the licensee has been told about the interference.

“the amateur station shall not be operated during the hours from 8 p.m. to 10:30 p.m., local time”

47 CFR 97.121(a)

Why not A? The rule limits certain hours and frequencies; it does not stop all operation.

E1B09

Which amateur stations may be operated under RACES rules?

  1. A Only those club stations licensed to Amateur Extra class operators
  2. B Any FCC-licensed amateur station except a Technician class
  3. C Any FCC-licensed amateur station certified by the responsible civil defense organization for the area served
  4. D Only stations meeting the FCC Part 97 technical standards for operation during an emergency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Any FCC-licensed amateur station certified by the responsible civil defense organization for the area served

Why C is right. C. RACES serves local emergency agencies, so the rule requires a licensed station that the civil defense organization has certified as registered with it.

RACES is the Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service. The station must be a primary, club or military recreation station. Control operators must also be certified as enrolled.

“unless it is an FCC-licensed primary, club, or military recreation station and it is certified by a civil defense organization as registered with that organization”

47 CFR 97.407(a)

Why not B? Any class of license can be used, if the civil defense group certifies the station.

E1B10

What frequencies are authorized to an amateur station operating under RACES rules?

  1. A All amateur service frequencies authorized to the control operator
  2. B Specific segments in the amateur service MF, HF, VHF, and UHF bands
  3. C Specific local government channels
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. All amateur service frequencies authorized to the control operator

Why A is right. A. RACES stations are ordinary amateur stations, so the rule gives them the same frequencies their control operator may already use.

RACES shares those bands with regular amateur use. Only if the President invokes war emergency powers can RACES be limited to specific segments.

“The frequency bands and segments and emissions authorized to the control operator are available to stations transmitting communications in RACES on a shared basis with the amateur service.”

47 CFR 97.407(b)

Why not C? RACES stations are amateur stations and do not use local government channels.

E1B11

What does PRB-1 require of state and local regulations affecting amateur radio antenna size and structures?

  1. A No limitations may be placed on antenna size or placement
  2. B Reasonable accommodations of amateur radio must be made
  3. C Such structures must be permitted when use for emergency communications can be demonstrated
  4. D Such structures must be permitted if certified by a registered professional engineer
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Reasonable accommodations of amateur radio must be made

Why B is right. B. The rule says local antenna rules must not shut out amateur radio, so they must make reasonable room for it with the least regulation needed.

Local governments can still regulate antennas for safety or appearance. They cannot use those powers to ban amateur antennas outright.

“Rather, it must reasonably accommodate such communications and must constitute the minimum practicable regulation to accomplish the state or local authority's legitimate purpose.”

47 CFR 97.15(b)

Why not A? Some limits are allowed, as long as they reasonably accommodate amateur use.
