E2C: Contest and DX operating; remote operation techniques; log data format; contact confirmation; RF network systems

Group E2C of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E2C01

What indicator is required to be used by US-licensed operators when operating a station via remote control and the remote transmitter is located in the US?

  1. A / followed by the USPS two-letter abbreviation for the state in which the remote station is located
  2. B /R# where # is the district of the remote station
  3. C / followed by the ARRL Section of the remote station
  4. D No additional indicator is required
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. No additional indicator is required

Why D is right. D. The ID rule requires only your call sign, and indicators are optional, so nothing extra is needed for a remote station inside the US.

You still send your call at the end of each communication and at least every 10 minutes.

“One or more indicators may be included with the call sign.”

47 CFR 97.119(c)

Why not A? A state abbreviation is not required; any indicator would be optional.

E2C02

Which of the following file formats is used for exchanging amateur radio log data?

  1. A NEC
  2. B ARLD
  3. C ADIF
  4. D OCF
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. ADIF

Why C is right. C. ADIF, the Amateur Data Interchange Format, is the common file format for moving log data between programs.

Logging programs, contest software and online confirmation services all read and write ADIF files. Each contact is stored as tagged fields such as call sign, band, mode, date and time. This lets you move your log from one program to another without retyping it.

Why not A? NEC is antenna modeling software, not a log format.

E2C03

From which of the following bands is amateur radio contesting generally excluded?

  1. A 30 meters
  2. B 6 meters
  3. C 70 centimeters
  4. D 33 centimeters
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 30 meters

Why A is right. A. By long-standing agreement, contests stay off 30 meters, a narrow band shared with other services.

The 30 meter band is only 50 kHz wide, and amateurs there are secondary users. Heavy contest traffic would crowd it quickly. Most contest rules also leave out 17 and 12 meters.

Why not B? 6 meters is popular in VHF contests.

E2C04

Which of the following frequencies can be used for amateur radio mesh networks?

  1. A HF frequencies where digital communications are permitted
  2. B Frequencies shared with various unlicensed wireless data services
  3. C Cable TV channels 41-43
  4. D The 60-meter band channel centered on 5373 kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Frequencies shared with various unlicensed wireless data services

Why B is right. B. Amateur mesh networks often use parts of the 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz amateur bands that overlap channels used by unlicensed Wi-Fi gear.

Because the frequencies overlap, ordinary Wi-Fi hardware can be reprogrammed to run on amateur channels. Licensed hams may then run the gear under amateur rules, which can allow more power and better antennas.

Why not A? HF data channels are far too narrow for a high-speed mesh network.

E2C05

What is the function of a DX QSL Manager?

  1. A Allocate frequencies for DXpeditions
  2. B Handle the receiving and sending of confirmations for a DX station
  3. C Run a net to allow many stations to contact a rare DX station
  4. D Communicate to a DXpedition about propagation, band openings, pileup conditions, etc.
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Handle the receiving and sending of confirmations for a DX station

Why B is right. B. A DX QSL manager handles QSL cards and other confirmations for a DX station so the operator can stay on the air.

A rare station may make thousands of contacts. Answering every card request takes time and postage. A manager, often in another country, holds the log copy and handles requests and replies. Check listings to see where to send your request.

Why not C? Running a net for a DX station is a different job, not QSL handling.

E2C06

During a VHF/UHF contest, in which band segment would you expect to find the highest level of SSB or CW activity?

  1. A At the top of each band, usually in a segment reserved for contests
  2. B In the middle of each band, usually on the national calling frequency
  3. C In the weak signal segment of the band, with most of the activity near the calling frequency
  4. D In the middle of the band, usually 25 kHz above the national calling frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. In the weak signal segment of the band, with most of the activity near the calling frequency

Why C is right. C. The SSB and CW calling frequencies, such as 144.200 MHz, sit in each band's weak-signal segment, so contest activity gathers there.

On VHF and UHF, the low part of each band is used for narrow modes like SSB and CW. Contest operators start near the calling frequency and spread out above and below it.

Why not A? The top of the band is used for FM and repeaters, not weak-signal work.

E2C07

What is the Cabrillo format?

  1. A A standard for submission of electronic contest logs
  2. B A method of exchanging information during a contest QSO
  3. C The most common set of contest rules
  4. D A digital protocol specifically designed for rapid contest exchanges
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A standard for submission of electronic contest logs

Why A is right. A. Sponsors cross-check thousands of logs by software, which needs everyone's log in one shared format, and Cabrillo is that format.

A Cabrillo file is plain text: a header with your call, category and other details, then one line per contact.

Why not B? Cabrillo is for the log you send after the contest, not the on-air exchange.

E2C08

Which of the following contacts may be confirmed through the Logbook of The World (LoTW)?

  1. A Special event contacts between stations in the US
  2. B Contacts between a US station and a non-US station
  3. C Contacts for Worked All States credit
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. Logbook of The World confirms any contact whose details match in both stations' uploaded logs, so A, B and C all qualify.

LoTW is an online system. Each station uploads digitally signed log records. When two records agree on call signs, band, mode and time, the contact is confirmed. That works for US-to-US, US-to-DX, and award credit such as Worked All States.

E2C09

What type of equipment is commonly used to implement an amateur radio mesh network?

  1. A A 2-meter VHF transceiver with a 1,200-baud modem
  2. B A computer running EchoLink to provide interface from the radio to the internet
  3. C A wireless router running custom firmware
  4. D A 440 MHz transceiver with a 9,600-baud modem
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A wireless router running custom firmware

Why C is right. C. Wi-Fi routers already have 2.4 and 5.8 GHz radios that overlap amateur bands, so new firmware turns them into mesh nodes.

The custom software lets the routers link to each other automatically, pass traffic, and identify as amateur stations.

Why not A? A 1,200-baud modem is far too slow for a mesh network.

E2C10

Why do DX stations often transmit and receive on different frequencies?

  1. A Because the DX station may be transmitting on a frequency that is prohibited to some responding stations
  2. B To separate the calling stations from the DX station
  3. C To improve operating efficiency by reducing interference
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. Listening away from its own transmit frequency keeps callers off the DX signal, lets the DX pick a range all callers may use, and speeds up contacts.

This is called split operation. Without it, a crowd of callers on one frequency covers up the very station they want to hear.

E2C11

How should you generally identify your station when attempting to contact a DX station during a contest or in a pileup?

  1. A Send your full call sign once or twice
  2. B Send only the last two letters of your call sign until you make contact
  3. C Send your full call sign and grid square
  4. D Send the call sign of the DX station three times, the words “this is,” then your call sign three times
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Send your full call sign once or twice

Why A is right. A. The DX station must copy your whole call to log you, and sending it once or twice gets it through without adding to the noise.

Repeating your call many times wastes time and covers other callers. Sending only part of your call does not identify your station and leads to confusion.

Why not B? Partial calls do not identify your station and slow things down.

E2C12

What indicates the delay between a control operator action and the corresponding change in the transmitted signal?

  1. A Jitter
  2. B Hang time
  3. C Latency
  4. D Anti-VOX
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Latency

Why C is right. C. Latency is the general name for delay through a system, here the time from your action to the change on the air.

With remote control over the internet, commands and audio take time to travel. A delay of even a fraction of a second can make CW sending or quick contest exchanges hard.

Why not A? Jitter is variation in the delay, not the delay itself.
