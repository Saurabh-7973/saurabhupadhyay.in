T4B: Operating controls: frequency tuning, use of filters, squelch function, AGC, memory channels, noise blanker, microphone gain, receiver incremental tuning (RIT), bandwidth selection, scanning function; Digital transceiver configuration; DMR code plugs and talk groups

Group T4B of the Technician pool (2026–2030) has 11 active questions. One of them is on every Technician exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

T4B01

What is the effect of excessive microphone gain on SSB transmissions?

  1. A Frequency instability
  2. B Distorted transmitted audio
  3. C Increased SWR
  4. D Sideband inversion
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Distorted transmitted audio

Why B is right. B. Distorted transmitted audio.

Microphone gain sets how strongly your voice drives the transmitter. Too much gain pushes the audio stages past their limits, so the peaks get clipped and your voice sounds harsh and hard to understand. It can also spread splatter onto nearby frequencies. Set gain so that normal speech stays clean.

Why not C? SWR depends on the antenna and feed line, not on how loud the audio is.

T4B02

Which of the following can be used to enter a transceiver’s operating frequency?

  1. A The keypad or VFO knob
  2. B The CTCSS or DTMF encoder
  3. C The Automatic Frequency Control
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The keypad or VFO knob

Why A is right. A. The keypad or VFO knob.

You can type a frequency on the radio's keypad or turn the VFO knob, the main tuning dial, to move up and down the band. Both directly set where the radio operates.

Why not B? CTCSS sends a sub-audible tone and DTMF sends touch tones; neither sets the operating frequency.

T4B03

How is squelch adjusted so that a weak FM signal can be heard?

  1. A Set the squelch threshold so that receiver output audio is on all the time
  2. B Turn up the audio level until it overcomes the squelch threshold
  3. C Turn on the anti-squelch function
  4. D Enable squelch enhancement
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Set the squelch threshold so that receiver output audio is on all the time

Why A is right. A. Set the squelch threshold so that receiver output audio is on all the time.

Squelch mutes the speaker until a signal is strong enough to pass a set threshold. A weak signal may never pass it, so you would not hear it. Lowering the threshold until the audio stays open lets even weak signals through, along with some background noise.

Why not B? The volume control acts after the squelch, so turning it up does not open a closed squelch.

T4B04

What does an FM signal sound like when received slightly off frequency?

  1. A The audio increases in pitch
  2. B The audio decrease in pitch
  3. C There is no effect except for reduction in amplitude
  4. D The audio becomes distorted
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The audio becomes distorted

Why D is right. D. The audio becomes distorted.

An FM receiver recovers audio from small shifts in frequency around the channel center. When you are tuned slightly off, the signal sits near the edge of the narrow slice of frequencies the receiver passes, so part of each swing is cut off or recovered unevenly. The voice comes out rough and distorted.

Why not A? A pitch change is what happens to mistuned SSB; FM audio distorts instead.

T4B05

What does the scanning function of an FM transceiver do?

  1. A Checks incoming signal deviation
  2. B Prevents interference to nearby repeaters
  3. C Tunes through a range of frequencies to check for activity
  4. D Tunes through a range of frequencies to determine the antenna’s resonant frequency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Tunes through a range of frequencies to check for activity

Why C is right. C. Tunes through a range of frequencies to check for activity.

Scanning lets the radio step through a list of channels or a band, pausing when it finds a signal. It saves you from tuning by hand to find who is on the air.

Why not D? Scanning listens for signals; it does not measure antenna resonance.

T4B06

Which of the following controls could be used if the voice pitch of a single-sideband signal returning to your CQ call seems too high or low?

  1. A The AGC or limiter
  2. B The bandwidth selection
  3. C The tone squelch
  4. D The RIT or Clarifier
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The RIT or Clarifier

Why D is right. D. The RIT or Clarifier.

If the other station is a little off your frequency, their SSB voice sounds too high or too low. RIT, short for receiver incremental tuning, shifts only your receive frequency a small amount without moving your transmit frequency. Some radios call this control a Clarifier. Adjust it until the voice sounds natural.

Why not A? AGC controls receiver gain; it does not change the pitch of the voice.

T4B07

What is a DMR “code plug”?

  1. A An adapter cable used to connect a DMR radio to a computer for internet access
  2. B Configuration data loaded onto your radio to access repeaters and talkgroups
  3. C An upgrade to DMR programming software provided by the radio manufacturer to accommodate new radio models
  4. D A Coder-Decoder (CODEC) that converts analog voice data to DMR digital data and vice versa
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Configuration data loaded onto your radio to access repeaters and talkgroups

Why B is right. B. Configuration data loaded onto your radio to access repeaters and talkgroups.

A code plug is a file of settings, built with programming software and loaded into a DMR radio. It holds frequencies, color codes, time slots, talkgroups and contacts. Without it, the radio does not know how to reach a repeater or which group to talk to.

Why not D? Converting voice to digital data is the job of the codec, not the code plug.

T4B08

What is the advantage of having a choice of receiver filter bandwidths in a multimode transceiver?

  1. A Permits monitoring several modes simultaneously by selecting a separate filter for each mode
  2. B Permits noise or interference reduction by selecting a bandwidth matching the mode
  3. C Increases the number of frequencies that can be stored in memory
  4. D Increases the amount of offset between receive and transmit frequencies
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Permits noise or interference reduction by selecting a bandwidth matching the mode

Why B is right. B. Permits noise or interference reduction by selecting a bandwidth matching the mode.

Each mode needs a certain width: CW is narrow, SSB voice is wider. A filter wider than the signal lets in extra noise and nearby stations. Choosing a bandwidth that fits the mode keeps the signal and blocks much of the rest.

Why not C? Filter bandwidth has no effect on how many memories the radio can store.

T4B09

How is a specific group of stations selected on a DMR digital voice transceiver?

  1. A By retrieving the frequencies from transceiver memory
  2. B By enabling the group’s CTCSS tone
  3. C By entering the group’s identification code
  4. D By inserting a five-pin, pre-programmed code plug
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. By entering the group’s identification code

Why C is right. C. By entering the group’s identification code.

On DMR, many groups can share one repeater. Each talkgroup has a number, its ID code. Selecting that ID tells the radio and network which group should hear you and which group you will hear.

Why not B? CTCSS tones are used with analog FM repeaters, not to choose DMR talkgroups.

T4B10

Which of the following receiver filter bandwidths provides the best signal-to-noise ratio for SSB reception?

  1. A 500 Hz
  2. B 1000 Hz
  3. C 2400 Hz
  4. D 5000 Hz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 2400 Hz

Why C is right. C. 2400 Hz.

A single-sideband voice signal is about 2400 to 3000 Hz wide. A 2400 Hz filter passes most of the voice while keeping out extra noise. A narrower filter cuts off parts of the voice, and a wider one lets in more noise without adding any voice.

Why not D? 5000 Hz is about twice the SSB voice width, so it adds noise with no gain in signal.

T4B11

Which of the following must be programmed into a D-STAR digital transceiver before transmitting?

  1. A Your call sign
  2. B Your output power
  3. C The codec type being used
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Your call sign

Why A is right. A. Your call sign.

D-STAR sends your call sign in digital form with every transmission. The radio fills that in from its settings, so you must program your call sign before you transmit. Repeaters and gateways also use it to route your signal.

Why not C? The D-STAR codec is fixed by the system; you do not choose or program it.
