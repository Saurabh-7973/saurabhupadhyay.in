G4A: Station configuration and operation

Group G4A of the General pool (2023–2027) has 13 active questions. One of them is on every General exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

G4A01

What is the purpose of the notch filter found on many HF transceivers?

  1. A To restrict the transmitter voice bandwidth
  2. B To reduce interference from carriers in the receiver passband
  3. C To eliminate receiver interference from impulse noise sources
  4. D To remove interfering splatter generated by signals on adjacent frequencies
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. To reduce interference from carriers in the receiver passband

Why B is right. B. The filter puts a very narrow, deep dip in the receiver's response, and you set it right on the unwanted carrier.

A carrier is a steady signal with no voice on it, heard in SSB as a constant whistle. Because the dip is so narrow, the voice around the whistle stays mostly untouched.

Why not D? Splatter is spread over a wide range of frequencies, so a narrow notch cannot remove it.

G4A02

What is the benefit of using the opposite or “reverse” sideband when receiving CW?

  1. A Interference from impulse noise will be eliminated
  2. B More stations can be accommodated within a given signal passband
  3. C It may be possible to reduce or eliminate interference from other signals
  4. D Accidental out-of-band operation can be prevented
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. It may be possible to reduce or eliminate interference from other signals

Why C is right. C. Reversing the sideband moves the receive passband to the other side of the signal you want, which can leave out an interferer that was close on the first side.

When you copy CW, the receiver's filter passband does not sit evenly around the signal. It sits off to one side, set by the sideband in use. A station close above or below you may fall inside that passband. Flip to the opposite sideband and the passband moves to the other side, so that station may drop out.

Why not A? Impulse noise covers a wide range of frequencies, so it is present on both sidebands.

G4A03

How does a noise blanker work?

  1. A By temporarily increasing received bandwidth
  2. B By redirecting noise pulses into a filter capacitor
  3. C By reducing receiver gain during a noise pulse
  4. D By clipping noise peaks
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. By reducing receiver gain during a noise pulse

Why C is right. C. Noise pulses are very short and strong, so muting the receiver for each one removes the noise and costs almost none of the signal.

Engine ignition and some power line problems make sharp pulses that last only millionths of a second. A noise blanker spots each pulse and briefly cuts the receiver's gain. Between pulses the receiver works normally.

Why not D? Clipping only trims the tops of the pulses, so most of each pulse would still pass through.

G4A04

What is the effect on plate current of the correct setting of a vacuum-tube RF power amplifier’s TUNE control?

  1. A A pronounced peak
  2. B A pronounced dip
  3. C No change will be observed
  4. D A slow, rhythmic oscillation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A pronounced dip

Why B is right. B. At the right TUNE setting, the plate current falls to a sharp minimum, called a dip.

The TUNE control brings the amplifier's output tank circuit, a coil and capacitor, to resonance at your operating frequency. Resonance means the coil's and capacitor's effects balance at that frequency. At resonance the tank offers the tube its highest impedance, so the DC plate current drops sharply. You turn the knob for the lowest reading.

Why not A? A rise in plate current tells you the tank is moving away from resonance, not toward it.

G4A05

Why is automatic level control (ALC) used with an RF power amplifier?

  1. A To balance the transmitter audio frequency response
  2. B To reduce harmonic radiation
  3. C To prevent excessive drive
  4. D To increase overall efficiency
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. To prevent excessive drive

Why C is right. C. ALC holds back the drive so the amplifier is not pushed past its limit.

ALC stands for automatic level control. The amplifier sends a control voltage back to the transceiver. When the drive gets too strong, that voltage lowers the transceiver's output. Too much drive makes an amplifier distort and spread its signal onto nearby frequencies.

Why not B? Harmonics are removed by filters; ALC controls how hard the amplifier is driven.

G4A06

What is the purpose of an antenna tuner?

  1. A Reduce the SWR in the feed line to the antenna
  2. B Reduce the power dissipation in the feedline to the antenna
  3. C Increase power transfer from the transmitter to the feed line
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Increase power transfer from the transmitter to the feed line

Why C is right. C. A tuner matches impedances so the transmitter can deliver its power into the feed line.

Impedance is the total opposition a circuit offers to AC. A transmitter works best into the impedance it was designed for, often 50 ohms. A tuner at the transmitter end transforms what the feed line presents into that value, so the transmitter delivers full power without stress. It does not change the conditions on the feed line beyond it.

Why not A? The SWR on the feed line between the tuner and the antenna stays the same, so A is wrong and D fails too.

G4A07

What happens as a receiver’s noise reduction control level is increased?

  1. A Received signals may become distorted
  2. B Received frequency may become unstable
  3. C CW signals may become severely attenuated
  4. D Received frequency may shift several kHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Received signals may become distorted

Why A is right. A. Stronger noise reduction can remove parts of the signal too, so it may sound distorted.

Noise reduction uses digital processing to guess which sounds are noise and remove them. At low settings the guesses are gentle. As you raise the level, it starts removing parts of the voice or tone as well. The result can sound hollow, warbly or robotic. Use the lowest setting that helps.

Why not D? Noise reduction works on the audio and does not change the frequency you are tuned to.

G4A08

What is the correct adjustment for the LOAD or COUPLING control of a vacuum tube RF power amplifier?

  1. A Minimum SWR on the antenna
  2. B Minimum plate current without exceeding maximum allowable grid current
  3. C Highest plate voltage while minimizing grid current
  4. D Desired power output without exceeding maximum allowable plate current
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Desired power output without exceeding maximum allowable plate current

Why D is right. D. LOAD sets how much power the tube delivers to the antenna, so you raise it until you reach your target output, stopping before plate current passes its rated limit.

The LOAD, or COUPLING, control sets how tightly the output circuit is tied to the antenna. More coupling draws more power from the tube and more plate current. Going past the rated plate current overheats and shortens the life of the tube.

Why not B? Very low plate current means little power is being drawn from the tube, so the amplifier is coupled too lightly to deliver its output.

G4A09

What is the purpose of delaying RF output after activating a transmitter’s keying line to an external amplifier?

  1. A To prevent key clicks on CW
  2. B To prevent transient overmodulation
  3. C To allow time for the amplifier to switch the antenna between the transceiver and the amplifier output
  4. D To allow time for the amplifier power supply to reach operating level
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. To allow time for the amplifier to switch the antenna between the transceiver and the amplifier output

Why C is right. C. The delay gives the amplifier's relay time to switch the antenna before RF arrives.

When you key an external amplifier, a relay inside it moves the antenna from bypass to the amplifier output. A relay takes a few thousandths of a second to close and settle. If RF arrives first, the contacts can arc and be damaged. A short delay before RF output lets the switching finish.

Why not D? The amplifier's power supply is already running in standby; the delay is for the relay.

G4A10

What is the function of an electronic keyer?

  1. A Automatic transmit/receive switching
  2. B Automatic generation of dots and dashes for CW operation
  3. C To allow time for switching the antenna from the receiver to the transmitter
  4. D Computer interface for PSK and RTTY operation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Automatic generation of dots and dashes for CW operation

Why B is right. B. You press paddles, and the keyer sends evenly timed elements at the speed you set.

Hold one paddle and a keyer sends a steady stream of dots; hold the other for dashes. It sets the length of each element and the spaces between them, so your timing stays steady even when your hand does not.

Why not A? Switching between transmit and receive is a separate function of the transceiver.

G4A11

Why should the ALC system be inactive when transmitting AFSK data signals?

  1. A ALC will invert the modulation of the AFSK mode
  2. B The ALC action distorts the signal
  3. C When using digital modes, too much ALC activity can cause the transmitter to overheat
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The ALC action distorts the signal

Why B is right. B. ALC action changes the gain while you transmit, and that distorts the data signal.

AFSK sends data as audio tones fed into an SSB transmitter. The tones must pass through cleanly. When ALC kicks in, it varies the transmitter's gain, which bends the tones out of shape. That can widen the signal and cause decoding errors. Set your audio level low enough that the ALC stays at or near zero.

Why not A? ALC adjusts signal level; it does not flip the modulation.

G4A12

Which of the following is a common use of the dual-VFO feature on a transceiver?

  1. A To allow transmitting on two frequencies at once
  2. B To permit full duplex operation -- that is, transmitting and receiving at the same time
  3. C To transmit on one frequency and listen on another
  4. D To improve frequency accuracy by allowing variable frequency output (VFO) operation
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. To transmit on one frequency and listen on another

Why C is right. C. With two VFOs, one can hold your receive frequency and the other your transmit frequency, so the radio switches between them for split operation.

A VFO is the tuning control that sets the radio's frequency. A rare station with many callers often listens a little away from its own frequency. Split lets you hear that station while you call where it is listening.

Why not B? The radio still switches between transmit and receive; it does not do both at the same moment.

G4A13

What is the purpose of using a receive attenuator?

  1. A To prevent receiver overload from strong incoming signals
  2. B To reduce the transmitter power when driving a linear amplifier
  3. C To reduce power consumption when operating from batteries
  4. D To reduce excessive audio level on strong signals
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. To prevent receiver overload from strong incoming signals

Why A is right. A. Cutting the signal level ahead of the first receiver stages keeps a very strong station from driving those stages past what they can handle.

An attenuator reduces signal strength by a fixed number of decibels. Overload causes distortion and false signals across the band. With the input level lowered, the receiver works normally, and weak signals often stay readable.

Why not D? Audio loudness is set by the volume control; overload happens before the audio stage.
