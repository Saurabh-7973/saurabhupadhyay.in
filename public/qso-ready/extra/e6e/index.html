E6E: Semiconductor materials and packages for RF use

Group E6E of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E6E01

Why is gallium arsenide (GaAs) useful for semiconductor devices operating at UHF and higher frequencies?

  1. A Higher noise figures
  2. B Higher electron mobility
  3. C Lower junction voltage drop
  4. D Lower transconductance
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Higher electron mobility

Why B is right. B. Electrons move much more easily through gallium arsenide than through silicon, so GaAs devices work at higher frequencies.

Electron mobility measures how fast electrons drift through a material for a given push. GaAs has roughly five to six times the electron mobility of silicon. Faster electrons let transistors respond to UHF and microwave signals, often with lower noise.

Why not A? A higher noise figure is worse; GaAs is prized for low noise.

E6E02

Which of the following device packages is a through-hole type?

  1. A DIP
  2. B PLCC
  3. C BGA
  4. D SOT
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. DIP

Why A is right. A. A DIP, or dual in-line package, has two rows of pins that pass through holes in the circuit board.

Through-hole parts push their leads through drilled holes and are soldered on the other side. PLCC, BGA and SOT are surface-mount packages that solder to pads on the board's surface. A BGA uses tiny solder balls under the chip; an SOT is a small outline transistor package.

Why not C? A BGA (ball grid array) solders to the board surface through balls underneath.

E6E03

Which of the following materials supports the highest frequency of operation when used in MMICs?

  1. A Silicon
  2. B Silicon nitride
  3. C Silicon dioxide
  4. D Gallium nitride
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Gallium nitride

Why D is right. D. Gallium nitride supports the highest frequencies of these choices, because electrons move very fast in it and it handles high voltage and power.

An MMIC, a monolithic microwave integrated circuit, puts a whole RF amplifier or mixer on one chip. Gallium nitride transistors combine fast electrons with a wide band gap, so they amplify at very high frequencies and power levels. Silicon nitride and silicon dioxide are insulators used inside chips, not transistor materials.

Why not A? Silicon transistors are slower than gallium nitride ones at microwave frequencies.

E6E04

Which is the most common input and output impedance of MMICs?

  1. A 50 ohms
  2. B 300 ohms
  3. C 450 ohms
  4. D 75 ohms
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 50 ohms

Why A is right. A. Most MMICs are designed for 50-ohm input and output, the standard impedance of RF coax and test gear.

Because MMICs are matched to 50 ohms inside the chip, you can connect them directly to 50-ohm coax, filters and other stages with no matching network. That makes them easy building blocks for amplifiers up through microwave frequencies.

Why not D? 75 ohms is common for TV and video cable, not MMICs.

E6E05

Which of the following noise figure values is typical of a low-noise UHF preamplifier?

  1. A 0.5 dB
  2. B -10 dB
  3. C 44 dBm
  4. D -20 dBm
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 0.5 dB

Why A is right. A. A good low-noise UHF preamp has a noise figure well under 1 dB, so 0.5 dB is typical.

Noise figure is how much the preamp worsens the signal-to-noise ratio, in dB. A perfect amplifier would be 0 dB, and a real one can never be below that. Modern low-noise transistors reach a few tenths of a dB at UHF.

Why not B? A negative noise figure would mean the amplifier removes noise, which is impossible.

E6E06

What characteristics of MMICs make them a popular choice for VHF through microwave circuits?

  1. A The ability to retrieve information from a single signal, even in the presence of other strong signals
  2. B Extremely high Q factor and high stability over a wide temperature range
  3. C Nearly infinite gain, very high input impedance, and very low output impedance
  4. D Controlled gain, low noise figure, and constant input and output impedance over the specified frequency range
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Controlled gain, low noise figure, and constant input and output impedance over the specified frequency range

Why D is right. D. MMICs give predictable gain, low noise and steady input and output impedance across their rated frequency range.

An MMIC is a complete RF amplifier on one chip, designed and tested by the maker. Within its listed frequency range you get a known gain, a low noise figure and 50-ohm ports without tuning. That makes it easy to drop into VHF, UHF and microwave projects.

Why not C? Nearly infinite gain and very high input impedance describe an op-amp, not an MMIC.

E6E07

What type of transmission line is often used for connections to MMICs?

  1. A Miniature coax
  2. B Circular waveguide
  3. C Parallel wire
  4. D Microstrip
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Microstrip

Why D is right. D. Microstrip, a copper trace above a ground plane on a circuit board, is the usual way to connect to MMICs.

A microstrip line is a strip of copper on one side of a board, with a solid ground plane on the other side. Its width and the board material set its impedance, usually 50 ohms. The MMIC solders right onto these traces, so there are no connectors or long leads to add stray inductance.

Why not C? Parallel-wire line is balanced and bulky, a poor fit for tiny chips on a board.

E6E08

How is power supplied to the most common type of MMIC?

  1. A Through a capacitor and RF choke connected to the amplifier input lead
  2. B MMICs require no operating bias
  3. C Through a resistor and/or RF choke connected to the amplifier output lead
  4. D Directly to the bias voltage (Vcc) lead
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Through a resistor and/or RF choke connected to the amplifier output lead

Why C is right. C. The common MMIC takes its DC power on its output lead, fed through a resistor or an RF choke that keeps RF off the supply.

Most simple MMIC amplifiers have only input, output and ground connections, so DC reaches the chip through the output pin. A resistor sets the current, and an RF choke, an inductor that blocks RF but passes DC, keeps the signal out of the power supply. Coupling capacitors keep the DC out of the next stage.

Why not D? The common type has no separate supply lead; power shares the output pin.

E6E09

Which of the following component package types have the least parasitic effects at frequencies above the HF range?

  1. A TO-220
  2. B Axial lead
  3. C Radial lead
  4. D Surface mount
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Surface mount

Why D is right. D. Surface-mount parts have little or no lead length, so they add the least stray inductance and capacitance.

Above HF, even a centimeter of lead adds enough inductance to matter. Surface-mount components solder flat onto pads with tiny end contacts. With almost no leads, their parasitic effects, meaning unwanted inductance and capacitance, are far lower than those of axial, radial or TO-220 parts.

Why not A? A TO-220 is a power package with long, thick leads.

E6E10

What advantage does surface-mount technology offer at RF compared to using through-hole components?

  1. A Smaller circuit area
  2. B Shorter circuit board traces
  3. C Components have less parasitic inductance and capacitance
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. Surface mount gives a smaller layout, shorter traces and less parasitic inductance and capacitance, so every choice applies.

Small components let you pack a circuit tightly, which shortens the board traces between them. Short traces and leadless parts both cut unwanted inductance and capacitance. At RF, that keeps circuits closer to their design values.

E6E11

What is a characteristic of DIP packaging used for integrated circuits?

  1. A Extremely low stray capacitance (dielectrically isolated package)
  2. B Extremely high resistance between pins (doubly insulated package)
  3. C Two chips in each package (dual in package)
  4. D Two rows of connecting pins on opposite sides of package (dual in-line package)
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Two rows of connecting pins on opposite sides of package (dual in-line package)

Why D is right. D. DIP stands for dual in-line package: two parallel rows of pins on opposite sides of the body.

The DIP is the classic rectangular chip package, with pins spaced 0.1 inch apart along its two long sides. It plugs into a socket or through holes in a board, which made it a long-time standard for hobby projects.

Why not C? Dual refers to the two rows of pins, not two chips.

E6E12

Why are DIP through-hole package ICs not typically used at UHF and higher frequencies?

  1. A Excessive dielectric loss
  2. B Epoxy coating is conductive above 300 MHz
  3. C Excessive lead length
  4. D Unsuitable for combining analog and digital signals
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Excessive lead length

Why C is right. C. A DIP's long leads add too much stray inductance and capacitance at UHF and above.

Each DIP pin runs several millimeters from the chip inside to the board. At UHF that length acts like a small inductor, and neighboring pins act like small capacitors. These unwanted reactances shift tuning and let signals couple between pins. Surface-mount packages avoid this.

Why not A? The package material is not the limit; the lead length is.
