E0A: Safety: RF radiation hazards; hazardous materials; grounding

Group E0A of the Amateur Extra pool (2024–2028) has 12 active questions. One of them is on every Amateur Extra exam. Each is shown word for word; open it to see the correct answer and our explanation.

E0A01

What is the primary function of an external earth connection or ground rod?

  1. A Prevent static build up on power lines
  2. B Lightning charge dissipation
  3. C Reduce RF current flow between pieces of equipment
  4. D Protect breaker panel from power surges
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Lightning charge dissipation

Why B is right. B. A ground rod gives the huge charge of a lightning strike a low-resistance path into the earth, away from your house and gear.

A ground rod is a metal stake driven deep into the soil. Lightning carries enormous current, and the rod plus heavy wire spreads that current into the earth. The rod does little for RF: a long wire out to a rod acts like an antenna at radio frequencies. Short bonding straps between equipment inside the station handle RF and keep chassis at the same voltage.

Why not C? Keeping RF current from flowing between units is the job of short bonding straps between chassis, not a distant earth rod.

E0A02

When evaluating RF exposure levels from your station at a neighbor’s home, what must you do?

  1. A Ensure signals from your station are less than the controlled maximum permissible exposure (MPE) limits
  2. B Ensure signals from your station are less than the uncontrolled maximum permissible exposure (MPE) limits
  3. C Ensure signals from your station are less than the controlled maximum permissible emission (MPE) limits
  4. D Ensure signals from your station are less than the uncontrolled maximum permissible emission (MPE) limits
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Ensure signals from your station are less than the uncontrolled maximum permissible exposure (MPE) limits

Why B is right. B. Neighbors neither know about nor control their exposure, so the stricter general-population (uncontrolled) limits apply to them.

MPE means maximum permissible exposure: the most RF a person may receive, averaged over time. Controlled limits cover people who know they are exposed and can manage it, such as you in your own station. Uncontrolled limits cover the general public and are lower or equal at every frequency. A neighbor's home is public ground for this purpose.

Why not D? MPE stands for maximum permissible exposure, not emission; the limit is about what reaches a person's body.

E0A03

Over what range of frequencies are the FCC human body RF exposure limits most restrictive?

  1. A 300 kHz - 3 MHz
  2. B 3 - 30 MHz
  3. C 30 - 300 MHz
  4. D 300 - 3000 MHz
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 30 - 300 MHz

Why C is right. C. Between 30 and 300 MHz a human body is close to a resonant length, so it soaks up RF energy most efficiently and the limits are lowest there.

An adult body behaves a bit like an antenna. Its whole-body absorption peaks in the range where the wavelength is a few times the body's height, which falls between 30 and 300 MHz. The FCC sets its lowest power-density limits in that range. Below and above it, the body absorbs less of the same field, so the limits rise.

Why not D? From 300 MHz the limits rise with frequency up to 1500 MHz and stay flat above, so they are looser than in the 30 to 300 MHz range.

E0A04

When evaluating a site with multiple transmitters operating at the same time, the operators and licensees of which transmitters are responsible for mitigating over-exposure situations?

  1. A Each transmitter that produces 20 percent or more of its MPE limit in areas where the total MPE limit is exceeded
  2. B Each transmitter operating with a duty cycle greater than 25 percent
  3. C Each transmitter that produces 5 percent or more of its MPE limit in areas where the total MPE limit is exceeded
  4. D Each transmitter operating with a duty cycle greater than 50 percent
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Each transmitter that produces 5 percent or more of its MPE limit in areas where the total MPE limit is exceeded

Why C is right. C. FCC rules share the cleanup among every transmitter that adds more than 5 percent of its own limit at the spot where the total is exceeded.

At a shared tower site, signals from many transmitters add up. If the combined exposure somewhere is too high, each licensee whose signal there is above 5 percent of its limit must help fix it. Smaller contributors are too minor to count. Duty cycle changes average power, but it is not the test for who is responsible.

“the shared responsibility of all licensees whose RF sources produce, at the area in question, levels that exceed 5% of the applicable exposure limit”

47 CFR 1.1307

Why not A? A 20 percent cutoff would let some real contributors off the hook; the rule draws the line at 5 percent.

E0A05

What hazard is created by operating at microwave frequencies?

  1. A Microwaves are ionizing radiation
  2. B The high gain antennas commonly used can result in high exposure levels
  3. C Microwaves are in the frequency range where wave velocity is higher
  4. D The extremely high frequency energy can damage the joints of antenna structures
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The high gain antennas commonly used can result in high exposure levels

Why B is right. B. A microwave dish focuses its power into a narrow beam, so even modest transmitter power can make a strong field in front of it.

Antenna gain does not create power; it squeezes the same power into one direction. A dish with 30 dB of gain makes its beam about 1,000 times stronger than an antenna radiating equally in all directions. Standing in that beam, or near the feed, can exceed exposure limits quickly. Microwaves heat tissue, and the eyes are especially sensitive.

Why not A? Microwaves are non-ionizing: each photon carries far too little energy to knock electrons off atoms.

E0A06

Why are there separate electric (E) and magnetic (H) MPE limits at frequencies below 300 MHz?

  1. A The body reacts to electromagnetic radiation from both the E and H fields
  2. B Ground reflections and scattering cause the field strength to vary with location
  3. C E field and H field radiation intensity peaks can occur at different locations
  4. D All these choices are correct
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. All these choices are correct

Why D is right. D. Each listed effect is real, so a safe check must measure the electric and magnetic fields separately.

Close to an antenna, in the near field, the electric (E) field and magnetic (H) field do not keep a fixed ratio. The body responds to both. Reflections from the ground and nearby objects make each field rise and fall from spot to spot, and their peaks can sit in different places. Measuring only one could miss a hot spot of the other.

E0A07

What is meant by “100% tie-off” regarding tower safety?

  1. A All loose ropes and guys secured to a fixed structure
  2. B At least one lanyard attached to the tower at all times
  3. C All tools secured to the climber’s harness
  4. D All circuit breakers feeding power to the tower must be tied closed with tape, cable, or ties
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. At least one lanyard attached to the tower at all times

Why B is right. B. With 100% tie-off you are never unclipped: at least one lanyard stays hooked to the tower, even while you move the other.

Climbers carry two lanyards. To pass a brace or other obstacle, you hook the second one on the far side before unhooking the first. A slip at any moment is then caught by the fall-arrest harness. A climber with a single lanyard is unprotected every time it is moved.

Why not A? Securing ropes and guy wires is good practice, but it does not keep the climber's body from falling.

E0A08

What does SAR measure?

  1. A Signal attenuation ratio
  2. B Signal amplification rating
  3. C The rate at which RF energy is absorbed by the body
  4. D The rate of RF energy reflected from stationary terrain
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The rate at which RF energy is absorbed by the body

Why C is right. C. SAR, specific absorption rate, tells how fast body tissue absorbs RF energy, in watts per kilogram.

Absorbed RF turns into heat. SAR tells how many watts end up in each kilogram of tissue, averaged over time and body mass. It is used to set limits for devices held close to the body, such as phones and handheld radios. The power-density limits used for stations are set so that SAR stays at safe levels.

Why not A? There is no exposure term called signal attenuation ratio; SAR is about the body, not the signal path.

E0A10

When must an RF exposure evaluation be performed on an amateur station operating on 80 meters?

  1. A An evaluation must always be performed
  2. B When the ERP of the station is less than 10 watts
  3. C When the station’s operating mode is CW
  4. D When the output power from the transmitter is less than 100 watts
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. An evaluation must always be performed

Why A is right. A. Apart from tiny sources of 1 mW or less, no power level or mode is automatically excused on 80 meters, so every practical station must be checked.

An evaluation can be short. For many stations it means using the FCC exemption formula, based on power, frequency and distance to people, to show the station is below the threshold. If it is not, you do a fuller calculation or a measurement. Either way, you must do the check before you can claim compliance.

Why not C? CW lowers average power because the transmitter is off between dots and dashes, but it does not remove the need to check.

E0A11

To what should lanyards be attached while climbing?

  1. A Antenna mast
  2. B Guy brackets
  3. C Tower rungs
  4. D Tower legs
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Tower legs

Why D is right. D. Tower legs are the main load-bearing members, strong enough to stop a falling climber.

A fall puts a sudden, large force on the anchor point. The legs carry the whole tower's weight and are built to take big loads. Rungs and cross braces are thinner and may bend or break, and a lanyard can slide along them. Masts and guy brackets are not designed to catch a person.

Why not C? Rungs are lighter parts than the legs and may fail under the shock of a fall.

E0A12

Where should a shock-absorbing lanyard be attached to a tower when working above ground?

  1. A Above the climber’s head level
  2. B To the belt of the fall-arrest harness
  3. C Even with the climber's waist
  4. D To the next lowest set of guys
Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Above the climber’s head level

Why A is right. A. Anchoring above your head keeps any fall short, so the lanyard and shock absorber stop you before you gain much speed.

If the anchor is below you, you drop past it before the lanyard goes tight, and the fall is much longer. A longer fall means more speed and a harder jolt on your body and the anchor. Clipping overhead limits the drop to about the lanyard's slack plus the absorber's stretch.

Why not C? At waist level you would fall about a full lanyard length before being caught.
